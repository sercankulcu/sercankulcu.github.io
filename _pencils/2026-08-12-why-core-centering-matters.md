Cut across a good pencil and the graphite core should sit close to the center of the wooden casing.

Centering matters most during sharpening. If the core is badly off center, one side of the graphite is exposed before the other. The resulting point becomes asymmetric and may be more fragile.

The problem begins during manufacturing, when the core is placed into grooves between wooden slats. Accurate milling, alignment, and bonding are necessary before the larger sandwich is shaped into individual pencils.

Minor variation is normal in a mass-produced object. I do not reject a pencil because the core is a fraction of a millimeter off. The question is whether the misalignment affects use.

Collectors can inspect centering at the unsharpened end if the graphite is visible, but sharpening provides the clearest evidence. A long-point sharpener exaggerates alignment problems because more of the core is exposed.

Core centering is one of those invisible quality measures that suddenly becomes obvious when it fails. It is a tiny geometric detail with a direct effect on daily use.
