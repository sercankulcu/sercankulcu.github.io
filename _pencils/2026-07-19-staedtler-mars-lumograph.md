The blue barrel of the Staedtler Mars Lumograph has become a visual shorthand for serious graphite work.

The modern Mars Lumograph range is unusually broad. Staedtler lists grades extending from very soft 12B through 10H. That spread makes the line useful for understanding just how different two objects called "graphite pencils" can become.

Core diameter changes with some grades as well. Staedtler specifies thicker leads for the softest pencils and slimmer leads through much of the harder range. That makes mechanical sense: extremely soft drawing grades benefit from more material, while harder technical grades can remain precise.

What I like about a full range is the continuity. Instead of switching brands when I need a different value, I can move along one manufacturer's interpretation of the hardness scale. That makes tonal studies more predictable.

For collecting, Mars Lumograph is interesting because the design is recognizable but not frozen in time. Older examples, packaging, logos, and country markings create a long history to investigate.

It is the sort of pencil that works equally well as a tool and as a reference specimen. A single HB tells you something. A sequence from H through the B grades tells you how an entire graphite philosophy is constructed.
