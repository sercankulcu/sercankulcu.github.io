The letters printed near the end of a pencil look simple, but they describe one of the most useful systems in the pencil world. H indicates the harder side of the scale, B indicates the softer and generally darker side, and HB sits around the practical middle. F is another middle grade found in many ranges.

The key is the formulation of the core. Graphite produces the characteristic dark mark; clay helps control hardness. Faber-Castell explains that a higher proportion of clay produces a harder-writing lead. That relationship is why pencils can be manufactured for very different jobs without changing the basic form of the tool.

A 2H pencil usually holds a fine point longer and makes a lighter mark than a 2B. A 2B generally feels softer, deposits more material, and produces darker writing or shading. Move farther toward grades such as 6B or 8B and the pencil becomes increasingly useful for broad dark values. Move toward 4H or 6H and fine, pale lines become easier to maintain.

The important collector's warning is that the scale is not perfectly standardized across manufacturers. An HB from one company can feel darker, smoother, or harder than an HB from another. That is not necessarily a defect. It is one of the reasons comparative testing is interesting.

For everyday writing I often start around HB, B, or 2B and then adjust according to the paper. Smooth paper, rough paper, handwriting pressure, point angle, and even personal speed change the result. The grade printed on the barrel is a useful coordinate, not an absolute law.
