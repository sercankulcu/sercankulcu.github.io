Good photographs turn a pencil collection into a searchable visual archive.

I begin with a neutral background and soft, even light. Glossy lacquer and metallic foil create reflections, so direct flash often hides the very lettering I want to record.

A full-length photograph establishes the overall design. Then I take closer views of the imprint, grade, end cap, ferrule, eraser, and sharpened point if the pencil is used. For a box, I photograph every side rather than only the front.

Scale is useful. A small ruler in one documentation image records length and diameter, although I usually keep the prettier catalog photograph free of measurement tools.

File names should carry information. Brand-model-grade-variant is far more useful than IMG_4837. I also keep acquisition information in a spreadsheet rather than trying to embed every detail in the photograph.

Old foil stamps can be difficult to read. Tilting the pencil slightly and making several exposures often reveals lettering through reflected light.

Photography is not separate from collecting. Once a collection grows, memory stops being reliable. Images become the map that lets you find, compare, and study what you already own.
