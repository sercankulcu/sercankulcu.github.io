Barrel shape changes a pencil more than photographs suggest.

Hexagonal pencils are stable on the desk and give the fingers clear reference surfaces. They are the classic compromise: easy to manufacture, easy to print, and comfortable for many writing styles.

Round pencils feel continuous in the hand. There are no edges to index finger position, which some writers find more relaxed. The disadvantage is obvious the first time one rolls off a table.

Triangular pencils deliberately guide the grip. They are common in educational products and can also feel comfortable for adults who like a broad resting surface under each finger. Some are standard diameter; others are oversized for children or long drawing sessions.

The amount of rounding matters too. A sharply cut hexagon feels different from one whose edges are softened under multiple lacquer coats. Diameter and paint texture may influence comfort as much as geometry.

Collectors should therefore record shape, not just brand and grade. Two pencils with similar cores can produce completely different physical experiences because one is round and the other hexagonal.

A pencil is a writing material, but it is also a handle. The barrel is the interface between the graphite and the hand.
