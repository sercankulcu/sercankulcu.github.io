Apple announced the Apple Watch at a September 9, 2014 event alongside the iPhone 6, with Tim Cook calling it "the most personal device we've ever created." The watch went on sale April 24, 2015 in three collections — Apple Watch Sport (aluminum, starting at $349), Apple Watch (stainless steel, starting at $549), and Apple Watch Edition (18-karat gold, starting at $10,000) — in 38mm and 42mm case sizes with interchangeable bands across nine styles.

The S1 chip was a fully encapsulated system-in-package: Apple sealed the entire logic board, RAM, and storage in epoxy before mounting it in the case, protecting it from sweat and humidity. Sensors included a heart-rate monitor using green LEDs and photodiodes (photoplethysmography), a three-axis accelerometer, and a gyroscope. The Digital Crown — a rotating mechanical crown on the case side — let users scroll and zoom without fingers covering the small display, and the Taptic Engine sent directional taps to the wrist for turn-by-turn navigation and notifications. At launch, Watch apps ran only their UI on the watch itself; all application logic executed on the paired iPhone over Bluetooth 4.0, severely limiting what developers could build and making the watch dependent on being within Bluetooth range.

Apple released watchOS 2 in September 2015, moving app execution fully onto the watch and exposing the microphone, Digital Crown, haptics, and sensors directly to third-party apps. GPS arrived in Series 2 in September 2016, enabling accurate workout tracking without an iPhone. LTE cellular arrived in Series 3 in September 2017. The ECG electrocardiogram sensor arrived in Series 4 in September 2018 with FDA clearance — the first over-the-counter ECG product, capable of detecting atrial fibrillation. By Series 4, Apple Watch had become the world's best-selling watch by unit volume, outselling the entire Swiss watch industry combined.
