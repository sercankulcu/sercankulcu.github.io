title: 'Do Not Believe Your Thinking Easily'

Thoughts feel convincing because they happen inside our own mind. That does not make them true. A fear can feel like a prediction. An assumption can feel like evidence. A memory can feel exact even when details have changed over time.

This is one reason I try not to believe every thought immediately. The mind is good at creating stories.

If someone does not answer a message, we can invent a reason before we have any information. If a project goes badly, we may decide that the whole idea was wrong. If one person criticizes us, we can suddenly imagine that everyone thinks the same thing.

These conclusions feel real because emotion gives them weight. A small pause can help. What evidence do I have? What else could explain this? Would I judge the same situation this way if it happened to a friend?

This is not about distrusting ourselves completely. Constant self-doubt can be just as harmful. The goal is to create a little distance between a thought and a conclusion. I especially value this habit when I am angry or afraid. Those emotions are real, but they are not always good investigators. Sometimes the mind needs time before it becomes a reliable narrator again.
