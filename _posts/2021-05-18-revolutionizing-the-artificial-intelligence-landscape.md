Artificial intelligence is surrounded by excitement. In 2021, that excitement often appears beside other fashionable technology discussions such as cryptocurrencies, NFTs, the metaverse, and Web3. I think AI deserves attention for a different reason. It is already solving real problems.

Deep learning has changed computer vision. Speech recognition has improved dramatically. Natural-language models can generate and analyze text in ways that felt unrealistic a few years ago. Reinforcement learning has produced impressive results in games and control problems.

The important progress is not only in spectacular demonstrations. AI is also entering medicine, manufacturing, recommendation systems, translation, fraud detection, agriculture, and scientific research. At the same time, we should be careful with hype. A model can perform extremely well on one benchmark and still fail badly outside the conditions it learned from. Large models require significant data and computing power.

Bias in data can become bias in predictions. A confident output is not the same as understanding. For me, the interesting question is not whether AI will "take over everything." The better question is where learning systems provide real value and where ordinary software remains the simpler solution. AI is becoming a normal part of computing. That may be more important than any dramatic headline.
