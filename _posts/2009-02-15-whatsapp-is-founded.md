Jan Koum and Brian Acton incorporated WhatsApp Inc. in California on February 24, 2009. Both had recently left Yahoo — Koum after nine years as an infrastructure engineer, Acton after eleven years in various engineering and product roles. They had applied to Facebook in 2009 and been rejected. Koum's initial concept was for an app that would show a short status beside each contact in your address book — not messaging, but presence: "at the gym," "on a call," "battery low."

Apple's push notification system, introduced in June 2009 with iPhone OS 3.0, changed the trajectory. WhatsApp could send a notification when a contact updated a status, and users began responding to status updates as if they were messages. Koum rebuilt the app around direct messaging in version 2.0 in August 2009, and downloads jumped from a few thousand to 250,000 within days. The key structural decision was using phone numbers as identity — verification happened via SMS, and the contact list was already populated with everyone the user knew, eliminating the discovery problem that had slowed adoption of earlier mobile messaging apps like BlackBerry Messenger (which required a PIN exchange) and AIM (which required a screen name).

WhatsApp reached 200 million active users by April 2013 and 450 million by February 2014, when Facebook announced its $19 billion acquisition. The team had grown to only about 55 engineers — an unusually small number attributed to their Erlang backend, which handled fault-tolerant concurrent connections efficiently, and a deliberate policy of no advertisements, no games, and no gimmicks that kept the product surface narrow. Acton co-founded Signal Foundation in 2018 after leaving Facebook over disagreements about monetization and data privacy; Koum resigned the same year for similar reasons.
