Amazon announced the Echo in November 2014 as an invitation-only product, priced at $179 ($99 for Amazon Prime members). It went on general sale in June 2015. The device was a black cylinder 9.25 inches tall containing two speakers — a 2-inch tweeter and a 2.5-inch woofer — and a seven-microphone array with 360° sound pickup. The microphone array used beamforming to isolate the direction of the speaker's voice and suppress background noise, allowing Alexa to hear commands from across a room while music was playing.

A small local processor ran a wake-word model continuously, detecting “Alexa” with low power consumption while the main speech recognition and natural-language processing ran on Amazon's cloud servers. After hearing the wake word, Echo streamed audio to AWS, which returned a response within roughly 1.5 seconds for simple queries. Amazon opened the Alexa Skills Kit in June 2015, enabling third-party developers to build voice-driven integrations — by 2017, over 15,000 Skills were available, covering smart-home control, games, news briefings, and commerce tied to Amazon's own shopping platform.

The Echo created a new product category: the always-on, ambient voice interface. Google Home followed in November 2016 and Apple HomePod in February 2018. The privacy trade-off — a microphone perpetually listening for a trigger word, with audio clips optionally retained on company servers for quality improvement — became a recurring concern. Amazon settled FTC allegations about retaining children's voice data under COPPA in 2023, paying $25 million. The underlying question of where the boundary between convenience and surveillance lay for a home microphone remained unresolved throughout the decade.
