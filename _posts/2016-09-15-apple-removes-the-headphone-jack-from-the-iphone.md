Apple announced the iPhone 7 and 7 Plus on September 7, 2016, with both models conspicuously missing the 3.5mm headphone jack that had been on every iPhone since the original in 2007. Apple marketing chief Phil Schiller justified the removal on stage with the word "courage," which became a widely mocked summary of the decision. In the box: a Lightning-to-3.5mm adapter (so existing wired headphones still worked with the included adapter plugged in, though simultaneously charging was then impossible) and EarPods with a Lightning connector. Separately, Apple introduced AirPods at $159 — its own wireless earbuds, not shipping until December 2016.

The internal engineering benefit was real if modest: removing the 3.5mm jack freed approximately 1mm of vertical space and one potential water ingress point, contributing to iPhone 7's IP67 dust and water resistance rating (the first waterproofed iPhone). Apple used a redesigned home button — now a pressure-sensitive surface with Taptic Engine feedback simulating a click rather than a mechanical button — partly enabled by the space reclaimed. Bluetooth 5.0 was not yet available (both AirPods and iPhone 7 used Bluetooth 4.2); the AirPods' W1 chip handled pairing, switching between Apple devices, and audio delivery, achieving Apple-to-AirPod latency low enough for video-synchronized audio.

Other Android manufacturers — Samsung, LG, Google — publicly mocked Apple's decision in 2016 while retaining their own headphone jacks. Samsung removed the headphone jack from the Galaxy S20 in February 2020; Google removed it from the Pixel 2 in October 2017 (one year after Apple). By 2021, the headphone jack was absent from most flagship Android phones. AirPods became Apple's fastest-growing product category by revenue; by 2020, Apple had sold over 100 million pairs. The transition Apple forced in 2016 normalized a wireless audio market that had been growing slowly and accelerated it by years.
