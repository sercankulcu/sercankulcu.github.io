There was a time when saying "I do not know" made me uncomfortable. It felt like admitting weakness.

This is especially easy to feel in technical work. People ask questions and expect answers. Experience creates another kind of pressure. The more you know, the more difficult it can feel to admit that there is still something you do not understand.

But there is always something we do not understand. Actually, there are many things. I slowly learned that pretending to know is much more dangerous than admitting uncertainty. A simple "I do not know, but I can check" keeps the door open. Pretending closes it.

The strange thing is that knowledge itself often makes uncertainty more visible. When we are beginners, a topic may look small. After learning more, we begin to see how large it really is.

This can be humbling. It can also be comforting. Nobody reaches a point where every question is easy.

I now like people who can say "I do not know" without fear. It usually means they care more about the truth than about looking impressive.

That is a quality I want to protect in myself. There is no shame in not knowing. The real problem begins when curiosity ends.
