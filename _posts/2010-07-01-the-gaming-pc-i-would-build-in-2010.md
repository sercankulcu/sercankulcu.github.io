If I were building a gaming PC on **July 1, 2010**, this is the machine I would actually want. I am not simply choosing the most expensive part in every category. The priority is gaming performance, then reliability, cooling and enough headroom to keep the system useful for years.

### The build

- **CPU:** Intel Core i7-930
- **GPU:** NVIDIA GeForce GTX 480 1.5 GB
- **Motherboard:** ASUS P6X58D-E (X58)
- **Memory:** 6 GB DDR3-1600 (3×2 GB)
- **Storage:** Intel X25-M 160 GB SSD + 1 TB WD Caviar Black
- **Power supply:** Corsair HX850 850 W
- **Case:** Cooler Master HAF X
- **CPU cooling:** Noctua NH-D14
- **Gaming target:** 1920×1080 or 1920×1200 at very high settings

### Why this CPU?

I would not buy the $999 six-core i7-980X just for games. Most 2010 titles cared more about clock speed than six cores, and the i7-930 left a huge amount of budget for the graphics card while still offering four cores, eight threads and strong overclocking.

### Why this GPU?

The GTX 480 was NVIDIA’s $499 single-GPU flagship in mid-2010. The Radeon HD 5970 could be faster, but it was a dual-GPU card with the usual scaling and frame-pacing complications. I would rather own one very fast GPU.

### What I would pay

A complete build would be roughly **$1,900–2,200** in the U.S. market of the period. This is an approximate enthusiast-system total, not a claim that every retailer sold every part at MSRP on July 1.

### The weak point

Heat and noise. The GTX 480 was famously power-hungry, so the large HAF X, NH-D14 and an oversized quality PSU are deliberate choices.

### My verdict

This is the machine I would want beside a 1080p high-refresh monitor in 2010: fast where games actually need speed, without spending $1,000 on a CPU for bragging rights.

The date matters. I am judging only hardware that was available by July 1, not products announced or released later in the year. That makes each build a snapshot of what I could realistically have assembled at that moment.
