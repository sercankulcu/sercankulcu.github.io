Modern life makes change very public.

We announce goals.

Share progress.

Count reactions.

Sometimes the announcement creates a feeling of movement before any real movement has happened.

I have started to appreciate quieter change.

Learn without announcing it.

Build something before explaining it.

Change a habit before turning it into an identity.

Silence removes an audience.

That can be uncomfortable because nobody is there to reward the beginning.

It can also be freeing.

You are allowed to fail privately.

You can change direction without explaining why.

You can discover that the goal was wrong before it becomes part of the image you created online.

Real reinvention is usually boring from outside.

It is repeated work.

Reading.

Practicing.

Saying no.

Leaving something.

Starting again.

The visible result arrives much later.

I think this is similar to programming.

The interesting part of a finished program is visible in seconds.

The hours of quiet debugging are not.

Maybe personal change works the same way.

The strongest parts are often built where nobody is watching.
