The web is becoming larger faster than I can follow it. There are more blogs, technology sites, news pages, and personal websites every month. Visiting all of them one by one is possible, but after a while it starts to feel like checking twenty mailboxes every morning.

RSS gives me a calmer way to do this. A website publishes a feed, I add that feed to a reader, and new posts arrive in one place. Instead of remembering which sites may have changed, I can let the reader tell me. It is a simple idea, but it changes the way I move around the web.

What I like most is the feeling of choosing the sources myself. There is no single front page deciding what I should read. I can follow a programmer's small personal blog beside a large technology publication. A quiet writer with fifty readers can appear in the same list as a famous website.

RSS also makes the web feel less temporary. A social website can encourage us to keep refreshing because something new may appear at any moment. A feed reader feels different. New items wait until I have time. I can close the program and return later without feeling that I missed the conversation.

There are problems, of course. Some sites publish only short excerpts because they want readers to visit the original page. Feeds are not always formatted well. After subscribing to too many sources, the unread count can become another kind of pressure. A tool that was supposed to simplify reading can become a large pile of things I feel guilty about not reading.

That has taught me to unsubscribe more easily. Following a website is not a lifetime promise. If a source no longer teaches me anything or gives me joy, I can remove it. A smaller list that I actually read is better than an impressive list that becomes background noise.

I do not know whether RSS will remain the main way people follow websites in the future. The internet changes too quickly for that kind of confidence. For now, I enjoy the quiet control it gives me. The web comes to me, but I still decide which parts are invited.
