Microsoft announced the Surface Pro 3 on May 20, 2014, with retail availability beginning June 20, 2014 at prices from $799 (Intel Core i3, 64GB) through $1,949 (Core i7, 512GB). The device used a 12-inch display at 2160×1440 resolution (216 PPI, 3:2 aspect ratio) — a change from the 10.6-inch 16:9 display used on Surface Pro 1 and 2, motivated by the observation that 16:9 screens waste vertical space when displaying documents, making them awkward for productivity work while they're optimized for video. The Surface Pen (256 pressure levels, AAA battery, magnetic attachment to the right edge) and the redesigned kickstand (adjustable to any angle from 22° to 150°, versus the Surface Pro 2's two fixed positions) were positioned to make the device genuinely viable for handwriting, annotation, and note-taking workflows.

Microsoft's explicit marketing position was "the tablet that can replace your laptop." Surface Pro 1 (February 2013) and Pro 2 (October 2013) had failed this claim: both used 16:9 screens suited to video consumption, both weighed over 900 grams without the Type Cover, and both ran hot under sustained CPU load with inadequate cooling for Ivy Bridge and Haswell processors respectively. Surface Pro 3 weighed 800 grams (versus the Pro 2's 907 grams) at 9.1mm thin, used Haswell Core i3, i5, and i7 processors with better thermal management, and introduced a Type Cover 3 that lay flat against the screen when closed. The keyboard covered the bottom third of the screen when attached in tablet position, a compromise that later revisions (Surface Pro 4, October 2015) addressed with an improved cover design.

The Surface Pro 3 sold approximately 1 million units in its first few months and generated meaningful revenue — Microsoft's Surface division reported $908 million in revenue for fiscal Q4 2014, the first quarter that included Pro 3 sales. The design established the "modern detachable" laptop category that competitors subsequently matched: Apple announced the iPad Pro with Smart Keyboard in September 2015, and Samsung, Lenovo, and HP introduced their own 12-inch productivity tablets with full x86 Windows through 2015–2016. Surface Pro 4 (October 2015) refined the formula — thinner at 8.4mm, lighter at 786g, with a 5-core Intel Skylake option and a redesigned Type Cover 4 with glass trackpad — extending the product into a sustained Microsoft franchise that continued through Surface Pro 11 (2024).
