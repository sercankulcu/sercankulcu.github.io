Students who want to become software engineers often ask the same questions.

Which language should I learn?

Do grades matter?

Do I need mathematics?

How many projects should I build?

Should I learn web development, mobile development, artificial intelligence, or something else?

My answer usually begins with the same idea: do not try to build the whole career at once.

Learn one language well enough to solve problems.

Study data structures and algorithms.

Understand basic operating systems, databases, networking, and software engineering.

These foundations make later choices easier.

Projects matter because they turn knowledge into decisions. A small project is enough at first. The goal is not to impress a company. The goal is to discover what you do not understand yet.

Internships are valuable for the same reason. They show that professional software includes meetings, version control, testing, maintenance, old code, deadlines, and teamwork.

Grades matter, but they are not the entire story. A strong transcript can open some doors. So can a good project, clear communication, or real experience.

Mathematics matters differently depending on the field. Every developer needs logical thinking. Areas such as graphics, machine learning, signal processing, and scientific computing require much more mathematics than ordinary application development.

English is also important because so much technical material is written in English.

The most important habit is learning how to learn.

The technology you study today will not be the last technology you need.

That is not a problem.

It is the job.
