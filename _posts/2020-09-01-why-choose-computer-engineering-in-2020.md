The year 2020 has made the importance of digital infrastructure unusually visible. Work, education, communication, shopping, entertainment, and many public services have moved online at a scale few people expected, and in many cases at a speed that revealed which systems were ready and which were not.

## What 2020 revealed about computing

Zoom began 2020 with roughly 10 million daily meeting participants. By April, that number had reached 300 million. The platform had to scale its infrastructure at a rate that would ordinarily take years to plan and execute. AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure all reported record growth as businesses that had delayed cloud migration accelerated it under necessity. Remote work that had been a negotiation for years became a default for technical teams in weeks.

The cracks also showed. Healthcare systems built on legacy software struggled to share patient data across institutions. Schools discovered that "moving online" meant far more than uploading existing course materials. Government services designed for in-person access ran into queues, crashes, and capacity failures. The lesson was not only that computing matters — it was that poorly designed computing systems fail visibly and at scale when they are suddenly load-tested by millions of users.

Behind those services are software systems, networks, databases, cloud platforms, and security technologies. Computer Engineering is one of the disciplines that teaches how these pieces fit together — not just how to write code that works on your machine, but how to design systems that hold up under real conditions.

## What the degree teaches

A Computer Engineering education provides a broad foundation for these challenges. Students typically study programming, algorithms, data structures, operating systems, databases, networks, computer architecture, and mathematics. Later, they can specialise in areas such as cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, cloud systems, embedded devices, or software engineering.

The remote work shift of 2020 has also changed what collaboration looks like. Software development was already international, with open-source projects and distributed teams crossing time zones. This year demonstrated at scale that technical collaboration across cities and countries works — and that communication, documentation, and teamwork are skills as important as the technical ones.

There is also a durable lesson about technology trends that this year illustrates clearly. Tools can change quickly, and the right architecture for 2015 may be inadequate for 2020. A student who understands algorithms, operating systems, networking, and software design will be better prepared for the next change than someone who knows only one current framework.

Computer Engineering is challenging. It requires persistence, mathematics, and many hours of practice. But it also gives students the ability to create useful systems from an idea and a computer. In 2020, the world received a very clear demonstration of how much modern life depends on computing. For students who want to understand, improve, and build those systems, Computer Engineering is a meaningful choice.
