Google launched the public DNS resolvers 8.8.8.8 and 8.8.4.4 in December 2009. DNS is the Internet's distributed naming system: it translates human-readable domain names into addresses and other records computers use.

Most users historically relied on DNS resolvers provided by their Internet service provider. Google offered a globally operated alternative and focused on speed, reliability, and security. Running a large recursive resolver requires caching huge numbers of records, following delegations across the DNS hierarchy, handling malicious traffic, and distributing queries across data centers.

The launch made DNS infrastructure visible to ordinary users in a way it rarely had been before. Public resolvers later became common, with competitors emphasizing privacy, filtering, or encrypted DNS. The simple address 8.8.8.8 also became one of the Internet's most recognizable troubleshooting tools.
