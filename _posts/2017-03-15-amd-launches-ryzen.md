AMD launched the first Ryzen desktop processors in March 2017, introducing the Zen microarchitecture and ending years in which Intel had faced relatively weak competition in high-performance x86 CPUs.

Zen was a major redesign rather than another update to AMD's Bulldozer family. It added simultaneous multithreading, a much larger instruction window, improved branch prediction, stronger cache hierarchy, and much higher instructions-per-clock performance. The first Ryzen 7 chips offered eight cores and sixteen threads at prices that challenged Intel's more expensive enthusiast processors.

Ryzen changed the desktop CPU market quickly. Core counts increased, pricing pressure returned, and AMD regained credibility in servers with EPYC. The launch also showed how much architectural efficiency matters: AMD's comeback was driven by redesigning the core, not simply adding clock speed.
