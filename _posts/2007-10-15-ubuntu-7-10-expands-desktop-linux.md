Ubuntu 7.10 “Gutsy Gibbon” was released in October 2007 with a stronger focus on desktop polish. Compiz desktop effects were enabled by default on supported hardware, and the distribution improved monitor configuration, printing, and automatic handling of graphics drivers.

Ubuntu also introduced fast desktop search through Tracker and continued simplifying tasks that had traditionally required manual Linux configuration. These details mattered because desktop adoption is often limited less by the kernel than by dozens of small hardware and usability problems.

Gutsy arrived during a period when Linux distributions were competing not only on packages and performance but also on whether a non-expert could install the system and immediately use Wi-Fi, graphics acceleration, printers, and media. Ubuntu's six-month releases made these improvements visible quickly, while also revealing how much desktop Linux depended on cooperation from hardware vendors.
