Sony's PlayStation 4 launched November 15, 2013 in North America at $399, followed by Microsoft's Xbox One on November 22, 2013 at $499. Both used AMD's "Jaguar" x86-64 CPU core architecture (8 cores at 1.6 GHz in PS4, 1.75 GHz in Xbox One) and AMD GCN (Graphics Core Next) GPU architectures, representing a sharp architectural departure from the previous generation: PS3 had used IBM's Cell processor (a heterogeneous multi-core with 7 Synergistic Processing Elements running at 3.2 GHz) and PS4 replaced it entirely with x86-64 that any developer with PC experience could program directly. Xbox 360 had used a PowerPC-based IBM Xenon CPU. The x86-64 transition eliminated the platform-specific optimization work that had defined PS3 development (where Cell's SPE parallelism required explicit task allocation), making initial multi-platform games significantly cheaper to develop and port.

The memory architectures differed in ways that affected performance characteristics for developers. PS4 used a unified 8 GB GDDR5 pool (176 GB/s bandwidth) accessible to both CPU and GPU, allowing developers to allocate memory between CPU tasks and GPU framebuffers dynamically rather than managing separate CPU RAM and GPU VRAM budgets. Xbox One used 8 GB DDR3 (68.3 GB/s system bandwidth) plus 32 MB ESRAM (204 GB/s) — the ESRAM served as a high-bandwidth buffer for the GPU's most performance-critical framebuffer operations, but required developers to explicitly stage data between DDR3 and ESRAM in game engines. The GPU performance gap was significant: PS4's GPU delivered 1.84 TFLOPS versus Xbox One's 1.31 TFLOPS, a difference of about 40% that showed in cross-platform comparisons throughout the generation (games running at native 1080p on PS4 often ran at 900p or upscaled on Xbox One).

Xbox One launched with significant DRM controversy: Microsoft initially announced that all games would require periodic online check-in and restrict used-game sales, drawing widespread consumer backlash. Microsoft reversed both policies in June 2013 before launch. The Xbox One bundle included Kinect at launch ($499 was the Kinect-included price); Microsoft introduced a Kinect-free Xbox One bundle at $399 in June 2014. PS4 outsold Xbox One substantially throughout the generation: PS4 reached 100 million units by July 2020, while Xbox One sold approximately 50 million units total. Both consoles also marked the end of optical media for game delivery as primary: while both included Blu-ray drives, digital downloads via PlayStation Network and Xbox Live became the majority delivery mechanism for both platforms by the end of the generation.
