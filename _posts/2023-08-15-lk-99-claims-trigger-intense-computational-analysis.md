Claims about a material called LK-99 spread rapidly in July and August 2023 after researchers suggested it might be a room-temperature, ambient-pressure superconductor. Laboratories and computational researchers around the world rushed to test the claim.

Computational work used electronic-structure methods such as density functional theory to estimate how the proposed crystal structure might behave. Those calculations could identify unusual electronic bands or plausible mechanisms, but they could not by themselves prove superconductivity.

Experimental replication ultimately failed to support the original claim. The episode was a useful example of how modern scientific verification mixes simulation, open preprints, rapid online collaboration, and physical laboratory work—and why exciting computational results still require experimental evidence.
