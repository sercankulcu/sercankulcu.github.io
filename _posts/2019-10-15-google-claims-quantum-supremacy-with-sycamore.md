Google published a Nature paper in October 2019 claiming “quantum supremacy” with its 53-qubit Sycamore processor. The machine performed a specially designed random-circuit sampling task that Google estimated would take a classical supercomputer vastly longer.

The experiment did not solve a useful business problem. Its purpose was to show that a programmable quantum device could perform one computation beyond practical classical simulation. Qubits are fragile, so the processor required extremely low temperatures and careful calibration to maintain gate fidelity.

IBM disputed Google's estimate of the classical runtime and argued that a supercomputer with a better simulation method could complete the task much faster. The debate itself was useful: quantum advantage depends not only on quantum hardware but on how efficiently classical algorithms improve in response.
