Google introduced the first commercial Chromebooks in May 2011, with hardware from Samsung and Acer. The machines ran Chrome OS and were designed around a browser-first model rather than the traditional idea of installing desktop applications locally.

Chrome OS used a Linux kernel, automatic updates, verified boot, and user data synchronized with Google's online services. The system was intentionally difficult to modify accidentally: each boot could check whether important system components had been altered. If corruption was detected, recovery was simpler than on a conventional desktop installation.

Chromebooks became especially successful in schools because administrators could manage large fleets centrally and users could sign into almost any device to recover their environment. Their rise showed that low maintenance and predictable management could matter more than compatibility with decades of desktop software.
