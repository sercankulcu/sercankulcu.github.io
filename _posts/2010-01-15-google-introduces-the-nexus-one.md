Google unveiled the Nexus One at a press event on January 5, 2010, with the phone going on sale the same day at $179 with a T-Mobile contract or $529 unlocked — a price significantly higher than competitors expected from a carrier-free unlocked Android phone at the time. HTC manufactured the hardware; Google designed the software experience and sold the phone directly from google.com/phone without requiring a carrier store or contract. The phone ran Android 2.1 (Eclair) on a 1 GHz Qualcomm Snapdragon QSD8250 processor with 512MB RAM, a 3.7-inch 480×800 AMOLED display, and a 5MP camera.

The direct-sale model was Google's attempt to demonstrate that smartphones could be sold like computers — unlocked, carrier-independent, and updated by the device maker — rather than through carrier subsidies that gave operators control over software modifications, bloatware, and update timing. The model failed commercially: most US consumers expected carrier financing (a $199 subsidized phone felt cheaper than a $529 phone regardless of total cost), T-Mobile's customer-service lines were unprepared for the volume of support calls, and Google closed the online store for consumer sales in May 2010, transitioning Nexus devices to carrier and retail channels.

Google continued the Nexus program through hardware partners — Samsung (Nexus S 2010, Galaxy Nexus 2011), ASUS (Nexus 7 tablets 2012–2013), LG (Nexus 4 2012, Nexus 5 2013), and Motorola (Nexus 6 2014) — as a platform for releasing clean Android with rapid updates and setting a software-compatibility baseline for developers. The Nexus line was retired in 2016 when Google launched Pixel, taking full control of design, manufacturing oversight (through HTC), camera software, and the Tensor chip beginning in 2021.
