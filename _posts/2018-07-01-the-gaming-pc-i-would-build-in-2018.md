If I were building a gaming PC on **July 1, 2018**, this is the machine I would actually want. I am not simply choosing the most expensive part in every category. The priority is gaming performance, then reliability, cooling and enough headroom to keep the system useful for years.

### The build

- **CPU:** Intel Core i7-8700K
- **GPU:** NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080 Ti 11 GB
- **Motherboard:** ASUS ROG Maximus X Hero
- **Memory:** 16 GB DDR4-3200
- **Storage:** Samsung 970 EVO 1 TB NVMe
- **Power supply:** Seasonic Focus Plus Gold 750 W
- **Case:** Fractal Design Meshify C
- **CPU cooling:** Noctua NH-D15
- **Gaming target:** 1440p/144 Hz or 4K/60

### Why this CPU?

Coffee Lake finally moved Intel’s mainstream gaming flagship to six cores. Contemporary testing called the 8700K an incredibly powerful gaming CPU, and those extra cores gave it a better future than the 7700K.

### Why this GPU?

RTX had not been announced by July 1. The 1080 Ti was therefore still the card I would actually buy rather than waiting for an unknown product. Its 11 GB frame buffer remained exceptional.

### What I would pay

A complete build would be roughly **$1,850–2,100** in the U.S. market of the period. This is an approximate enthusiast-system total, not a claim that every retailer sold every part at MSRP on July 1.

### The weak point

This is a strange year because the GPU is unchanged. Buying at mid-year means accepting that a new NVIDIA generation may appear soon.

### My verdict

I would rather own six months of excellent gaming than spend six months waiting. The 8700K finally gives the legendary 1080 Ti a CPU platform with real longevity.

The date matters. I am judging only hardware that was available by July 1, not products announced or released later in the year. That makes each build a snapshot of what I could realistically have assembled at that moment.
