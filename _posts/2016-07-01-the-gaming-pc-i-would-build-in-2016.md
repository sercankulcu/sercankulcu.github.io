If I were building a gaming PC on **July 1, 2016**, this is the machine I would actually want. I am not simply choosing the most expensive part in every category. The priority is gaming performance, then reliability, cooling and enough headroom to keep the system useful for years.

### The build

- **CPU:** Intel Core i7-6700K
- **GPU:** NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080 8 GB
- **Motherboard:** ASUS Maximus VIII Hero (Z170)
- **Memory:** 16 GB DDR4-3000
- **Storage:** Samsung 950 Pro 512 GB NVMe + 2 TB HDD
- **Power supply:** Corsair RM750x
- **Case:** Fractal Design Define S
- **CPU cooling:** Corsair H110i
- **Gaming target:** 1440p/144 Hz, with credible 4K gaming

### Why this CPU?

The 6700K was the fast, unlocked Skylake gaming choice. Four cores look modest now, but games in 2016 still rewarded clock speed heavily and DDR4/Z170 gave the platform a modern foundation.

### Why this GPU?

The GTX 1080 went on sale May 27, with partner cards expected from $599. NVIDIA’s 16 nm Pascal architecture delivered a rare jump in both performance and efficiency and made one-card 1440p high-refresh gaming feel easy.

### What I would pay

A complete build would be roughly **$1,850–2,100** in the U.S. market of the period. This is an approximate enthusiast-system total, not a claim that every retailer sold every part at MSRP on July 1.

### The weak point

Early availability and Founders Edition pricing could push the real GPU cost above MSRP. I would wait for a good partner cooler rather than pay extra for the reference card.

### My verdict

This is one of the clearest builds in the series. If I loved games in 2016, the GTX 1080 is the part around which I would design everything else.

The date matters. I am judging only hardware that was available by July 1, not products announced or released later in the year. That makes each build a snapshot of what I could realistically have assembled at that moment.
