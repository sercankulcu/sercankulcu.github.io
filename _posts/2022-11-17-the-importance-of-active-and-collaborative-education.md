A student can sit quietly through an entire lesson and still learn very little.

This is one weakness of passive education.

Listening is useful, but understanding becomes stronger when students have to do something with the information.

Ask a question.

Explain an idea.

Solve a problem.

Compare two approaches.

Argue for a decision.

Build a small example.

These actions make knowledge less comfortable, and that is often good.

Collaborative learning adds another layer.

When students work together, they hear explanations in language different from the teacher's language.

One student may notice a detail another missed.

Explaining an idea to a classmate also reveals whether we actually understand it.

Seminars, discussions, group exercises, and short student presentations can make a class more active.

Of course, group work is not automatically good. One person can do everything while others watch.

Tasks need enough structure that everybody has a reason to participate.

I do not think active learning means removing lectures.

Sometimes a clear explanation is exactly what students need.

The balance matters.

A lecture can give the map.

Activity gives students a chance to walk on it.
