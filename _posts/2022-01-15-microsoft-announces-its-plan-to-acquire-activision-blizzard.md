Microsoft announced the acquisition of Activision Blizzard on January 18, 2022 for $68.7 billion in cash — $95 per share, a 45% premium over Activision's closing price on January 14. If completed, it would be the largest acquisition in Microsoft's history and the largest in gaming history, surpassing Take-Two's $12.7 billion acquisition of Zynga (also announced January 2022). Activision Blizzard's portfolio included Call of Duty (the best-selling annual game franchise globally), World of Warcraft, Diablo, Overwatch, Candy Crush (King subsidiary), Hearthstone, and StarCraft. CEO Bobby Kotick, who was facing shareholder pressure over workplace misconduct allegations at Activision Blizzard, would eventually exit after closing.

Microsoft's strategic motivation centered on Xbox Game Pass, its $14.99/month subscription with roughly 25 million subscribers in January 2022. Call of Duty alone sold approximately 30 million copies per year at $60–$70; including it in Game Pass day-one would make Game Pass substantially more valuable and potentially convert millions of annual CoD buyers into subscribers. Microsoft had already acquired ZeniMax Media (Doom, Elder Scrolls, Fallout) for $7.5 billion in March 2021 and made Bethesda games Game Pass exclusives, establishing the pattern of using exclusive content to differentiate the subscription.

The UK's CMA (Competition and Markets Authority) blocked the deal in April 2023 over concerns about Microsoft's cloud gaming market position. Microsoft restructured the transaction — licensing Activision's cloud gaming rights to Ubisoft for 15 years — which addressed the CMA's concerns, and the deal closed on October 13, 2023. Sony's PlayStation had been Microsoft's primary opponent in regulatory proceedings, arguing that making Call of Duty exclusive to Xbox would harm PlayStation users; Microsoft ultimately committed to 10-year agreements keeping Call of Duty on PlayStation and Nintendo platforms.
