If I were building a gaming PC on **July 1, 2011**, this is the machine I would actually want. I am not simply choosing the most expensive part in every category. The priority is gaming performance, then reliability, cooling and enough headroom to keep the system useful for years.

### The build

- **CPU:** Intel Core i5-2500K
- **GPU:** NVIDIA GeForce GTX 580 1.5 GB
- **Motherboard:** ASUS P8Z68-V Pro
- **Memory:** 8 GB DDR3-1600
- **Storage:** Crucial m4 256 GB SSD + 1 TB WD Caviar Black
- **Power supply:** Corsair AX750 750 W
- **Case:** Corsair Obsidian 650D
- **CPU cooling:** Cooler Master Hyper 212+
- **Gaming target:** 1080p at maximum settings, with enough headroom for 120 Hz in lighter games

### Why this CPU?

The tempting choice was the i7-2600K, but contemporary builders repeatedly noted that Hyper-Threading added little to gaming. The 2500K was cheaper, easy to overclock and became one of the great gaming CPUs of its generation.

### Why this GPU?

The GTX 580 remained the fastest conventional single-GPU GeForce. I would accept its poor value over the GTX 570 because this build is about the PC I would most enjoy owning, not the cheapest way to reach 60 fps.

### What I would pay

A complete build would be roughly **$1,600–1,900** in the U.S. market of the period. This is an approximate enthusiast-system total, not a claim that every retailer sold every part at MSRP on July 1.

### The weak point

The GPU gives diminishing returns for its price. At normal 1080p settings a GTX 570 could make more financial sense.

### My verdict

Sandy Bridge plus a GTX 580 is the clean 2011 enthusiast formula. The CPU is rational; the GPU is where I allow myself to be unreasonable.

The date matters. I am judging only hardware that was available by July 1, not products announced or released later in the year. That makes each build a snapshot of what I could realistically have assembled at that moment.
