Intel released the first Core 2 Duo desktop processors in July 2006. The launch was important because it marked a clear break from the Pentium 4 era, when Intel had pushed clock frequency aggressively but struggled with heat and power consumption.

Core 2 used Intel's new Core microarchitecture, derived partly from the more efficient Pentium M design rather than from NetBurst. It improved instructions executed per clock, used a shared L2 cache between two cores, and delivered much better performance per watt. In practice, a lower-clocked Core 2 chip could outperform faster-clocked Pentium 4 processors while consuming less power.

The product helped change the language of CPU competition. Raw GHz numbers became less meaningful, while architecture, cache behavior, multi-core scaling, and energy efficiency became central. That shift still shapes modern processor design.
