The COVID-19 pandemic has forced many software teams to work remotely for much longer than they expected. Software development was already more remote-friendly than many professions, but working from home occasionally is very different from building an entire working culture around distance.

The first change is communication. In an office, many questions are answered through small conversations that are never written down. Remote work makes those invisible exchanges harder. Teams need clearer documentation, more deliberate messages, and better decisions about which conversations deserve a meeting.

Video meetings solve some problems and create others. A day filled with calls can be more tiring than a day in an office because attention is constantly directed toward a screen. I have become more appreciative of asynchronous communication. A good written message gives people time to think and does not require everyone to be available at the same minute.

Remote work also changes visibility. In an office, effort can be confused with physical presence. Online, the focus can move toward results. That can be healthier, but only if managers trust people and expectations are clear.

There are personal trade-offs too. Removing commuting gives time back, but the boundary between work and home can disappear. A laptop on the kitchen table makes it easy to continue for another hour because there is no physical journey that ends the workday.

I do not know how much of this will remain after the pandemic. I suspect software teams will not return completely to the old model. Too many people and companies have now experienced another way of working.

The long-term lesson may not be that remote work is better. It may be that work has more possible shapes than we assumed.
