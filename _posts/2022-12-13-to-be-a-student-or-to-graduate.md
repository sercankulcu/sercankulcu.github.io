Being a student and being a graduate require different habits.

As a student, many problems arrive already prepared.

The course has a syllabus.

The assignment has a deadline.

The exam tells you which questions matter.

Professional life is less organized.

The problem itself may be unclear.

Requirements can change.

Nobody may know the best answer.

A graduate therefore needs more than technical knowledge.

Communication becomes more important.

So does responsibility.

If you do not understand a task, you need to ask.

If a deadline is unrealistic, you need to say it early.

If you make a mistake, hiding it usually makes the problem larger.

Another change is that learning becomes self-directed.

There is no final semester after which knowledge is complete.

A software engineer keeps learning because the work keeps changing.

I think university should prepare students for this transition by giving them some open problems, teamwork, projects, and chances to make decisions.

Still, the transition can feel surprising.

Graduation looks like an ending.

In practice, it is the moment when a more independent kind of learning begins.
