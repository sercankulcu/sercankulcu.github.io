Google opened the Chrome Web Store in December 2010 as a distribution system for browser applications, extensions, and themes. It reflected Google's belief that web applications could become a primary form of software, especially on the emerging Chrome OS platform.

Some “apps” were essentially packaged links to websites, while others used browser capabilities and local storage to behave more like installed software. Extensions could modify browser behavior through controlled APIs. Centralized distribution gave Google a way to manage discovery, updates, and permissions.

The store foreshadowed a broader shift in desktop software. Browsers were becoming platforms with their own extension ecosystems, and operating systems were adopting app-store distribution models. The boundaries between website, browser extension, progressive web app, and locally installed program became increasingly blurred.
