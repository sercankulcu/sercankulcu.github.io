October 29, 2023 marks the centenary of the Republic of Turkey.

One hundred years is long enough to contain several generations and short enough that the founding period still feels close through photographs, documents, family memories, and institutions.

The Republic was proclaimed on October 29, 1923, after years of war and enormous social change.

Mustafa Kemal Atatürk and the generation around him imagined a state built on national sovereignty and a broad program of modernization.

Over the following century, Turkey experienced political crises, economic difficulties, social conflicts, achievements, reforms, disasters, and periods of rapid development.

No honest national history can be reduced to only triumph or only failure.

A centenary should allow room for both pride and reflection.

For me, the strongest part of the anniversary is continuity.

People born into completely different worlds have lived under the same republic.

Institutions changed.

Cities changed.

Technology transformed daily life.

Yet October 29 remained a shared date.

A republic is not preserved only by ceremonies.

It continues through law, education, public institutions, civic responsibility, and the willingness of each generation to improve what it inherited.

One hundred years deserves celebration.

It also deserves a question:

What kind of second century do we want to build?
