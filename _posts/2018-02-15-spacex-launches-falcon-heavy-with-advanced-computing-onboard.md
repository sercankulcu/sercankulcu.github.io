SpaceX launched Falcon Heavy for the first time in February 2018. Although the spectacle centered on rockets, the mission also illustrated how deeply modern launch systems depend on redundant computers, software, sensors, and real-time control.

SpaceX's flight computers continuously combine measurements from inertial sensors, engines, and vehicle systems while adjusting guidance and control commands. Reliability is achieved partly through redundancy: multiple computers can run calculations in parallel so a single failure does not necessarily end the mission.

The synchronized landing attempts of the two side boosters demonstrated another computing challenge. Each booster had to estimate its position and velocity, restart engines at precise times, steer using grid fins, and land autonomously. Modern rocketry is therefore as much a distributed control-software problem as a mechanical engineering problem.
