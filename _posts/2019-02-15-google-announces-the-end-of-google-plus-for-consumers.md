Google shut down the consumer version of Google+ in 2019 after years of weak adoption and the discovery of privacy bugs in its APIs. One software flaw had exposed profile information that users might have expected to remain private.

The incident was not a dramatic intrusion into passwords; it was a permissions and API-design problem. Applications could receive access to data connected with a user's profile in ways Google's privacy controls did not make obvious. Later investigation found another bug, accelerating the shutdown schedule.

Google+ became a cautionary story about platform maintenance. A social network does not become low-risk when users stop paying attention to it. Old APIs, permission models, and stored personal data still require security work as long as the service remains online.
