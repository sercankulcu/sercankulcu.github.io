Google shut down the consumer version of Google+ on April 2, 2019. The shutdown had been announced October 8, 2018, when Google disclosed that a software bug introduced in March 2015 and patched in March 2018 had exposed the private profile fields of up to 500,000 Google+ accounts to third-party app developers through the People API — specifically, apps that were granted access to public profile data could also read private profile fields (name, email address, occupation, gender, age) that users had intended to share only with specific people. Google had not publicly disclosed this bug when it was discovered in March 2018 — citing that no evidence of misuse had been found — a decision criticized after The Wall Street Journal reported it in October 2018, suggesting Google's non-disclosure was influenced by concerns about regulatory and reputational consequences following the Cambridge Analytica/Facebook scandal (March 2018). A second API bug was found in November 2018, affecting approximately 52.5 million users by exposing additional profile fields (name, email address, occupation, age) to apps that users had not explicitly authorized, accelerating the shutdown timeline from August 2019 to April 2, 2019.

Google+ had launched June 28, 2011, initially invitation-only, and initially attracted users quickly through novelty and Google's forced integration across its products. Google+ profiles were required to comment on YouTube (mandatory 2013–2015), to set Google account profile photos, and were surfaced in Gmail and Google search results. The social network introduced "Circles" (sorting contacts into named groups for selective sharing) as its differentiating feature versus Facebook's binary friend/not-friend model, and Google Hangouts for video calls. However, engagement metrics consistently suggested that most Google+ accounts were created reluctantly (for access to other Google products) rather than for social use: a 2012 report estimated that the average Google+ user spent under 7 minutes per month on the site (compared to Facebook's approximately 6 hours/month). The "Ghost Town" characterization became standard shorthand for Google+'s engagement problem. Google's mandatory real-name policy (2011–2014) and the YouTube/Google+ forced integration generated substantial user backlash; the YouTube comment-to-Google+ requirement was removed in November 2015.

The Google+ shutdown affected approximately 300 million accounts (many inactive) and required Google to provide data export tools (Google Takeout) to allow users to download their posts, photos, and communities before shutdown. Google+ for G Suite (enterprise) continued operating under a different governance after the consumer shutdown, rebranded as Google Currents in 2020 before being discontinued in July 2023. The Google+ experience contributed to the EU's GDPR enforcement context (GDPR had taken effect May 25, 2018, weeks before the March 2018 bug discovery), raising questions about whether Google's non-disclosure of the March 2018 bug had violated GDPR's Article 33 breach notification requirement (which mandates notifying supervisory authorities within 72 hours of discovering a breach likely to result in risk to individuals' rights). The episode became a cited example in debates about social network maintenance debt: legacy APIs with permissive access scopes that predate modern privacy expectations required ongoing auditing, not just initial compliance review, as regulatory standards and user expectations evolved over time.
