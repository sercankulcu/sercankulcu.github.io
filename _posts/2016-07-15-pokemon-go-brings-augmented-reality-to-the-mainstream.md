Pokémon Go launched in July 2016 and turned location-based augmented-reality gaming into a global phenomenon. The game used GPS and map data to place virtual creatures and locations into the player's physical surroundings.

Its most visible “AR” mode overlaid Pokémon on the live camera feed, but the deeper technical system was geographic. Niantic used a map of real-world points of interest, location services, mobile networking, and server-side game state to coordinate millions of players moving through physical space.

The launch also exposed the difficulty of operating at sudden Internet scale. Servers struggled with demand, and outages became common during the first weeks. Pokémon Go demonstrated that successful AR applications depend as much on maps, networking, battery use, sensors, and backend capacity as on computer-generated graphics.
