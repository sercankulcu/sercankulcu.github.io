If I were building a gaming PC on **July 1, 2013**, this is the machine I would actually want. I am not simply choosing the most expensive part in every category. The priority is gaming performance, then reliability, cooling and enough headroom to keep the system useful for years.

### The build

- **CPU:** Intel Core i7-4770K
- **GPU:** NVIDIA GeForce GTX 780 3 GB
- **Motherboard:** ASUS Z87-Pro
- **Memory:** 16 GB DDR3-1866
- **Storage:** Samsung 840 Pro 256 GB SSD + 2 TB HDD
- **Power supply:** Corsair AX760 760 W
- **Case:** Fractal Design Define R4
- **CPU cooling:** Noctua NH-D14
- **Gaming target:** 2560×1440 at high/ultra settings

### Why this CPU?

Haswell was not a dramatic gaming leap over Ivy Bridge, but the 4770K was the new unlocked platform and offered eight threads for the years ahead. Since I am building fresh, I would start on Z87 rather than buy the previous socket.

### Why this GPU?

The GTX 780 arrived in May with 3 GB of GDDR5 and much of the performance character of the far more expensive Titan. Reviews described it as an ultra-high-end card that could approach Titan performance with tuning.

### What I would pay

A complete build would be roughly **$1,850–2,100** in the U.S. market of the period. This is an approximate enthusiast-system total, not a claim that every retailer sold every part at MSRP on July 1.

### The weak point

The GPU is expensive and the cheaper GTX 770 offers better value. I am paying for 1440p headroom rather than price/performance.

### My verdict

For 2013 I want a quiet, serious 1440p machine rather than a flashy benchmark rig. The 4770K and GTX 780 fit that goal.

The date matters. I am judging only hardware that was available by July 1, not products announced or released later in the year. That makes each build a snapshot of what I could realistically have assembled at that moment.
