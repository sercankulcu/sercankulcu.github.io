Online courses are becoming difficult to ignore.

Universities and new platforms are putting lectures, exercises, and complete courses on the internet. A student can sit at home and listen to a professor from another country. A person with a job can study at night. Someone can explore a subject without formally joining a university.

I find this very exciting.

For most of history, access to a good teacher depended heavily on location. The internet weakens that limit.

But watching a lecture is not the same as learning.

This is the part that interests me most.

It is easy to click "enroll." It is harder to continue after the first excitement disappears. At home, nobody notices if you stop. There is no classroom door to walk through and no teacher looking at the empty chair.

Online learning gives freedom, but freedom also requires discipline.

I do not think online courses will simply replace universities. A university is not only a collection of lectures. It is also classmates, questions, deadlines, laboratories, arguments, and the experience of learning with other people.

Still, online courses can change what a classroom is for.

If students can watch explanations before class, maybe class time can be used for discussion and problem solving. Maybe the teacher becomes less of a person who delivers information and more of a person who helps students use it.

We are still early in this change.

Some online courses will disappear. Some ideas will fail.

But education has gained another door, and I do not think that door will close.
