If I were building a gaming PC on **July 1, 2014**, this is the machine I would actually want. I am not simply choosing the most expensive part in every category. The priority is gaming performance, then reliability, cooling and enough headroom to keep the system useful for years.

### The build

- **CPU:** Intel Core i7-4790K
- **GPU:** NVIDIA GeForce GTX 780 Ti 3 GB
- **Motherboard:** ASUS Maximus VII Hero (Z97)
- **Memory:** 16 GB DDR3-2133
- **Storage:** Samsung 840 EVO 500 GB SSD + 2 TB WD Black
- **Power supply:** Seasonic X-750 750 W
- **Case:** Fractal Design Define R4
- **CPU cooling:** Noctua NH-D15
- **Gaming target:** 1440p ultra settings

### Why this CPU?

The 4790K had just pushed Intel’s mainstream flagship to a 4.0 GHz base clock. For games that still favored fast individual cores, that mattered more to me than buying an expensive many-core workstation platform.

### Why this GPU?

On July 1 the Maxwell GTX 980 did not exist yet. The GTX 780 Ti, launched at $699, was still NVIDIA’s gaming performance crown and was faster than the original Titan in many games.

### What I would pay

A complete build would be roughly **$1,900–2,150** in the U.S. market of the period. This is an approximate enthusiast-system total, not a claim that every retailer sold every part at MSRP on July 1.

### The weak point

Three gigabytes of VRAM is the obvious limitation. At 1440p it is fine in 2014, but it is the component I would expect to replace first.

### My verdict

This is a late-Kepler monster: very fast, mature and straightforward. I would build it knowing the next GPU generation is coming, but I would not wait months just to own a computer.

The date matters. I am judging only hardware that was available by July 1, not products announced or released later in the year. That makes each build a snapshot of what I could realistically have assembled at that moment.
