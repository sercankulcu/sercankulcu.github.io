Apple opened Vision Pro preorders on January 19, 2024, selling out the first wave in minutes; early pre-order confirmations showed delivery dates in late February. Review embargoes lifted February 1, 2024, one day before the retail launch on February 2. The $3,499 starting price (with prescription lens inserts adding $149–$299) resulted in a device that journalists handled as a premium technology artifact rather than a consumer product, and reviewer consensus reflected that tension: extraordinary engineering at a price and form factor that limited its audience.

The hardware received near-universal technical praise: the two Sony micro-OLED displays at 23 million pixels total — calibrated per unit at Apple — produced images reviewers described as the sharpest and most color-accurate they had seen in any headset. Passthrough (seeing the real world through cameras) was the best on any headset, running at 96 fps with the R1 chip's 12ms latency keeping it below the human perceptual threshold for motion sickness. Eye-and-hand tracking was precise and natural: looking at an app icon to select it, pinching to activate it, and flicking a wrist to scroll felt like natural extensions of intent. The 1.45 lb (650g) weight, pressed against the face by the headband, became uncomfortable after 30–60 minutes for most reviewers; the external battery cable was cumbersome.

The software situation at launch was the sharpest limit. visionOS launched with no Netflix app, no YouTube app, no Amazon Prime Video app, and no major social networking apps — those companies had not yet developed spatial versions. Spatial FaceTime, Photos, and Safari were refined; productivity apps from Microsoft Office and Zoom appeared at launch. Apple launched Vision Pro only in the US in February 2024; international availability expanded to China, Japan, and several European countries in June 2024. Apple estimated it sold approximately 200,000–400,000 units in 2024, significantly below some analysts' projections of 800,000+, and did not release official sales figures.
