If I were building a gaming PC on **July 1, 2023**, this is the machine I would actually want. I am not simply choosing the most expensive part in every category. The priority is gaming performance, then reliability, cooling and enough headroom to keep the system useful for years.

### The build

- **CPU:** AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D
- **GPU:** NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 24 GB
- **Motherboard:** MSI MAG X670E Tomahawk WiFi
- **Memory:** 32 GB DDR5-6000 CL30
- **Storage:** Samsung 990 Pro 2 TB NVMe
- **Power supply:** Corsair RM1000x Shift
- **Case:** Fractal Design Torrent
- **CPU cooling:** Arctic Liquid Freezer II 360
- **Gaming target:** 4K high-refresh, including ray tracing

### Why this CPU?

The 7800X3D launched April 6 and is the kind of CPU a gaming build should celebrate: eight cores plus a large 3D V-Cache rather than extra cores that mostly sit idle. I would take it over a more expensive Ryzen 9 for gaming.

### Why this GPU?

The RTX 4090 had been available since October 2022 at $1,599, with 24 GB of GDDR6X. NVIDIA positioned it for maximum-settings 4K high-frame-rate gaming, and nothing else changes the experience as much as this GPU.

### What I would pay

A complete build would be roughly **$2,700–3,100** in the U.S. market of the period. This is an approximate enthusiast-system total, not a claim that every retailer sold every part at MSRP on July 1.

### The weak point

The GPU is physically enormous and power hungry. Case clearance, the 12VHPWR cable bend and PSU quality are engineering concerns, not cosmetic details.

### My verdict

This is one of the best-balanced extreme builds in the series. The CPU is chosen for games, not status; almost all of the extravagance is concentrated where it matters: the RTX 4090.

The date matters. I am judging only hardware that was available by July 1, not products announced or released later in the year. That makes each build a snapshot of what I could realistically have assembled at that moment.
