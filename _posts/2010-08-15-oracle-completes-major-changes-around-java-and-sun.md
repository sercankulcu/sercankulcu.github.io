After Oracle completed its acquisition of Sun Microsystems in 2010, developers began seeing what new ownership meant for Java, Solaris, MySQL, and Sun's open-source projects. Oracle inherited both commercial products and communities that had grown around Sun's comparatively open development culture.

Java was particularly sensitive because the language and virtual machine formed a platform used by banks, servers, Android tooling, universities, and enterprise applications. Oracle became responsible for the JDK roadmap while also controlling the Java trademark and much of the reference implementation.

The transition generated both investment and conflict. Projects such as OpenOffice.org and Hudson later forked into LibreOffice and Jenkins after disagreements with Oracle. The episode showed that open source protects access to code, but community governance and corporate stewardship can still determine where developers choose to invest their effort.
