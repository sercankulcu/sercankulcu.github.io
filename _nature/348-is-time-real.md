Yesterday is gone.

Tomorrow has not arrived.

The present seems uniquely real.

But modern physics challenges this intuition.

What is time?

And is its apparent flow a feature of reality itself?

## Time as Dimension

Physics often represents time as a coordinate:

\[
t
\]

Events occur at different temporal locations.

In relativity, time is integrated with space into spacetime.

## Everyday Time

Ordinary experience distinguishes:

- past,
- present,
- future.

The present appears to move.

This is the phenomenology of temporal passage.

## Is Passage Physical?

Does reality contain an objective moving **now**?

Or is passage something minds experience while events simply occupy different temporal positions?

This is the core metaphysical dispute.

## Presentism

**Presentism** says:

only the present exists.

Past events no longer exist.

Future events do not yet exist.

This matches ordinary intuition.

## Eternalism

**Eternalism** says:

past,
present,
future

are equally real.

They occupy different locations in a four-dimensional spacetime.

This is often associated with the **block universe**.

## Growing Block

A third view says:

past and present exist,
future does not.

Reality grows as new events become actual.

This is the **growing block** theory.

## Moving Spotlight

Another view accepts a block universe but adds an objectively privileged present that moves through it.

This is the **moving spotlight** theory.

## Special Relativity

Einstein's special relativity challenges universal simultaneity.

Observers in relative motion can disagree about which distant events occur:

at the same time.

This has major implications for presentism.

## Relativity of Simultaneity

Suppose two distant events are simultaneous for one observer.

Another observer moving relative to the first may judge:

one happened before the other.

There is no observer-independent global simultaneity in ordinary special relativity.

## The Present Problem

If reality contains one universal present, which observer's simultaneity defines it?

Special relativity provides no obvious preferred frame.

This makes simple presentism difficult.

## Does Relativity Prove Eternalism?

Not strictly.

Presentists can modify their metaphysics.

They may posit:

- hidden preferred foliation.

But such structure is not part of standard relativistic physics.

## Block Universe

In a four-dimensional spacetime picture, your life is a worldline.

Different moments are different spacetime locations.

The universe does not literally "become" in the equations.

## Spatial Analogy

Paris and Istanbul both exist even though you are only in one place.

Eternalists suggest:

past and future events may similarly exist even though you occupy one time.

## The Analogy Has Limits

We move freely through space in ways we do not move through time.

Temporal order has asymmetries.

Time is not simply another spatial dimension.

## Proper Time

In relativity, each observer follows a worldline and measures **proper time** along it.

There is no single universal cosmic clock.

## Time Dilation

Moving clocks and clocks in different gravitational fields can accumulate different amounts of time.

Time is physically flexible.

## Twin Effect

A traveling twin can return younger than a stay-at-home twin.

This is not illusion.

Different worldlines contain different proper times.

## General Relativity

Gravity affects time.

Clocks deeper in gravitational fields run differently relative to distant clocks.

GPS systems must account for relativistic time effects.

## Is Time Therefore Real?

Relativistic effects strongly suggest temporal structure is physically real.

But they do not settle whether:

temporal passage

is fundamental.

## Metric Structure

Physics clearly contains:

- durations,
- causal order.

The controversial part is:

flow.

## The Arrow of Time

Many fundamental equations work almost equally well forward and backward in time.

Yet everyday processes have direction.

Eggs break.

They do not spontaneously reassemble.

Why?

## Thermodynamic Arrow

The second law of thermodynamics says entropy tends to increase in isolated systems.

This gives a temporal direction:

low entropy → high entropy.

## Entropy

Entropy measures, roughly, the number of microscopic configurations compatible with a macroscopic state.

Higher entropy states are overwhelmingly more numerous.

## Why Was Entropy Low?

The deepest puzzle is not why entropy increases.

It is why the early universe had extraordinarily low entropy.

This special boundary condition gives the arrow its direction.

## Past Hypothesis

The **Past Hypothesis** proposes that the universe began in a very low-entropy macrostate.

Combined with statistical mechanics, this explains temporal asymmetry.

## Memory Arrow

We remember:

past

not:

future.

Why?

Memory formation itself is a physical process that increases entropy.

Psychological time may inherit thermodynamic direction.

## Causal Arrow

We think causes precede effects.

Is this fundamental?

Some accounts derive causal asymmetry partly from entropy and intervention structure.

## Radiation Arrow

Waves spread outward from sources.

We rarely observe converging radiation that spontaneously creates a source.

Boundary conditions again matter.

## Cosmological Arrow

Expansion of the universe provides another time-asymmetric feature.

Its relation to thermodynamic time is complex.

Expansion alone does not fully explain the arrow.

## Time-Reversal Symmetry

Many laws are approximately time-reversal symmetric.

But not all fundamental interactions are perfectly symmetric under time reversal.

Certain weak-interaction processes violate T symmetry.

## CPT

Quantum field theory has deep connections among:

- charge conjugation,
- parity,
- time reversal.

CPT symmetry is a fundamental structural principle in standard relativistic quantum field theory.

## Does T-Violation Explain Everyday Time?

No.

The tiny asymmetries of particle physics are not the main explanation for eggs not unbreaking.

Thermodynamic asymmetry dominates ordinary temporal direction.

## Psychological Flow

Why does time feel like it flows?

The brain integrates information over intervals.

Experience contains:

- memory of recent past,
- anticipation.

This can generate a moving-present phenomenology.

## Specious Present

William James and others discussed the **specious present**:

the short temporal window experienced as "now."

The present may not be mathematically instantaneous in consciousness.

## Temporal Integration

Music requires holding previous notes while hearing the current note.

Consciousness integrates across time.

The experienced present has duration.

## Does the Brain Create Flow?

Some philosophers argue temporal flow is a feature of consciousness rather than external physics.

Reality may be static block structure.

Minds experience succession.

## Illusion of Passage?

Calling passage an "illusion" can mislead.

The experience of passage is real.

The question is whether external time itself literally flows.

## Rate of Flow Problem

If time flows, how fast?

One second per second?

That expression is dimensionally trivial.

This suggests the flow metaphor may be conceptually confused.

## Meta-Time

If time moves, movement seems to require another parameter:

meta-time.

Then we can ask:

Does meta-time flow?

A regress threatens.

## McTaggart

J. M. E. McTaggart distinguished:

- A-series:
  past/present/future,
- B-series:
  earlier/later.

He argued time is unreal because the A-series is contradictory.

His conclusion remains controversial.

## A-Series

The A-series gives events changing properties:

future → present → past.

Temporal becoming belongs here.

## B-Series

The B-series gives fixed relations:

earlier than,
later than.

Eternalists often favor this structure.

## Presentism and Experience

Presentism naturally fits:

only now feels actual.

But fitting phenomenology is not enough.

Physics imposes constraints.

## Eternalism and Free Will

If the future already exists in a block universe, is choice meaningless?

Not necessarily.

Your decisions are part of the block's causal structure.

Existence does not mean external compulsion.

## Fixed Future?

Eternalism is not identical to determinism.

A block representation can describe even indeterministic theories.

Ontology and dynamical determination are distinct.

## Future Truth

Are statements about future events already true?

This connects metaphysics of time to:

- logic,
- free will.

The issue is ancient.

## Aristotle's Sea Battle

Aristotle discussed whether:

"There will be a sea battle tomorrow"

is already true or false.

If yes, does that make the battle necessary?

Temporal logic separates truth from necessity.

## Growing Block Problem

If past and present exist but future does not, how do we know we are at the edge of the block?

A person in the past would also think:

I am present.

This creates epistemic difficulty.

## Presentism and Physics

Presentism may fit human intuition while requiring extra metaphysical structure beyond relativity.

Some philosophers accept that cost.

## Eternalism and Common Sense

Eternalism fits spacetime physics elegantly.

But it makes the vivid sense of becoming difficult to explain.

No position is cost-free.

## Quantum Mechanics and Time

Quantum theory treats time differently from other observables in standard formulations.

This creates foundational issues.

## Problem of Time

In some approaches to quantum gravity, fundamental equations appear timeless.

This is the famous **problem of time**.

How ordinary temporal evolution emerges becomes a major question.

## Wheeler–DeWitt Equation

Canonical quantum gravity leads to the Wheeler–DeWitt equation, often schematically written without an external time parameter.

This suggests time may be emergent at a fundamental level.

## Emergent Time

If time emerges from:

- correlations,
- entanglement,
- thermodynamics,

then temporal reality could be real but nonfundamental.

## Relational Time

One approach defines time through change relative to other systems.

There is no independent cosmic clock.

Time is relational.

## Machian Intuition

Ernst Mach influenced relational views where temporal measures derive from physical change.

No change, no operational time.

## Time and Change

Can time exist without change?

Newtonian intuition says yes.

Relationalists often say time is inseparable from change.

The question remains metaphysical.

## Time in Empty Universe

Imagine a universe containing nothing that changes.

Would time pass?

There may be no observable difference between:

ten seconds,
one million years.

This tests whether time is substantival.

## Present Experience

Whatever the metaphysics, agents operate through:

memory,
prediction.

Temporal structure is essential to cognition.

## Mortality

Time is existentially important because lives are finite.

The metaphysics of time does not remove urgency.

## The Philosophical Lesson

Physics strongly supports real temporal structure:

- order,
- duration,
- relativistic proper time.

It does not clearly establish an objectively moving present.

The passage we experience may arise from:

- thermodynamic asymmetry,
- memory,
- cognition.

Time may be real without flowing in the way it feels.

## The Next Question

Quantum mechanics challenged:

- particles,

- determinism.

It also challenges our picture of reality itself.

What does a quantum state represent?

Do outcomes exist before measurement?
