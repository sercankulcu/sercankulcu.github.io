Global Workspace Theory begins with a functional question:

What information becomes globally available?

Integrated Information Theory begins somewhere else.

It asks:

What must be true of a physical system **from its own intrinsic causal perspective** for it to have experience?

This is the starting point of **Integrated Information Theory**, or IIT.

## The Central Ambition

IIT attempts something unusually bold.

It does not merely seek correlates of consciousness.

It tries to connect the structure of experience directly to the causal structure of a physical system.

## Giulio Tononi

IIT was developed primarily by neuroscientist Giulio Tononi and collaborators.

The theory has evolved through several versions.

Its terminology and mathematics have become increasingly formal.

## Start from Experience

IIT begins with features said to be evident in consciousness itself.

These are often called **axioms**.

The theory then proposes physical requirements corresponding to those axioms.

## Phenomenological Axioms

Different versions formulate them somewhat differently, but the core ideas include:

- intrinsic existence,
- composition,
- information,
- integration,
- exclusion.

These describe supposed properties of every conscious experience.

## Intrinsic Existence

Experience exists:

for the experiencing system.

It is not merely something an external observer assigns.

This motivates IIT's emphasis on intrinsic causal power.

## Composition

Experience contains parts and relations.

A visual scene may include:

- red object,
- background,
- spatial relation.

Consciousness has internal structure.

## Information

Each experience is specific.

Seeing:

a red triangle

excludes countless alternatives.

The experience is one particular state among possibilities.

## Integration

Experience is unified.

You do not experience:

color

and:

shape

as entirely independent consciousnesses.

They belong to one integrated scene.

## Exclusion

At any moment, experience has definite boundaries and grain.

It is:

this experience,

not every overlapping possible decomposition simultaneously.

This is one of IIT's more controversial principles.

## From Axioms to Postulates

IIT then proposes physical **postulates**.

A conscious system must possess causal organization corresponding to the phenomenological axioms.

The theory therefore moves:

experience → causal structure.

## Cause–Effect Power

A physical mechanism matters if its current state makes a difference to:

- possible past states,
- possible future states.

IIT treats causal power as intrinsic.

## Information in IIT

IIT's use of "information" is not identical to ordinary Shannon information.

It concerns how a system's current state constrains its own possible causes and effects.

This distinction is essential.

## Intrinsic Information

A system has intrinsic information when its current state specifies a particular cause–effect structure relative to alternatives.

The observer's description is not enough.

The system's own causal organization matters.

## Integration

A system may contain information but still decompose into independent parts.

IIT asks:

How much causal structure is lost if the system is partitioned?

If much is lost, the system is highly integrated.

## Φ

The symbol:

\[
\Phi
\]

is associated with integrated information.

Informally, it attempts to quantify irreducibility of the system's intrinsic cause–effect structure.

The exact mathematical definition depends on the IIT version.

## Important Caution

It is misleading to treat Φ as:

a simple consciousness meter.

Computing IIT quantities is technically difficult.

The formalism is more elaborate than one scalar summary suggests.

## Irreducibility

Imagine a system split into two independent halves.

If nothing important changes, the whole adds little beyond the parts.

If partition destroys essential causal structure, the whole is irreducible.

IIT associates such irreducibility with consciousness.

## Feedforward Systems

A purely feedforward network may perform sophisticated input–output transformations.

But if its causal structure decomposes in the relevant way, IIT may assign it little or no consciousness.

This creates a striking prediction.

## Recurrent Systems

Recurrent networks contain feedback.

Their current states can causally constrain one another in richer ways.

IIT therefore tends to favor recurrent, integrated architectures.

## Functional Equivalence

Two systems can produce identical external behavior but possess different internal causal structures.

IIT may assign them different consciousness.

This directly rejects pure input–output functionalism.

## Philosophical Zombies by Architecture

Under IIT, a sophisticated feedforward system could behave like a conscious system while lacking the required intrinsic integration.

Behavior alone is not decisive.

This makes IIT structurally anti-behaviorist.

## Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Description

A digital computer can be described by an observer as implementing:

- software,
- variables,
- algorithms.

IIT asks whether the underlying hardware itself possesses the right intrinsic causal unity.

Abstract computation is not automatically consciousness.

## Simulation Problem

A simulation of a conscious brain may reproduce its functional behavior.

Would it be conscious?

IIT's answer depends on the causal organization of the physical implementation, not merely the simulated pattern.

## Simulation Is Not Duplication

A weather simulation does not become wet.

Similarly, IIT suggests a simulated causal structure may not instantiate the same intrinsic cause–effect power.

This is a controversial claim.

## Substrate Matters

This distinguishes IIT from many forms of computational functionalism.

Implementation is not arbitrary.

The physical causal structure matters directly.

## Maximal Complex

IIT proposes that consciousness corresponds to a **maximally irreducible cause–effect structure**.

The system with the relevant maximum defines the conscious complex.

This attempts to solve subject-boundary problems.

## Why Not Brain Plus Chair?

Your brain interacts with a chair.

Why is the combined system not one consciousness?

IIT answers:

the strongest integrated causal complex lies primarily within the brain, not across weak external coupling.

## Exclusion Principle

The exclusion principle selects one dominant causal complex rather than every overlapping subset being a separate subject.

This is intended to prevent uncontrolled proliferation of subjects.

It is also one of the theory's debated features.

## Consciousness Quantity and Quality

IIT tries to address two questions.

### Quantity
How much consciousness exists?

### Quality
What is the structure of that experience?

The cause–effect structure is supposed to answer both.

## Qualia Geometry

The theory associates the quality of experience with a high-dimensional causal structure.

Different experiences correspond to different intrinsic relational forms.

This is an ambitious formalization of phenomenology.

## Why IIT Appeals to Some Researchers

It takes phenomenal structure seriously.

It does not reduce consciousness merely to:

- report,
- attention,
- access.

It attempts to explain why experience is:

unified,
specific,
structured.

## Connection to Complexity

IIT resonates with earlier themes:

- integration,
- emergence,
- causal organization.

But it is not simply a generic complexity theory.

Its criteria are specific and intrinsic.

## Consciousness Under Anesthesia

Anesthesia often reduces:

- effective connectivity,
- complexity,
- integration.

This is broadly compatible with IIT.

But similar observations can support other theories too.

## PCI Connection

The Perturbational Complexity Index was inspired partly by ideas about integrated and differentiated brain dynamics.

PCI can help assess conscious capacity clinically.

It is not identical to calculating IIT's Φ.

## Sleep

Deep dreamless sleep tends to show lower complex causal responsiveness than wakefulness.

Dreaming can restore rich internal dynamics.

Again, this broadly fits integration-based theories.

## Disorders of Consciousness

Measures of complex neural interaction may help distinguish:

- unresponsive,
- minimally conscious,
- covertly conscious states.

Clinical usefulness can exist even if IIT's metaphysics remains disputed.

## Panpsychist-Like Consequences

If very simple integrated systems have some nonzero intrinsic causal structure, IIT may attribute extremely minimal consciousness widely.

This gives the theory panpsychist or panprotopsychist implications.

## But IIT Is Not Ordinary Panpsychism

IIT does not simply say:

all matter is conscious.

It provides specific structural criteria.

Many physical aggregates would fail to form a unified conscious complex.

## Simple Systems

Some simple circuits may receive tiny consciousness assignments under some versions of IIT.

Critics find this counterintuitive.

Defenders say intuition should not override theory.

## The Cerebellum Puzzle

The human cerebellum contains enormous numbers of neurons.

Yet damage there does not usually eliminate consciousness.

IIT points to its relatively modular architecture.

Neuron count alone is not enough.

Integration matters.

## Cortex vs Cerebellum

The cerebral cortex has dense recurrent integration across many regions.

The cerebellum has a more feedforward and modular organization.

This is often used as an intuitive example favoring integration-based approaches.

## Major Strength

IIT offers unusually explicit principles connecting:

phenomenology

to:

physical causal architecture.

It makes consciousness depend on more than behavior.

## Major Weakness

Its formalism can generate surprising or counterintuitive assignments.

Critics question whether the axioms are truly self-evident.

If the starting axioms are wrong, the mathematical machinery inherits the error.

## Axiom Criticism

Why must every conscious experience satisfy:

exclusion

in exactly the IIT sense?

Why should phenomenological unity map to this specific causal measure?

These are substantive assumptions.

## Mathematical Complexity

Exact IIT calculations become computationally expensive for large systems.

This makes direct application to brains difficult.

Approximation is often necessary.

## Empirical Testing

A strong theory should make predictions that distinguish it from:

- GNWT,
- recurrent-processing theories.

Many experiments so far fit multiple theories.

This limits decisive confirmation.

## Adversarial Collaboration

Researchers from competing consciousness theories have participated in adversarial collaborations designed to pre-register differing predictions and test them fairly.

This is a valuable methodological development.

## Results and Interpretation

Even when experiments favor one prediction, interpreting what they show about:

- access,
- phenomenology,
- report

remains difficult.

Consciousness theories are not easily killed by one result.

## Functional Objection

A functionalist asks:

Why should intrinsic causal integration matter if two systems behave identically?

If no functional difference exists, what explanatory work does Φ perform?

IIT answers:

consciousness is an intrinsic property, not merely a behavioral disposition.

## Hard Problem

Does IIT solve the hard problem?

Defenders argue it identifies consciousness with intrinsic causal structure.

Critics reply:

Why should that structure feel like anything?

The bridge may still appear asserted rather than derived.

## Identity Strategy

IIT effectively treats:

experience structure

and:

intrinsic causal structure

as two descriptions of one thing.

This is closer to an identity theory than a causal-production theory.

## Fundamental Consciousness

Because IIT starts from consciousness as an intrinsic property tied directly to causal organization, it can be interpreted as treating consciousness as fundamental in the natural order.

This explains its affinity with panpsychist ideas.

## The Explanatory Gamble

IIT's central gamble is bold:

If we characterize the internal causal structure precisely enough, phenomenal structure will no longer be an extra mystery.

Whether that succeeds remains contested.

## The Philosophical Lesson

Integrated Information Theory takes consciousness seriously as an intrinsic, unified structure.

It asks not:

What does the system report?

but:

What causal whole exists from the system's own perspective?

Its strength is conceptual ambition.

Its weakness is that the bridge from causal structure to experience remains controversial.

## The Next Question

IIT's implications bring us back to a much older metaphysical possibility.

What if consciousness is not rare?

What if some form of experience is present throughout nature?

That is the claim of:

**Panpsychism.**
