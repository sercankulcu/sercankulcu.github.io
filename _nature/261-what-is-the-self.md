You wake up and say:

"I am still me."

The body has changed.

Memories have changed.

Cells have changed.

Beliefs have changed.

Yet something seems continuous.

What is that something?

What is the **self**?

## Several Meanings of Self

The word can refer to different things:

- organism,
- conscious subject,
- personality,
- autobiographical identity,
- social role.

These should not be collapsed.

## Biological Self

At the simplest level, you are a living organism.

Your body maintains a boundary between:

self

and:

environment.

Immune systems distinguish:

self-like

from:

foreign.

This is a biological form of selfhood.

## Bodily Self

Experience is organized around one body.

You perceive the world from:

this location.

You control:

these limbs.

The body provides a stable coordinate system for self.

## Ownership

We normally experience:

this hand is mine.

This is **body ownership**.

It can be experimentally manipulated.

## Rubber Hand Illusion

In the rubber hand illusion, synchronized visual and tactile stimulation can make a fake hand feel partly like one's own.

Body ownership is constructed from sensory integration.

It is not an infallible given.

## Agency

Another component is the sense:

"I caused this action."

This is the **sense of agency**.

Ownership and agency can dissociate.

You can feel:

this is my arm,

without feeling:

I intentionally caused its movement.

## Agency as Prediction

One theory compares:

predicted sensory consequences of action

with:

actual feedback.

Matching signals contribute to a sense of agency.

Selfhood depends partly on control.

## Minimal Self

Some philosophers use **minimal self** for the immediate first-person structure of experience.

It includes:

- embodied perspective,
- ownership,
- agency.

It does not require autobiography.

## Narrative Self

The **narrative self** is the story we tell about who we are.

It includes:

- memories,
- values,
- roles,
- goals.

This self extends across time.

## Autobiographical Memory

Memory binds past events into personal history.

Without autobiographical memory, narrative identity weakens.

But identity does not disappear completely.

## Memory Theory of Identity

John Locke linked personal identity strongly to continuity of consciousness and memory.

If you remember performing an action as your own, that memory contributes to personal continuity.

The view has deep influence.

## Problems with Memory Theory

Memory can be:

- lost,
- distorted,
- false.

If identity depended only on memory, amnesia would create strange consequences.

Memory may be important without being sufficient.

## Psychological Continuity

Modern views often broaden Locke's idea.

Identity may depend on overlapping chains of:

- memory,
- intention,
- belief,
- personality.

No single memory is required.

Continuity can be gradual.

## Bodily Continuity

Another view identifies the person with the continuing living organism.

Your body persists through change.

Psychological disruption does not create a new organism.

This is sometimes called **animalism**.

## Brain-Based Identity

One might say:

where the functioning brain goes, the person goes.

Thought experiments about brain transplants make this intuition attractive.

But real cases are far less clean than science fiction.

## Teletransportation

Imagine a machine scans your complete physical state, destroys your body, and reconstructs an exact copy elsewhere.

Did you travel?

Or did you die while a copy appeared?

The thought experiment exposes competing intuitions about identity.

## Duplication Problem

Suppose the original is not destroyed and two identical copies are produced.

Both remember being you.

They cannot both be numerically identical to one pre-duplication person in the ordinary one-to-one sense.

Identity and psychological continuity separate.

## Parfit

Derek Parfit argued that strict personal identity may matter less than:

psychological continuity and connectedness.

Survival-like relations may exist without simple identity.

This challenges ordinary intuitions.

## Ship of Theseus

The ancient Ship of Theseus asks:

If every component is gradually replaced, is it still the same ship?

The self faces a similar puzzle.

Biological components change while organization continues.

## Pattern Continuity

Perhaps the self is a persistent pattern rather than fixed material.

A flame persists while molecules pass through it.

An organism persists through metabolism.

Identity may be organizational.

## But Which Pattern?

A person includes many patterns:

- body,
- memories,
- habits,
- relationships.

No one level obviously defines the self completely.

## Self as Process

Instead of asking:

"What thing is the self?"

we might ask:

"What process maintains selfhood?"

The self may be continuously enacted.

## Self-Model

The brain maintains models of:

- body,
- goals,
- social identity,
- autobiographical history.

These models help coordinate behavior.

The experienced self may partly be a model.

## Metzinger

Philosopher Thomas Metzinger argues that the self as a separate entity is not found.

What exists is a transparent **self-model**.

The system experiences the model as reality rather than as a model.

This is a strong version of the constructed-self view.

## Hume

David Hume described the mind as a bundle of changing perceptions.

He found no separate enduring self behind them.

This **bundle theory** remains influential.

## Buddhist No-Self Traditions

Some Buddhist traditions also reject a permanent independent self.

They emphasize changing processes rather than enduring essence.

These traditions differ internally and should not be reduced to one doctrine.

## No-Self Does Not Mean Nothing Exists

Rejecting a permanent self-substance does not mean:

there is no person,
no experience,
no responsibility.

It means identity may be processual rather than fixed.

## Self as Control Architecture

A functional account treats self as a system coordinating:

- goals,
- memory,
- action,
- social behavior.

The self integrates competing processes.

It may be an organizational construct.

## Minsky's Society

If the mind is composed of many agents, the self need not be a central controller.

It may be the emergent organization of their interaction.

This idea will return shortly.

## Unity

Why does experience feel unified?

The brain contains many semi-independent systems.

The sense of one self may arise from:

- integration,
- shared memory,
- common control.

Unity may be achieved rather than given.

## Split-Brain Challenge

Split-brain cases raise difficult questions.

If communication between hemispheres is reduced, does one self become two?

The evidence does not support a simplistic yes/no answer.

It shows that unity depends on information integration.

## Dissociative Cases

Psychological and neurological dissociations can disrupt:

- memory,
- agency,
- ownership.

Selfhood can fragment along dimensions.

This suggests it is composite.

## Social Self

Who we are depends partly on relationships.

A person is:

- friend,
- parent,
- teacher,
- colleague

relative to social structures.

Self is not purely internal.

## Reflected Self

We partly understand ourselves through how others respond to us.

Social feedback shapes identity.

The self is constructed interactively.

## Cultural Self

Cultures differ in how they emphasize:

- independence,
- interdependence,
- role,
- individual preference.

Self-concept has cultural structure.

## Online Self

Digital environments let people maintain:

- profiles,
- histories,
- personas.

These can become extensions of identity.

But representation and person remain distinct.

## Self and Memory Editing

If memories change, the narrative self changes.

We continually reinterpret our own past.

Identity is partly reconstruction.

## Self and Future

The self is not only memory.

We define ourselves through future commitments:

- goals,
- promises,
- imagined possibilities.

Identity projects forward.

## Moral Responsibility

Responsibility assumes some continuity between:

the person who acted

and:

the person held accountable.

Personal identity therefore matters ethically and legally.

## Legal Identity

Law treats persons as persisting entities with:

- rights,
- obligations,
- records.

Legal identity is more stable and categorical than psychological continuity.

Different systems define self differently.

## Self and Free Will

If the self is distributed rather than a central inner controller, what does it mean to choose freely?

Agency may belong to the organized system as a whole.

This will become central in the free-will section.

## Self and Consciousness

Does consciousness require a self?

Some experiences may contain awareness with reduced self-reference.

Meditative reports and altered states suggest self-consciousness and consciousness are separable to some degree.

## Minimal vs Narrative Self Again

A person may lose narrative memory while retaining:

- bodily ownership,
- immediate perspective.

These layers can dissociate.

There may be no single self-property.

## Self as Useful Fiction?

Some theorists call the self a fiction.

That phrase can mislead.

A corporation is not a simple physical object either.

Yet it can be causally and socially real.

Constructed does not mean nonexistent.

## Emergent Reality

The self may be emergent.

It depends on lower-level processes.

Yet it has higher-level explanatory power.

We explain behavior using:

- commitments,
- character,
- identity.

These concepts can be real without being fundamental particles.

## The Philosophical Lesson

The self is likely not one indivisible inner object.

It is a layered organization involving:

- body,
- agency,
- memory,
- self-model,
- narrative,
- social relation.

Continuity is maintained through change.

The self may be a process that persists by continually reconstructing itself.

## The Next Question

If the self is constructed from many interacting processes, perhaps there is no central executive at all.

Could mind be a society of smaller agents?

Before that, one stronger question comes first:

**Is the Self an Illusion?**
