Look at a black shape on a white page.

Which part is the object?

Which part is background?

Perception usually selects one region as **figure** and another as **ground**.

But sometimes the roles can reverse.

This simple visual phenomenon has deep connections to:

- perception,
- representation,
- self-reference,
- formal systems.

## Gestalt Psychology

Gestalt psychologists studied how perception organizes sensory input into coherent wholes.

One key distinction is:

- figure,
- ground.

The figure stands out.

The ground recedes.

The visual field is structured rather than passively received.

## Rubin Vase

The classic **Rubin vase** image can be seen as:

- a vase,
- two faces.

The same physical marks support two perceptual organizations.

When the vase becomes figure, the faces become ground.

Then the relation reverses.

## Perception Selects Structure

The retinal image does not uniquely determine one interpretation.

The visual system organizes it.

Perception is active.

It separates:

- object,
- context.

This resembles interpretation elsewhere.

## Edges Belong Somewhere

In figure-ground perception, a boundary is usually assigned to the figure.

The contour seems to belong to one side.

Perception does not merely detect edges.

It interprets ownership.

## Attention

Attention strengthens figure-ground organization.

A sound can become foreground while others become background.

A conversation at a crowded event illustrates this.

Selective attention constructs relevance.

## Context Is Ground

Earlier we saw that meaning depends on context.

In figure-ground terms:

the explicit message is figure.

Context is ground.

The ground may be less noticeable while still determining interpretation.

## Words and Background Assumptions

A statement appears self-contained.

It rarely is.

Consider:

"The meeting starts at nine."

This depends on background assumptions about:

- date,
- time zone,
- which meeting.

The linguistic figure sits on conceptual ground.

## Models and Background

Scientific models foreground certain variables.

They background others.

A model of planetary motion may foreground:

- mass,
- position,
- velocity.

It may ignore:

- color,
- chemistry.

Modeling is figure-ground selection.

## Abstraction as Foregrounding

Abstraction chooses which properties matter.

A graph represents:

- nodes,
- edges.

Everything else about the underlying objects is ignored.

Formalization creates a figure by suppressing ground.

## Logic as Foregrounded Structure

Logic strips away content.

It foregrounds:

- inference form,
- quantifier structure,
- connectives.

Subject matter becomes background.

This is why one logical pattern can apply across domains.

## Formal Systems Hide Their Metalanguage

When working inside a formal system, we focus on:

- formulas,
- derivations.

The rules defining the system often remain in the background.

Metalogic reverses the figure-ground relation.

The system itself becomes figure.

Its statements become objects of study.

## Object and Meta-Level Reversal

Inside arithmetic:

numbers are figure.

Proof rules are background.

In metamathematics:

proof rules become figure.

Arithmetic statements become data.

Changing level changes what counts as object.

## Gödel's Reversal

Gödel numbering performs a particularly striking reversal.

Formulas, normally used to talk about numbers, are themselves encoded as numbers.

The former representational medium becomes represented object.

Figure becomes ground and then figure again.

## Use and Mention Revisited

When we write:

Paris is in France.

"Paris" is used.

When we write:

"Paris" has five letters.

the word itself becomes figure.

The referent recedes.

Use–mention is a linguistic form of figure-ground reversal.

## Quotation as Reframing

Quotation marks visually and semantically move an expression from:

tool of reference

to

object of attention.

We stop looking through the word.

We look at the word.

Representation becomes object.

## Self-Reference as Figure Folding Back

A self-referential system makes itself figure.

A sentence talks about itself.

A program prints itself.

A theory represents its own proofs.

The background machinery enters the foreground.

## Maps

A map normally represents territory.

But a map can contain a symbol representing:

"you are here."

Now the map partially represents its own relation to the user.

The representational frame becomes part of the represented scene.

## Frames in Art

A painting's frame is normally background infrastructure.

Some artworks incorporate the frame into the composition.

The boundary between artwork and context becomes part of the work.

Self-reference often begins with such boundary crossing.

## Frames in Language

A quotation, title, footnote, or comment can act as a frame around content.

Meta-language comments on object-language text.

Frames tell us how to interpret what is inside.

## Frames in Computing

A program executes instructions.

A debugger frames those instructions as inspectable data.

An operating system treats processes as objects.

What is executable at one level becomes data at another.

## Frames in Biology

A DNA sequence functions only inside a cellular context.

The sequence is figure.

The translation machinery is background.

But when studying gene regulation, the machinery itself becomes figure.

Scientific explanation repeatedly changes scale and frame.

## Perspective Dependence

Figure-ground organization is observer-relative.

The same scene can support multiple foregrounds.

This does not mean reality is arbitrary.

It means representation is selective.

Different questions foreground different structures.

## Ambiguous Figures

Ambiguous images show that one input can support multiple coherent organizations.

This resembles linguistic ambiguity.

Interpretation resolves competition among possible structures.

## Stable Ground

Not every background element can be changed freely.

Perception is constrained by:

- geometry,
- contrast,
- prior structure.

Likewise, interpretation is not unlimited.

Figure-ground flexibility exists inside constraints.

## Figure-Ground and Scientific Revolutions

A scientific revolution can sometimes be understood as a shift in what is foregrounded.

One theory treats a phenomenon as primary.

Another treats it as derived.

Examples include shifts between:

- absolute space,
- spacetime relations,
- particles,
- fields.

Conceptual background can become theoretical figure.

## Figure-Ground and Consciousness

Later, theories of consciousness will face similar questions.

What becomes the focus of awareness?

What remains unconscious background processing?

Attention may determine figure.

Consciousness may depend on more.

The distinction will return.

## Figure-Ground and Identity

An object is often identified by separating it from background.

Where does the object end?

Where does environment begin?

For organisms, tools, and extended cognition, those boundaries can be controversial.

Perceptual organization becomes metaphysical question.

## Figure-Ground and Formal Limits

A formal system cannot usually include every aspect of its own metatheory as ordinary object-level content without consequence.

Some background assumptions must remain outside.

Gödel and Tarski show that attempts to internalize everything encounter limits.

The frame cannot always fully contain itself.

## The Philosophical Lesson

Figure and ground are not only visual categories.

They express a general principle of representation:

to make something explicit, something else becomes context.

Formal reasoning works by selecting a figure.

Metalogic changes the selection.

Self-reference makes the frame itself visible.

## The Next Question

Once formal systems and their encodings are treated as mathematical objects, we can classify sets by whether algorithms can recognize their members.

Some sets are decidable.

Others can only be enumerated.

This leads to:

**recursive and recursively enumerable sets.**
