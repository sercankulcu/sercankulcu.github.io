Reality appears layered.

Physics.

Chemistry.

Biology.

Psychology.

Society.

Each level depends on others.

Yet each also develops concepts and regularities of its own.

This raises a difficult question:

**Can one level of reality completely explain another?**

One provocative answer is that levels can be partly **inviolate**.

Not disconnected.

Not independent.

But resistant to replacement by descriptions from other levels.

## What "Inviolate" Means

The term does not mean lower-level laws can be broken.

Biology cannot violate physics.

Psychology cannot suspend chemistry.

Instead, the idea is that explanations may have level-specific integrity.

A higher level cannot rewrite lower-level laws.

A lower level may not contain the vocabulary needed to replace higher-level explanations.

## Dependence Without Identity

A cell depends on molecules.

But a cell is not usefully identical to a list of molecules.

A language depends on brains and bodies.

But grammar is not a law of neurochemistry.

Dependence does not imply sameness.

## The Hierarchy of Sciences

A traditional hierarchy looks like:

physics → chemistry → biology → psychology → sociology.

The arrow suggests dependence.

But the hierarchy is not a simple ladder of derivation.

Higher sciences introduce concepts such as:

- function,
- history,
- meaning,
- norm,
- institution.

These do not appear naturally in fundamental physics.

## Physics Has Veto Power

Any viable higher-level theory must respect lower-level physical constraints.

An organism cannot create energy from nothing.

A brain cannot transmit ordinary signals instantaneously.

This gives lower levels a kind of veto power.

They constrain possibility.

## Higher Levels Also Constrain

Higher-level organization can restrict lower-level trajectories.

A cell membrane constrains diffusion.

A software protocol constrains allowed messages.

A legal institution constrains behavior.

These constraints are physically implemented but intelligible at the higher level.

## Multiple Realizability

The same higher-level state can often be realized by different lower-level states.

An algorithm can run on different hardware.

A biological function can sometimes be implemented by different structures.

A social role can be occupied by different people.

Higher-level identity can survive lower-level substitution.

## Bridge Principles

Science connects levels through **bridge principles**.

Temperature connects to statistical molecular behavior.

Genetic inheritance connects to molecular replication.

Mental states connect to neural systems.

But these bridges are rarely simple one-to-one identities.

They may be context-dependent and many-to-one.

## Inviolate Does Not Mean Isolated

Levels interact constantly.

Hormones affect behavior.

Behavior affects hormones.

Social stress affects physiology.

Physiology affects social participation.

The levels are entangled.

"Inviolate" therefore means explanatory integrity, not causal isolation.

## Approximate Explanatory Closure

A level can be approximately self-contained for many purposes.

Meteorology predicts using atmospheric variables.

Ecology models populations.

Computer science analyzes algorithms.

None requires rebuilding every result from particle physics.

Approximate closure gives a science practical autonomy.

## Computer Science Example

Computer systems reveal layered explanation clearly:

- hardware,
- machine instructions,
- operating system,
- application,
- user interaction.

A spreadsheet bug is physically realized in electronics.

But the useful explanation may be:

the formula references the wrong cell.

Lower-level truth does not replace higher-level explanation.

## Biology's Level-Specific Concepts

Biology uses:

- fitness,
- adaptation,
- niche,
- selection,
- function.

These cannot violate chemistry.

But chemistry alone does not tell us why a trait spread through a population.

Evolutionary history creates a distinct explanatory dimension.

## Psychology's Level-Specific Concepts

Psychology uses:

- belief,
- goal,
- memory,
- attention.

These are physically realized.

Yet replacing them with descriptions of ion channels often destroys the explanatory pattern.

The concepts remain useful because they capture stable organization.

## Social Rules

Social systems add normativity.

A banknote has value because institutions and people treat it according to shared rules.

Its physical chemistry does not contain monetary value.

Yet the rule changes physical behavior.

People work, trade, save, and migrate because of socially represented meanings.

## Can Physics Explain Money?

In a broad causal sense, every monetary event has a physical realization.

But a physics-only description omits:

- ownership,
- debt,
- price,
- legal obligation.

These are not new forces.

They are higher-level relational facts.

So "physics explains everything" becomes ambiguous.

## Ontological Monism, Explanatory Pluralism

One can believe there is one physical world while accepting many real explanatory levels.

This position combines:

**ontological monism**

with

**explanatory pluralism**.

It avoids both dualism and explanatory imperialism.

## Emergence and Level Autonomy

Emergent properties strengthen level autonomy.

Once stable higher-level patterns appear, they support:

- variables,
- laws,
- interventions,
- counterfactuals.

The lower level explains realization.

The higher level explains organization.

Both can be necessary.

## Levels Cannot Contradict Each Other Freely

Autonomy is not permission for contradiction.

If a higher-level theory genuinely predicted violation of well-tested lower-level physics, then something would be wrong.

Either the higher-level theory fails, the lower-level theory is incomplete, or the connection has been misunderstood.

Cross-level consistency remains essential.

## Reduction as Translation

Reduction may be better understood as translation rather than deletion.

How does one level map onto another?

Which variables correspond?

Which details disappear?

Which regularities become visible only after coarse-graining?

This turns reduction into relationship rather than conquest.

## Limits of Translation

Some concepts may resist natural translation.

"Meaning" may not be expressible cleanly in particle variables.

"Fitness" may not map to one molecular quantity.

"Consciousness" may resist current physical vocabulary.

This does not prove irreducibility in principle.

It shows explanatory languages are not interchangeable.

## A Research Heuristic

The idea of inviolate levels encourages a useful discipline:

do not assume psychology must vanish into neuroscience,
biology into chemistry,
or sociology into psychology.

Ask first what each level explains.

Reduction should earn elimination, not assume it.

## The Risk of Reification

Levels should not be treated as rigid walls built into the universe.

Nature may not come pre-labeled as physics, chemistry, and biology.

Levels emerge from:

- scale,
- stable patterns,
- useful variables,
- observer goals.

They are neither wholly arbitrary nor necessarily fundamental.

## A Better Picture

Think of reality as nested and interacting patterns.

Lower-level dynamics constrain what can exist.

Higher-level organization creates new regularities.

Explanation moves:

- downward toward mechanism,
- upward toward function and structure,
- sideways across systems.

No single direction monopolizes understanding.

## The Next Question

If new effective levels can emerge inside matter, perhaps even spacetime is not fundamental.

Some approaches to quantum gravity suggest that space, time, and gravity may arise from deeper structures.

If so, the stage on which physics occurs would itself be emergent.

**Could space, time, and gravity be emergent?**
