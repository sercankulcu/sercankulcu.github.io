A pencil review that ignores paper is incomplete. The same pencil can feel creamy on one notebook and dry on another.

Paper has texture, sizing, coating, density, and absorbency characteristics that affect graphite. A rougher surface abrades the core more aggressively, often producing a darker mark while wearing the point faster. A very smooth sheet can preserve the point but reveal less of the pencil's softness.

The interaction also changes sound and feedback. On toothy paper, the point communicates every movement. On smooth paper, it may glide quietly. Neither experience is universally superior.

Smudging changes too. A soft pencil that deposits a thick layer of graphite can smear noticeably on a smooth sheet. Paper with more tooth may hold some of the material in its surface texture.

When comparing pencils, I use at least two papers: a common writing paper and a smoother or heavier sheet. That prevents me from declaring a pencil "scratchy" when the real issue is the notebook.

This is one reason pencil collecting inevitably expands into paper collecting. The pencil is only half of the writing surface relationship. Once you notice the interaction, a notebook stops being a neutral background.
