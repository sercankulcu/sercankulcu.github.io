HB is the familiar default, but 2B is one of my favorite grades for everyday handwriting.

A good 2B produces a dark line with relatively little pressure. That matters during long writing sessions because I can relax my grip instead of forcing the point into the page. The mark is also easy to read in ordinary indoor light.

The trade-off is wear. A 2B point usually rounds more quickly than HB or H. Small handwriting can become broader unless the pencil is rotated or sharpened more often. Smudging may also increase depending on the formulation and paper.

Those disadvantages are not always problems. I enjoy the rhythm of rotating a wooden pencil as I write. The changing point creates subtle variation in line width, and a dark 2B makes even quick notes look deliberate.

Manufacturers interpret 2B differently, so testing matters. Some 2B pencils feel close to a dark HB; others are dramatically softer. That variation makes 2B a good grade for comparing brands.

For people who say they dislike pencils because they are pale or require pressure, I often suggest trying a quality B or 2B before giving up on the medium. Sometimes the problem was never "pencils." It was simply the wrong grade.
