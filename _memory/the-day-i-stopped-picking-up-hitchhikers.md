At first, giving someone a ride seemed simple.

They were going somewhere along my route. I had an empty seat. I stopped.

Then one passenger taught me about the phrase “a little farther.”

We reached the place we had agreed on.

“Could you take me a little farther?”

I said yes.

The route changed.

After a while, helping someone on my way had become driving someone on theirs.

That would have been fine if I had decided it clearly. Instead, each extra kilometer arrived as a small request that felt too minor to refuse.

By the end, I was annoyed mostly with myself.

There had been no danger. No theft. No dramatic event.

Just a favor that had expanded.

What stayed with me was the small absence of courtesy at the end. The door opened. The passenger left. The whole thing ended without enough acknowledgment to justify the extra distance I had privately decided should matter.

That was when generosity became accounting.

I had stopped.

I had changed my route.

I had driven farther.

Therefore I expected gratitude.

The passenger had never agreed to this emotional invoice.

After that, I became more hesitant about stopping for strangers.

It was not fear.

It was memory.

Experience sometimes enters the car before the next passenger does.

And once it is sitting beside you, even “a little farther” sounds longer.
