title: "The Graduate Who Had Not Graduated"

The document said that I had graduated on February 3. The university computer, however, did not appear to share this opinion.

I discovered the disagreement because I needed a temporary graduation certificate for another official procedure. I had submitted the request five working days earlier and returned expecting to collect a piece of paper. Instead, I learned that the paper was still somewhere inside the university, moving through a world of stamps, signatures, desks, and people who were apparently all essential to its existence.

The clerk checked the screen.

“You are not shown as graduated yet.”

“But I graduated.”

“Yes.”

“The certificate says I graduated on February 3.”

“Yes.”

“And today is February 16.”

“Yes.”

I waited for the contradiction to trouble him. It did not.

The document cost fifty lira. I found this impressive. After years of tuition, exams, projects, and sleepless nights, the final proof that the whole thing had happened required another payment.

I explained that the delay was creating a problem with another institution. This information changed nothing at first. The university had its time, the other institution had its time, and I stood between them carrying the consequences.

Eventually my voice became louder.

I do not remember the exact argument, only the feeling that I had entered a strange country where facts needed official permission before they could become true. My professors knew I had finished. My department knew. I knew. None of us had the authority of the missing paper.

Someone finally found a way to move the process forward.

The next day, the certificate was ready.

I held it in my hand and read the date again: February 3.

For nearly two weeks, I had apparently been a graduate in the past but not in the present.

I left the office with the paper carefully folded among my other documents. Nothing had changed about my education during those twenty-four hours. I had not passed another exam. I had not completed another course.

Only the system had caught up with reality.

That was when I understood that graduation was not a single event. First you finish university. Then, sometime later, the university finishes with you.
