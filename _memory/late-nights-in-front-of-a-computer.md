title: "One More Link"
date: 2006-10-14

After midnight, the computer became a different machine.

During the day it was a tool for studying, writing code, searching for information, or finishing whatever needed to be finished. At night, when the house became silent and the screen was the brightest object in the room, it seemed to contain much more than that.

I would sit down to solve one problem.

Perhaps a program did not work. Perhaps I wanted to understand something I had seen on a forum. I would tell myself that I only needed one more attempt.

The attempt produced an error.

The error produced a search.

The search produced a forum.

The forum contained a link.

The link led somewhere else.

At some point, the original problem was no longer the center of the night. I had entered a small labyrinth built from pages, messages, code, music, and things I had not planned to learn.

The clock continued its ordinary work behind me.

At two in the morning, I might still believe that sleep was close. At three, the idea became less convincing. By four, morning had begun to feel like another technical problem approaching from outside.

Sometimes I solved what I had started.

Those were satisfying nights. A mistake that had resisted me for hours would suddenly become obvious, and the computer would do exactly what I had wanted from the beginning. The solution could be one character, one setting, one idea I should have seen much earlier.

Other nights, morning won.

I would look away from the screen and notice that the room had changed. The darkness near the window was thinner. Birds had started making noise. The private world of the night was closing.

There was something unhealthy in those hours, of course. Coffee could not replace sleep, and curiosity was not a medical excuse. But I remember the nights with affection because nobody had ordered me to stay awake.

I stayed because the next answer always seemed close.

The computer offered an almost endless promise: one more attempt, one more page, one more link.

And somewhere inside that promise, morning waited.
