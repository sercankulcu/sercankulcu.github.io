title: 'Late Nights in Front of a Computer'
date: 2006-10-14

There is a special silence in a room when everyone else is sleeping and only the computer screen is still bright.

I have spent many nights like this. Sometimes I was studying. Sometimes I was trying to solve a programming problem. Sometimes I was doing nothing important at all. I was reading forums, listening to music, or following one link after another without noticing the time.

Night changes the computer somehow. During the day it is a tool. At night it can feel like a small world.

The phone does not ring. Nobody asks a question. The street becomes quiet. A difficult problem suddenly gets all of your attention. You tell yourself that you will stop after one more attempt. Then another hour passes.

There is something productive about these hours, but there is also something dangerous. It is easy to forget the body when the mind is busy. You stop noticing that you are tired. You drink another coffee and continue because the problem feels close to being solved.

Sometimes the problem is solved. Sometimes morning arrives first.

I like the memory of those nights, even when they were not healthy. They remind me of the strange energy that comes from curiosity. Nobody is forcing you to continue. There is no teacher standing behind you. You simply want to know why something does not work.

Programming often creates this feeling. A small error can become personal after midnight. You know the computer is not angry with you, but somehow the blinking cursor feels like a challenge.

Years from now, computers will probably become faster and screens will become better. Maybe we will work in completely different ways.

But I think this feeling will remain familiar to many people who enjoy computers: the quiet room, the bright screen, the unfinished problem, and the thought that sleep can wait just a little longer.
