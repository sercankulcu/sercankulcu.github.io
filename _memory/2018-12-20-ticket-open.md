The task should have taken a few minutes.

I wanted a domain name to point somewhere else. Nothing complicated was changing behind it. The site was ready. The destination was ready. Only the address needed to be told where to go.

I opened a support ticket.

The first day passed.

The domain continued pointing in the wrong direction.

On the second day, I wrote again. The system informed me that support was busy. This was useful information in the same way that being told a locked door was locked was useful information.

On the third day, I called.

No answer.

By the fourth day, the original technical problem had almost disappeared from my mind. I was no longer thinking about DNS or redirects. I was thinking about the ticket.

The ticket had become the project.

I checked it in the morning. Open.

I checked after lunch. Open.

I checked in the evening. Still open.

Somewhere, I imagined, there was a person with the power to perform the tiny action that would release me. Perhaps the person was drinking tea. Perhaps the request was hidden beneath a larger request. Perhaps there was no person at all and the ticket had entered an administrative afterlife.

I sent another message.

The system thanked me for my patience.

I had not offered any patience.

That was the interesting part of the sentence.

If they had told me at the beginning that the operation would take five days, I could have accepted it or found another solution. Instead, every day arrived without an answer, which made a small delay feel much larger.

The site itself was finished. Everything important was waiting behind one small instruction.

Change this.

Point that there.

Done.

But nothing moved.

Four days after opening the request, I looked at the screen and laughed. Technical work often contains difficult problems: bugs that take hours to understand, systems that fail for reasons nobody expects, code that behaves differently in production.

This was not one of those problems.

That was why it was unbearable.

The problem was easy.

The waiting had become complex.
