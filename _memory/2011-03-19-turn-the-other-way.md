The place felt almost like a house, which made the objects inside it more suspicious.

The first handle moved in the direction I did not expect. Fine. One unusual handle proves nothing. Then another one behaved the same way. Windows, doors, even the bathroom fixtures seemed to have agreed on a private system that I had not been told about.

After a while, I began defending myself.

I had not drunk that much.

This became important because repeated failure changes the question. The first time, you blame yourself. The second time, you become less certain. By the third, you begin to suspect architecture.

Nothing was seriously wrong. Every object worked. It simply required my hand to forget years of habit and learn the local rule for several seconds.

That was enough to make the place memorable.

Good design often disappears. You open a door without thinking about it, use a faucet without remembering the movement, and continue with your life. An object becomes visible when it refuses to behave according to the body’s expectations.

This place was very visible.

Its strongest advantage, according to my old note, was that parking was easy. I like the balance of that review. Outside, the car had no trouble at all.

Inside, almost everything else required a second attempt.
