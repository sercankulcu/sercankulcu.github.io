title: "A Face From Another Life"

I was in Montreal for only a short visit, so the city still felt temporary to me. I had no routine there, no familiar streets, and no place that truly belonged to my daily life. Every door led into a world I would probably never see again.

One night, I entered a crowded place with friends. The room was dark and loud, and I was paying attention to nothing in particular when I saw a face that seemed familiar. I looked away, then looked back. For a few seconds, I thought my mind had made a mistake.

It had not. She belonged to a much earlier part of my life, someone I had once cared about deeply and never expected to meet again. Certainly not there, and certainly not in Montreal.

Recognition passed between us before either of us said anything. Soon we were talking, but the conversation felt strange because too much time was missing. We knew who each other had been, yet we hardly knew who we had become.

She told me some of what had happened in the years between. I listened more than I spoke. In my memory, she had remained almost unchanged, protected from time because I had not been there to watch it happen. Now the person in front of me carried years that I knew nothing about.

Some parts of her story were difficult to hear. I did not want anything from her, and I was not imagining a new beginning. What surprised me was something simpler: I wanted life to have treated her more kindly.

The room around us continued as if nothing unusual was happening. Music played, people laughed, and glasses moved across tables. For everyone else, it was an ordinary night. For me, two different periods of my life had suddenly been placed in the same room.

Eventually the conversation ended. There was no dramatic goodbye and no promise to meet again. I stepped outside and stood on the street for a while, listening to the city.

Montreal looked exactly the same as it had an hour earlier. Cars passed, people walked by, and the music continued somewhere behind me. I had travelled far from home only to meet someone from my past in a room full of strangers.

I started walking. After a few streets, I stopped asking myself what the meeting meant. Some encounters do not need a meaning or a sequel. Sometimes the past simply opens a door, looks at you for a moment, and closes it again.
