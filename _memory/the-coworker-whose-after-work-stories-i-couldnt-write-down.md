---
title: "What I Will Not Write Down"
date: 2010-04-16
tags:

---

On the day a coworker left, I tried to write down what I would remember about him.

He was proud of his hometown in a way that made it part of every conversation. He worked hard. We expected to see him get married the following summer, and I could easily imagine him becoming a director one day.

These were the respectable facts.

Then there were the evenings after work.

I decided not to write those down.

The joke was that I did not want the stories used as evidence against him. The truth was simpler: some memories belong to the people who were there.

Work friendships are strange because they begin inside a structure neither person chose. A company puts two desks in the same building, a project creates repeated contact, and eventually the person becomes more important than the job that introduced them.

During the day, you know the professional version. The hardworking one. The reliable one. The one who sends messages, joins meetings, finishes tasks.

After work, another version appears.

That version rarely belongs in a performance review.

When he left, the office lost more than an employee. It lost a familiar voice, a repeated joke, a person who carried his hometown into every room and had already become part of the place.

I do not remember the final handshake.

I do not remember what we said at the door.

I remember deciding that the best stories should remain unwritten.

Years later, that decision has preserved him surprisingly well.

The archive knows there were stories.

It simply does not get to hear them.
