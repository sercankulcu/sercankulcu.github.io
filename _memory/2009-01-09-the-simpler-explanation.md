title: "The Simpler Explanation"
date: 2009-01-09

She said she did not want to get married.

It was a clear sentence, and I immediately began trying to improve it.

Perhaps there was someone else. Perhaps she loved him but could not say so openly. Maybe he did not have a stable job yet. Maybe the families would object. Maybe saying “I do not want marriage” was easier than explaining a complicated private story.

Within a short time, I had created an entire life for a man I had never met.

He had problems, hopes, bad timing, and probably a serious expression. In my version, she was protecting him. This explanation was painful, but it was also romantic enough to make sense.

Then another possibility appeared.

Maybe there was no other man.

Maybe she simply did not want to marry me.

This was an unpleasantly efficient explanation. It required no family conflict, no hidden love, no financial difficulty, and no tragic timing. It explained everything with one sentence and therefore seemed suspicious.

I preferred the complicated theory.

Complicated problems can be solved. A job can improve. Families can change their minds. Time can pass. But if a person simply does not want you, there is nothing to repair.

For a while I moved between the two explanations, giving each one evidence that did not really exist. The mind is generous when it is defending itself.

Eventually I had an idea that seemed brilliant.

I would call her and ask.

I imagined the conversation. I would ask whether she truly did not want marriage or whether she had only meant that she did not want marriage with me. She would answer honestly, the mystery would end, and the world would become organized again.

Then I realized that even the answer would probably require interpretation.

People are polite. People avoid hurting each other. Sometimes they do not fully understand their own reasons. A direct question does not always produce a direct world.

I never needed to solve every possibility.

She had already given me the only answer that mattered for what happened next.

No.

Years later, I remember the story mostly because of how much work I did to avoid the simplest explanation.

It is strange how far the mind will travel when one short sentence is waiting at home.
