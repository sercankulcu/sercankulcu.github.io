title: "The Name From Home"

I have two names, but they have never belonged equally to the same world.

At home, my family used one of them. At school, among friends, and later at work, people used the other. Nobody planned this division. It formed quietly, the way a path appears across grass because enough people keep walking in the same place.

After many years, each name began carrying its own atmosphere. One belonged to family rooms, old furniture, childhood jokes, relatives, and people who could remember versions of me that I had forgotten. The other belonged to classrooms, offices, new friendships, work, and the person I became outside the house.

Sometimes a small accident revealed how strong the division had become. If someone from work used the family name, I would feel a moment of surprise, as if they had entered the house without knocking. If a relative used the public name, it sounded formal, almost as if an official letter had arrived at dinner.

The names were only sounds, yet each came with its own furniture.

I once tried to remember when the separation had started. There was no useful answer. Perhaps someone at home preferred one name when I was a child, while school records naturally used the other. A habit became a rule simply because nobody challenged it.

This is how many parts of identity are built. We imagine that we choose ourselves carefully, but some things are decided by repetition. A nickname survives. A pronunciation stays. One group calls us one thing and another group calls us something else until each word becomes a key.

The family name has the older key. When I hear it, I do not become a child again, but the distance to childhood becomes shorter. The people who use it knew me before degrees, jobs, responsibilities, and all the other evidence of adulthood.

The public name moves forward more easily. New people learn it without knowing what came before. It belongs to the version of me that arrived later.

Neither name is more real.

One tells people who I am in the world. The other sometimes reminds me where the world began.
