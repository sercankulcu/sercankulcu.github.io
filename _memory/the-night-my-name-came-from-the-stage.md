title: "The Night My Name Came from the Stage"
date: 2010-05-15
tags:

---

University festivals have a strange ability to make ordinary nights feel larger than they really are.

The crowd is bigger.

The music is louder.

People behave with a little more courage than usual.

Someone falls in love.

Someone confesses it.

Someone spends the next week pretending nothing happened.

And sometimes, if you are unlucky enough, your own nickname comes out of the mouth of a famous singer in front of thousands of people.

That happened to me at the ODTÜ Spring Festival in 2010.

Leman Sam was on stage.

The concert had already felt special.

She had begun with her own songs, moved into folk songs, brought Şehnaz Sam onto the stage for a while, and eventually turned the whole thing into the kind of festival concert where people stop caring about the exact set list and simply enjoy being there.

Before the concert, she had told the audience not to pull each other's hair and, more importantly, that lovers should confess their feelings before the night ended.

That second suggestion was dangerous.

People began sending notes to the stage.

Leman Sam read them aloud.

Public declarations of love.

Exactly the sort of thing that seems like a wonderful idea when written on a piece of paper in a festival crowd.

Then came the second confession.

She read:

"sipahi loves Funda."

sipahi was my nickname.

Not exactly the most common nickname in the country.

The time between hearing "sipahi" and hearing "Funda" was probably less than a second.

My brain managed to fit an entire year into it.

First thought:

Did I write something and somehow forget about it?

Impossible.

Probably.

But the mind does not need probability when panic is available.

Second thought:

Was this directed at me?

Had some beautiful mysterious woman in the crowd decided to send me a message?

Was this one of those absurdly perfect scenes that only happens in films?

A singer on stage.

Thousands of people.

A secret declaration.

My nickname.

For less than a second, reality became extremely generous.

Then came the name.

Funda.

I did not know anyone named Funda.

The entire romantic universe collapsed instantly.

That one second had managed to create hope and disappointment with impressive efficiency.

The concert continued.

I did too.

But the name stayed in my head.

Funda.

Who was Funda?

Who was the other sipahi?

Was there another person walking around with the same strange nickname?

Had somebody simply written the wrong nickname?

Was the universe trying to tell me something and failing at data validation?

There was no useful answer.

So the event became one of those tiny absurd memories that stay alive precisely because they never made sense.

Days passed.

The festival ended.

The name remained.

Then something happened later that made the whole story even better.

I went to dinner with a very close male friend, his girlfriend, and one of his girlfriend's closest female friends.

Normal dinner.

Normal conversation.

At some point, for no obvious reason, the subject of the festival came up.

I began telling the story.

I was quick, as usual.

I reached the interesting part.

"At the beginning of the concert, she said 'sipahi...'"

Then I stopped.

Completely.

Because the woman sitting across from me — the woman I had been quietly impressed by — was named Funda.

There are moments when the brain works very quickly.

This was not one of them.

The entire old story returned in one piece.

The festival.

The stage.

Leman Sam.

The note.

My nickname.

Funda.

And now, several days later, a Funda was sitting directly across from me.

Excellent.

Exactly the kind of coincidence that makes a reasonable person say nothing.

Unfortunately, I had already started the sentence.

There was no graceful way to reverse.

So I changed the story in the middle.

Instead of saying:

"Leman Sam said, 'sipahi loves Funda.'"

I finished with something vague like:

"She said sipahi loves someone."

Wonderful save.

Completely natural.

Nobody could possibly notice that I had suddenly rewritten the sentence while speaking it.

My friend remembered the actual story.

Of course he did.

This was the moment when friendship proved its practical value.

He immediately changed the subject and moved the conversation to another concert.

No questions.

No explanations.

No attempt to make the situation funnier at my expense.

Just a clean rescue.

There are friends who help you move house.

Friends who lend you money.

Friends who pick you up when your car breaks down.

And then there are friends who recognize, within half a second, that you are about to tell a story involving the exact name of the woman sitting across from you and quietly redirect the conversation.

That deserves its own category of loyalty.

The women did not investigate.

Thankfully.

The dinner continued.

But inside my head, the coincidence had become ridiculous.

A few days earlier, the name Funda had meant absolutely nothing to me.

Then Leman Sam said it from a stage next to my nickname.

For days I could not get the name out of my mind.

And now I was sitting across from a woman named Funda.

A woman I liked.

Life occasionally produces coincidences that are too neat to ignore and too meaningless to trust.

That is the dangerous territory where people begin inventing destiny.

If this had happened in a film, the audience would immediately understand the structure.

Of course the woman is named Funda.

Of course the name was announced at the concert first.

Of course the two events are connected.

Films reward this kind of coincidence.

Real life usually does not.

Real life creates the coincidence and then refuses to explain it.

That may be why we remember these moments so clearly.

Most days do not have narrative structure.

We wake up.

Work.

Eat.

Talk.

Return home.

Nothing foreshadows anything.

Then suddenly a small detail repeats.

A name.

A song.

A place.

A sentence.

The mind becomes alert.

Surely this means something.

Sometimes it does.

Usually we decide what it means afterward.

At the time, I joked that if the story ever received a second update, I would start believing Leman Sam really was some kind of messenger of the heart.

That was the beauty of the situation.

I did not genuinely believe the universe had arranged the concert for me.

But for a moment, believing it was entertaining.

University years contain many memories like this.

Not necessarily important enough to change your life.

Important enough to survive in your memory.

A concert.

A strange announcement.

A name you did not know.

A dinner.

A friend saving you from your own sentence.

These are not the events that appear on official timelines.

Nobody writes them on a CV.

There is no certificate.

No photograph may even exist.

Yet years later they often feel more alive than things that seemed important at the time.

Maybe because they preserve atmosphere.

The exact kind of person you were.

The way your brain could build an entire romantic possibility in less than one second.

The way a name could stay in your mind for days.

The way a coincidence could make an ordinary dinner suddenly feel scripted.

I remember that festival for the music.

I remember Leman Sam.

I remember Şehnaz Sam.

But most of all, I remember hearing my nickname from the stage and briefly wondering whether the universe had finally decided to become interesting.

Then I heard the name Funda.

I knew no Funda.

Problem solved.

Until, of course, I did.
