title: "His Voice in the Film"
date: 2013-03-10

I was watching a film when the father character began speaking to his son, and for a few seconds I stopped hearing the actor.

I heard my father.

The words were not identical to anything I can remember him saying. It was the tone that felt familiar: the insistence that an opportunity was in front of you and that you should not be careless enough to let it pass. The fictional father was speaking inside another family, but the sentence crossed into mine.

At that time, I had also started running to reduce stress. That made the running scenes in the film feel closer than they otherwise might have. It was one of those periods when something enters your own life and suddenly becomes more visible everywhere else.

The father scene stayed with me more strongly.

Parents repeat themselves. Advice becomes familiar enough to lose its edges, especially when it comes from someone who has been advising you since childhood. The same sentence, spoken by a stranger in a film, can suddenly sound new because the history around it is gone.

Then recognition arrives.

I have heard this before.

Years later, I do not remember most of what I wrote about the movie. I had opinions about the plot and the performances, as people usually do after watching something.

What remained was smaller.

A father on a screen began talking.

For a moment, mine answered.
