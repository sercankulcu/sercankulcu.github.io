The assignment was a programming version of Pişti, the Turkish card game.

For reasons known only to the educational universe, the assignment had been given an English title.

*Cooked.*

I received the assignment and followed the standard student procedure of the time.

I searched Google.

Nothing useful appeared.

This was unexpected.

The internet was supposed to contain everything, especially homework that someone else had already suffered through. There were old course pages, forgotten ZIP files, forum posts, sample code, personal websites, and mysterious files uploaded years earlier by students who had no idea they were helping future generations.

Normally, hope began with the search box.

This time, the search box had been defeated by vocabulary.

I typed *Cooked*.

Google gave me the internet’s understanding of the word.

Food.

Recipes.

Heat.

Almost anything except a Turkish card game implemented for a university assignment.

The translation had accidentally become an anti-cheating system.

No special software was required. No hidden repository. No clever detection method. Someone had simply given Pişti an English name so ordinary that the assignment vanished inside the language.

I tried different searches.

Still nothing useful.

There is a particular moment in student life when you discover that the internet has declined to solve the problem for you. The screen remains full of results, yet somehow the remaining work is yours.

I stared at the assignment again.

Apparently education expected me to do the assignment.

This seemed excessive.

Still, there was nowhere else to go.

I closed the browser and opened the editor.

Years later, I remember almost nothing about the code.

I remember the failed search.

The entire internet was available.

The answer was not.

For once, Google had sent me back to class.
