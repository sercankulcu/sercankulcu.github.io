The computer was working perfectly.

The connection was fine, programs opened when asked, and nothing on the screen suggested a technical problem. Yet the machine felt useless because the one thing I wanted from it was a message from one person, and that was the only thing it could not produce.

So I waited.

At first, a few minutes meant nothing. Then time began collecting explanations. Perhaps she was busy. Perhaps she had not seen the message. Perhaps I had written something wrong. After enough silence, I could reread my own sentence until every word looked suspicious.

The computer encouraged this behavior because computers usually respond. You click and something opens. You type and letters appear. Even an error message has the courtesy to tell you that something happened.

A person can simply remain silent.

There is no progress bar for that.

I remember opening other windows, doing something unrelated, then returning to check again. Nothing. Music could make the situation worse, especially the wrong music, because a sad song was perfectly capable of turning an ordinary delay into a private tragedy.

The helplessness came from having no useful next action. The message had already been sent. Another message might look impatient. There was nothing to repair, nothing to restart, nothing to diagnose.

Only the empty space where a reply might appear.

The computer had no fault.

That did not make waiting in front of it any easier.

## Around No New Message

Looking back, I trust the ordinary detail more than the tidy conclusion. What hurts, or amuses, is not always the event; sometimes it is the interpretation that arrived too late.

Attachment rarely leaves through the main door. It stays in side details and waits there. It remains as a modest proof that ordinary life was never as empty as it looked.
