title: "A Name from the Stage"
date: 2010-05-15

At a university spring festival, the singer on stage encouraged people in the crowd to confess their feelings before the night ended. Notes began traveling toward the stage, and she read some of them aloud between songs.

Then I heard my nickname.

For less than a second, the world became unusually generous. My nickname was uncommon enough that I immediately wondered whether the message had somehow been meant for me. The thought arrived before reason could stop it.

Then she read the woman's name.

I knew nobody by that name.

The little private universe disappeared as quickly as it had formed, but the name stayed in my head. I wondered who the other person with my nickname was, or whether the note contained some mistake. There was no answer, which made the coincidence more useful to memory.

Several days later, I went to dinner with friends. A woman at the table caught my attention, and during the conversation the festival came up. I began telling the story, reached the moment where my nickname had been announced, and then realized that the woman sitting across from me had the same first name as the one from the note.

I changed the sentence halfway through.

Instead of repeating the name, I said only that the singer had announced that my nickname “loved someone.” My friend remembered the original version and understood the problem immediately. Without asking anything, he changed the subject.

The dinner continued normally.

That was the strange elegance of the whole thing. A meaningless name had appeared from a stage, remained in my mind for days, and then sat down across from me at dinner. Nothing proved that the two events were connected, and nothing needed to.

Real life occasionally produces the structure of a story and then refuses to provide the explanation.
