The interview went so well that I left the building thinking about how I would resign from my current job.

Nobody had offered me anything.

That detail became important later.

During the interview, everything had encouraged confidence. The conversation flowed easily, the people I met were friendly, and the role was explained in enough detail that I could already imagine myself doing it. At some point they asked when I could start, and that question moved the future much closer in my head than it had any right to be.

By the time I walked outside, I was no longer wondering whether they liked me. I was calculating how long I would need to leave my current workplace properly. Two months? Three? Could I finish everything cleanly before moving on?

The next day there was no call. That seemed normal. The day after that, still nothing. A week passed, then another, and eventually a month had gone by without an offer, a rejection, or any explanation.

Silence forces you to review a conversation differently. The smiles become ordinary politeness. The detailed description of the job becomes something they may tell every candidate. Even “When can you start?” returns to its proper meaning: a question, not a promise.

What embarrassed me was not the rejection, because technically there had been no rejection. It was how quickly I had built the next stage of my life from a few encouraging signals. The company had returned to its normal work after the interview; I had already begun leaving mine in imagination.

After that, I became more careful with good interviews. A pleasant conversation could be pleasant. Interest could be interest. Until an actual offer arrived, the future was still only grammar.
