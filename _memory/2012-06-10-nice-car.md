I had built a very good argument against a certain car.

Another model had more horsepower, cost less, and looked better to me. I had numbers, prices, and enough confidence to make the conclusion feel almost mathematical. I even mocked features that seemed unnecessary.

Then I got into the car I had been criticizing.

My old entry gained a short edit:

Nice car.

That was enough.

Nothing about the price changed. The other car did not suddenly become worse. The specifications were still correct. What changed was the object itself, because until then I had been comparing numbers rather than experiencing a car.

A car is not horsepower divided by price. It is the seat, the steering, the cabin, the way the controls meet the hand, the way the machine feels when all the separate parts become one thing.

Numbers are useful because they let us compare before touching. They are dangerous because they can make us believe touching is unnecessary.

The edit remains my favorite part of the memory. I did not need a long defense or an excuse for changing my mind.

I had more information now.

The spreadsheet had spoken first.

Then the car did.
