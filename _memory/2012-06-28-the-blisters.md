Fitness gloves looked ridiculous to me at first. They seemed like the gym version of wearing sunglasses at night: an accessory designed to make an ordinary activity look more serious than it was.

Then my hands developed blisters.

The argument ended quickly.

The gloves had not changed. The gym had not changed. Only my experience had changed enough for the object to make sense. What had looked like decoration now had a clear purpose.

This happens with specialized equipment in many fields. From the outside, a strange tool can look excessive because the observer has never encountered the problem it solves. Once the problem arrives, the tool becomes much less theatrical.

Pain is especially persuasive. Advice can be ignored, explanations can be doubted, but a blister presents its case directly. The body has little interest in maintaining an opinion for the sake of consistency.

I still think there is something funny about beginners buying every possible accessory before they know whether they will continue an activity. But the opposite mistake exists too: rejecting useful equipment simply because it looks unnecessary from a distance.

My hands settled the question.

They did not care how the gloves looked.
