---
title: 'Some Voices in Your Head Are Inherited'
date: 2021-04-22
description: "Some inner voices are inherited, and self-talk becomes healthier when we learn which ones deserve authority."
tags:

A mistake can produce a sentence almost before I have looked at what happened: you always do this; you should have known; that was stupid.

Sometimes the wording is familiar because I have heard it before. A parent's impatience, a teacher's criticism, or an old relationship can leave a phrase that survives long after the circumstances have changed. Repetition makes it sound authoritative.

The useful test is what the sentence helps me see or do now. "I missed a step" points toward something I can check. "I am incompetent" turns one event into a verdict without identifying a repair. Its harshness can feel like honesty, but the description is less precise.

I am not looking for an inner voice that excuses everything. Carelessness needs to be recognized. A promise I failed to keep may require an apology. Replacing every criticism with reassurance would lose information I need.

What I want is criticism at the right scale. What happened? Which part was under my control? What would reduce the chance of repeating it? These questions can lead to a difficult answer without making that answer a description of my entire character.

The same precision matters with fatigue. Sometimes I am avoiding work. Sometimes I am tired. Calling both laziness makes the sentence consistent while making the response less useful.

An inherited phrase need not be discarded just because it came from someone else. It may still contain good advice. But familiarity alone should not give it authority. I can keep the useful instruction and stop repeating the contempt attached to it.

The aim is a voice I can work with after something goes wrong: clear enough to identify the mistake, and specific enough to leave a next step.
