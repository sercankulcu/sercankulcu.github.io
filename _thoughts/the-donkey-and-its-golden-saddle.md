---
title: 'Status Is a Shortcut, Not a Verdict'
date: 2023-03-20
tags:
  - status

---

A title changes the way people listen before it changes anything about the person holding it.

Professor. Director. Founder. Expert. Wealthy. Award-winning.

These labels are useful because we cannot investigate everyone from the beginning. Status gives us a shortcut. It tells us that some institution, market, audience, or group has already made a judgment.

The problem begins when the shortcut becomes the conclusion.

An old saying puts this crudely: a donkey does not become a horse because it wears a golden saddle.

The insult is unnecessary. The distinction is not.

External signals can tell us something without telling us everything.

A prestigious position may indicate competence. Wealth may reflect skill, inheritance, luck, risk, exploitation, persistence, or some mixture of them. A large audience may indicate insight, entertainment, controversy, good timing, or excellent marketing.

La Rochefoucauld was suspicious of the stories people tell about virtue because reputation and self-interest so often mix together. I think the same suspicion is useful with status.

We regularly turn evidence of recognition into evidence of character.

Someone succeeds, therefore we assume they are wise.

Someone is powerful, therefore we assume they deserve influence outside the field that made them powerful.

Someone speaks confidently, therefore uncertainty begins to look like ignorance.

This error works in reverse too.

A person without visible credentials may know far more than their social position suggests. Lack of status is not proof of hidden greatness, but status should not become the gate through which all credibility must pass.

The same problem appears when judging ourselves.

It is tempting to pursue the visible symbol because the symbol is easier to measure than the underlying quality. Get the title. Buy the object. Collect the credential. Increase the number.

Sometimes those achievements are meaningful.

But symbols are dangerous when they begin substituting for the thing they were supposed to represent.

A title can represent competence without creating it.

Money can represent success without defining it.

Recognition can represent value without guaranteeing it.

Status is information.

It is not a verdict.
