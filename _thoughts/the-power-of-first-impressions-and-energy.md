---
title: 'A First Impression Is a Hypothesis'
date: 2022-10-06
tags:
  - perception

---

Sometimes we meet a person and feel comfortable almost immediately.

Other times something feels wrong before we can explain why.

People often call this "energy." I think the experience is real even when the explanation is vague. Our minds notice tone, timing, posture, eye contact, distance, facial expression, hesitation, and dozens of other small signals before conscious reasoning has finished organizing them.

The mistake is not having a first impression.

The mistake is treating it as a verdict.

A first impression is closer to a hypothesis.

It deserves attention because perception can detect patterns quickly. A strange inconsistency, an aggressive joke, the way someone treats a waiter, or a small mismatch between words and behavior may be worth remembering.

But quick perception also carries old errors with it.

A person may resemble someone we once disliked. Their silence may look arrogant when they are only anxious. Confidence can be mistaken for competence. Familiarity can be mistaken for trustworthiness. A good mood can make us generous in judgment; a bad one can make us suspicious.

David Hume was deeply interested in how much of human judgment begins in feeling before reason arrives to explain it. That seems relevant here. Reason is not always the origin of our conclusions. Sometimes it is the lawyer hired afterward.

So I try to give intuition neither complete authority nor complete dismissal.

Notice the first feeling.

Then collect evidence.

Does the person keep promises? Do their actions remain consistent when there is no advantage in being kind? Can they tolerate disagreement? How do they behave after making a mistake?

Time is useful because character is difficult to perform perfectly for long.

The first impression may turn out to have noticed something important.

Or it may turn out to have mostly described me.

That is why I trust first impressions as questions, not answers.
