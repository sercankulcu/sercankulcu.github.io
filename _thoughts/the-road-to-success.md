---
title: 'Choose What Is Worth the Cost'
date: 2023-07-08
tags:

---

Success is usually presented as a destination. I have become more interested in its price.

A degree costs years. A demanding career costs attention. A business may cost evenings and weekends. Expertise costs repetition. Even a quiet life has a price: some ambitions must be refused so that time remains for other things.

There is no serious choice without an opportunity cost.

This is easy to forget because we usually see other people's results without seeing what disappeared to make room for them. The promotion is visible. The missed dinners are not. The published book is visible. The abandoned drafts, tired mornings, and years of routine are not.

Effort matters, but effort is not a moral guarantee. Luck, health, timing, family, geography, money, and other people can alter the same amount of work dramatically. Pretending otherwise turns success into a flattering story told backward.

Still, circumstances do not remove the need to choose.

The useful question is not simply, "How badly do I want this?"

It is, "What am I willing to give up for it, and will I still respect that bargain later?"

Some prices are worth paying. Others only look reasonable while the goal is distant and glamorous.

I distrust the idea that every ambition deserves persistence. Persistence is a virtue only when the object deserves it. Continuing for five years in the wrong direction is not necessarily more admirable than stopping after one.

That makes success a more private concept than I once thought.

For one person it may mean building something large. For another it may mean having enough control over time to refuse work. It may mean research, family, financial security, craftsmanship, independence, or simply being able to sleep without carrying tomorrow into bed.

There is no universal finish line because there is no universal price worth paying.

Perhaps the better ambition is not to succeed at any cost.

It is to choose a cost consciously, pay it without pretending it is free, and make sure the life built around the achievement is still a life we wanted.
