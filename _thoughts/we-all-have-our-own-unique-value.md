---
title: 'A Truffle Is Not a Failed Apple'
date: 2019-09-14
tags:
  - identity

  - reflection
---

A truffle would make a terrible apple.

It is the wrong shape, grows in the wrong place, cannot be eaten in the same way, and would fail almost every test designed for apples. None of this tells us very much about the truffle.

I think people make a similar mistake when judging themselves.

We enter an environment with a particular definition of what counts: fast speech, social confidence, mathematical skill, physical strength, commercial instinct, academic credentials, leadership, patience, obedience, creativity. Then we confuse the local definition of usefulness with an absolute judgment of worth.

A quiet person can look unimpressive in a room that rewards performance and become indispensable when a difficult problem needs sustained attention. Someone who struggles inside a rigid institution may thrive when given autonomy. A person celebrated in one profession can be painfully ordinary in another.

This does not mean everyone possesses a hidden genius waiting to be discovered. That idea is comforting but unnecessary.

The more modest claim is enough: qualities have contexts.

Even strengths contain weaknesses when moved into the wrong environment. Caution becomes indecision. Confidence becomes arrogance. Persistence becomes stubbornness. Sensitivity becomes exhaustion. The same trait can be rewarded, punished, or ignored depending on what the situation demands.

That is why comparison is often intellectually lazy. We compare outcomes without comparing games.

The question is not whether I am uniquely valuable in some cosmic sense. I do not know how such a thing could be measured.

A more useful question is whether I understand the combination of abilities, limitations, temperament, and interests I actually have—and whether I am placing them somewhere they can do useful work.

Sometimes self-improvement is necessary.

Sometimes the environment is the problem.

Wisdom is knowing which one needs to change.

A truffle is not a failed apple.

But it still has to grow somewhere.
