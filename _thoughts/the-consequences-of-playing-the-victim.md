---
title: 'You Can Be Wronged Without Becoming Powerless'
date: 2024-09-21
description: "Being wronged does not have to mean becoming powerless or building identity around the wound."

---

There are situations in which a person really is the victim.

Someone lies. Someone betrays trust. A system is unjust. An accident changes a life. Pretending otherwise in the name of "personal responsibility" is not wisdom. It is another way of refusing to see reality.

But a second problem can begin after the first one.

A true description of what happened can slowly become an identity.

I was wronged becomes I am the person to whom wrong is done. From there, it can begin to feel as though every next step depends on someone else: their apology, their recognition, their punishment, their change.

Epictetus offers a difficult but useful distinction here. We do not control what has already been done to us. We do have some control over what authority that event receives over the next decision.

That does not erase the harm.

It changes the location of agency.

A person can say, "This was not my fault," and still ask, "What choices are available to me now?" Those choices may be limited by safety, money, health, or support. Recognizing agency should not become a demand to recover without help.

Recognizing the harm and looking for an available choice can belong in the same account. Responsibility becomes self-blame when it erases what another person did. It becomes more useful when it identifies a next step that is actually within reach.

There are things we may never receive: an explanation, an apology, justice, closure. Waiting for them can become another form of captivity.

The past can explain why a door is closed.

It does not always get to decide which door we try next.
