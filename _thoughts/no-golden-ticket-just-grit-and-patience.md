---
title: 'Luck Needs Something to Land On'
date: 2021-04-12

---

We like stories in which one moment changes everything.

A chance meeting. An unexpected offer. A lucky break. Someone notices the right person at the right time, and the rest of the story seems inevitable.

Those moments are real.

What the story often hides is everything that had to exist before the moment could matter.

Machiavelli wrote about *fortuna* as a force that can shape human affairs but cannot be fully controlled. His answer was not to deny luck. It was to ask what kind of person is ready when circumstances change.

That feels closer to reality than the usual argument between "success is hard work" and "success is luck."

Both are incomplete.

A person can work seriously and still encounter bad timing, illness, economic limits, family obligations, or simple misfortune. Another person can receive an opportunity they did little to create.

But opportunity is not the same as outcome.

A useful conversation matters more if you have something to show. An unexpected opening matters more if you have already learned the skill. A recommendation matters more if earlier work has given someone a reason to trust you.

Preparation cannot summon luck.

It can only give luck somewhere to land.

This also changes how I think about patience. Patience is not passively waiting for a door to open. It is continuing to build while no door is visible.

That is difficult because preparation often produces no immediate evidence that it will ever be useful. You read, practice, save, revise, maintain relationships, and become slightly more capable without knowing which part will matter later.

Sometimes none of it produces the outcome you wanted. That possibility has to remain in the picture too.

There is no golden ticket that effort guarantees.

But when chance finally enters the room, I would rather have prepared a place for it to sit.
