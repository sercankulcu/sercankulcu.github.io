---
title: 'Not Every Voice in Your Head Is Yours'
date: 2010-10-06
description: "Not every voice in our head began with us, and healing can require learning where those voices came from."
tags:

---

Some expectations feel like personal convictions because we learned them before we were able to examine them.

A respectable job looks a certain way. Rest needs to be earned. Asking for help reveals weakness. A family should stay close even when a move would make sense. Nobody has to repeat the rule once it has become part of how we interpret a decision.

That is what interests me in the image of emotional baggage: who packed the suitcase, and what circumstances were they packing for?

Caution may have been sensible in a family with little financial security. Constant usefulness may have earned approval in an environment where approval was scarce. A rule can be understandable in its original setting and still fit the present badly.

Knowing its origin does not prove it wrong. A parent's advice may remain wise decades later. A custom may protect a relationship in ways I would miss if I dismissed it simply because it was inherited.

The work is to make the expectation explicit enough to examine. What does this rule protect? What does following it cost? Do those conditions still apply? Would I ask someone else in my current situation to make the same sacrifice?

That last question can expose a difference between a value I endorse and a demand I have become accustomed to making only of myself. I might defend a friend's right to change careers while treating my own wish to do so as disloyalty. The inconsistency deserves attention before it deserves a verdict.

Some inherited beliefs become more genuinely ours through that examination. Others need limits. Valuing work does not require treating every free hour as waste. Loyalty can matter without requiring permanent agreement.

I do not expect to discover a self untouched by other people. The language of the inquiry came from them too. I want to become more deliberate about what I continue carrying, and able to explain why it still belongs.
