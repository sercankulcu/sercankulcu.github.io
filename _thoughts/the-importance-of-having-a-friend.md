---
title: 'A Good Friend Is Someone You Can Be Less Edited Around'
date: 2020-08-01
tags:

---

Friendship is often measured by time.

How long have you known each other?

How often do you meet?

How many messages pass between you?

Those things matter, but I have come to think there is another measure that tells me more:

**How edited do I have to be around this person?**

With some people, conversation requires constant management. Do not mention this. Explain that carefully. Make the story shorter. Hide the uncertainty. Soften the good news so it does not sound like showing off. Hide the bad news so it does not sound like complaining.

The conversation may still be pleasant.

But it is expensive.

A good friendship reduces that cost.

You can say, "I do not know what I am doing," without immediately losing status. You can share good news without performing modesty. You can admit envy, embarrassment, fear, or confusion before turning the feeling into a respectable explanation.

This does not mean friendship is a place without judgment.

Sometimes a good friend should tell us that we are wrong.

Aristotle distinguished friendships of utility and pleasure from friendships grounded more deeply in character. I find that useful because the strongest friendships are not simply comfortable. They are safe enough for honesty.

A friend who agrees with everything can be easy to keep and difficult to trust.

A friend who can disagree without turning disagreement into distance is rarer.

This is also why friendship can survive periods of silence.

Frequency matters less when the underlying permission remains intact. Months may pass, but the next conversation does not begin from zero. The relationship still contains a memory of who each person has been, together with enough curiosity to notice who they have become.

Of course, no relationship removes all editing.

We still choose timing. We still protect privacy. We still avoid using honesty as an excuse for cruelty.

But there is a difference between tact and self-erasure.

The friendships I value most are the ones where I do not have to construct a polished version of myself before entering the room.

Perhaps that is one reason friendship matters so much.

It gives us somewhere to be known before we are finished explaining ourselves.
