Some goodbyes are not born from anger. They come after anger is already gone.

*Üzgünüm* makes me think about that kind of ending. The difficult kind, where you cannot point to one terrible moment and say, "This is why it ended." Instead, you try different ways to save something, and each one slowly proves that the problem is deeper than you wanted to believe.

I know this feeling.

Sometimes you stay because you think patience will solve it. Then staying begins to feel like repeating the same day. You leave for a while because you think distance will help. Then you discover that distance only makes the truth easier to see.

Neither choice feels right.

That is when exhaustion begins.

I think people often misunderstand the sentence "I tried everything." From the outside, it can sound dramatic. But inside a relationship, trying everything can be very ordinary. It means having the same conversation again. Giving one more chance. Waiting for a change. Staying silent because you do not want another argument. Speaking because silence is no longer helping.

Then one day you realize you have reached the end of your patience.

Not because you are cruel.

Because there is nothing left to spend.

The song also makes me think about guilt. Even when leaving is the right decision, you may still feel guilty for being the person who says the final words. You know the other person will be hurt. You know they may think you gave up too easily.

You want them to understand that leaving was not easy at all.

Sometimes it was harder than staying.

There have been moments in my life when I wished another person could see all the private arguments I had with myself before making a decision. From outside, a goodbye may look sudden. Inside, it may have been happening for months.

Maybe years.

By the time you finally say it aloud, you are already tired from carrying it alone.

What makes this song powerful for me is the idea that a relationship can be wrong without either person being completely bad.

Sometimes two people simply cannot build something healthy together.

That is difficult to accept because we prefer clear stories. One person should be guilty, the other innocent. One should leave, the other should suffer. Real life is usually less organized.

Two people can love each other and still fail each other.

They can try sincerely and still reach a dead end.

I used to think that enough effort could repair almost anything. Now I think some things become more damaged every time we try to force them back into shape.

There is a point where effort stops being hope.

It becomes fear of ending.

And fear is not a good reason to stay.

I also understand the need to say, "Do not hate me."

When you leave someone you once cared about, you do not necessarily want to become the villain in their memory. You want them to know that the good parts were real. You want them to understand that saying goodbye does not erase everything that came before.

But we cannot control how people remember us.

Maybe that is part of the cost.

In the end, some relationships do not finish because love disappears. They finish because love cannot carry everything by itself.

You try staying.

You try leaving.

You try speaking.

You try silence.

And eventually there is only one honest sentence left.

I am sorry.

But this has to end.
