Some songs remind me of a person. Others remind me of a period of life. *Denize Doğru* does something slightly different: it reminds me of the desire to leave.

Not to disappear completely. Not to escape life. Just to leave one place before everything inside it becomes too cold.

Mustafa Sandal's *Denize Doğru* has always felt much more melancholic to me than its simple melody first suggests. There is separation in it, but also movement. Someone has been left behind. Someone else has done the leaving. There is regret, loneliness, and confusion, but the song does not remain in a room thinking about what went wrong. It keeps looking toward the sea.

That direction matters.

The sea is not simply scenery here. It feels like another possibility.

When a place becomes filled with memories, leaving it can begin to feel like the only way to breathe differently. The streets remain the same, but they no longer feel neutral. A café becomes the café where something happened. A corner becomes the place where someone waited. A song belongs to one evening. Even the weather starts carrying memories.

Then the city becomes crowded with ghosts.

Perhaps this is why going toward the sea feels so natural.

The sea has no memory of us.

## Drinking a Memory

One of the most beautiful images in the song is also one of its strangest: taking a breath and almost drinking someone's memory.

I understand the feeling behind this image. After someone leaves, memories stop behaving like ordinary thoughts. They become physical. A smell can bring someone back immediately. A song can change the atmosphere of an entire room. An object that meant nothing before suddenly becomes difficult to throw away.

Memory enters through the senses.

Sometimes we do not decide to remember. The body remembers before the mind does.

You walk somewhere and feel something before you understand why. Then you realize that years ago you stood in the same place with someone who is no longer part of your life.

This is one of the unsettling things about loving another person. They slowly enter the geography of your world.

When they leave, the geography remains.

## Who Left Whom?

There is an interesting uncertainty in the song. At one moment, the narrator seems responsible for leaving the other person alone. At another, he is the one who has been left.

I like this contradiction because real relationships rarely produce perfectly clean stories.

After separation, both people often create different histories of what happened. One remembers being neglected. The other remembers trying. One believes the relationship ended on a particular night. The other thinks it had already been dying for months.

Sometimes both are right.

We want breakups to contain a clear guilty person because guilt makes the story easier to understand. If one person destroyed everything, then the past has an explanation.

But many relationships end through accumulation.

A conversation avoided.

A disappointment not discussed.

Pride.

Silence.

Timing.

Small injuries.

Two people slowly becoming different people.

Eventually someone leaves, but leaving may only be the final visible event in a process that began much earlier.

This is why the question “Who left whom?” is sometimes less useful than it appears.

Perhaps both people had already been leaving for a long time.

## The People Who Come After

Another thought in the song is what happens after the relationship: the strange people we trust, choose, or remain with after losing someone important.

This is something separation rarely ends.

When somebody leaves our life, they continue affecting relationships they will never see.

We compare.

Not always consciously.

A new person's voice reminds us of an old voice. We notice what is different. We search for familiar qualities or deliberately avoid them. Sometimes we choose somebody completely opposite because we think difference will protect us.

But emotional history travels with us.

We do not enter new relationships as blank people.

This can be unfair to the person in front of us, but it is also unavoidable to some degree. Every important relationship teaches us a language. Later relationships are partly understood through that language.

Over time, hopefully, comparison becomes less important.

The new person stops being “not the old person” and becomes themselves.

Until then, we can make strange choices.

Perhaps this is what it means to ask, after someone, “Who did I believe?”

## Let Us Go

Then the song suddenly offers a very simple invitation.

Come.

Let us go.

Toward the sea.

I love this change because the answer to emotional confusion is not another explanation. It is movement.

There are moments when talking about something more does not help. We have understood the problem intellectually. We know who said what. We know why the relationship ended. We have repeated the story to friends.

Still, something remains.

At that point, perhaps we need geography rather than psychology.

A different street.

A train.

A coast.

A hotel room where none of our objects are present.

A breakfast somewhere nobody knows us.

Travel does not solve grief, but it interrupts its routines.

And sometimes interruption is the first form of healing.

## Planting Fallen Leaves

The image I find most beautiful in *Denize Doğru* is the desire to plant the fallen leaves again.

It is impossible.

That is why it is beautiful.

A fallen leaf cannot simply be returned to the tree. Its season has already happened.

Love often creates the same impossible desire.

We want to return to the evening before the argument. The year before things changed. The moment when both people were still certain.

We imagine that if we could just go back and do one thing differently, everything might continue.

But life does not work backwards.

The fallen leaf cannot become what it was.

Still, the desire to plant it again says something very human. We are creatures who try to repair things even after time has made repair impossible.

Sometimes we apologize too late.

Sometimes we understand too late.

Sometimes we become the person someone needed only after they are gone.

There is pain in this, but perhaps also maturity. To understand something late is still to understand it.

The knowledge may belong to another relationship, another year, another version of ourselves.

Nothing learned is completely wasted.

## Before Winter

Then comes winter.

The narrator wants to leave before winter because he knows he will become cold.

Of course, winter can simply mean winter. But songs allow us to hear more than literal weather.

Winter is what happens when warmth disappears from a place.

Some relationships have winters.

There is no dramatic ending at first. The conversations simply become shorter. Touch becomes less natural. People stop telling each other small things. Silence changes from peaceful to uncomfortable.

The temperature falls gradually.

By the time someone says “something is wrong,” both people may already have been cold for months.

Maybe this is why the song's desire to leave feels urgent.

Go before winter.

Before the emotional cold becomes permanent.

Before two people become strangers who happen to know everything about each other.

There are times when staying is courage.

There are also times when leaving is.

Wisdom is knowing which season you are in.

## Why the Sea?

I keep returning to the title.

Why not “Let's Go”?

Why not “Before Winter”?

Why specifically toward the sea?

Perhaps because the sea has always been one of our oldest images of freedom. A road continues between defined places. The sea opens.

Standing in front of it makes ordinary problems look slightly smaller, not because they become less real but because the horizon gives them competition.

The eye needs distance.

Maybe the mind does too.

There is also something honest about the sea. It never promises permanence. The surface changes constantly. A wave appears, exists for a moment, and disappears.

Another follows it.

Nothing stays, yet the sea remains.

Perhaps human life is closer to this than we want to admit.

People enter.

People leave.

Homes change.

Bodies age.

Friendships transform.

The things that appear permanent are often only moving more slowly.

The sea makes change visible.

## Can We Repair the Past?

Philosophically, the song leaves me with a question: what does it mean to repair something that has already happened?

We cannot repair the event itself.

If we hurt someone ten years ago, no action today can create a new version of those ten years.

The past has a frightening property: permanence.

It is the only part of life that can no longer change.

But its meaning can.

A mistake can later become a warning.

A lost relationship can become a lesson in attention.

Regret can become kindness toward someone else.

This does not erase what happened.

Perhaps nothing should.

But it allows damage to produce something other than more damage.

Maybe this is the only realistic version of planting fallen leaves.

We cannot return them to the old tree.

We can put something new into the ground.

## Some Places Become Too Small

There are periods when staying in the same place begins to feel impossible.

Not because the place is bad, but because we have already become too many versions of ourselves there.

Every street contains history.

Every room has an earlier self inside it.

At some point we want somewhere that does not know us yet.

I think this is one reason the idea of going toward the sea touches me.

It is not necessarily running away.

Sometimes leaving is how we discover whether the prison was the place or our way of seeing the place.

We go somewhere else and find that the pain followed us.

That can be disappointing.

But it is also useful information.

Eventually, we understand that geography can give us space, but it cannot perform the entire work.

The real journey is slower.

## Denize Doğru

I do not hear *Denize Doğru* simply as a breakup song.

For me, it is about the moment after something has ended but before we have accepted the new season.

There is still regret. There is still a desire to repair what cannot be repaired. There is still confusion about who left whom and what happened afterward.

But there is also an invitation.

Come.

There is somewhere else to go.

Maybe that is enough.

We do not always need to know exactly what the next life will look like before leaving the old one.

Sometimes direction is enough.

Toward somewhere with air.

Toward somewhere without walls.

Toward a horizon.

We cannot put fallen leaves back on their branches, and winter will eventually come whether we are ready or not.

But perhaps we can choose where we will meet it.

And if I had to choose, I understand the instinct of this song completely.

I would go toward the sea.
