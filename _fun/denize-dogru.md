Some songs remind me of a person. Others remind me of a period of life. *Denize Doğru* does something slightly different: it reminds me of the desire to leave.

Not to disappear completely. Not to escape life. Just to leave one place before everything inside it becomes too cold.

Mustafa Sandal's *Denize Doğru* has always felt much more melancholic than its simple melody first suggests. There is separation in it, but also movement. Someone has been left behind. Someone else has done the leaving. There is regret, loneliness, confusion — but the song does not remain in a room thinking about what went wrong. It keeps looking toward the sea.

That direction matters.

When a place becomes filled with memories, leaving it can begin to feel like the only way to breathe differently. The streets remain the same, but they no longer feel neutral. A café becomes the café where something happened. A corner becomes the place where someone waited. Even the weather starts carrying memories. The city becomes crowded with ghosts.

Perhaps this is why going toward the sea feels so natural.

The sea has no memory of us.

One of the most beautiful images in the song is also one of its strangest: taking a breath and almost drinking someone's memory. After someone leaves, memories stop behaving like ordinary thoughts. They become physical. A smell can bring someone back immediately. A song can change the atmosphere of an entire room. Memory enters through the senses. Sometimes I do not decide to remember — the body remembers before the mind does. I walk somewhere and feel something before I understand why. Then I realize that years ago I stood in the same place with someone who is no longer part of my life. When they leave, the geography remains.

There is an interesting uncertainty in the song. At one moment, the narrator seems responsible for leaving the other person alone. At another, he is the one who has been left. Real relationships rarely produce perfectly clean stories. After separation, both people often create different histories. One remembers being neglected. The other remembers trying. Sometimes both are right. Many relationships end through accumulation: a conversation avoided, a disappointment not discussed, pride, silence, timing. Eventually someone leaves, but leaving may only be the final visible event in a process that began much earlier.

Then the song offers a very simple invitation. Come. Let us go. Toward the sea.

The answer to emotional confusion is sometimes not another explanation. It is movement. There are moments when talking about something more does not help. I have understood the problem intellectually. I know why the relationship ended. I have repeated the story to friends. Still, something remains. At that point I need geography rather than psychology. A different street. A coast. A breakfast somewhere nobody knows me. Travel does not solve grief, but it interrupts its routines. And sometimes interruption is the first form of healing.

The image I find most beautiful in *Denize Doğru* is the desire to plant the fallen leaves again. It is impossible. That is why it is beautiful. A fallen leaf cannot simply be returned to the tree. Its season has already happened. Love often creates the same impossible desire. I want to return to the evening before the argument. The year before things changed. The moment when both people were still certain. But life does not work backwards. Still, the desire to plant the leaf again says something very human. We are creatures who try to repair things even after time has made repair impossible. Sometimes we apologize too late. Sometimes we become the person someone needed only after they are gone. Nothing learned is completely wasted.

Then comes winter. The narrator wants to leave before winter because he knows he will become cold. Some relationships have winters. The conversations become shorter. Touch becomes less natural. The temperature falls gradually. By the time someone says "something is wrong," both people may already have been cold for months. Go before winter. Before the emotional cold becomes permanent. Before two people become strangers who happen to know everything about each other. There are times when staying is courage. There are also times when leaving is.

I keep returning to the title. Not "Let's Go," not "Before Winter," but *Denize Doğru* — toward the sea. Perhaps because the sea has always been one of our oldest images of freedom. A road continues between defined places. The sea opens. Standing in front of it makes ordinary problems look slightly smaller, not because they become less real but because the horizon gives them competition. The sea makes change visible. A wave appears, exists for a moment, and disappears. Nothing stays, yet the sea remains.

We cannot put fallen leaves back on their branches, and winter will eventually come whether we are ready or not.

But perhaps we can choose where we will meet it.

And if I had to choose, I understand the instinct of this song completely.

I would go toward the sea.
