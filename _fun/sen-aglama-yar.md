There is a kind of love that does not ask to be saved.

It only asks the other person not to suffer.

*Sen Ağlama Yar* makes me think about that kind of love. The world in the song is not safe. There are roads, mountains, storms and danger. The person speaking seems to know that something bad may happen, yet his first thought is not about himself. He worries about the person who will remain behind.

I find that deeply human.

When we really love someone, we sometimes begin to imagine that we can carry pain for them. We know it is impossible, but the instinct is still there. If sadness must come, let it come to me. If somebody must be hurt, let it be me. If life has to choose one of us, choose me.

It sounds dramatic when written like that.

But smaller versions of this feeling exist in ordinary life.

A parent hides fear from a child. A friend says, "I'm fine," because the other person already has enough problems. Someone going through a difficult time still asks whether everybody else is okay.

Love can make people strangely generous with their own pain.

I have done this too.

There were times when I was worried, tired or hurt, but I did not want the people close to me to carry the same weight. It was easier to say that everything was under control. Sometimes that was strength. Sometimes it was probably pride.

We often confuse the two.

There is also something powerful about the landscape of this song. Mountains, roads, snow and storms are not only background. They make life itself feel difficult, almost like a journey where the next turn may contain something you cannot control.

I understand that image more as I get older.

When we are young, we imagine that careful decisions can protect us from most problems. Later, we discover how much of life depends on things outside our control. Illness, accidents, timing, other people's choices, simple bad luck.

The road can change suddenly.

And when that happens, our thoughts often go first to the people we love.

What will happen to them?

Will they be all right?

Will they understand?

That is why the wish to protect someone from sadness feels almost more painful than sadness itself. You know you cannot stay beside them forever. You know one day one of you will have to continue without the other.

Nobody likes to think about that.

But every deep relationship contains this fact somewhere inside it.

Perhaps that is also why love can feel frightening. The more people you care about, the more things the world can take from you. Yet we continue loving anyway.

Maybe there is courage in that.

The song also makes me think about flowers in bad weather. Something fragile surviving where it should not survive. We often treat the people we love in the same way. We want the storms to somehow avoid them.

Of course, storms do not listen.

Life does not protect people because they are loved.

So we do what we can.

We stand in front when possible. We take some of the weight. We hide our fear for a while. And sometimes, when there is nothing else left to do, we simply hope that the people we love will suffer less than we do.

There is sadness in that hope.

But there is also tenderness.

Maybe one of the purest forms of love is not saying, "Do not leave me."

It is saying, "Whatever happens to me, please be all right."
