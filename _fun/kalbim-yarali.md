There are love songs about separation, and then there are songs about what remains after separation. Murat Göğebakan's *Yaralı* belongs to the second kind. Its pain does not come only from the fact that someone has left. The deeper wound is that the person who leaves takes a part of the future with them.

> "Kalbim yaralı, gönlüm yaralı."

A wounded heart, a wounded inner world.

When pain is real enough, people return to very simple words. Philosophy comes later.

What makes the song more interesting to me is the thought that the lost person has created a distance between the narrator and his most beautiful tomorrows. This is much more painful than ordinary nostalgia. The problem is no longer only yesterday. The future itself has been damaged.

When I love someone deeply, I do not only love the person standing in front of me. Without noticing it, I begin placing them inside imaginary scenes. A trip that has not happened yet. A home that does not exist. Future conversations. Growing older together. I quietly populate tomorrow with the person I love. Then separation arrives, and all of those imaginary scenes collapse at the same time. This is why losing someone can feel strangely larger than losing one person. I also lose a possible life.

One of the shortest images in the song is also one of the strongest:

> "Kırıldı kanadım."

My wing was broken.

I like this image because it describes heartbreak not simply as pain but as loss of movement. A wound hurts; a broken wing prevents flight.

After emotional loss, the most surprising thing is sometimes not sadness itself. It is the disappearance of desire. Places no longer seem worth visiting. Plans feel unnecessary. Even things unrelated to the lost person lose their brightness. Nothing physical stops movement. Yet movement becomes difficult. A person can become connected not only with happiness but with wanting the future. When they disappear, motivation can disappear with them. This is dangerous because I may begin believing the person was the source of life itself. Usually they were not. They were connected to a version of myself that felt alive. That version can exist again. But immediately after loss, this is difficult to believe.

The song does not protect itself with irony. There is crying, longing, waiting, and open vulnerability. Modern people often feel embarrassed by direct emotion. We try to appear controlled even when we are not. We use humor to hide attachment. We avoid saying that someone hurt us because admitting pain seems to give the other person power. This song does the opposite. It says: I was hurt. I cried. I waited. There is a strange dignity in that honesty. Being affected by another person is not automatically weakness. You cannot love deeply and remain completely protected. The same door allows both joy and injury to enter.

There is also something I understand about sad songs. Private pain is chaotic. A song gives it rhythm, melody, repetition, beginning, and end. Something shapeless inside us suddenly has structure. The song does not remove sadness. It organizes it. Someone else has stood here. Someone else has waited. Someone else's heart was wounded too. This is why sad music can make loneliness smaller rather than larger.

A wounded heart is not a broken heart. Something broken may appear useless; something wounded is still alive. A wound proves two things at once: something damaged us, and we survived the damage. Over time, wounds become scars. The goal is not forgetting. The goal is to remember without reopening the wound every time.

The line that remains with me most is the idea that someone can separate us from our beautiful tomorrows. After certain losses, the future becomes difficult to imagine without the person who was supposed to be inside it. But those are different things. One future ended. The future did not. This difference can take a long time to understand. We build one possible life so carefully in our imagination that it begins to feel like the only possible life. When reality destroys it, we experience not only disappointment but a kind of existential confusion: if that life will not happen, then what happens now?

There is no immediate answer.

The beautiful tomorrows that disappeared with one person will never happen. That deserves grief. But there are other tomorrows that were impossible to imagine while that person was still beside us.

A wounded heart does not know this yet.

Time does.

And maybe that is why the heart keeps beating even when, for a while, it has no idea where it is going.
