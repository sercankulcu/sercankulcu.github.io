Some songs speak about heartbreak. *Kırmış Kalbini* speaks about what happens after the heartbreak, when somebody still decides to stay beside you.

That is what I hear in it.

There are times when people hurt us simply because they can. Sometimes it is jealousy. Sometimes it is pride. Sometimes they cannot accept something in us that they do not understand. The reason may change, but the result is often the same: after enough years, you begin to carry the marks of things that were said and done to you.

The difficult part is that these marks are not always visible.

A person can laugh, work, meet friends and continue with life while still carrying old damage. Someone may seem strong only because they have learned how to hide where they were hurt.

I think we all know people like that.

Maybe we are people like that.

What touches me most in this song is not the pain itself. It is the presence beside the pain. There is someone saying, in effect, that whatever happened, whatever was done, we are still here.

I value that more as I get older.

Many people are around when life is easy. They enjoy the good version of you. They like the jokes, the energy, the evenings when nobody needs anything serious.

Then life becomes difficult.

That is when the number becomes smaller.

Real friendship often reveals itself in very ordinary ways. Someone calls when there is nothing useful to say. Someone sits beside you without trying to repair you. Someone remembers what you went through even after everybody else has forgotten.

They stay.

I used to think helping someone meant finding the right words. Now I think words are sometimes the least important part. When a person is truly hurt, explanations can sound empty.

Presence is different.

Presence says you do not have to perform strength for me.

It says I remember what happened.

It says you are not carrying this alone.

There have been times in my life when the people who helped me most probably had no idea they were helping. We talked about ordinary things. We ate something. We laughed at something stupid. Nobody gave a speech about surviving difficult days.

Still, I went home feeling lighter.

Maybe that is friendship at its best.

Not saving someone.

Simply refusing to disappear.

The song also makes me think about how much damage can come from people who are supposedly close to us. We expect strangers to misunderstand us. It hurts differently when the wound comes from someone walking the same road.

Perhaps betrayal always needs closeness first.

A stranger cannot disappoint you in the same way because you never trusted them with anything important. The people who can truly break your heart are usually the people you allowed near it.

That can make trusting again difficult.

After enough disappointment, you begin to protect yourself. You share less. You expect less. You convince yourself that needing nobody is a kind of strength.

I am not sure it is.

There is strength in surviving alone, but there is another kind of strength in accepting that someone wants to stand beside you.

Not everyone leaves.

That may be the part of *Kırmış Kalbini* I like most.

It does not pretend that the past was harmless. The heart has already been broken. Years have already been lost. There is no promise that everything will suddenly become good.

There is only companionship.

And sometimes that is enough.

You look behind you and see all the roads, the bad decisions, the people who hurt you, the nights you thought would never end.

Then you look beside you.

Someone is still there.

Maybe both of you are a little damaged. Maybe neither of you became the person you expected to become.

But you are still here.

There are worse ways to survive.
