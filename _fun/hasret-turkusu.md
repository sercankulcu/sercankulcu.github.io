Some kinds of longing are easy to understand. You miss a person, a city, a house, a familiar voice. You know exactly what is absent.

But sometimes longing is less clear. I feel that something is missing, yet I cannot easily name it. *Hasret Türküsü* makes me think about this second kind of longing: not only missing someone, but missing a time, a place, or even an older version of myself.

I can sit next to someone and still feel far away. I can return to the street where I grew up and realize that the place is close, but the life I remember is gone. This is perhaps the most difficult kind of distance. There is no road that can take me back.

Sometimes I believe I miss a person, but what I really miss is the life around that person. The old house. The people who used to visit. The sound of the neighborhood. The evenings when nobody was in a hurry. I miss being younger without knowing I was young. Memory connects all these things, so one person can become the symbol of an entire period. When I remember them, a whole world returns. That is why some memories feel larger than the people inside them.

I have returned to places I remembered and found a different sadness waiting. The building is still there, but the people are gone. The shop has another name. The garden is smaller than I remember. I recognize everything, yet nothing feels exactly the same. Then I understand that the place was never the whole memory. The people were part of it. My age was part of it. My hopes were part of it. I can return to the place, but not to the moment.

Longing is not always a weakness. Sometimes it is proof that something mattered. I carry certain people and places because they helped shape me. They taught me how to love, how to trust, how to lose, or simply how to belong somewhere. A person can live in one city and carry another city inside. There is enough space inside us for more than one time.

The Turkish word *hasret* is difficult to translate. "Longing" comes close, but *hasret* often feels heavier. It contains distance. It contains waiting. It contains affection. And sometimes it contains the knowledge that what we miss may never return. Missing something that will return tomorrow is one feeling. Missing something that belongs only to the past is another. One is waiting. The other is learning to live with absence.

For me, *Hasret Türküsü* is not only about separation. It is about the strange relationship I have with everything I leave behind. I continue walking, but some part of me keeps looking back. This does not mean I want to live in the past. It simply means that the past has not completely left me.

We miss because we cared.

We remember because something mattered.

A life without longing would also be a life without strong connections.

And sometimes, when a familiar melody begins, a place that is hundreds of kilometers away can suddenly feel very close. An old room appears. A forgotten face becomes clear. A road returns.

For a few minutes, distance disappears.

Then the song ends.

We are still here. The past is still there.

And somewhere between the two, longing continues to live.
