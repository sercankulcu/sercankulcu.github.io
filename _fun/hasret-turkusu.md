Some kinds of longing are easy to understand. You miss a person, a city, a house, or a familiar voice. You know exactly what is absent.

But sometimes longing is less clear.

You feel that something is missing, yet you cannot easily name it.

*Hasret Türküsü* makes me think about this second kind of longing: not only missing someone, but missing a time, a place, or even an older version of yourself.

## Distance Is Not Always Measured in Kilometers

We usually think of distance as something between two places.

One city is far from another. One person lives in another country. A road separates us.

But emotional distance can be much greater.

You can sit next to someone and still feel far away. You can return to the street where you grew up and realize that the place is close, but the life you remember is gone.

This is perhaps the most difficult kind of distance.

There is no road that can take you back.

## Missing a Time

Sometimes we believe we miss a person, but what we really miss is the life around that person.

We miss the old house.

The people who used to visit.

The sound of the neighborhood.

The evenings when nobody was in a hurry.

We miss being younger without knowing that we were young.

Memory connects all these things, so one person can become the symbol of an entire period.

When we remember them, a whole world returns.

That is why some memories feel larger than the people inside them.

## Returning Is Not Always Possible

There is a common idea that returning to a place will bring back an old feeling.

Sometimes it does.

You walk through a familiar street and suddenly remember details you had forgotten for years.

But sometimes returning creates a different kind of sadness.

The building is still there, but the people are gone.

The shop has another name.

The garden is smaller than you remember.

You recognize everything, yet nothing feels exactly the same.

Then you understand that the place was never the whole memory.

The people were part of it.

Your age was part of it.

Your hopes were part of it.

You can return to the place, but not to the moment.

## The Things We Carry

Longing is not always a weakness.

Sometimes it is proof that something mattered.

We carry certain people and places because they helped shape us. They taught us how to love, how to trust, how to lose, or simply how to belong somewhere.

This is why forgetting is not always necessary.

We can continue with our lives without removing the past.

A person can live in one city and carry another city inside.

A grown adult can still carry the child they once were.

Someone who has built a new life can still remember an old door, an old table, or an old voice with great tenderness.

There is enough space inside us for more than one time.

## Hasret

The Turkish word *hasret* is difficult to translate with only one English word.

"Longing" comes close, but *hasret* often feels heavier.

It contains distance.

It contains waiting.

It contains affection.

And sometimes it contains the knowledge that what we miss may never return.

That last part changes everything.

Missing something that will return tomorrow is one feeling.

Missing something that belongs only to the past is another.

One is waiting.

The other is learning to live with absence.

## Hasret Türküsü

For me, *Hasret Türküsü* is not only about separation. It is about the strange relationship we have with everything we leave behind.

We continue walking, but some part of us keeps looking back.

This does not mean we want to live in the past.

It simply means that the past has not completely left us.

Maybe this is normal.

A life without longing would also be a life without strong connections.

We miss because we cared.

We remember because something mattered.

And sometimes, when a familiar melody begins, a place that is hundreds of kilometers away can suddenly feel very close.

An old room appears.

A forgotten face becomes clear.

A road returns.

For a few minutes, distance disappears.

Then the song ends.

The room becomes quiet again.

We are still here.

The past is still there.

And somewhere between the two, longing continues to live.
