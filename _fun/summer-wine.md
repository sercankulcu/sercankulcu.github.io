Lana Del Rey's *Summer Wine* does not remind me of summer. It reminds me of the moment when summer is already ending.

There is warmth in the song, but it is the kind of warmth you know you cannot keep. A late evening, an open window, someone beside you, and the strange feeling that this moment will hurt one day. Nothing has happened yet, but nostalgia has somehow arrived early.

That is what I love about Lana's version. She does not sound as if she is living the story. She sounds as if she is remembering it years later.

The wine is sweet, the night is beautiful, and something is already being lost.

I think some relationships are like that. You know they are not built for ordinary life. They belong to one season, one city, one version of yourself. You know they may end badly, but knowing does not make the experience less beautiful. Sometimes it makes it more beautiful.

This is the cruel side of memory. It does not always preserve what was good for us. It preserves what made us feel alive.

Years later, we forget the arguments and remember the light in the room. We forget why we were unhappy and remember the way someone looked at us once. Memory removes the ordinary pain and keeps the dangerous beauty.

Maybe that is why the song feels less like a warning and more like temptation.

You already know the morning will come.

You already know something will be missing when you wake up.

Still, you drink.

There are people I would never want back in my life, but I would not want to erase the time I had with them either. That contradiction feels very close to *Summer Wine*. Something can be wrong for us and still become a beautiful memory. A summer does not become meaningless because autumn came.

Perhaps melancholy begins there.

Not in wanting the past back, but in knowing that even if it returned, it would not be the same. The people would be older. We would know too much. The innocence of the moment would be gone.

So we listen to a song instead.

For a few minutes, the air becomes warm again. The road is empty. Someone is still beside us. There is still time.

Then the song ends.

And maybe that is exactly why we play it again.
