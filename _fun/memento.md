I watched *Memento* with a notebook beside me.

I thought this was clever.

The film disagreed.

At first I tried to record the order of events.

Names.

Connections.

What happened before what.

I believed that if I collected enough information, I could reconstruct the story faster than the film wanted me to.

Eventually I stopped writing.

That was when the film became more interesting.

Leonard cannot create new long-term memories.

He uses photographs, notes, and tattoos to build an external memory system.

That premise immediately appealed to the technical part of my mind.

If internal storage is unreliable, use external storage.

Document.

Label.

Persist state.

Create a system.

But the film quickly exposes the weakness.

A system is only as trustworthy as the information entered into it.

That is a very different problem.

A note can be permanent and still be wrong.

A photograph can be accurate and still be interpreted badly.

A tattoo looks authoritative because it cannot be easily erased.

Permanence is not truth.

That idea stayed with me.

We trust written things more than memory because writing appears stable.

Minutes.

Logs.

Documents.

Messages.

Databases.

They feel objective.

But every record begins with someone deciding what to record.

The failure may happen before storage.

Leonard's condition makes this obvious, but ordinary people do something similar.

We keep photographs and forget the argument outside the frame.

We save a message and lose the tone of the conversation.

We remember the conclusion and forget how uncertain we were when we reached it.

Memory is selective.

Documentation is selective too.

The film's reverse structure puts the audience inside Leonard's problem.

We enter scenes without knowing what caused them.

A person appears trustworthy.

Then dangerous.

Then trustworthy again.

Our judgment changes because context changes.

That was uncomfortable.

We like to believe character is stable.

Good person.

Bad person.

Friend.

Enemy.

But our interpretation of someone depends heavily on what we know about the previous five minutes.

Remove the previous five minutes and certainty collapses.

The most disturbing part is Leonard's relationship with his own system.

He depends on it.

He also has the ability to manipulate it.

That means the person who most needs objective truth can manufacture the evidence that will later convince him.

A closed loop.

The system becomes dangerous because future Leonard trusts past Leonard more than he should.

That made me think about identity.

If memory changes, what keeps the person continuous?

Is Leonard the same person from moment to moment because his body continues?

Because his purpose continues?

Because the notes tell him who he is?

We all build identity partly through stories about our past.

I did this.

This happened to me.

I am this kind of person.

If those stories are wrong, does identity become wrong too?

When the film ended, I looked at the notebook beside me.

My neat attempt to control the story had become funny.

I had written down facts.

The film had been asking a more difficult question.

Who decides what the facts mean?
