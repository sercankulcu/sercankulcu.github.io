There are people we miss quietly, and there are people we want to suffer a little because they miss us too.

I think *Beni Hatırla* lives somewhere between these two feelings.

It is not a polite goodbye. There is anger in it, but also weakness. There is almost a childish wish hidden inside the anger: when the phone rings, think it may be me. When somebody knocks on the door, hope for one second that I have come back. When the evening arrives and everything becomes quieter, remember that somewhere in another city I once existed in your life.

I understand that feeling more than I would like to admit.

After some separations, we try to behave like reasonable adults. We say that life goes on, that people change, that everything has an ending. All of this is true. Yet there is another part of us that is not reasonable at all. That part wants the other person to look at old photographs. It wants them to find an old message by accident. It wants a familiar song to ruin an otherwise normal evening.

Not forever.

Just for a few minutes.

There is something strange about the objects left after a relationship. A photograph is only paper or pixels until the right person looks at it. A letter is only writing until somebody recognizes the handwriting. Even a pillow can become evidence that another person once slept beside you.

Then one day these ordinary things become dangerous.

You open a drawer and suddenly you are years younger. You hear a song in a supermarket and forget what you were buying. You see an old photograph and notice details that meant nothing at the time. The shirt. The table. The light coming from the window.

Memory is unfair like that.

It keeps useless details and loses important ones.

I have forgotten entire conversations with people who once mattered to me, but I can still remember the road we were walking on. I have forgotten what an argument was about, but I remember the silence afterwards. Sometimes I cannot remember a person's exact voice, yet I remember perfectly how hearing that voice made me feel.

Maybe that is why being forgotten frightens us.

It is not only about losing a place in somebody's mind. It makes us wonder whether the past can disappear if nobody carries it anymore.

Two people can once know almost everything about each other and later become complete strangers. They may live in different cities, have different homes, different friends and different lives. Years can pass without a single word between them.

Still, evening has a strange power.

The day keeps us busy. There is work, traffic, noise, people, messages and small problems demanding attention. Then evening comes, the world becomes slower, and memory finds the empty spaces.

That is when certain people return.

Not because we want them back.

That distinction matters.

There are people I remember with love whom I would never invite back into my life. Some stories were beautiful partly because they ended. If we opened them again, perhaps we would only discover that the people inside those memories no longer exist.

But I would still like to know that somewhere, on some ordinary evening, they remember me too.

Maybe they hear a song and stop for a moment. Maybe they find an old photograph and cannot immediately put it away. Maybe the phone rings and, for one foolish second, an old name passes through their mind.

That is enough.

You do not have to come back.

You do not even have to forgive me.

You can throw away the letters, hate the songs and close every door.

Just do not make me completely disappear.
