There is a moment, after some relationships end, when you discover that your body has stopped answering first.

You hear a name and the reaction arrives a second later than it used to. Then a moment later than that. Then not at all.

That is when you know something has truly ended.

*Herkes Gibisin* understands that moment.

Love distinguishes. That is part of what it is. One person steps into a room and becomes different from everyone else in it. Their messages carry more weight than other messages. Their silence is louder than other people's words. Even the way they are absent is specific.

For a while, they are not simply one person among many.

Then something changes.

Sometimes the change is sudden. More often it is slow enough that you only understand it afterwards. You stop checking whether they are online. You stop preparing what you would say if they called. A song that once changed the temperature of a room plays somewhere and does nothing.

That is when you know.

I used to think the opposite of love was anger.

I was wrong.

Anger still creates a connection. If you are angry with someone, they still occupy live space inside you. You are still having arguments with them, still imagining the conversation that would finally settle something, still waiting for them to understand what they did.

Indifference is different.

It is not dramatic. It is not satisfying. It is simply the withdrawal of attention.

The strongest sign that a relationship is truly over is not when you stop loving someone.

It is when you stop needing them to understand what they did.

No more waiting for an apology. No more imagined explanations that would make everything legible. No more final conversation playing out in your head before sleep.

You simply continue.

What I find most honest about this song is the absence of hatred. Because when even anger disappears, the person loses the last special position they held. They are no longer the great love, the great disappointment, the great mistake.

They become someone you once knew.

Before love, two people are strangers among millions. For a time, they become the center of each other's world. Then, slowly, life moves them back into the crowd.

You still recognize the face.

You still remember what it was like when that face was the one you looked for first in a room.

But the mechanism has stopped.

And one day, without noticing the exact moment it happened, the person you once separated from everyone else has quietly returned to where they started.

They are simply one of them again.
