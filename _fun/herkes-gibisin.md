There is a strange moment after some relationships end.

You do not hate the person anymore. You do not miss them the same way either. Their name can still bring back a memory, but it no longer changes your breathing. The person who once seemed different from everyone else slowly becomes ordinary.

*Herkes Gibisin* makes me think about that moment.

Love has a way of giving one person a special position in the world. They enter a room and you notice. Their message matters more than other messages. Their silence feels heavier than other people's words.

For a while, they are not simply one person among many.

They are the person.

Then something changes.

Not always suddenly. Sometimes the change is so slow that you only understand it afterwards. You stop waiting for their message. You stop checking whether they are online. You hear their name and realize that your body no longer reacts before your mind does.

That is when you know something has ended.

I think this is very different from anger.

Anger still creates a connection. If you are angry with someone, they still occupy space inside you. You are still having conversations with them in your head. You still imagine what you should have said and what they should have understood.

Indifference is quieter.

And perhaps more final.

There have been people in my life I thought I would never be able to forget. At the time, that feeling seemed completely reasonable. Some people become connected to a whole period of your life, so losing them feels like losing the period itself.

But time is very patient.

It does not always erase memories. Instead, it removes their power.

You can remember a person clearly and still feel almost nothing.

I find that both sad and comforting.

Sad, because there was once so much feeling there. Comforting, because no pain keeps its original shape forever.

Sometimes I think the strongest sign that a relationship is truly over is not when you stop loving someone.

It is when you stop needing them to understand what they did.

You no longer wait for an apology. You do not need justice from them. You do not need one final conversation that explains everything perfectly.

You simply continue.

That is probably why the absence of hatred in this song feels important to me.

When even anger disappears, the person loses the last special place they had. They are no longer the great love, the great disappointment or the great mistake.

They become someone you once knew.

I used to think this would be a terrible ending.

Now I am not so sure.

Maybe becoming ordinary again is the natural end of some relationships. Before love, two people are strangers among millions. For a while, they become the center of each other's attention.

Then life slowly moves them back into the crowd.

You may still recognize the face.

You may still remember the voice.

You may even remember how much you once loved them.

But the magic is gone.

And one day, almost without noticing it, you understand that the person you once separated from everyone else has returned to where they started.

They are simply one of them again.
