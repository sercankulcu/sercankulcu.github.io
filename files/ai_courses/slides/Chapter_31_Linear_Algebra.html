Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How do vectors and matrices represent data and transformations?

Linear algebra studies vectors, matrices and linear transformations. In AI and data courses almost everything is a vector: an image, a word, a user. A neural network layer is, to a large extent, a matrix multiplication.

Introduction

A Short History

1844

Grassmann sets out the idea of a multidimensional vector space systematically for the first time.

1858

Cayley treats matrices as algebraic objects in their own right; matrix multiplication is defined.

1901

Pearson finds the lines and planes that best fit data: principal component analysis (PCA).

1936

Eckart and Young show that the SVD gives the best low-rank approximation of a matrix.

1965

Golub and Kahan give a numerically stable way to compute the SVD on a computer.

1979

BLAS is published: the basic matrix operations beneath today's scientific and AI libraries.

SVD

Projection

The closest point to a vector in a subspace.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: In face recognition each face image is a long vector of pixels. The faces are stacked into a matrix, the mean is subtracted, SVD finds a few most important directions (eigenfaces), and each face is represented and compared by its weights on those few directions.

Try It

Augmenting a Digit Image

A handwritten digit recognizer has few examples; making new ones by rotating and shearing each image (data augmentation) means multiplying every pixel by a 2×2 matrix. Change the matrix and watch the “7”.

The determinant tells how much area is scaled; eigenvectors give the directions the map does not bend. The same eigenvector idea finds the directions of greatest spread in PCA.

Risks and Ethics

Ill-conditioning

With a large condition number, a tiny input error is magnified many times; the answer fills up with meaningless digits.

The normal equations trap

Forming AᵀA for least squares squares the condition number; solving with QR or SVD is safer.

Silent shape errors

NumPy broadcasting combines mismatched shapes without an error; the result is wrong but looks fine.

Misreading PCA

On unscaled features PCA favors the variable with the largest units; a component is not always a meaningful factor.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Linear algebra is just solving equations.Its real power is understanding transformations and decompositions: PCA, SVD and embeddings are built on them.
Computing the inverse and multiplying is the right way.In practice you do not invert; you solve with LU, QR or Cholesky, which is faster and more stable.
High-dimensional space behaves like 3D.In high dimensions points sit at similar distances from each other; intuition misleads (curse of dimensionality).
With libraries around, theory is unnecessary.Rank deficiency, ill-conditioning and shape errors make sense only if you know the theory.
