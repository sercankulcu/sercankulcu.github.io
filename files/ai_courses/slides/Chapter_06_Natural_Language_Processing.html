Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How does a computer analyze, understand and generate human language?

Natural language processing studies how computers analyze, understand and generate human language; text mining extracts information and patterns from large collections of text. At the heart of the course are text representation, which turns text into numbers, and language models that predict the next word.

Introduction

A Short History

1954

Georgetown-IBM experiment: more than sixty Russian sentences are translated into English automatically.

1966

Weizenbaum's ELIZA imitates a therapist with simple pattern matching.

1990s

Statistical methods replace rule-based systems; IBM's translation models lead the way.

2013

Mikolov et al.'s word2vec embeds words as vectors that reflect meaning.

2017

The transformer architecture is introduced, followed by pretrained models such as BERT and GPT.

2022

Large language models reach a mass audience through chat interfaces.

The Course

Core Concepts

Tokenization

Splitting text into word or subword units.

Morphology

Root and suffix analysis; critical in agglutinative languages like Turkish.

Bag of words

Representing text by word counts, ignoring order.

TF-IDF

High weight for words frequent in a document but rare in the corpus.

Word embeddings

Words with similar meanings lie close in vector space.

Language model

A model that predicts the probability of the next word.

Named entity recognition

Finding names of people, places and organizations in text.

Topic modeling

Finding hidden topics in documents with methods such as LDA.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: An online store collects product reviews, tokenizes the text and analyzes suffixes, turns the reviews into vectors with a pretrained transformer, trains a positive/negative sentiment classifier and measures it with the F1 score. The result shows which topics the complaints cluster around.

The Course

What Does the Course Cover?

  1. Regular expressions, tokenization, normalization
  2. Morphology: stemming and morphological analysis
  3. N-gram language models and smoothing
  4. Text classification: naive Bayes, logistic regression
  5. Vector semantics: TF-IDF and word2vec
  6. Part-of-speech tagging and hidden Markov models
  7. Named entity recognition and sequence labeling (CRF)
  8. Syntactic parsing: dependency trees
  9. Language modeling with recurrent networks
  10. Transformers, BERT and pretraining
  11. Machine translation and evaluation with BLEU
  12. Topic modeling (LDA) and information extraction

Try It

Is the Customer Review Positive?

An online store gets thousands of reviews a day; negative ones should go straight to customer service. Pick a sample review or write your own; the model scores the words one by one.

The computer first splits the text into words, then gives each word a weight. A word like “not” or “didn't” flips the meaning of what follows; language processing has to teach the model this structure.

Risks and Ethics

Hallucination

Language models fluently invent sources that do not exist; in the US, lawyers have filed briefs citing case law a chatbot made up.

Stereotypes in embeddings

Embeddings produce analogies like "man is to programmer as woman is to homemaker"; translation renders neutral pronouns by stereotype.

Low-resource languages

Models work best in English; on Turkish and languages with little data the same system makes noticeably more errors.

Surveillance through text

Emails, messages and posts are personal data under GDPR and similar laws; text mining can also be used to monitor and profile people.

In depth: Explainable AI and AI Ethics.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
The model understands text like a human.Language models learn statistical patterns; fluent text is not always correct or consistent.
Any method built for English works for Turkish.In agglutinative Turkish one root takes hundreds of forms; tokenization and morphology need separate care.
Large language models ended classic NLP.TF-IDF, regular expressions and small models are still fast, cheap and enough for most jobs.
Text mining is just counting words.Counting is the start; extracting entities, relations and topics requires language structure.
