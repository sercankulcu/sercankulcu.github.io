Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How are sampled signals analyzed, filtered and transformed?

Digital signal processing analyzes, filters and transforms audio, vibration, ECG or radio signals that have been sampled at regular intervals, using mathematical transforms. The course rests on exact mathematics, not learning; it is the common foundation of the audio, image and communications courses.

Introduction

A Short History

1822

In his book on heat, Fourier proposes writing functions as sums of sinusoids.

1949

Shannon publishes sampling theory: a band-limited signal sampled fast enough is recovered exactly.

1965

Cooley and Tukey publish the fast Fourier transform (FFT); computing spectra becomes practical.

1974

Ahmed, Natarajan and Rao introduce the discrete cosine transform (DCT); JPEG and MP3 build on it.

1975

Oppenheim and Schafer's Digital Signal Processing appears; the field becomes a standard course.

1978

Texas Instruments' Speak & Spell brings single-chip digital speech synthesis into homes.

The Course

Core Concepts

Sampling

Measuring a continuous signal at equal intervals to get a sequence of numbers.

Nyquist criterion

Sample faster than twice the highest frequency.

Aliasing

With undersampling, a high frequency shows up as a low one.

Quantization

Rounding amplitude to a finite number of levels; bit depth.

Convolution

The response of a linear time-invariant system to its input.

Fourier and FFT

A fast way to split a signal into frequency components.

FIR and IIR filters

Low-pass, high-pass and band-pass digital filters.

z-transform

Poles and zeros of a filter; stability analysis.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: An ECG device samples the heart signal 360 times per second. The spectrum shows 50 Hz mains noise; a notch filter suppresses only that frequency. R peaks are found in the cleaned signal and heart rate is computed from the time between them. No step involves learning.

Try It

Cleaning a Noisy Pulse

A chest-strap ECG samples at 200 Hz; 50 Hz mains hum and muscle noise have crept into the recording. Change the moving-average window length and watch the signal in both time and frequency.

The dashed line is the filter’s frequency response: at M = 4 its zero falls exactly on 50 Hz. Larger M means less noise, but the sharp R peak gets flattened too.
To separate noise you first look at its frequency. The course teaches you to design a filter from its frequency response, not by trial, and to leave the component you want intact.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
A higher sampling rate is always better.Beyond the Nyquist limit you gain no information; only data and computation grow.
The FFT is a different transform from the Fourier transform.The FFT is an algorithm that computes the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) fast; the result is the same.
A filter tells noise from signal and removes it.A filter separates by frequency; if noise shares the signal's band, the filter cannot separate it.
Deep learning has made signal processing obsolete.Audio and sensor models usually take a spectrogram or a filtered signal as input.
