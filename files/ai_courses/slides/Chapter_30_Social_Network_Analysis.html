Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How are networks of people and their relationships analyzed?

Social network analysis studies the graph formed by treating people as nodes and relationships as edges. The question is not the traits of individuals but the structure of their ties: who is central, which groups exist, and how a rumor or a disease spreads through the network.

Introduction

A Short History

1736

Euler solves the Königsberg bridges problem; graph theory is born.

1934

Moreno draws relationships in diagrams he calls sociograms.

1967

Milgram's letter experiment popularizes the idea of "six degrees".

1973

Granovetter shows "the strength of weak ties": job leads come from distant acquaintances.

1998

Watts and Strogatz publish the small-world network model.

2008

The Louvain method makes fast community detection possible on networks with millions of nodes.

The Course

Core Concepts

Nodes and edges

Directed or undirected, weighted or unweighted.

Degree distribution

A few highly connected nodes, many with few links.

Centrality

Degree, betweenness, closeness and eigenvector measures.

Clustering coefficient

How many of my friends know each other.

Small world

High clustering but short average paths.

Community

Nodes densely linked inside, sparsely to the outside.

Modularity

How good a partition into communities is.

Diffusion models

Spread of information or disease on a network (SIR).

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A researcher builds a network from a school's friendship survey, looks at the degree distribution, finds "bridge" students with high betweenness, extracts friend groups that cross classes with Louvain, and discusses the drawn result with the teachers.

Try It

Who Should Start the Campaign?

A club will hand its announcement to one person in the friendship network of its 18 members. Click a person to see their centrality, find the communities, then watch the news spread from that person step by step.

Size:
By degree, people with many friends such as Mert stand out, but the bridge between groups is Deniz (betweenness). Starting the news at a bridge carries it into several communities; this is how influencers are chosen.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
The person with the most friends is the most influential.Degree is one measure; a bridge joining two groups (betweenness) can matter more.
Looking at a network drawing is enough to understand it.Large networks draw as a "hairball"; numerical measures and tests are needed.
Community detection has one right answer.Different methods give different partitions; results are read with domain knowledge.
Connected people have influenced each other.Similar people choose each other anyway (homophily); influence is hard to separate from selection.
