Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How does a machine sense the physical world, know where it is and move?

Robotics studies how to design and control machines that sense and move in the physical world. The course combines perception, localization, planning and control under noisy measurements and real-time constraints; robot vision is the eye of this loop.

Introduction

A Short History

1961

Unimate, the first industrial robot, starts work at a General Motors plant.

1966

The Shakey project starts at SRI: the first mobile robot to sense and plan; A* is developed for it.

1986

Brooks proposes the subsumption architecture: direct sense-act behaviors instead of planning.

1997

The Sojourner rover explores the surface of Mars on the Mars Pathfinder mission.

2002

The Roomba vacuum goes on sale; mobile robots enter homes.

2005

Stanford's Stanley completes and wins the DARPA Grand Challenge desert race with no driver.

The Course

Core Concepts

Degrees of freedom

The number of axes along which a robot can move independently.

Coordinate frame

World, robot, camera; transformation matrices between them.

Forward kinematics

Finding the end-effector position from joint angles.

Inverse kinematics

Finding the joint angles for a desired end-effector position.

PID control

Feedback that corrects error by proportional, integral and derivative terms.

Localization

Position from noisy measurements with Kalman and particle filters.

SLAM

Building a map while locating yourself in it.

Path planning

A*, RRT: a collision-free path to the goal.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A warehouse robot scans its surroundings with lidar and a camera, estimates its position on the map with a particle filter, plans a path between shelves with A* and sets wheel speeds with PID. The loop runs tens of times a second; each pass, measurements correct the plan.

The Course

What Does the Course Cover?

  1. Position and orientation: rotation matrices, frames
  2. Forward kinematics and Denavit-Hartenberg notation
  3. Inverse kinematics and the Jacobian
  4. Robot dynamics: forces, torques, equations of motion
  5. Feedback control: PID and stability
  6. Trajectory generation and manipulator control
  7. Mobile robot kinematics and odometry
  8. Sensors: cameras, lidar, IMU, encoders
  9. Probabilistic state estimation: the Kalman filter
  10. Particle filters and Monte Carlo localization
  11. Mapping and SLAM
  12. Motion planning: A*, probabilistic roadmaps, RRT

Try It

A Robot Arm Picks Up a Part

The tip of a two-joint arm on an assembly line must reach a part on the table, but motors take joint angles, not tip positions. Click a target: inverse kinematics computes the angles and the arm turns there.

Robotics turns “where?” into “which angles?”: inverse kinematics, workspace limits and choosing the collision-free one among several solutions. A real arm has 6 joints and obstacles.

Practice

Risks and Ethics

Collisions with people

Robot arms exert large forces; speed and force limits, emergency stops and safety zones are part of collaborative robot design.

Sensor failure and spoofing

A dirty lidar, a camera blind spot or a spoofed GPS signal can lead a robot confidently to the wrong place.

Liability in autonomous crashes

In crashes such as the 2018 Uber test vehicle that killed a pedestrian in Arizona, blame is split between maker, operator and driver.

Surveillance and weapons

Camera-equipped mobile robots and drones open private spaces to monitoring; autonomous weapons are a separate international debate.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Robotics is only mechanics and electronics.Today's hard problems are mostly in software: perception, localization and planning.
A good plan gets the robot to the goal.Wheels slip and sensors are noisy; a plan holds only with continuous feedback.
What works in simulation works in reality.The sim-to-real gap is a research topic of its own in robotics.
Robot vision is the same as computer vision.Robot vision stresses depth, pose and real time; its output is a motion.
