Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How do we model and query many interdependent, uncertain variables?

Probabilistic graphical models represent the joint distribution of many uncertain variables with a graph: nodes are variables and edges are direct dependencies. The conditional independences encoded by the graph break a huge distribution into small pieces, making it possible both to model and to query it.

Introduction

A Short History

1906

Markov studies chains in which the next state depends only on the current one.

1966

Baum and Petrie publish the statistical basis of the hidden Markov model (HMM).

1977

Dempster, Laird and Rubin publish the EM algorithm for learning with missing data.

1988

Pearl's "Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems" brings Bayesian networks into AI.

1989

Rabiner's HMM tutorial becomes the standard reference in speech recognition.

2001

Lafferty, McCallum and Pereira propose conditional random fields (CRFs) for sequence labeling.

The Course

Core Concepts

Joint distribution

Probability of every combination of values of all variables.

Conditional independence

Once one variable is known, the others no longer affect each other.

Bayesian network

Directed acyclic graph; a conditional probability table at each node.

Markov network

Undirected graph; relations given by potential functions.

Hidden Markov model

An unobserved chain of states, each emitting an observation.

Exact inference

Variable elimination, junction tree.

Approximate inference

Sampling (MCMC), loopy belief propagation, variational.

Learning

Parameters (EM) and structure: finding the graph itself from data.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A diagnostic network has nodes for smoking, cancer and cough. A physician fills the tables from the literature. If a patient coughs and smokes, these two pieces of evidence are entered; the network recomputes the probability of cancer and shows which test would reduce uncertainty the most.

Try It

Why Is the Grass Wet?

The grass is wet in the morning: did it rain, or did the sprinkler run? Click a node in the four-variable Bayesian network to set evidence (yes / no / unknown); the other nodes are updated by exact probability calculation.

The 16 joint states of the four variables are not stored as a table; the network keeps only local conditional probabilities. When evidence arrives, enumeration computes the requested posterior from these small tables.

Practice

Risks and Ethics

Tables filled with guesses

Without data, probability tables are filled with expert guesses; a precise-looking 83 per cent hides how uncertain those guesses are.

False independence assumptions

Treating two dependent pieces of evidence as independent counts the same information twice and makes the model overconfident.

Probability errors in court

Mistaking the chance of the evidence for the chance of guilt (the prosecutor's fallacy) and ignoring base rates can convict the innocent.

Unconverged sampling

Stop an MCMC chain too early and the posterior is wrong; results cannot be trusted without reported convergence diagnostics.

In depth: Explainable AI and AI Ethics.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
An edge means cause and effect.In a Bayesian network an edge is only a probabilistic dependency; causality needs extra assumptions or experiments.
Unconnected variables are independent.Independence is conditional; two nodes can become dependent once a common child is observed.
Inference is always easy to compute.Exact inference on general graphs is NP-hard; large models need approximate methods.
Deep learning made this field obsolete.PGMs remain strong where data is scarce and explainability or uncertainty estimates matter; the two are also combined.
