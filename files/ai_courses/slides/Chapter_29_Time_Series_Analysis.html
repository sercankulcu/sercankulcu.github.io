Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How do we find structure in measurements over time and forecast the future?

Time series analysis studies measurements whose order matters: yesterday's value affects today's. The course teaches how to split a series into trend, seasonality and noise, how to model autocorrelation, and how to forecast the future together with its uncertainty.

Introduction

A Short History

1927

Yule explains sunspot numbers with an autoregressive (AR) model.

1960

The Kalman filter is published: it estimates a hidden state step by step from noisy measurements.

1970

Box and Jenkins's book establishes ARIMA models and how to build them.

1982

Engle proposes the ARCH model, in which volatility changes over time.

1997

Hochreiter and Schmidhuber introduce the LSTM; long dependencies become learnable.

2017

Facebook open-sources Prophet, which separates seasonality and holidays.

The Course

Core Concepts

Trend and seasonality

Long-run direction and regularly repeating cycles.

Stationarity

Mean and variance do not change over time.

Autocorrelation (ACF)

Correlation of a series with its own past.

Differencing

Removing the trend to make a series stationary.

ARIMA

Autoregression, differencing and moving average together.

Exponential smoothing

A forecast that weights the recent past more.

Prediction interval

A forecast with its uncertainty, not a single number.

Time-based split

The test set always comes after training; no shuffling.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A power company plots hourly consumption, separates the daily and weekly cycles, builds a model that also takes temperature as input, backtests it on past weeks, and gives the next day's load with a 95% prediction interval.

Try It

Planning Next Summer's Ice Cream

An ice cream maker plans the coming months' production from three years of monthly sales. Pick a method and a horizon; the last year's real sales are held out and the forecast is scored on them.

Naive and moving average forecasts cannot see the season: they draw a flat line through summer. Seasonal methods carry last year's shape forward; Holt-Winters also adds the growth trend.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Time series data can be tested with a random split.Future values leak into training; testing always follows time order.
Deep networks beat classic models on every series.In the M competitions, simple and statistical methods have often been very strong.
A good model gives one correct number.Forecasts are uncertain; for decisions the prediction interval matters at least as much as the point forecast.
A model that worked in the past always works.Structural breaks (a pandemic, a crisis) change the series; models must be monitored and refreshed.
