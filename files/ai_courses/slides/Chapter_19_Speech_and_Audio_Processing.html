Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How are words, speakers and events extracted from a sound wave?

Speech recognition and audio processing study how to analyze the sound wave from a microphone and extract words, the speaker's identity or events in the environment. Sound is first converted to time-frequency features; the acoustic model and the language model together find the most likely text.

Introduction

A Short History

1952

Bell Labs' Audrey system recognizes digits spoken by a single speaker.

1962

IBM Shoebox is demonstrated, recognizing sixteen spoken English words, including the ten digits.

1970s

In DARPA-funded projects, Carnegie Mellon's Harpy system recognizes about a thousand words.

1980s

Hidden Markov models (HMMs) become the standard method of speech recognition.

2012

Deep neural networks begin replacing Gaussian mixtures in acoustic modeling.

2022

OpenAI's Whisper is trained end to end on about 680,000 hours of multilingual data.

The Course

Core Concepts

Sampling

Measuring the sound wave thousands of times per second.

Spectrogram

A time-frequency image from the short-time Fourier transform.

MFCC

Features adapted to how the human ear perceives frequency.

Acoustic model

Predicts the sound unit (phoneme) from a stretch of audio.

Language model

Says which word sequence is more likely.

Decoding

Finding the most likely word sequence with beam search.

Speaker recognition

Verifying or identifying whose voice it is.

Word error rate

WER: the rate of insertion, deletion and substitution errors.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A voice assistant records the question "what's the weather tomorrow" at 16 kHz, computes a spectrogram from 25 ms frames, the acoustic model gives phoneme probabilities for each frame, and the language model turns them into the most likely sentence. The text is passed to NLP for meaning.

Try It

A Smart Home Recognizes Commands

A smart home assistant must recognize four voice commands: yes, no, stop, start. Pick a command, change the speaker, raise the noise; the system compares the sound with stored templates.

Sound is first turned into a spectrogram, a time–frequency picture. The same word can be said fast or slow; DTW matches them by stretching the time axis. As noise grows, confidence drops.

Practice

Risks and Ethics

Voice cloning

A voice imitated from a few seconds of recording is used in fraud and to defeat voice authentication.

Always-on listening

Devices waiting for a wake word can record by mistake, and a voice also carries health and emotion information.

Accent and dialect gaps

Error rates are clearly higher for some accents and dialects, so these speakers gain less from captions and assistants.

Hallucinated transcripts

End-to-end models can write sentences nobody said during silence or noise, which is dangerous in medical transcription.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Speech recognition maps sound directly to words.The same word sounds very different across accents, speeds and noise; the language model resolves the ambiguity.
The louder the recording, the better.Clipping and reverberation hurt recognition; a clean, balanced recording is what matters.
A model that works well in English works in Turkish too.Each language needs enough data; agglutinative Turkish also has a vocabulary problem to solve.
Audio processing is only about speech.Music, environmental sounds and medical sounds (heart, cough) are also part of the course.
