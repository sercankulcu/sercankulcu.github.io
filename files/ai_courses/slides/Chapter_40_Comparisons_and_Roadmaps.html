Pairs

Often-Confused Pairs (1/2)

PairDifference
Image Processing ↔ Computer VisionIn image processing both input and output are images; in computer vision the input is an image and the output is meaning: a label, a box, depth.
Artificial Intelligence ↔ Machine LearningAI can find how to reach a goal by search or inference; ML learns the same behavior by generalizing from data.
Machine Learning ↔ Data MiningThey share algorithms; ML aims to predict new examples, data mining to discover patterns in data.
Data Science ↔ Data MiningData mining focuses on finding patterns in large data; data science covers the whole path from question to decision.
Neural Networks ↔ Deep LearningDeep learning moves on to convolutional, recurrent and transformer architectures; this course builds the basic network and training they rest on.
Pattern Recognition ↔ Machine LearningML studies learning algorithms in general; this course focuses on the design from measurement to class decision and on the Bayes error.
NLP ↔ Speech and AudioSpeech recognition goes from the sound wave to text; NLP analyzes meaning once the text is available.

Often-Confused Pairs (2/2)

PairDifference
Generative Models ↔ Deep LearningDeep learning is the general toolkit and mostly makes predictions; this course uses those tools to generate new samples from a data distribution.
Computer Graphics ↔ Computer VisionComputer vision tries to recover the scene from an image; computer graphics produces the image from the scene.
Machine Learning ↔ Probability & StatisticsStatistics emphasizes a model's meaning and uncertainty, ML its predictive performance.
IR & Recommenders ↔ Data MiningData mining discovers patterns in data; this course turns discovery into a ranked list for one user's current need.
Expert Systems ↔ Fuzzy LogicFuzzy logic lets rules be partly true; this course teaches representing knowledge and chaining rules in general.
Evolutionary Optimization ↔ OptimizationOptimization reaches a provable optimum using derivatives and convexity; this course teaches derivative-free search with no guarantee but wide applicability.
MLOps ↔ Big DataBig data systems store and process data at scale; this course manages the lifecycle of models trained on that data.

Which course?

Which Course? (1/2)

For each problem, decide which course it belongs to, then open the answer.

  1. Automatically outlining tumours in a hospital's MRI scans.
    AnswerMedical Imaging, Computer Vision, Image Processing. Segmentation comes from image processing and computer vision; clinical data, scarce labels and accountability make it medical image processing.
  2. Building a "customers who bought this also bought" list for an online shop.
    AnswerIR & Recommenders, Data Mining. Association rules come from data mining; presenting a ranked list to the user is the job of recommender systems.
  3. Controlling an oven's temperature with rules like "a bit hot" and "very hot".
    AnswerFuzzy Logic. Linguistic rules without sharp boundaries, used for control, are the classic application of fuzzy logic.
  4. A drone learning to stay level in the wind by trying.
    AnswerReinforcement Learning, Robotics. No labels, only a reward: reinforcement learning. The physical body and its sensors are the business of robotics.
  5. Storing and querying per-second readings from a hundred thousand sensors.
    AnswerBig Data, Time Series. Streaming data too large for one machine belongs to big data systems; analysing it over time is time series work.

Which course?

Which Course? (2/2)

For each problem, decide which course it belongs to, then open the answer.

  1. Finding the shortest total route plan for school buses.
    AnswerEvolutionary Optimization, Optimization, Artificial Intelligence. A combinatorial optimization problem: exact methods come from optimization, good solutions to large instances from metaheuristics.
  2. Auditing whether a credit model treats some groups of applicants unfairly.
    AnswerExplainable AI & Ethics, Data Science. Fairness measures, explanations and accountability are the subject of explainable AI and ethics.
  3. Voice commands that work on a phone without an internet connection.
    AnswerEmbedded AI, Speech and Audio. The recognition model comes from speech and audio processing; fitting it onto the device is the job of embedded AI.
  4. Finding the genes linked to a disease from expression data of thousands of samples.
    AnswerBioinformatics, Probability & Statistics. Biological data and its interpretation are bioinformatics; significance across many tests needs statistics.
  5. Catching unusual connections in a bank's network traffic.
    AnswerAI in Cybersecurity, Data Mining. Outlier detection comes from data mining; an attacker who adapts makes it a cybersecurity problem.

Final Thoughts

A common language

Linear algebra, probability and optimization are shared by almost every course; with these in place, moving between courses is easy.

The hub

Machine learning sits at the centre of the map: the branches rest on it and the applications use it.

The question decides

Two courses may use the same algorithm; what separates them is the question they ask and how they measure success.

The field moves

Separate courses today may merge tomorrow; understanding the kinds of relation lasts longer than memorizing the map.
