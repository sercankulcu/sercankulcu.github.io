Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How do we ask the right questions of data and turn the answers into decisions?

Data science is the process that starts with a question and covers finding, cleaning and analyzing data, then communicating the result so that it leads to a decision. It combines statistics, programming and domain knowledge; data mining and machine learning are its tools.

Introduction

A Short History

1962

In "The Future of Data Analysis", Tukey argues for data analysis as a science separate from statistics.

1977

Tukey publishes "Exploratory Data Analysis": look first, then model.

2001

Cleveland proposes "data science" as an academic field that extends statistics.

2010

Kaggle is founded; data science competitions and open datasets spread.

2012

Harvard Business Review calls data scientist "the sexiest job of the 21st century".

2014

Project Jupyter splits from IPython; code, plots and text meet in one notebook.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A city council asks, "Should we add more bus trips?" Card tap records are collected, bad timestamps removed, occupancy by line and hour explored with plots, demand estimated with regression, and the result presented to the decision maker in a one-page report.

Try It

What Drives Grades?

The school asks: “Which habit should we advise students on?” We have survey and grade data for 26 students. Pick variables to see the relationship; click a point to drop it from the analysis.

Data science starts with a question and ends in a decision: exploratory analysis, cleaning, measuring relationships and communicating the result. Correlation is not causation: students who study more also attend more; separate the two before giving advice.

Risks and Ethics

Confusing correlation with causation

Reading causes into correlations leads to wrong decisions: ice cream sales and drownings rise together because both happen in summer.

p-hacking

Run enough tests and something will come out "significant"; the hypothesis must be fixed before looking at the data.

Re-identification

In data with names removed, ZIP code, birth date and sex are enough to re-identify most people.

Misleading charts

A truncated axis or a cherry-picked time window can tell the opposite story with the same data.

In depth: Explainable AI and AI Ethics.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Data science is training models.Most of the time goes into sharpening the question, finding and cleaning data, and communicating results.
Data speaks for itself.Without a question, context and domain knowledge, the same data can lead to wrong conclusions.
A significant p-value is an important result.Statistical significance says nothing about effect size; many tests produce misleading results.
A chart is decoration for the result.Anscombe's quartet shows it: data with identical summaries look completely different when plotted.
