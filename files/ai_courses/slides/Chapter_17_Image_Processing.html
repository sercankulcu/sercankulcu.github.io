Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How is an image enhanced, transformed and divided into regions?

Image processing studies methods that take an image and turn it into a new image or into regions: reducing noise, boosting contrast, finding edges and object boundaries. Both input and output are images; understanding what is in the image is the job of computer vision.

Introduction

A Short History

1920s

The Bartlane system sends digitally coded newspaper photos across the Atlantic by cable.

1957

Russell Kirsch makes the first digitally scanned photo: a 176×176-pixel image of his infant son.

1964

JPL corrects Ranger 7's lunar images by computer; digital image processing is born.

1979

Otsu publishes his method for picking a threshold from the histogram; still the most widely used thresholding method.

1986

The Canny edge detector appears: noise-robust, thin and continuous edges.

1992

The JPEG standard is adopted; DCT-based lossy compression becomes the common language of photos.

The Course

Core Concepts

Pixels and channels

An image is a matrix of numbers; color has three channels.

Histogram

Brightness distribution; contrast via histogram equalization.

Spatial filter

Convolution with neighboring pixels: blurring, sharpening.

Frequency domain

2D Fourier transform; filtering periodic noise.

Edge detection

Brightness jumps via gradient, Sobel and Canny.

Thresholding

Separating foreground from background; Otsu's method.

Morphology

Erosion and dilation: cleaning up binary shapes.

Segmentation

Splitting an image into regions: region growing, watershed.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: An old scanned document is first cleaned of specks with a median filter, its contrast is boosted by histogram equalization, it is binarized with Otsu's threshold, and the letters are repaired with morphological operations. The output is a clean image; reading the letters is the job of computer vision.

Try It

Finding Edges in a Noisy Image

A quality control camera returned a 32×32 grayscale image of a part; the sensor is noisy. Toggle the steps and slide the threshold: how many of the detected edges lie on a real shape boundary?

The blur and Sobel steps are real 3×3 convolutions. Blur and lower the threshold a little: noise edges drop and F1 rises.
In image processing both input and output are images: filters transform pixels, they do not decide. The course teaches what each kernel does and how the order of steps changes the result.

Practice

Risks and Ethics

Image forgery

The same tools can remove or add objects in a photo, and an edited image can be passed off as evidence.

Corrupted evidence

Lossy JPEG and sharpening change detail in forensic or medical images; the original file must be kept.

Invented detail

Super-resolution and denoising can produce detail that was never in the scene, and it may be taken as real.

Hand-tuned thresholds

Thresholds and parameters picked by looking at one image fail silently when the lighting changes.

In depth: Explainable AI and AI Ethics.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Image processing and computer vision are the same thing.Image processing transforms the image; vision extracts meaning from it. One is a step before the other.
Sharpening brings back lost detail.Sharpening accentuates existing edges; detail that was never recorded does not come back.
Deep learning does everything now.Classical methods are still essential in calibration, compression, preprocessing and fast industrial inspection.
Enlarging an image increases its resolution.Interpolation creates new pixels but no new information; the image just gets bigger.
