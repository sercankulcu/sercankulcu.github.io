Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How do AI models run on small, low-power devices?

Embedded AI studies running trained models on devices with limited memory, compute and energy, such as phones, cameras or microcontrollers. The course covers shrinking a model by quantization, pruning and distillation, moving it onto the device, and measuring latency and energy; the extreme form is called TinyML.

Introduction

A Short History

2015

Hinton, Vinyals and Dean introduce knowledge distillation: a large model's knowledge passes to a small one.

2016

Han et al. combine pruning and quantization in Deep Compression, shrinking networks by tens of times.

2017

MobileNets is published and TensorFlow Lite announced: deep networks on phones become routine.

2018

Google announces the Edge TPU accelerator for edge devices.

2019

The first tinyML Summit is held; Warden and Situnayake publish the book TinyML.

2020

MCUNet runs ImageNet classification on a microcontroller with over 70% accuracy.

The Course

Core Concepts

Quantization

8-bit or smaller integers instead of 32-bit floats.

Pruning

Removing unimportant weights, channels or layers.

Knowledge distillation

A small student model mimics a large teacher.

Efficient architectures

MobileNet, depthwise separable convolution.

Inference engine

The thin software layer that runs the model on the device.

Accelerators

NPU, DSP, Edge TPU: hardware built for neural networks.

Memory budget

A few hundred KB of SRAM and flash for weights and activations.

Latency and energy

Two performance metrics as important as accuracy.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A small keyword-spotting network is trained on a computer with 32-bit floats, quantized to 8-bit integers and loaded onto a microcontroller with a few hundred kilobytes of memory. The device listens all the time, never sends audio to the cloud and uses very little power.

Risks and Ethics

The hidden cost of shrinking

Quantization and pruning barely move average accuracy, yet rare classes and minority groups can lose far more.

Devices that cannot be updated

A faulty or vulnerable model spread over thousands of devices in the field is hard to recall.

Unnoticed surveillance

Always-listening microphones and cameras can monitor bystanders unawares, even when data stays on the device.

Model theft

The model file on a device can be read and copied, and reverse engineering opens it to adversarial attacks.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Models are trained on the small device.Training is mostly done on a server; only inference runs on the device.
Quantization always ruins accuracy.8-bit quantization costs most models very little accuracy; quantization-aware training reduces the loss further.
Fewer parameters means a faster model.Speed depends on memory access, operation types and hardware support; parameter count is not enough.
Embedded AI is only needed without internet.For privacy, latency and energy, on-device processing is preferred even when a connection exists.
