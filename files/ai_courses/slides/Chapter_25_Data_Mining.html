Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How are hidden, useful patterns found in large piles of data?

Data mining studies how to discover patterns in large data collections that are previously unknown, valid and useful. It uses the same algorithms as machine learning, but the goal is not prediction on new examples; it is finding rules, clusters and anomalies in the data itself.

Introduction

A Short History

1967

MacQueen publishes the clustering method he names "k-means".

1989

The first workshop on knowledge discovery in databases (KDD) is held; the field gets its name.

1994

Agrawal and Srikant popularize association rule mining with the Apriori algorithm.

1996

Fayyad and colleagues define the KDD process; density-based DBSCAN is published the same year.

2000

CRISP-DM 1.0 is released: an industry-neutral process model for data mining projects.

2006

The Netflix Prize opens: one million dollars for improving recommendation accuracy by 10 percent.

Sequential pattern

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A supermarket selects a year of receipts, removes returns and bad records, turns each receipt into an itemset, finds rules such as "customers who buy pasta also buy sauce" with Apriori, and presents the high-lift rules to the manager for shelf layout.

Try It

Hidden Rules in the Shopping Basket

A corner shop looks at 14 receipts: nobody asked a question; let the data tell which items are bought together. Change the thresholds and Apriori finds the rules again; click a rule to see it in the baskets.

Data mining is discovery: frequent patterns are sought without asking a question first. A rule with lift near 1 is empty (Bread is in everyone's basket anyway); a useful rule has lift clearly above 1.

Practice

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Data mining is collecting data.The name misleads: what is mined is knowledge, not data; the data already exists.
Every rule found is useful.Thousands of rules come out; most are obvious or accidental. Interestingness measures and expert review are needed.
A strong association means causation.Occurring together does not mean one causes the other; there may be a common cause.
Clustering has one right answer.The result depends on the number of clusters, distance and scaling; different choices, different clusters.
