Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How does a model generate new samples that look like real data?

Generative models learn the probability distribution of data and generate new samples from it. A discriminative model asks "is this image a cat?", while a generative model asks "how are cat images distributed, and how do I draw a new one?"

Introduction

A Short History

2013

Kingma and Welling propose the variational autoencoder (VAE).

2014

Goodfellow and colleagues introduce generative adversarial networks (GANs), pitting a generator against a discriminator.

2015

Sohl-Dickstein and colleagues publish the idea of diffusion models that undo noise step by step.

2018

OpenAI's first GPT: an autoregressive transformer pretrained on a large text corpus.

2020

Ho, Jain and Abbeel's DDPM brings diffusion models to the front of image generation.

2022

Stable Diffusion is released with open weights; ChatGPT brings generative models to a wide audience.

The Course

Core Concepts

Likelihood

The probability the model assigns to real data; training increases it.

Latent space

Low-dimensional hidden variables that summarize samples.

Autoencoder and VAE

Encode, decode; a VAE forces the latent space into a regular distribution.

GAN

A generator makes fake samples, a discriminator tries to tell them from real ones.

Diffusion

Adds noise to data; a network learns to remove it step by step.

Autoregressive model

Predicts each token from the previous ones: GPT.

Conditional generation

Generation guided by text, a class or a sketch.

Evaluation

FID, perplexity and human evaluation.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A diffusion model adds noise step by step to millions of images and trains a network to predict that noise. At generation time it starts from pure noise, removes it over dozens of steps guided by a text prompt, and arrives at an image never seen before.

Try It

New Faces from Latent Space

A game studio wants new characters from 60 cartoon faces. The model learned to compress each face into two numbers (z₁, z₂); generate a new face with the sliders or by clicking the plane on the left.

The model did not memorize the faces; it learned the latent structure of the data. Every point on the plane gives a new but plausible face; VAEs and diffusion carry the same idea to millions of dimensions.

Practice

Risks and Ethics

Deepfakes

A person's face and voice are imitated without consent and used for fraud, blackmail and election disinformation.

Training data without consent

Web-scraped datasets such as LAION-5B contain works without their owners' permission; copyright lawsuits are ongoing.

Model collapse

Models retrained on generated data lose the tails of the distribution over generations, and their outputs grow uniform.

The metric illusion

Metrics such as FID do not always agree with human judgment; a model tuned to the metric can look worse to the eye.

In depth: Explainable AI and AI Ethics.

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
A generative model copies its training data.The goal is to learn the distribution; memorization can still happen and must be checked.
Generative AI is just chatbots.Models that generate images, audio, video, molecules and code are also part of this course.
Realistic-looking output is correct output.A model can produce plausible but wrong content; correctness is checked separately.
GANs are outdated; diffusion is always better.Each family trades off speed, quality and diversity differently; a GAN generates in one step.
