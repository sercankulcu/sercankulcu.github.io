Introduction

What Question Does It Ask?

How do networks of simple units learn by adjusting their weights?

Artificial neural networks are models built by connecting, in layers, simple units that take a weighted sum and pass it through an activation function. Learning means adjusting the weights so as to reduce a loss function; the main tools for this are backpropagation and gradient descent.

Introduction

A Short History

1943

McCulloch and Pitts propose the first artificial neuron model with threshold logic units.

1949

Hebb suggests that connections between neurons that fire together grow stronger: the Hebbian rule.

1957

Rosenblatt introduces the perceptron; its weights are tuned from examples.

1969

Minsky and Papert show that a single-layer perceptron cannot solve problems such as XOR.

1982

Hopfield publishes a recurrent network that recalls memories by minimizing energy.

1986

Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams popularize backpropagation; multilayer networks become trainable.

The Course

Core Concepts

Artificial neuron

Weighted sum of inputs, a bias and an output.

Activation

Sigmoid, tanh, ReLU: adds nonlinearity to the network.

Perceptron

Single-layer network that separates linearly separable classes.

Multilayer network

Hidden layers learn nonlinear boundaries.

Forward pass

Computing the prediction layer by layer from input to output.

Backpropagation

Finding the gradient of every weight with the chain rule.

Gradient descent

Moves the weights in small steps where the loss decreases.

Regularization

Weight penalties and early stopping prevent overfitting.

The Course

A Typical Workflow

Example: A small network that reads handwritten digits takes a 28×28-pixel image as 784 inputs, passes it through a hidden layer and outputs probabilities for 10 classes. A wrong guess means a large loss; backpropagation computes each weight's share and gradient descent corrects the weights a little.

Try It

Tangerine or Lemon?

A camera on the sorting belt measures the weight and color hue of each fruit. A single artificial neuron (a perceptron) learns to separate the two kinds: train it, or click a fruit to flip its label.

● tangerine   ● lemon. Relabel a fruit deep among the others: no line can separate them, and the error never reaches zero.
Nobody wrote the rule: after each mistake the neuron nudged its weights toward the error. A single neuron can only draw a linear boundary; data that cannot be separated that way needs a multilayer network.

Common Misconceptions

Common beliefIn fact
Neural networks imitate the brain.Biology is the inspiration, but the artificial neuron is a very crude model and its learning rule differs from the brain's.
More neurons are always better.A large network can memorize the training data; size is chosen with a validation set.
Backpropagation is a separate learning method.Backpropagation only computes the gradient; gradient descent updates the weights.
Training gives the same result every time.Random initialization and shuffling can lead to different local minima.
