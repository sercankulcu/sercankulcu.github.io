Algorithmic Game Theory

Course Map

This page puts every chapter of the course on one map. Each chapter is a node, and an arrow shows which idea from an earlier topic the next one relies on. Read it before the course starts to see where the topics are heading, or during the course to see where you are.

Figure 1 shows the connections between the topics. Each node is a chapter, and an arrow indicates which idea of the earlier topic the later one relies on.

Table 1: The connections on the map.
Topic (chapter)Builds onConnection
Preliminaries (0)—Σ and argmax, probability and expected value, reading a payoff matrix, the indifference equation; every chapter uses these tools.
Decision Theory (1)PreliminariesThe single-person decision: expected utility, risk attitudes, Bayesian decisions and the value of information.
Games and Representations (2)Decision TheoryWhen decision makers multiply, a game arises: normal and extensive form, backward induction.
Nash Equilibrium (3)Games and RepresentationsThe equilibria marked in 2×2 games lead to the general Nash definition and the existence theorem.
Mechanism Design (4)Nash EquilibriumEquilibrium + incentives: instead of solving a game, design the rules that produce the desired equilibrium.
Auctions (5)Mechanism DesignThe single-item case of VCG is the second-price (Vickrey) auction.
Online Platforms (6)AuctionsAd-slot auctions: GSP is the multi-slot generalization of Vickrey's idea.
Social Choice and Voting (7)Mechanism DesignSocial choice function → voting rules; Gibbard–Satterthwaite.
Network Design and Routing (8)Nash EquilibriumCongestion games: the Wardrop equilibrium in nonatomic routing (Beckmann potential), the pure Nash equilibrium in finite atomic games (Rosenthal potential).
Price of Anarchy (9)Network Design and RoutingEquilibrium quality in Pigou and Braess networks → a price of anarchy of 4/3; congestion tolls.
Multi-Agent Systems (10)Games and RepresentationsThe cooperative / noncooperative distinction → coalitional games, the Shapley value.
Algorithmic Fairness and Ethics (11)Social Choice and VotingFrom collective decisions to fairness: criteria and fair division.
Behavioral and Collective Decisions (12)Decision TheoryPeople deviate from expected utility (loss aversion); with common goods, individual and collective interest diverge.
Learning and Adaptation (13)Nash EquilibriumHow is an equilibrium found? Players can converge to equilibrium by learning.
Future Directions (14)Price of Anarchy, Learning and Adaptation, Algorithmic Fairness and EthicsScale, learning agents and ethics come together in today's open questions.

The backbone of the map can be read as equilibrium → design → application → learning → future. We move from the single-person decision to games, and from games to equilibrium. The equilibrium concept then opens in two directions: designing the rules (mechanisms, auctions, voting) and measuring the outcome of existing rules (networks, the price of anarchy). Learning asks how equilibrium is reached. The topics of the last chapter (Future Directions) sit where these branches meet. For example, algorithmic collusion rests on both repeated games and learning, and transaction fee mechanisms rest on both auctions and congestion pricing.
