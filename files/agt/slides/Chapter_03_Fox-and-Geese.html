Fox wins with 7 captures. Geese win by trapping the fox.

How to play

  1. The board is a cross of 33 points. 17 geese (◉) start at the top, the fox (🦊) near the bottom. Geese move first.
  2. Click one of your pieces, then a highlighted point. Green dashed points are steps; red points are captures.
  3. Geese move one step to an adjacent empty point: sideways, down, or diagonally down. They never move backwards (up) and cannot capture.
  4. The fox moves one step in any of the eight directions, or jumps over an adjacent goose onto the empty point directly behind it, capturing that goose (one jump per turn).
  5. The fox wins after capturing 7 geese (or if the geese have no legal move). The geese win when the fox has no legal move.

Computer levels

  • Easy: mostly random moves (the fox takes captures half of the time).
  • Medium: looks two plies ahead.
  • Hard: alpha-beta search four plies ahead, valuing captures, fox mobility and goose advance.

Playing the geese

Individual geese are weak, but together they form a wall. Keep the group compact, advance in a coordinated line and never leave a goose with an empty point behind it next to the fox. Reduce the fox's mobility gradually instead of chasing it.

Playing the fox

The fox relies on mobility and tactics. Look for escape corridors, avoid being pushed into a closed arm of the cross, and create positions where one jump opens another. A greedy capture is not always best if it walks into a trap.

Game-theory connection

This is an asymmetric, sequential game of perfect information: the players have different action sets, powers and winning conditions. It illustrates attacker–defender reasoning, mobility versus coordination, and game-tree search with an evaluation function when the tree is too large to solve by hand.
