Fox & Geese

Geese cooperate to trap the fox. The fox tries to capture seven geese.

How to Play

  1. The two sides have different pieces and different objectives.
  2. Geese move one step to an adjacent legal point and cannot capture.
  3. The fox moves one step in any allowed direction and may jump over an adjacent goose into an empty point to capture that goose.
  4. The fox wins after capturing the required number of geese.
  5. The geese win by surrounding the fox so that it has no legal move.

Playing the Geese

Individual geese are weak, but together they can form a wall. Keep the group compact, advance in a coordinated way and avoid isolated pieces that the fox can jump. The geese are usually strongest when they reduce the fox's mobility gradually instead of chasing it directly.

Playing the Fox

The fox relies on mobility and tactical captures. Look for escape corridors, avoid being pushed toward closed regions, and create situations where one jump opens another route. A greedy capture is not always best if it moves the fox into a trap.

Game-Theory Connection

This is an asymmetric game: the players have different action spaces, powers and winning conditions. It provides a clear example of attacker-defender reasoning, mobility versus coordination, local tactical advantage and the difficulty of comparing positions when the two sides value completely different resources.
