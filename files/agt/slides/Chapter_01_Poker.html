How to play

Texas Hold'em against 1–9 computer opponents. Everyone starts with 500 TL. You first choose the number of opponents, then play hand after hand until you run out of money or are the last player with chips.

A hand

  1. Each player gets two face-down cards. The two players after the dealer button post the forced bets that start the pot, the small blind and the big blind.
  2. Betting round: each player calls (matches the current bet), raises (puts in more) or folds (drops the hand). If nobody has bet, calling means check.
  3. The flop (three community cards), the turn (the fourth) and the river (the fifth) are dealt face up; a betting round follows each.
  4. At the showdown, the best five-card hand made from a player's two hole cards and the five community cards wins the pot.

Controls

  • The Call / Check and Fold buttons under the table, or the C and F keys.
  • Quick raise: click an amount (or All in!) to raise.
  • After a hand, Continue deals the next hand and Restart starts a new game; details lists what everyone put in and won.
  • Name sets the player name; Speed (1 slow – 5 fast) sets how quickly the computer players act.
  • Show move reasoning lists the computation behind every computer decision under the table (see the How the AI decides tab).
  • A card enlarges when you hover over it.

Why poker in a game theory course?

Poker is a game of incomplete information: players act on their hidden cards and must reason about the beliefs of the others. Bluffing and calling bluffs are mixed strategies; a predictable player can be exploited. Betting works as a signal, and the pot rules determine the value of every action.

The game engine is the open-source JS/CSS Poker (jsholdem + tansey/js_poker), version of 15 January 2024.

These opponents do not search: they apply a rule table

The AI in chess or tic-tac-toe searches a game tree (Minimax and Alpha-Beta Pruning). The poker opponents do not: they cannot see your cards, so there is no tree to build. Every decision takes four steps:

  1. Estimate strength. Preflop, the bot's two cards are looked up in a fixed table that gives a score from 0 to 100, HCONF. After the flop, the best hand the bot can make is classified: GREAT, GOOD, MAYBE, PLAY BOARD (the best hand is on the board) or none; it also checks for a flush draw (FLUSH_DRAW).
  2. Turn the situation into numbers. The amount to call, the current bet and the pot are turned into coarse levels using their size relative to the bot's stack (CALL_LEVEL, BET_LEVEL, POT_LEVEL); the bot's stack counts as LO, MID or HI compared with the table average.
  3. Walk down the rule table. The first rule whose condition holds fires. Some rules are fixed (return CALL, return FOLD); most give a probability table and a random number picks from it. "40:SMALL,45:CALL" means "40% small bet, 45% call"; if the percentages do not reach 100, the rest is fold.
  4. Turn the name into an amount. SMALL, MED, BIG and ALLIN become TL amounts for the current situation; the amount is then adjusted by the game's rules (below).

Starting-hand score (preflop)

The table is hand-written: AA:100, KK:96, AKs:94 (suited), AK:85 (offsuit), 77:56, 22:34 and so on. A hand that is not in the table (for example 7-2 offsuit) gets 0. The score looks at the two cards only; not at how many opponents are left, not at the position relative to the dealer, not at what the others did.

Classification after the flop

The bot looks for the highest hand it can make (straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair), in that order, and checks whether at least one of its own cards is part of it. Examples:

ClassWhen (examples)
GREATfour of a kind, full house or straight flush that uses its own cards; a flush made with both its cards; top pair (a pair of Q, K or A with no board card above the pair); two pair with two different own cards
GOODa pair of J or lower with no board card above it; one board card above the pair but the pair is above 7; a flush with a single low own card
MAYBEa weak pair (two board cards above it, or one above and the pair is 7 or lower); a flush with none of its own cards; three of a kind, two pair and a pair are downgraded to this class when the board shows three cards of one suit or four cards close to a straight
PLAY BOARDthe best hand is already on the board; the bot's cards add nothing
nonenone of the above; the decision uses HCONF and the highest card

Levels and bet sizes

The bet level is the first row that matches, by the ratio of an amount to the bot's stack or by the absolute amount: ratio ≤ 1.5% or amount ≤ 5 TL → 5; ≤ 2% or ≤ 10 → 10; ≤ 3% or ≤ 15 → 20; ≤ 6% or ≤ 30 → 30; ≤ 12% or ≤ 60 → 40; ≤ 21% or ≤ 100 → 50; ≤ 35% or ≤ 150 → 70; ≤ 41% or ≤ 200 → 80; larger → 100. The pot level is 100 if the pot is more than half the stack, 51 if more than a quarter, otherwise 1 (so POT_LEVEL > 75 in the rules means "pot above half the stack").

Bet sizes (BIG_BLIND is the big blind, CALL the amount to call): SMALL = CALL + 2·BIG_BLIND (+5 if the pot level is above 40); with more than 3 players at the table MED = CALL + 4·BIG_BLIND and BIG = CALL + 10·BIG_BLIND; with 3 or fewer players SMALL grows by 5 TL, MED = CALL + 10% of the stack and BIG = CALL + 20% of the stack (rounded up to a multiple of 5). ALLIN is the whole stack.

After a rule proposes an amount, the game applies: more than the stack → all in; less than the amount to call → fold; equal → call (a check if there is nothing to call); more than the call but the raise is below the minimum raise → just call.

Is it optimal? No, and why

  • No expected-value computation. The bot never computes "win probability × pot − loss probability × call amount". The decision depends on coarse levels and hand-written probabilities. The right yardstick is the expected value of Chapter 1: calling pays when your win probability exceeds the required win probability, call ÷ (pot + call).
  • Hand strength is coarse. The preflop score ignores the number of opponents: AA wins about 85% against one opponent and about 31% against nine, yet the bot says 100 for both. Hands missing from the table score 0, although even 7-2 wins about 35% against a random hand.
  • There is a mixed strategy, but no equilibrium. Random choice makes the bot unpredictable, but the probabilities are hand-tuned. They were not computed from the bluffing and calling frequencies that would leave an opponent indifferent, as in a Nash equilibrium (Nash Equilibrium).
  • Bluffing is an accident. Betting with nothing (for example 75% on the river) comes from a probability in the table, not from a strategic calculation.
  • It can be exploited. Facing an expensive bet, weak hands always fold, so a big raise often works against these bots. The bot also never learns how you play; it only sees the size of the bet.

The ideas poker shows here: hand-strength estimation, expected value, mixed strategies and bluffing, and bets as information.

Two examples with real numbers

Preflop. Six players, everyone has 500 TL, big blind 10 TL (small blind 5, pot 15). The bot holds A♠ K♠ and it is its turn; it needs 10 TL to call.

  • AKs is 94 in the table → HCONF = 94 (above 90).
  • The call amount of 10 TL is 2% of the stack → CALL_LEVEL = 10. The 15 TL pot is under a quarter of the stack → POT_LEVEL = 1. The stack is at the table average → MID.
  • Rule: HCONF > 90, pot not big, more than 3 players in the hand → "5:ALLIN,15:BIG,35:MED,35:SMALL,10:CALL".
  • Amounts: SMALL = 10 + 2·10 = 30, MED = 10 + 4·10 = 50, BIG = 10 + 10·10 = 110, ALLIN = 500.
  • Say the random number is 0.52: the running totals are 5%, 20%, 55% → 0.52 ≤ 0.55, so MED. The bot puts in 50 TL in total (a raise of 40 TL).

Flop. The bot holds Q♥ 9♣, the board is Q♠ 7♦ 2♣, the pot is 80 TL, nobody has bet, and the bot has 440 TL.

  • It has a pair of queens, one of its own cards is in the pair, and no board card is above Q → GOOD; because the pair is above J it is GREAT. The board has no three-suit or straight danger, so there is no downgrade.
  • Rule: VERDICT == "GREAT" and the flop → "5:ALLIN,5:BIG,25:MED,45:SMALL,20:CALL".
  • The amount to call is 0; MED = 0 + 4·10 = 40. A random number of 0.30 gives running totals 5%, 10%, 35% → MED: the bot bets 40 TL.

What the panel shows

With Show move reasoning ticked, a row is added to the panel each time a computer player acts: the bot's name and the street; the hand-strength estimate (HCONF preflop, then the class); the pot and the amount to call with the levels; the conditions of the rule that fired; the probability table and the random draw, if any; and the decision (fold, call, raise, all in) with its amount. The bot's cards stay hidden until the hand ends; then they are added to the row. While the panel is on the table is a little smaller; it returns to its normal size when you turn the panel off.

Runs once at the start of every decision; HCONF, the levels and the bet sizes are computed here.

  var avg_bankroll = total_bankrolls / number_of_players_in_game;

2. Coarse levels: internal_get_bet_level, internal_get_pot_level

Turns an amount into a step relative to the stack; the rules compare against these steps.

function internal_get_bet_level(b) {
  var size = b / P.bankroll;  // amount as a fraction of the stack
  if (size <= .015 || b <= 5) return 5;
  if (size <= .02 || b <= 10) return 10;
  if (size <= .03 || b <= 15) return 20;
  if (size <= .06 || b <= 30) return 30;
  if (size <= .12 || b <= 60) return 40;
  if (size <= .21 || b <= 100) return 50;
  if (size <= .35 || b <= 150) return 70;
  if (size <= .41 || b <= 200) return 80;
  return 100;
}

function internal_get_pot_level() {
  var p = get_pot_size();
  var b = players[current_bettor_index].bankroll;
  if (p > .5 * b) {
    return 100;               // pot is more than half the stack
  }
  if (p > .25 * b) {
    return 51;                // pot is more than a quarter of the stack
  }
  return 1;
}

3. Pick from a probability table: internal_what_do_x

Random choice with a running total: a mixed strategy.

function internal_what_do_x(q, r) {             // q: a table such as "40:SMALL,45:CALL"
  q += ",";
  if (!r) r = Math.random();                    // draw a number in [0, 1)
  var p = 0;
  while (1) {
    var a = q.indexOf(":");
    var b = q.indexOf(",", a);
    if (a < 0 || b < 0) {
      return FOLD;                              // table ended without reaching 100%: fold
    }
    var probability = (q.substring(0, a) - 0) / 100;
    var action = q.substring(a + 1, b);
    q = q.substring(b + 1);
    p += probability;                           // running total
    if (r <= p) {
      return internal_tokenize_string(action);  // turn a name such as "SMALL" into an amount
    }
  }
}

4. Starting-hand score: internal_lookup_hole_ranking

A lookup in a fixed table.

var hole_rankings = "AA:100,KK:96,QQ:95,JJ:93,AKs:94," + "TT:86,AQs:85,AJs:84,KQs:84,AK:85," + ...  // fixed lookup table

function internal_lookup_hole_ranking(h) {              // h: such as "AKs" (suited), "77" (pair), "AJ" (offsuit)
  var i = hole_rankings.indexOf(h + ":");
  if (i < 0) return 0;                                  // not in the table: score 0
  var j = hole_rankings.indexOf(",", i);
  var r = hole_rankings.substring(i + h.length + 1, j);
  return r - 0;
}

8. Turn the proposal into an action: bet_from_bot

The amount the rule proposed is adjusted to the game rules.

function bet_from_bot(x) {
  var b = 0;
  var n = current_bet_amount - players[x].subtotal_bet;  // needed to call
  if (!board[0]) b = bot_get_preflop_bet(); else b = bot_get_postflop_bet();
  if (b >= players[x].bankroll) {
    players[x].status = "";                             // more than the stack: all in
  } else if (b < n) {
    b = 0;
    players[x].status = "FOLD";                         // less than the call: fold
  } else if (b == n) {
    players[x].status = "CALL";                         // exactly the call (a check if n = 0)
  } else if (b > n) {
    if (b - n < current_min_raise) {
      b = n;                                            // raise below the minimum: just calls
      players[x].status = "CALL";
    } else {
      players[x].status = "";
    }
  }
  ...
}

The same idea in Python

In the pattern of the course's Python examples: levels, picking from a probability table, and expected value (the same idea as Chapter01/value.py).

import random

# (fraction limit, TL limit, level)
LEVELS = [(.015, 5, 5), (.02, 10, 10), (.03, 15, 20), (.06, 30, 30),
          (.12, 60, 40), (.21, 100, 50), (.35, 150, 70), (.41, 200, 80)]

def bet_level(b, stack):                                # internal_get_bet_level
    ratio = b / stack
    for ratio_limit, tl_limit, level in LEVELS:
        if ratio <= ratio_limit or b <= tl_limit:
            return level
    return 100

def pot_level(pot, stack):                              # internal_get_pot_level
    if pot > .5 * stack: return 100
    if pot > .25 * stack: return 51
    return 1

def pick(table, r=None):                                # internal_what_do_x
    # table: [(percent, action), ...]; if the percentages do not reach 100, the rest = "FOLD"
    r = random.random() if r is None else r
    total = 0
    for percent, action in table:
        total += percent / 100
        if r <= total:
            return action
    return "FOLD"

def preflop(hconf, pot_lv, players_in_hand):            # top bands only
    if hconf > 99:                                      # AA
        if pot_lv > 75:
            return pick([(60, "ALLIN"), (10, "BIG"), (20, "MED"), (5, "SMALL"), (5, "CALL")])
        if players_in_hand < 4:
            return pick([(2, "BIG"), (15, "MED"), (33, "SMALL"), (50, "CALL")])
        return pick([(2, "ALLIN"), (8, "BIG"), (40, "MED"), (40, "SMALL"), (10, "CALL")])
    if hconf > 90:                                      # KK, QQ, JJ, AKs
        if pot_lv > 50:
            return pick([(15, "ALLIN"), (35, "BIG"), (30, "MED"), (15, "SMALL"), (5, "CALL")])
        if players_in_hand > 3:
            return pick([(5, "ALLIN"), (15, "BIG"), (35, "MED"), (35, "SMALL"), (10, "CALL")])
        return pick([(2, "ALLIN"), (6, "BIG"), (15, "MED"), (55, "SMALL"), (22, "CALL")])
    ...                                                 # the remaining bands follow the same pattern

def required_probability(pot, call):                    # the criterion the bot never computes
    return call / (pot + call)

def expected_value(p, pot, call):                       # expected value of calling (Chapter 1)
    return p * pot - (1 - p) * call

print(pick([(5, "ALLIN"), (15, "BIG"), (35, "MED"), (35, "SMALL"), (10, "CALL")], r=0.52))  # MED
print(required_probability(80, 20))                     # 0.2
print(expected_value(0.3, 60, 20))                      # 4.0

Discussion questions

  • Is this bot's strategy optimal? Does it bluff?
    Answer

    It is not optimal. It does no computation of expected value or of the opponent's possible hands; hand strength, coarse levels and hand-tuned probabilities make the decision. You cannot really say it "bluffs", but it does bet with nothing in its hand (75% on the river): that is a probability in a table, not a plan. A bot that really bluffed would have to set its bluffing frequency so as to leave the opponent indifferent about calling.

  • Why does the bot not make the same decision every time in the same situation? What does random behaviour gain it?
    Answer

    A player who always acts the same way is predictable and gets exploited: the opponent learns rules such as "big bet = strong hand" and plays against them. Random choice is a mixed strategy (Chapter 3). A mixed strategy also has a yardstick: a player who bets 100 TL into a 100 TL pot on the river must bluff one third of the bets to keep the opponent indifferent. The expected value of calling is b·200 − (1 − b)·100 = 0, which gives b = 1/3. This bot's probabilities were not computed that way; they were chosen by hand.

  • The preflop hand strength looks at two cards only. Why is that a problem? What would be the right measure?
    Answer

    The strength of a hand depends on the number of opponents: AA wins about 85% against one opponent and about 31% against nine, yet the bot uses the same score (100) for both. The right measure is the win probability, given the bot's cards and the board, computed over the opponents' possible hands (for example by random deals). Then the expected value could be computed.

  • The pot is 60 TL and you need 20 TL to call. At what win probability does calling pay?
    Answer

    The expected value of calling is p·60 − (1 − p)·20. Setting it to zero gives p = 20 ÷ (60 + 20) = 25%. With a 30% win probability the expected value is 0.3·60 − 0.7·20 = +4 TL; with 20% it is 0.2·60 − 0.8·20 = −4 TL. This is the poker form of the expected-value calculation of Chapter 1: the odds a bet needs are the pot odds.
