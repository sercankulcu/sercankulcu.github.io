    How to play

    1. Four players form two partnerships: Team A = You (P1) + Partner (P3, opposite you) against Team B = P2 and P4.
    2. You are the Hakem: after seeing your first five cards you choose a suit as Hokm (trump) and press Declare Hokm. Then the rest of the cards are dealt and you lead the first trick.
    3. Play goes clockwise: You → P2 → Partner → P4. The first card of a trick sets the lead suit.
    4. If you hold a card of the lead suit you must follow suit (other cards are greyed out). Otherwise you may play any card, including a trump.
    5. The highest trump wins the trick; if no trump is played, the highest card of the lead suit wins. The winner leads the next trick.
    6. The first team to take seven of the thirteen tricks wins the hand. “Hands” counts hands won by Team A–Team B.

    Bot levels

    • Easy: random legal cards.
    • Medium: wins a trick with the cheapest card that beats the current best, plays low when its partner is already winning, otherwise discards its lowest card.
    • Hard: like Medium, but also remembers played cards, leads cards that are the highest still unplayed in their suit and avoids leading trumps early.

    Partnership play

    Your goal is not to win every trick personally. A card that loses to your partner can still be excellent if it preserves a valuable trump or gives your team control of the next lead. Watch which suits players fail to follow — it reveals information about their remaining cards.

    Strategy tips

    • Do not spend high trumps on tricks your partner is already likely to win.
    • When an opponent cannot follow a suit, expect them to trump that suit later.
    • Leading a suit repeatedly strips opponents of that suit and prepares later winners.
    • Coordinate indirectly through the cards you lead and the strengths you reveal.

    Game-theory connection

    Hokm combines partnership incentives, hidden information and sequential play. Players reason from partial observations, update beliefs about unseen cards and choose actions that benefit the team — a natural setting for signalling, cooperation and coordinated strategy under imperfect information.
