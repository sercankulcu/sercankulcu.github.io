How to play

  1. Click a cell to open it. Reveal every safe cell without opening a mine. The first click is always safe and opens an empty region.
  2. A number tells you exactly how many mines are among the eight neighbouring cells.
  3. Right-click (or switch on Flag mode on touch screens) to place or remove a flag where you believe a mine is.
  4. Opening a zero cell automatically opens the surrounding safe region.
  5. You win when every non-mine cell is open; opening a mine ends the game. Wrongly placed flags are shaded when the game ends.

Safe hint

The hint first looks for a cell that is logically forced to be safe by the visible numbers and opens it (outlined). If no forced move exists, it reveals a random safe cell and tells you that you would otherwise have had to guess.

How to deduce safe cells

Treat every number as a local constraint. If a “1” touches exactly one unopened cell, that cell is a mine. If a “2” already touches two known mines, all its other unopened neighbours are safe. Combining overlapping constraints solves larger boards without guessing.

Strategy tips

  • Flags are memory aids: a flag is only your hypothesis until the clues confirm it.
  • Work from the areas with the most information first.
  • Compare neighbouring numbers to infer which unopened cells differ between their constraint sets.

Reasoning connection

Minesweeper is a constraint-satisfaction and inference puzzle rather than a strategic game against an opponent. It demonstrates local reasoning, information propagation, uncertainty reduction and the difference between logically forced moves and guesses.
