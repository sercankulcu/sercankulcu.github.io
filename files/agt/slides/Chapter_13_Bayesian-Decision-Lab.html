    How to play

    Each case has a hidden state (for example Rain or Dry). You know the prior probability and receive a noisy signal. Bayes' rule turns the prior into a posterior. Choose one of two actions; the hidden state is then revealed and you receive the payoff from the table.

    • EU-optimal counts decisions that maximised expected utility given the information you had — not decisions that happened to be lucky.
    • Expected utility of an action = P(state 1)·payoff(state 1) + P(state 2)·payoff(state 2).
    • Bayes' rule: P(risk | risky signal) = a·p / (a·p + (1−a)(1−p)), where p is the prior and a the signal accuracy.

    How this teaches game theory

    Rational decisions depend on beliefs as well as payoffs. A decision maker should choose the action with the highest expected utility, not the action that would have been best after seeing the hidden state. A good decision can still produce a bad realised outcome.

    Scenarios

    • Weather-sensitive event: choose an outdoor or indoor plan after a weather forecast.
    • Cybersecurity investment: invest heavily or lightly in defence after a risk signal.
    • Product launch: launch big or small after imperfect market research.

    Modes

    • Human decision maker: interpret the signal and choose without help.
    • Human + recommendation: the Bayesian expected-utility recommendation (and, in Level 3, the value of information) is shown; you still decide.
    • Computer decision maker: the computer plays 50 cases automatically, always maximising expected utility.

    Levels

    • Level 1 — Clear signal: accuracy 90%, posteriors move strongly.
    • Level 2 — Noisy signal: accuracy 68%, so the prior matters more.
    • Level 3 — Costly information: accuracy 78%, but the signal is hidden until you pay 1 to observe it. Buying is worth it only when the value of information — expected payoff when deciding after the signal minus expected payoff when deciding on the prior alone — exceeds the cost.
