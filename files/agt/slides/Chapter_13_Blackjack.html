How to play

  1. Choose a bet (minimum 10) and press Deal.
  2. Finish closer to 21 than the dealer without exceeding 21.
  3. Number cards count face value, J/Q/K count 10, an Ace counts 11 or 1.
  4. Hit takes another card. Stand ends your decisions and the dealer plays.
  5. Double (first two cards only) doubles the stake, gives exactly one more card and then stands.
  6. The dealer follows a fixed policy: draw to 16, stand on 17 or more.

Outcomes

Over 21 is a bust and loses at once. If the dealer busts while you are at 21 or below, you win. Otherwise the higher total wins and equal totals push (bet returned). A natural blackjack (Ace + ten-value card) pays 3:2; two naturals push. The six-deck shoe is not used: one 52-card deck is reshuffled when fewer than 15 cards remain. If your bankroll falls below the minimum bet, press New bankroll.

Difficulty

  • Practice: strategy hint plus your probability of busting on the next card (computed from all unseen cards, including the dealer's hole card).
  • Standard: strategy hint only.
  • Strategy: no help — decide on your own.

Decision strategy

  • Compare your total with the dealer's visible card.
  • Low totals usually benefit from another card; high totals carry greater bust risk.
  • A weak dealer up-card (2–6) justifies standing on 13–16, because the dealer may bust.
  • The hint uses simplified basic-strategy intuition; it is a teaching aid, not gambling advice.

Decision-theory connection

Blackjack is sequential decision-making under uncertainty. You know your hand and one dealer card but not the hole card or future draws. Each action trades expected reward against risk, which makes the game useful for discussing conditional probability, expected utility, stopping decisions and why optimal actions depend on the state rather than on one fixed rule.
