Dutch auction (descending price)

The price starts high (200 TL) and falls continuously, as in the Dutch flower auctions. The first bidder to press “I'll take it!” wins the item and pays the current price. If nobody takes it by 0 TL, the item goes unsold.

Usage

  • Each card shows a bidder's private valuation. When the price falls to or below that valuation, the card turns orange.
  • To buy on behalf of a bidder, click that bidder's I'll take it! button. Buying above the valuation gives negative surplus.
  • Speed sets how fast the clock falls; New bidders draws new valuations.

What does it show?

Waiting is a gamble: a lower price means a larger surplus, but someone else may buy first. Since a bidder must choose the price at which to stop the clock without learning anything useful about the others, the Dutch auction is strategically equivalent to the first-price sealed-bid auction. Under standard assumptions both yield the same expected revenue as the English and Vickrey auctions (revenue equivalence).
