Dutch auction (descending price)

The price starts high ($200) and falls continuously, as in the Dutch flower auctions. The first bidder to click “I take it!” wins the item and pays the current price. If nobody claims before $0, the item stays unsold.

How to use

  • Each card shows a bidder's private valuation. A card turns orange once the price has dropped to or below that valuation.
  • Click a bidder's I take it! button to claim for that bidder. Claiming above the valuation gives a negative surplus.
  • Speed sets how fast the clock falls; New bidders draws new valuations.

What this shows

Waiting is a gamble: a lower price means a bigger surplus, but someone else may claim first. Because a bidder must choose a price at which to stop the clock without learning anything useful about the others, the Dutch auction is strategically equivalent to the first-price sealed-bid auction. Under the standard assumptions both give the same expected revenue as the English and Vickrey auctions (revenue equivalence).
