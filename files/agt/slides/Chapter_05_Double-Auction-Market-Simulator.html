Double auction market

A double auction is a two-sided market: buyers submit bids and sellers submit asks at the same time. Bids are sorted from high to low, asks from low to high, and pairs trade as long as the bid is at least the ask. All trades happen at one clearing price, the midpoint of the last matched bid and ask.

How to use

  • Run clearing round clears the current order book. Matched rows turn green and the metrics update.
  • Next market event lets the bots revise their bids and asks (and, on Level 3, a new buyer or seller arrives), then clears again.
  • New market draws new values and costs. Changing scenario, mode or level also starts a new market.
  • Your row is marked with *. Your utility is value − price for a buyer or price − cost for a seller if you trade, 0 otherwise.

Scenarios

  • Stock Market: buyers value an asset and sellers have reservation prices; bid–ask matching mimics a simplified exchange.
  • Energy Market: buyers represent demand and sellers represent generators with different marginal costs.
  • Online Marketplace: buyers and sellers submit offers for one standardized item through a platform.

Modes

  • Human Buyer vs Bots: you control buyer B1's bid; you gain by buying below your private value.
  • Human Seller vs Bots: you control seller S1's ask; you gain by selling above your private cost.
  • Human Controls Market: the slider sets B1's bid and S1's ask (110 − slider) together, so you can watch the clearing point move.
  • Computer Market: all orders are set by bots, which shade bids below values and asks above costs.

Levels

  • Level 1: 3 buyers × 3 sellers, easy to follow.
  • Level 2: 5 × 5, deeper competition and a more stable price.
  • Level 3: a new buyer or seller arrives with every market event (up to 8 per side), so conditions change over time.

Key idea

Trade is efficient when a buyer's value exceeds a seller's cost. Strategic bids (below value) and asks (above cost) decide who trades and how the gains from trade are divided, and can block efficient trades. Welfare = buyer surplus + seller surplus; it turns negative when someone bids above their value or asks below their cost and trades.
