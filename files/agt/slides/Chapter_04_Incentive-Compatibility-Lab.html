How to play

You hold a private true value for the item. Choose a reported value and press Submit report. The mechanism decides the allocation and the payment from your report only; your utility is true value − payment if you are allocated and 0 otherwise. The gain vs truth compares that utility with what the truthful report would have given.

A mechanism is incentive compatible when agents never gain by misreporting private information. It is dominant-strategy incentive compatible when truthful reporting is never worse than any alternative report, whatever the others do.

The chart

The curve shows your utility for every possible report (0–120), the dashed line marks the critical threshold, the green dot the truthful report and the red dot the report you submitted. If any part of the curve lies above the green dot, a profitable manipulation exists.

Scenarios

  • Public Project: your value measures how much a shared project is worth to you; the mechanism decides whether it is built.
  • Service Priority: your report affects access to a scarce high-priority service and the payment charged.
  • Resource Allocation: your report competes for one indivisible resource.

The scenarios share the same rule; only the story changes.

Modes

  • Human reports value: you choose the report and test whether exaggeration or understatement helps. The utility curve is hidden; you only see the outcomes you try.
  • Human + truthfulness advisor: the full utility curve is shown and the advisor reports, while you move the slider, whether your report beats the truth.
  • Computer strategy search: the computer scans every report from 0 to 120 and submits the one with the highest utility (ties go to the truthful report).

Levels

  • Level 1 — Simple threshold: you are allocated when the report is at least 50 and pay a fixed 20. Manipulation is easy to see: with a true value between 20 and 50, over-reporting pays.
  • Level 2 — Strategic payment: the threshold is 60 and the winner pays exactly the critical value 60, a posted price. Truth-telling is a dominant strategy.
  • Level 3 — Competing agent: a rival's hidden value is the threshold and the winner pays it, as in a second-price auction. Truthfulness is again dominant.

Key distinction: truthfulness concerns the utility from reporting honestly, not whether the mechanism always produces the outcome you personally prefer.
