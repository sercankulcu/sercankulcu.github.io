How to play

You have a private true value for the item. Choose a reported value and press Submit report. The mechanism decides the allocation and payment from your report alone; if you are allocated the item your utility is true value − payment, otherwise it is 0. Gain vs. truth compares this utility with what a truthful report would have given.

A mechanism is incentive compatible if agents can never gain by misreporting their private information. It is dominant-strategy incentive compatible (DSIC) if, whatever the others do, a truthful report is never worse than any alternative report.

Chart

The curve shows your utility for every possible report (0–120); the dashed line marks the critical threshold, the green dot the truthful report and the red dot the report you submitted. If any part of the curve lies above the green dot, a profitable manipulation exists.

Scenarios

  • Public project: your value measures how much a shared project is worth to you; the mechanism decides whether the project is built.
  • Service priority: your report affects access to a scarce, high-priority service and the payment charged.
  • Resource allocation: your report competes for a single indivisible resource.

The scenarios share the same rule; only the story changes.

Modes

  • Human reports: you choose the report and test whether overbidding or underbidding pays off. The utility curve is hidden; you only see the outcomes you try.
  • Human + advisor: the whole utility curve is visible, and as you move the slider the advisor tells you whether your report beats the truthful one.
  • Computer searches: the computer scans every report from 0 to 120 and submits the one with the highest utility (ties go to the truthful report).

Levels

  • Level 1 — Simple threshold: if your report is at least 50 you are allocated the item and pay a flat 20. Manipulation is easy to spot: if your true value is between 20 and 50, overreporting pays.
  • Level 2 — Strategic payment: the threshold is 60 and the winner pays exactly the critical value, 60 (a posted price). Truthfulness is a dominant strategy.
  • Level 3 — Rival agent: a rival's hidden value is the threshold and the winner pays it, just as in a second-price auction. Truthfulness is dominant again.

Key distinction: truthfulness is about the utility from a truthful report, not about whether the mechanism always produces the outcome you would prefer.
