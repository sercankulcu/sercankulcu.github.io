How to play

  1. Black moves first. In computer mode you play Black.
  2. Opening. In the original Reversi (Free centre) the board starts empty and the first four discs are placed, one per turn, on any of the four centre squares without flipping. With Fixed cross the game starts from the Othello position with four discs already placed.
  3. After that, place a disc on an empty square only if it traps one or more opponent discs in a straight horizontal, vertical or diagonal line between the new disc and one of your existing discs. Every trapped disc flips to your color.
  4. Yellow dots mark the legal moves; the red frame marks the last move. If you have no legal move, your turn is skipped automatically.
  5. The game ends when neither player can move. The player with more discs wins.

Computer levels

  • Easy plays a random legal move.
  • Medium uses a square-weight table (corners good, squares next to corners bad) plus the number of flipped discs.
  • Hard searches four plies with Minimax and alpha-beta pruning using the same weights plus mobility.

Why disc count can mislead

Having more discs early is not necessarily an advantage. Many discs can create a large frontier that gives the opponent extra legal moves. Corners, by contrast, can never be flipped and often provide access to stable edge structures. Position and mobility (the Moves counter) are therefore more important than immediate material count.

Strategy tips

  • Prioritize corners whenever they are safely available.
  • Avoid giving the opponent easy access to squares next to an empty corner.
  • Try to maximize your future legal moves while reducing the opponent's mobility.
  • Near the endgame, exact disc count becomes more important because fewer future reversals remain.

Game-theory connection

Reversi is a perfect-information, deterministic, zero-sum game with strong positional effects. It demonstrates adversarial search, delayed reward, mobility, stable states and why a locally profitable move can be strategically harmful several turns later.
