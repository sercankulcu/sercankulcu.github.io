How to play

Each voter ranks the candidates. The lab compares every pair of candidates head to head: X beats Y when a majority of voters rank X above Y. A Condorcet winner beats every rival. Collective majority preferences can be cyclic (A beats B, B beats C, C beats A) even when every voter has a perfectly consistent ranking: this is the Condorcet paradox, a central difficulty in social choice.

Screen

  • Preference profile: one column per voter, ranks from top to bottom. Your ballot is voter 1 (highlighted). With four candidates your ballot lists D last.
  • Pairwise table: cell (row X, column Y) shows the votes for X against Y; green means X wins. In Human vs computer electorate mode an amber frame marks a contest your ballot decides (pivotal).
  • Majority graph: an arrow X → Y means a majority prefers X to Y. A Condorcet winner has only outgoing arrows (red outline); arrows on a cycle are drawn in red.

Scenarios

  • Condorcet cycle: Level 1 uses the classic profile A>B>C, B>C>A, C>A>B; larger levels draw random profiles until a cycle appears.
  • Condorcet winner: profiles in which one candidate beats every rival.
  • Agenda manipulation: with sequential pairwise voting (the winner of one contest meets the next candidate), the order of the agenda can decide the winner when preferences cycle. The lab lists the winner of every possible agenda.

Modes

  • Human sets ballot: your ranking is voter 1, so you can test whether one ballot changes pairwise outcomes.
  • Human vs computer electorate: as above, and the lab marks the contests in which your ballot is pivotal.
  • Computer simulation: all ballots are generated; Randomize electorate draws a new profile.

Levels

  • Level 1: three voters and three candidates expose the classic cycle cleanly.
  • Level 2: seven voters and a fourth candidate create richer pairwise structures.
  • Level 3: nine voters with random profiles show that paradoxes are structural possibilities, not one hand-crafted example.

Important: a Condorcet winner is defined by pairwise victories. It need not be the candidate chosen by plurality, Borda count or approval voting.
