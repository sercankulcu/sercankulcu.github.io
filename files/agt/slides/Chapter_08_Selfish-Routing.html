    How to play

    A fixed number of drivers travel from the same origin to the same destination over parallel routes. Click a route to send the next driver there. The small text under each route shows its cost function and the travel time the next driver would get. When everyone is assigned, the Price of Anarchy is shown.

    Routes

    • Route A is a congestible road: time = 2 + 1.5 × (drivers on A).
    • Route B is a bypass with fixed time 8 (Level 1) or 10 (Levels 2–3).
    • Route C (Level 3 only) is a fast but very congestible extra road: time = 1 + 2.2 × (drivers on C).

    Levels: 8 drivers on two routes; 14 drivers on two routes; 12 drivers on three routes.

    Scenarios

    • Selfish routing: the interactive experiment. Computer drivers pick the route with the lowest travel time for themselves (counting their own congestion).
    • Social optimum: a central planner fills the network with the allocation of smallest total travel time; route buttons are disabled.
    • Price of Anarchy: the selfish allocation is generated automatically and compared with the optimum.

    Modes

    • Human vs Computer drivers: after each of your choices the computer sends one selfish driver.
    • Human controls all drivers: you assign everyone — try to reach the optimum.
    • Computer simulation: the network is filled by selfish best responses on every reset.

    What this shows

    Selfish routing models individual best responses: each driver minimises their own travel time, ignoring the extra delay they cause others. The social optimum may send some drivers to a route that is not individually cheapest. The Price of Anarchy is the ratio of the selfish outcome's total cost to the minimum possible total cost; values above 1 quantify the efficiency lost through decentralised choices. In Level 3, the extra route attracts selfish traffic without necessarily improving total welfare, the intuition behind Braess-type effects.

    The cost functions are simplified teaching values, not a real road network.
