How to play

  • You play White; the computer plays Black. Click (or focus and press Enter on) one of your pieces, then a marked square. Dots are quiet moves, rings are captures.
  • All standard rules apply (castling, en passant, check, checkmate, stalemate, draws by repetition, the 50-move rule and insufficient material). Pawns always promote to a queen.
  • Undo takes back your last move and the AI's reply. New game starts over.
  • The last move is highlighted in yellow; a king in check is marked red.

How the AI thinks

The computer uses minimax search with alpha-beta pruning. It looks ahead the chosen number of plies (half-moves), assumes that you always pick the reply that is worst for it, and chooses the move with the best guaranteed outcome. Alpha-beta pruning skips branches that cannot change the decision, which makes the search much faster without changing the result.

At the end of the search, positions are scored by an evaluation function: material (pawn 100, knight 320, bishop 330, rook 500, queen 900) plus piece-square tables that reward good placement (central knights, advanced pawns, a sheltered king). Checkmate is scored as a win or loss and stalemate as a draw.

Game-theory connection

Chess is a finite, two-player, zero-sum game of perfect information. By Zermelo's theorem it has a determined value (win for White, win for Black or draw) found by backward induction, but the game tree is far too large to solve, so programs search a limited depth and approximate the value with heuristics.
