    How to play

    You run a platform that serves two groups of users (side A and side B). Set a fee for each side with the sliders and press Run market. A negative fee is a subsidy: the platform pays users on that side to join. The indicators show how many users from each side join, the platform profit (fee A × users A + fee B × users B) and total activity.

    The profit map shows the profit of every fee pair: the horizontal axis is fee A (−5 … 10), the vertical axis is fee B (−5 at the bottom, 10 at the top). Darker cells mean higher profit, red cells mean a loss. The framed cell (□) marks the current pair, the dot (●) marks the most profitable pair.

    Model

    Participation on each side depends on that side's own fee and on the number of users on the other side (cross-side network effects):

    users A = base A − sA·fee A + eA·users B
    users B = base B − sB·fee B + eB·users A

    Both equations hold at the same time (participation equilibrium); each side is capped at 0…100 users. Side A (riders, buyers, viewers) is very price sensitive (sA = 11–13 users per unit of fee), side B (drivers, sellers, creators) is less sensitive (sB = 5) but cares a lot about how many users there are on side A. The level sets (eA, eB) to (0.10, 0.30), (0.15, 0.60) or (0.20, 0.85).

    Why game theory?

    Two-sided platforms often subsidize one side of the market to attract the other. The best price on each side depends not only on direct willingness to pay but also on the cross-side network effects.

    Scenarios

    • Ride sharing: side A is riders, side B is drivers. More drivers attract riders; more riders attract drivers.
    • Marketplace: buyers value seller variety, sellers value buyer demand.
    • Content platform: viewers attract creators, and creator variety attracts viewers.

    Modes

    • You are the platform: you choose both fees and watch participation and profit (no suggestions).
    • You + suggestion: you stay in control, but you can ask the computer to search the fee grid for the most profitable pair.
    • Computer: the computer searches the whole grid and automatically picks the best pair it finds.

    Levels

    The levels set the cross-side network effects. Strong effects make asymmetric pricing and subsidies more valuable.

    Experiment

    Raise the fee on one side until participation collapses, then lower the fee on the other side. Watch whether subsidizing one side can raise the total profit.
