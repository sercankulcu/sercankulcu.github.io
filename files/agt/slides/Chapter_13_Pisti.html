Pişti

← Bölüm 13
You 0 Computer 0 Deck 52

    How to play

    1. Four cards start on the table (only the top one is face up); each player gets four cards. Players take turns playing one card onto the pile.
    2. If your card has the same rank as the top card, you capture the whole pile.
    3. A Jack captures the pile whatever the top card is.
    4. If the pile is a single card and you capture it with the same rank, you make a Pişti: 10 points (a Jack on a single Jack is worth 20).
    5. When both hands are empty, four new cards are dealt to each player until the deck runs out. The last player to capture takes the cards left on the table.
    6. Scoring: each Ace and each Jack 1 point, 2♣ 2 points, 10♦ 3 points, and 3 points for capturing the most cards. The score chips show Pişti and card points collected so far; the most-cards bonus is added at the end.

    Modes

    • Human vs Computer: you play the bottom hand; the computer's hand stays hidden.
    • Human vs Human: two people share the screen. After each move the next hand is hidden until its owner presses “show my hand”.

    AI styles

    • Random: plays any card.
    • Opportunistic: always captures when it can (Pişti first, Jacks included); otherwise discards its least valuable card.
    • Conservative: captures with matching ranks, saves Jacks for valuable piles, and discards ranks that have already been seen often, so you are less likely to hold a match.

    What to watch

    The important information is not only your hand but also the visible history of cards. A card that seems safe now may give the opponent an easy capture next turn. Jacks collect any exposed pile, so using one too early can sacrifice future value.

    Strategy tips

    • Capture when the pile is valuable, but do not waste a Jack on a small pile.
    • Remember ranks that have already appeared to estimate whether a matching card can still come.
    • When you cannot capture, discard the card that gives the opponent the least attractive opportunity.

    Game-theory connection

    Pişti mixes strategic interaction with incomplete information. Players see the public pile and their own cards but not the opponent's hand or future cards — illustrating hidden information, belief formation, tactical sacrifice and the value of remembering revealed actions.
