Guess 2/3 of the Average

    –Average

    How to play

    1. Each player secretly picks a number between 0 and 90 (whole or decimal).
    2. Guesses are entered one after another; each one stands for the next player.
    3. The Find winner button computes the target: two thirds of the average of all guesses.
    4. The guess closest to the target wins. Guesses at equal distance share the win.
    5. The +5 random button adds uniformly distributed random guesses; this is how a completely naive crowd would behave.

    Iterated elimination

    Since no average can exceed 90, the target can never exceed 60; so a guess above 60 is never better than 60 (it is weakly dominated). If everyone knows this, nobody names a number above 60 and the target cannot exceed 40; then 26.7, and so on. As the reasoning is repeated, every rational guess falls to 0: this is the unique Nash equilibrium. (If only whole numbers were allowed, "everyone says 1" would also be an equilibrium with four or more players; allowing decimals removes it.)

    Levels of reasoning

    Real groups rarely reach 0. A level-0 player guesses at random (average about 45), a level-1 player best-responds to that with 30, a level-2 player says 20, and so on. In classroom experiments the winning guesses are usually between 15 and 30: winning requires predicting how far others will reason, rather than being fully rational.

    Reading the display

    The number line shows each guess as a dot, the average (orange) and the target (red). The winning guess is highlighted in green.
