    How to play

    Compete for three ranked ad slots using bids, quality scores, click-through rates (CTR) and auction pricing rules. Set your bid per click with the slider and press Run. Best response tries every bid from 1 to 12 and picks the one that maximizes your expected utility against the current rivals. New market draws new values, qualities and bot bids.

    Ads are ranked by rank score = bid × quality. Your utility is (value per click − price per click) × expected clicks; if you get no slot, your utility is 0.

    Why game theory?

    Sponsored search auctions are multi-slot allocation mechanisms. Advertisers care about position, expected clicks, conversion value and price. When the platform ranks advertisers, it has to create incentives that balance revenue and efficient allocation.

    Scenarios

    • Search: a steep CTR curve (12%, 7%, 3.5%): top positions get far more clicks. Quality and bid together determine the ranking.
    • Social media: engagement quality varies a lot (0.3–1.0), so a low bid with high quality can beat a high bid with poor quality.
    • Sponsored product: a flatter CTR curve (10%, 6%, 4%): the lower slots are valuable too, and relevance affects the ranking.

    Modes

    • You vs. bots: you choose a single bid against fixed bot advertisers.
    • Advisor: after each auction the advisor tells you whether another bid would raise your expected utility.
    • You run the market: your slider also moves the rival bids (each bot bids 0.8 less than the previous one), which makes strategic interactions easier to study.
    • Simulation: all bidders, you included, automatically shade their bids to 65–95% of their values.

    Levels

    • Level 1 — First price: winners pay their own bid per click. Bid shading matters immediately.
    • Level 2 — GSP price: ranking uses bid × quality; the payment per click is the next rival's rank score divided by your quality (never above your bid).
    • Level 3 — Strategic: GSP pricing; in addition, before every auction each bot moves its bid to its own best response to the current bids.

    Key idea

    When quality matters, the highest bid does not always win the best slot. Payment rules change bidding incentives: under GSP, bidding your true value is not always the best response, but bids far above your value are not as costly as in a first-price auction.
