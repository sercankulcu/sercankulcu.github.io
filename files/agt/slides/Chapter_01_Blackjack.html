How to play

  1. Choose a bet (minimum 10) and press Deal.
  2. The goal is to finish closer to 21 than the dealer without going over 21.
  3. Number cards count their face value, J/Q/K count 10, and an Ace counts 11 or 1.
  4. Hit takes one more card. Stand ends your decisions and the dealer plays.
  5. Double down (first two cards only) doubles the bet, deals exactly one more card and then stands.
  6. The dealer follows a fixed rule: draw up to 16, stand on 17 and above.

Outcomes

Going over 21 is a bust and loses immediately. If the dealer busts while you are at 21 or below, you win. Otherwise the higher total wins; equal totals are a push (the bet is returned). A natural blackjack (an Ace plus a ten-value card) pays 3:2; two naturals make a push. No six-deck shoe is used: when fewer than 15 cards remain, a single 52-card deck is reshuffled. If your bankroll falls below the minimum bet, press New bankroll.

Difficulty

  • Practice: a strategy hint and the probability of busting on the next card (computed from all unseen cards, including the dealer's hole card).
  • Standard: strategy hint only.
  • Strategy: no help; the decision is entirely yours.

Decision strategy

  • Compare your total with the dealer's up card.
  • With low totals, drawing another card is usually worthwhile; with high totals the bust risk is large.
  • If the dealer's up card is weak (2–6), standing on 13–16 makes sense because the dealer may bust.
  • The hint is based on a simplified basic-strategy heuristic; it is a teaching tool, not gambling advice.

Link to decision theory

Blackjack is sequential decision making under uncertainty. Your own hand and one dealer card are known, but the hole card and later draws are not. Every action weighs expected reward against risk, which makes the game useful for discussing conditional probability, expected utility, stopping decisions, and why the best action depends on the situation rather than on one fixed rule.
