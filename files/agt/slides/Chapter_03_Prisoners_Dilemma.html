    How to play

    Sam and John are arrested and questioned separately. Each chooses to stay silent or betray the other without knowing the other's choice.

    • Both silent: 1 year each. Both betray: 2 years each.
    • One betrays, the other stays silent: the betrayer goes free (0), the silent one gets 3 years.

    In hot-seat mode the first choice is hidden ("locked") until the second player has chosen, then both are revealed and the matching matrix cell is highlighted. Press Next round to play again; years add up over the rounds. You can also let the computer play John.

    Sam's thought process

    • If John stays silent: betraying gives Sam 0 years, staying silent gives 1 year. Betray is better.
    • If John betrays: betraying gives Sam 2 years, staying silent gives 3 years. Betray is better.

    Conclusion: betraying is a dominant strategy for Sam, and John reasons the same way. (Betray, Betray) is the unique Nash equilibrium, even though both would be better off with (Silent, Silent). Individual rationality leads to a collectively worse outcome.

    Computer strategies for John

    • Tit-for-Tat: stays silent in round 1, then copies Sam's previous choice.
    • Always Betray / Always Silent: fixed choice every round.
    • Random: each choice with probability 1/2.

    In the repeated game, cooperation can be sustained by strategies such as Tit-for-Tat that reward cooperation and punish betrayal.
