How to play

Customers (blue dots) are scattered on a map. Choose how many facilities (red dots) to open and an algorithm for placing them, then press Place facilities. Every customer uses its nearest facility (thin lines). Two objectives are reported:

  • Average distance to the nearest facility, the k-median style objective (total travel cost).
  • Maximum distance, the k-center objective (the worst-served customer); the dashed amber line marks that trip.

Place facilities keeps the current customers so the algorithms can be compared fairly; New customers draws a fresh map (it also happens when you change the number of customers). The table runs all three algorithms on the same customers and marks the best value in green. Distances are in map units (the map is 800 × 400).

Algorithms

  • Random placement: facilities are dropped uniformly at random, a baseline.
  • K-means clustering: start from random customers, then alternate between assigning customers to the nearest centre and moving each centre to the mean of its customers (Lloyd's algorithm, 20 rounds). It targets the average squared distance, so it is usually good on the average.
  • Greedy farthest-point: open the first facility at a random customer, then repeatedly open the next one at the customer farthest from all open facilities. This is Gonzalez's 2-approximation for the k-center (maximum distance) problem.

Connection to the chapter

Facility location is also a social-choice problem: on a line, placing one facility at the median of the reported locations is strategy-proof, because no agent can move the median toward itself by misreporting. Placing it at the mean is not. Compare the objectives: no single placement minimises both the average and the maximum distance.
