How to play

One facility (a school, a hospital, a bus stop) is built somewhere on a road from 0 to 100. Every agent (blue disc) has an ideal point xi and pays the distance to the facility, costi = |xi − t|. Agents only report their points, and a rule turns the reports into a location t.

  • Drag the discs on the line or type the ideal points in the table. Random draws a new profile; Example loads 10, 30, 45, 80, 95.
  • The red triangle is the facility. Below the axis, the green diamond marks the point with the minimum total cost (a median) and the amber square the point with the minimum maximum cost (the midpoint of the two extreme agents). The table compares the rule with both.
  • Manipulation: pick an agent and move the slider (or drag the dashed disc) to let it report a false location while everyone else tells the truth. The chart shows the agent's own true cost for every integer report from 0 to 100; the green dashed line is its cost when it tells the truth, and red stretches are profitable lies. Find best lie tries all 101 reports.

Rules

  • Median: sort the reports and take the middle one. With an even number of agents there are two middle reports; this simulator always takes the left median (the n/2-th smallest report). Any point between the two middle reports minimises the total cost, and a fixed choice such as "always left" keeps the rule strategy-proof.
  • Mean: the average report. An agent can drag the average towards itself by exaggerating, so it is not strategy-proof.
  • Generalized median (Moulin, 1980): add fixed phantom points that nobody controls and take the (left) median of reports and phantoms together. Strategy-proof for every choice of phantoms; phantoms at 0 pull the result left, phantoms at 100 pull it right.
  • Midpoint of extremes: halfway between the smallest and largest report. Optimal for the maximum cost, but the extreme agents can manipulate it.
  • Leftmost and Dictator: strategy-proof too, but they ignore most of the agents.

Theory in brief

On a line with distance costs, preferences are single-peaked: each agent prefers locations closer to its peak. Then the median voter's peak is a Condorcet winner (Black), and the median rule is strategy-proof: an agent left of the median can only move the median further right by reporting a point right of it, and reporting anything left of it changes nothing; the same holds on the other side. Moulin showed that the strategy-proof, anonymous rules that use only the reported peaks are exactly the generalized medians with n + 1 phantoms (n − 1 phantoms if the rule must also be Pareto-efficient), so the Gibbard–Satterthwaite impossibility disappears on single-peaked preferences. The median also minimises the total cost; for the maximum cost the best strategy-proof deterministic rule is a 2-approximation (the median achieves it), while the optimal midpoint can be manipulated (Procaccia and Tennenholtz).
