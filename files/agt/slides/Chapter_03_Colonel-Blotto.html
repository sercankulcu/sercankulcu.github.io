How to play

  1. Each round you have a fixed troop budget (Total troops).
  2. Split the whole budget across battlefields A, B and C. Even and Random fill the fields for you; the sum must equal the budget.
  3. The computer chooses its own allocation at the same time.
  4. Each battlefield goes to the side with more troops there; a tie gives ½ point to each. The side with more battlefield points wins the round.
  5. Points accumulate over rounds. The table shows the last rounds; watch for patterns in the bot's play and in your own.

Computer strategies

  • Random: a uniformly random split of the budget; ignores history.
  • Greedy: concentrates on two random battlefields and abandons the third.
  • Predictive: computes your average allocation and best-responds to it: it beats your two cheapest fields by a small margin and puts the rest on the third.
  • Adaptive: tries many candidate allocations, keeps the one that would have scored best against all your past rounds (a fictitious-play style learner), with some randomness.

Why predictability is dangerous

No single allocation is best against every opponent. If you always concentrate in the same way, an observant rival can sacrifice one battlefield and win the other two cheaply. That is why randomization and opponent modelling are central to Blotto.

Game-theory connection

Colonel Blotto is a simultaneous, zero-sum resource-allocation game with no pure-strategy Nash equilibrium: every pure allocation has a best response that beats it. Equilibria are mixed strategies. It illustrates best responses, budget constraints, opponent exploitation and the tension between concentrating and spreading resources.
