How to play

The proposer splits a pool of tokens by choosing the responder's share. The responder either accepts and the split is carried out, or rejects and both get zero.

With purely monetary preferences and whole-token offers there are two subgame-perfect equilibria: the responder accepts every offer including 0 and the proposer keeps all 10 tokens (10, 0); or the responder rejects 0 (being indifferent) and accepts every positive offer, and the proposer offers 1 token (9, 1). Real people often reject unequal offers even though rejecting costs them money, and proposers anticipate this. The ultimatum game separates purely monetary rationality from fairness-sensitive behavior.

Display

The token bar shows the split (blue: proposer, red: responder). The chart below shows each round's offer as a share of the pool: green bars were accepted, red bars rejected; the dashed line marks the equal split.

Scenarios

  • Classic split: 10 tokens are divided.
  • Scarce resource: 6 tokens are divided. With so few possible splits, each token matters more strategically.
  • Repeated bargaining: rounds of 10 tokens in which the computer adapts. As proposer, the computer raises its offer by one after a rejection and lowers it by one after an acceptance; as responder, it hardens after a low offer (under 40%) and softens after a generous one (40% or more).

Modes

  • You propose: click an offer; the computer decides whether to accept.
  • You respond: the computer makes an offer; you accept or reject it.
  • Human – Human: on the same device, Player 1 proposes and Player 2 responds.
  • Computer – Computer: each New round simulates a round automatically.

Levels

  • Level 1 — Lenient: computer responders accept every positive offer; computer proposers offer 1 token.
  • Level 2 — Strategic: computer responders demand about 30% of the pool; proposers aim near this threshold (± 1 token).
  • Level 3 — Fairness-minded: the acceptance threshold rises to about 45%; fairness norms and the risk of rejection come to the fore; proposers again aim near the threshold.

Suggested use: start as proposer at Level 1, then raise the level. Swap roles and compare the smallest offer you would personally accept with the computer's threshold.
