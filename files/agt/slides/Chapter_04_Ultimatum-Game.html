How this teaches game theory

The ultimatum game separates purely monetary rationality from fairness-sensitive behavior. A responder can reject an unequal offer even though rejection gives both players zero, while a proposer anticipates that possibility when choosing a split.

Scenario explanations

Classic split

Divide 10 tokens. The proposer chooses the responder's share; acceptance implements the split, rejection gives both zero.

Scarce resource

Divide 6 tokens. Fewer feasible splits make each token more strategically important.

Repeated bargaining

Play repeated 10-token rounds and compare how offers evolve after acceptance or rejection.

Modes

Human proposer

You choose the offer; the computer decides whether to accept.

Human responder

The computer proposes; you accept or reject.

Human vs Human

Player 1 proposes and Player 2 responds on the same device.

Computer vs Computer

Automated bargaining reveals how different behavioral rules interact.

Levels

Level 1 — Lenient

Computer responders accept any positive offer; computer proposers make low but nonzero offers.

Level 2 — Strategic

Computer responders demand roughly 30%; proposers anticipate that threshold.

Level 3 — Fairness-aware

Acceptance thresholds move toward an even split, emphasizing fairness norms and rejection risk.

Suggested use: Begin as proposer at Level 1, then increase the level. Switch roles and compare the smallest offer you personally accept with the computer's threshold.
