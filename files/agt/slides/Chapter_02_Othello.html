How to play

  1. You play Black and move first; the computer plays White.
  2. Place a disc on an empty square so that one or more white discs lie in a straight line (horizontal, vertical or diagonal) between the new disc and another black disc. All of those discs flip to black.
  3. Legal moves are marked with a gold ring. A player without a legal move must pass; the game ends when neither side can move, and the player with more discs wins.
  4. The small red dot marks the computer's last move.

Move scores

With Show move scores on, each ring shows how good that move looks for you (higher is better, the best one has a white ring):

  • Simple: discs flipped plus a bonus for corners (+15) and edges (+5) and a penalty for squares next to a corner (−15).
  • Smart: a short Minimax look-ahead (your move, then two more plies) relative to your worst move, using the same evaluation as the Smart computer.

Computer opponents

  • Simple picks the move with the best one-move heuristic score.
  • Smart searches five plies with Minimax and alpha-beta pruning. Its evaluation counts discs, rewards corners (100), edges (15) and mobility (8 per legal move) and punishes squares next to corners (50).

Game-theory connection

Othello is a two-player, zero-sum game of perfect information. Its 8×8 version was shown by computation in 2023 to be a draw under perfect play. Comparing the two opponents shows how a deeper search and a better evaluation function change the quality of decisions.
