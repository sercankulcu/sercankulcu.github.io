How to play

Proposers P1…Pn and receivers R1…Rn rank the other side. The simulator runs the Gale–Shapley deferred acceptance algorithm:

  1. A free proposer proposes to the best receiver he or she has not yet tried.
  2. A free receiver tentatively accepts. A matched receiver keeps the proposer it prefers and rejects the other; the rejected proposer becomes free again.
  3. The process ends when no free proposer has anyone left to propose to.

In the preference lists, a green chip is the current partner, struck-through chips are those already tried, and a dashed chip is the next receiver the proposer will try. In the diagram, green lines are tentative matches; the dashed blue line shows the latest accepted proposal and the dotted red line the latest rejected one; the small numbers give the rank each side assigns to its partner (1 = first choice).

A matching is stable if no proposer and receiver prefer each other to their assigned partners (there is no blocking pair). The simulator checks this when the run ends.

Modes

  • Human picks: you choose the next free proposer with the buttons above the diagram; the receiving side answers automatically. Whatever order you choose, the final matching is the same, a property of deferred acceptance.
  • Step by step: Next proposal advances the algorithm one proposal at a time (the lowest-numbered free proposer first). Ideal for classroom demonstrations.
  • Automatic: every new instance is run to the end at once; use New preferences to compare many random profiles.

Levels

  • Level 1 — 3 × 3: with short lists, every proposal, rejection and replacement is easy to follow.
  • Level 2 — 4 × 4: longer rejection chains; local decisions still end in a globally stable matching.
  • Level 3 — Compare: a 4 × 4 market. When the run ends, the simulator also runs deferred acceptance with the receivers proposing and compares the average rank each side obtains. Deferred acceptance is the best of all stable matchings for the proposing side, so the proposing side usually does better.

Suggested path: start with Level 1 in step-by-step mode, try Level 2 as the proposing side's coordinator, then run Level 3 a few times and compare the averages. Stability does not mean equal satisfaction for both sides.
