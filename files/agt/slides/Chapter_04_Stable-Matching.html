How to play

Proposers P1…Pn and receivers R1…Rn each rank the other side. The simulator runs the Gale–Shapley deferred-acceptance algorithm:

  1. A free proposer proposes to the best receiver it has not yet tried.
  2. A free receiver tentatively accepts. A matched receiver keeps the proposer it prefers and rejects the other, who becomes free again.
  3. The process ends when no free proposer has anyone left to propose to.

In the preference lists a green chip is the current partner, struck-through chips have already been tried, and the dashed chip is the next receiver the proposer will try. In the diagram green lines are tentative matches, the dashed blue line is the last accepted proposal and the dotted red line the last rejected one; the small numbers show the rank each side gives its partner (1 = first choice).

A matching is stable when no proposer and receiver would both prefer each other to their assigned partners (no blocking pair). The simulator checks this when the run ends.

Modes

  • Human proposes vs market: you choose which free proposer moves next with the buttons above the diagram; the receiving side responds automatically. The final matching is the same whatever order you choose, a property of deferred acceptance.
  • Step through algorithm: Next proposal advances the algorithm one proposal at a time (lowest-numbered free proposer first). Ideal for classroom demonstrations.
  • Automatic simulation: every new instance is run to completion immediately; use New preferences to compare many random profiles.

Levels

  • Level 1 — 3 × 3: short lists make every proposal, rejection and replacement easy to follow.
  • Level 2 — 4 × 4: longer rejection chains; local decisions still end in a globally stable matching.
  • Level 3 — Compare proposer sides: a 4 × 4 market. When the run ends the simulator also runs deferred acceptance with the receivers proposing and compares the average rank each side obtains. Deferred acceptance is optimal for the proposing side among all stable matchings, so the side that proposes usually does better.

Suggested path: start with Level 1 in step-through mode, try Level 2 as the proposer side's coordinator, then run Level 3 several times and compare the averages. Stability does not mean equal satisfaction for both sides.
