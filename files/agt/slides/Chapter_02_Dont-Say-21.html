    How to play

    1. Players count in turn, starting from 1.
    2. On each turn a player says one, two or three consecutive numbers.
    3. The next player continues from the next number.
    4. The player who is forced to say the losing number (21 by default) loses.
    5. Changing the losing number shows how the winning pattern shifts.

    Control numbers

    Instead of thinking forward from 1, reason backward from the losing number: which number can you leave the opponent so that you can answer every reply? Since at most three numbers can be said per turn, the gap between control positions is four: for 21 the control numbers are 20, 16, 12, 8 and 4.

    Strategy tips

    • Work backward before the game starts to find the numbers on which you want your turns to end.
    • If the opponent says one number, answer with three; if two, answer with two; if three, answer with one.
    • Changing the losing number tests whether you understand the pattern instead of having memorised one sequence.

    Connection to game theory

    This is a small impartial sequential game closely related to subtraction games. It illustrates backward induction, winning and losing positions, strategy invariants, and the fact that a game that looks like a conversation can have an exact mathematical solution.
