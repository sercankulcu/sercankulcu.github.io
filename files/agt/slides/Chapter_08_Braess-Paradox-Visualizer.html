    How to play

    N drivers travel from S to T. Click a route button to send the next driver; the small number on each button is the travel time that driver would get. Fill selfishly lets every driver pick their own fastest route; Show social optimum applies the assignment with the smallest total travel time. Toggle the shortcut A→B to compare the two networks.

    The network (classic Braess example)

    • S→A and B→T are congestible: travel time = 40 × (drivers on the edge) / N.
    • A→T and S→B are wide roads with a fixed time of 45.
    • The shortcut A→B takes 0 time.

    Without the shortcut, drivers split evenly and everyone needs 20 + 45 = 65. With the shortcut, the zigzag route S→A→B→T is always at least as fast for an individual driver, so in equilibrium everyone uses it and needs 40 + 40 = 80. Adding a free road made everyone slower — Braess's paradox.

    What this shows

    This is a congestion game: a route's cost depends on how many players use its edges, so each driver's choice changes the others' incentives. Each driver ignores the delay imposed on others (a congestion externality), so the selfish equilibrium can differ from the social optimum. More options do not always imply better collective outcomes; network design must consider equilibrium behaviour, not only physical capacity.

    Modes and levels

    • Human assigns drivers: try to beat the selfish outcome or reproduce the optimum by hand.
    • Human + best-route hint: the currently fastest route for the next driver is highlighted (myopic best response).
    • Computer simulation: the network is filled selfishly at every reset.
    • Levels change the number of drivers (8, 16, 24); edge times are scaled so the classic 65 vs 80 comparison holds.

    Reading the results

    Average cost is the mean travel time of assigned drivers. Optimum avg. is the smallest achievable average for the current network. Cost / optimum (once all drivers are assigned) is 1.00 for an optimal assignment; above 1 means efficiency loss. The table on the left compares selfish equilibrium and optimum with and without the shortcut. The status line tells you whether a full assignment is a Nash equilibrium (no driver can switch to a faster route).
