    How to play

    Pick a scenario, a mode, and a level; then choose an action each round. Payoffs are shown as (row player, column player); you are the row player. The cell of the last round is highlighted and pure Nash equilibria are outlined. The log keeps the most recent rounds.

    Scenarios

    • Prisoner's Dilemma (Silent / Betray): Betray is a dominant strategy, so (Betray, Betray) is the unique Nash equilibrium, yet mutual silence pays both players more. Individual rationality and collective welfare pull in opposite directions.
    • Coordination (Concert / Cinema): both players gain from choosing the same event but prefer different outcomes. There are two pure Nash equilibria: the problem is picking the same one.
    • Matching Pennies (Heads / Tails): the row player wins if the choices match, the column player if they differ. There is no pure equilibrium; the only Nash equilibrium is mixed: each action with probability 1/2. Predictable play gets exploited.

    Modes

    • Human–Computer: you choose and the computer responds according to the level.
    • Human–Human: on one device; player 1's choice is kept hidden, then player 2 chooses and the round is revealed. The level has no effect.
    • Computer–Computer: when the match starts, 20 rounds are simulated; both agents use the chosen level. Press New match to run it again.

    Levels

    • 1 — Random: each action with probability 1/2; ignores the history.
    • 2 — Best response: the best reply to the opponent's last action (assumes it will be repeated).
    • 3 — Adaptive: counts the opponent's past actions and best-responds to the most frequent one (fictitious play). Repeated patterns become exploitable.

    Suggested path

    Play each scenario first against level 1, then levels 2 and 3. Use Computer–Computer to compare long-run behavior (level 2 against level 2 cycles in Matching Pennies and settles in the Prisoner's Dilemma). Finally play Human–Human and explain your strategy before the choices are revealed.
