    How to play

    • Click Rock, Paper or Scissors (or press R, P, S). The computer chooses at the same time.
    • Rock beats Scissors, Scissors beats Paper, Paper beats Rock. Equal choices are a tie.
    • The panel on the left counts wins, losses and ties and shows how often you used each move.

    Opponents

    • Random: plays each move with probability 1/3. This is the unique Nash equilibrium of the game; no strategy can beat it on average, and it cannot exploit you either.
    • Frequency learner: counts your past moves and plays the move that beats your most frequent one (fictitious play).
    • Pattern learner: looks at what you played after your previous move in earlier rounds and counters your most likely next move.

    What this shows

    Rock Paper Scissors is a symmetric zero-sum game with no pure-strategy equilibrium: any predictable choice has a best response that beats it. The only equilibrium is the mixed strategy (1/3, 1/3, 1/3). Against the learners, a biased or repetitive pattern loses; mixing uniformly brings your expected score back to zero.
