    How to play

    1. Each player owns six pits on their side and one store (Player 1: bottom row and the right store; Player 2: top row and the left store). Every pit starts with 4 stones.
    2. Click one of your non-empty pits. All its stones are picked up; the first stone goes back into the same pit and the rest are sown one by one counter-clockwise (a single stone simply moves to the next pit). The opponent's store is skipped.
    3. If your last stone lands in your own store, you get an extra turn.
    4. If your last stone makes an opponent's pit even, you capture that pit.
    5. If your last stone lands in one of your own empty pits while the opposite pit has stones, you capture both.
    6. The game ends when one side runs out of stones; the player who emptied their side first also collects all stones left on the opponent's side. The higher store total wins.

    The blue ring marks the pits changed by the last move's final stone and starting pit.

    Computer levels

    • Easy: random legal pit.
    • Medium: greedy — the move with the largest immediate gain (extra turns count as a bonus).
    • Hard: Minimax with Alpha-Beta pruning, six plies deep (extra turns keep the same player to move).

    Planning a move

    Before selecting a pit, count where the final stone will land. The last stone matters most: it may produce an extra turn, create a capture or expose stones to the opponent.

    Strategy tips

    • Extra turns can be worth more than a small capture because they keep the initiative.
    • Watch the opponent's pits for positions that allow large captures.
    • Do not empty your side too quickly unless the endgame favours you.

    Game-theory connection

    Mangala is a sequential, deterministic, perfect-information game. It highlights backward planning, tempo, state evaluation and the difference between immediate payoff and long-horizon value — a natural setting for Minimax and Alpha-Beta search.
