    How to play

    A large population plays a symmetric game. Each strategy's fitness is its average payoff against the current population; in every generation a strategy's share is multiplied by fitness ÷ average fitness. Press Next generation or Run 25 generations and watch the bars and the history chart.

    What to observe: a strategy grows when its fitness is above the population average. Evolutionary stability is about resistance to invasion, not necessarily maximising total welfare.

    Scenarios

    • Hawk–Dove: aggressive and peaceful behaviour compete for resources. Selection supports a mixed population instead of a single winner; the third strategy, Bourgeois, respects ownership.
    • Coordination: strategies earn more when they match the population. A small initial majority becomes a self-reinforcing convention (path dependence).
    • Rock–Paper–Scissors: each strategy beats one rival and loses to another; cyclic dominance keeps diversity alive. Use Level 2 or 3 (three strategies).

    Modes

    • Human Sets Population: use the boost buttons to shape the initial shares (generation 0 only), then observe selection.
    • Human Intervention: boosts are allowed at any time, so you can apply shocks mid-run and see how evolutionary paths change.
    • Computer Simulation: the population evolves without intervention.

    Levels

    • Level 1: the first two strategies only — basic selection pressure (start 70% / 30%).
    • Level 2: all three strategies (start 50% / 30% / 20%).
    • Level 3: three strategies plus small mutation and random noise, so extinct strategies can reappear and paths are less deterministic.
