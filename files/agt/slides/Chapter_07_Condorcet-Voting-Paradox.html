Pairwise majorities

How to play

Each voter ranks the candidates. The lab compares every pair of candidates in a pairwise contest: X beats Y if a majority of voters rank X above Y. A Condorcet winner beats every rival. Even when every voter’s ranking is perfectly consistent, the collective majority preference can be cyclic (A beats B, B beats C, C beats A): this is the Condorcet paradox, one of the basic difficulties of social choice.

Display

  • Preference profile: one column per voter (V1, V2, …); rankings run from top to bottom. Your vote is voter 1 (highlighted). With four candidates, your vote places D last.
  • Pairwise table: the cell (row X, column Y) shows the votes of X against Y; green means X wins. In Pivotal vote mode an amber frame marks contests whose outcome your vote decides (pivotal).
  • Majority graph: an arrow X → Y means a majority prefers X to Y. A Condorcet winner has only outgoing arrows (red outline); arrows on a cycle are drawn in red.

Scenarios

  • Condorcet cycle: Level 1 uses the classic profile A>B>C, B>C>A, C>A>B; higher levels draw random profiles until a cycle appears.
  • Condorcet winner: profiles in which one candidate beats every rival.
  • Agenda manipulation: in a sequential pairwise elimination (the winner of one contest meets the next candidate), when preferences are cyclic the order of the agenda can decide the winner. The lab lists the winner of every possible agenda.

Modes

  • You vote: your ranking is voter 1; you can test whether a single vote changes the pairwise results.
  • Pivotal vote: as above; the lab also marks the contests in which your vote is pivotal.
  • Simulation: all votes are generated; Shuffle voters draws a new profile.

Levels

  • Level 1: three voters and three candidates show the classic cycle plainly.
  • Level 2: seven voters and a fourth candidate create richer pairwise structures.
  • Level 3: nine voters with random profiles show that paradoxes are structural possibilities, not a single hand-built example.

Important: the Condorcet winner is defined by pairwise victories. It need not be the candidate chosen by plurality, the Borda count or approval voting.
