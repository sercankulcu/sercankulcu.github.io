Rule of thumb

One slide carries about 1–1.5 minutes of talk; 30 slides won't fit in 10 minutes.

Q&A

A 15-minute slot = 10 minutes of talk + 5 minutes of questions; put that in the plan from the start.

Backup slides

Detailed tables go at the end as hidden slides, ready if a question comes up.

Planning

Slide Outline

View → Outline View: every line you type becomes the title of a new slide. 5 lines → 5 slides.

Tab on lines 3 and 4: they drop a level and become bullets on the “Purpose” slide. The slide count falls to 3.

After “Method”, Enter + Tab adds a bullet. Outline first, design later: the content flow is built before the slides.

Managing Slides · Step by step

Adding and Reordering Sections

In a seven-slide presentation, the method slides (Survey, Sample) have ended up behind the findings by mistake. Sections group slides so you can move them a group at a time.

Add Section starts a section at the selected slide and asks for a name in the Rename Section box. For now all seven slides are in “Opening”.

The new section starts at the selected slide (3); the earlier ones stay in “Opening”.

The third section starts at Slide 6. The Method section still comes after the findings.

Move Section Up: the section moves up one place along with its two slides; the slides are renumbered (Survey is now 3).

Collapse All: navigating a long presentation gets easier. Sections don't appear in the show; they are only for editing and navigation.

select Slide 1
Home → Section → Add Section: “Opening”
select Slide 3 → Add Section: “Findings”
select Slide 6 → Add Section: “Method”
right-click “Method” → Move Section Up
right-click a section name → Collapse All

Themes and the Slide Master

Theme

Sets the system of colors, fonts, effects and backgrounds.

Slide Master

Manages the shared placement, logo, font and footer rules of the layouts.

View → Slide Master: the rules of the master at the top (font, color, position) pass to the layouts, and from the layouts to the slides.

Visual Hierarchy

Before: the title, the key finding and the source share the same size and weight. The audience has to hunt for the most important fact (the growth rate).

After: the title states the result, the key number (38%) is the biggest element, the detail is small and the source faint. The eye goes to the most important thing first.

Visuals · Example

Picture Size and Compression

Example: a 4000 × 3000-pixel photo will be shown full screen on a 16:9 slide; the screen is 1920 × 1080 pixels.

Uncompressed (3 bytes per pixel):
4000 × 3000 × 3 = 36 MB · 1920 × 1080 × 3 ≈ 6.2 MB

The extra pixels never show on screen; they only make the file about 5.8 times larger.

Compress Pictures

On the Picture Format tab; choose a resolution for the target (e.g. 150 ppi for web).

Cropped areas

The part hidden by Crop stays in the file; unless it is deleted when compressing, the size doesn't shrink.

Remove Background

Makes the picture's background transparent so the object sits on the slide color.

Align and Distribute Step by Step

Three shapes placed by eye: top edges and gaps differ. The slide looks messy.

Select all, then Shape Format → Align → Align Top: the top edges line up with the highest shape (y = 40).

Align → Distribute Horizontally: the leftmost and rightmost stay put, and the gaps between become equal (95 px).

View → Guides / Gridlines: shared lines help keep the same placement across slides; the dashed line in the middle is the slide center.

All are Grouped and moved to the slide center with Align Center (shifted +10 px): they now move as one object and the gaps stay intact.

Motion

Transitions vs Animations

The show has started: Slide 1 is on screen.

Click → a transition effect (Push): Slide 2 comes in from the right and pushes Slide 1 off the screen. A transition applies to the whole slide.

Slide 2 is on screen; the bullets are animated and not visible yet. The small numbers show the animation order (in editing only).

Click → bullet 1 appears with an entrance animation. The slide didn't change; only one object on it moved.

Click → bullet 2 arrives. The talk moves step by step; the audience isn't reading a bullet you haven't reached yet.

Click → an emphasis animation on bullet 1: attention is drawn to an object already on screen.

Use motion to direct attention or explain a process; overusing it just for decoration weakens the talk.

Motion

Designer, Morph and Copilot

Designer

Design tab → Designer. Suggests layouts based on the slide content; suggestions may need an internet connection and a Microsoft 365 subscription.

Morph

Transitions tab → Morph. When the same object has a different position, size or color on two slides, it generates the motion in between automatically.

Copilot

Offers AI help such as drafting and summarizing. With a personal Microsoft 365 subscription it runs on monthly AI credits; work accounts need a separate Copilot license.

Whether these features exist, and what they are called, depends on the PowerPoint version, license and update level; tab and button names may differ in your version.

Animation Timing

StartBehavior

With PreviousStarts at the same time as the previous effect

  • Duration sets the speed of an effect; delay sets the wait before it starts.
  • Rehearse animations in Slide Show view.

Motion

The Animation Timeline

Animation Pane: four effects and their start types. The number shows which click starts each effect.

Click 1: “Title” starts On Click: 0–0.5 s.

“Picture” is With Previous: it starts together with the title (0–1 s); no extra click needed.

“Bullet 1” is After Previous with a 0.25 s delay: once the previous effect (Picture) ends at 1 s it waits, then starts at 1.25 s.

The automatic chain is over. The next effect starts on click, so PowerPoint waits for the presenter.

Click 2 (the presenter clicks at 3 s): “Chart” starts, 3–4 s. Duration sets the speed of an effect; delay sets the wait before it starts.

Motion · Step by step

Rearranging the Animation Order

Effects are listed in the order they were added: the chart went in first by mistake. The small numbers on the slide are the click order in the pane.

The selected effect is moved up one place with the ▲ button in the pane (or by dragging); the numbers are reassigned.

▼ twice: the chart drops to the end of the talk. The order now matches the flow of speech.

After Previous takes no click of its own: its number disappears from the pane and on the slide it shares bullet 1's number (2). Clicks drop from 4 to 3.

Three clicks are enough in the show. The fourth click goes to the next slide: the slide never changes before all its effects are done.

Animations → Animation Pane
select “Title” → ▲ (up one)
select “Chart” → ▼ ▼ (to the end)
“Bullet 2”: Start → After Previous
try it in Slide Show

Presenting · Step by step

Rehearse Timings and Self-Running Shows

Rehearse Timings starts the show; the toolbar in the corner counts the time on the slide and the total.

Rehearse at real speaking speed; on the advance click, Slide 1's time (8 s) is recorded.

All slides done: 8 + 12 + 10 + 10 = 40 s.

The recorded times go into each slide's transition setting (Transitions tab → After) and show under the thumbnails in Slide Sorter.

Kiosk type (browsed at a kiosk): the show opens full screen, doesn't advance on click and loops until Esc is pressed. That is why timings are required; only hyperlinks and action buttons work.

After the last slide the show starts over. At t = 46 s Slide 1 is on screen again: 46 − 40 = 6, second 6 of the second round.

Slide Show → Rehearse Timings
talk over Slide 1: 8 s → next
Slide 2: 12 s, Slide 3: 10 s, Slide 4: 10 s
Keep the new slide timings? → Yes
Set Up Slide Show → kiosk
F5: the show advances by itself

Presenting

Presenting Effectively

  • Rehearse in advance with the real screen and connections.
  • Don't read the slides; keep eye contact and engage the audience.
  • Tie every part clearly to the key message.
  • Use the pointer and animations only to direct attention.
  • Keep to time; leave room for Q&A.
  • Have fallbacks ready: a PDF backup, adapters and an offline copy.

Summary

Chapter Summary

  • An effective presentation rests on a narrative flow planned for its purpose and audience.
  • Themes and the Slide Master keep the design consistent across the presentation.
  • The slide title states the topic; visual hierarchy shows what matters most.
  • Transitions work between slides; animations work on objects within a slide.
  • Speaker notes and Presenter View support the delivery.
  • Finish accessibility checks, technical rehearsal and the final check before sharing.

Exercises

  1. In Outline View, write the titles and bullets of a 5-slide presentation; then apply a layout and a theme.
