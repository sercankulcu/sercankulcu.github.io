Microprocessors · History of Computing

Important People in Microprocessors

The history of microprocessors brings together semiconductor technology, instruction set architecture, CPU implementation, memory systems, performance engineering and the development that runs from the first single-chip processors to today's x86 and RISC designs.

From the integrated circuit to today's processors

These cards introduce the people who contributed to the invention of the transistor, semiconductor integration, the first microprocessors, the 8086/x86 family, RISC architectures, memory technology, processor performance, the classification of parallel processors and the first widely used high-level language.

People · Chips · Architecture

Notable contributions

The leading people behind integrated circuits, the first commercial microprocessors, the Intel 8086/x86 family, RISC and ARM architectures, memory technology and processor performance engineering.

FF

Federico Faggin

Intel 4004 · 8080 · silicon-gate design

Led the design of the Intel 4004 and contributed to the development of the first general-purpose microprocessors.

Technical significance

His work helped move CPU functions from boards full of logic circuits into highly integrated single-chip processors.

40048080MOS integration
Faggin developed silicon-gate MOS technology and led the implementation work that made the 4004 usable as a commercial microprocessor. He later contributed to the 8008 and 8080 era.

Technical concepts: 4004, 8080, MOS integration, instruction decoding, datapath integration, semiconductor scaling.
TH

Ted Hoff

Microprocessor architecture · Intel 4004

Proposed a general-purpose, programmable processor architecture for the project that became the Intel 4004.

Technical significance

Replacing many special-purpose logic circuits with a programmable CPU turned the microprocessor into a computing component that could be reused in different products.

4004architecturestored-program control
Hoff's architectural contribution was to simplify a chipset designed for a calculator into a programmable processor plus memory and I/O components.

Technical concepts: 4004, architecture, stored-program control, instruction set, programmable control.
SM

Stanley Mazor

Intel 4004 architecture

Worked on the architectural definition and the instruction set of the Intel 4004.

Technical significance

The choices made in an instruction set determine how software expresses data movement, arithmetic, branching and control.

ISA4004opcode design
Mazor contributed to defining the instructions and the architectural behavior of Intel's first microprocessor family.

Technical concepts: instruction set architecture (ISA), 4004, opcode design, instruction formats, control flow.
MS

Masatoshi Shima

4004 implementation · CPU design

Played a central role in the detailed logic design of the Intel 4004 and of later microprocessors.

Technical significance

Turning an architectural definition into a working chip takes concrete control logic, datapaths, timing and interfaces.

4004logic designdatapath
Shima translated architectural requirements into detailed processor logic and later worked on the design of other influential CPUs.

Technical concepts: 4004, logic design, datapath, timing, register transfer, chip implementation.
SM

Stephen Morse

Intel 8086 architecture · 1970s

Was the principal architect of the Intel 8086 instruction set and programming model.

Technical significance

The 8086 laid the foundation of the x86 architecture studied in the course.

8086x86ISA
The 8086 introduced 16-bit registers, segmented addressing, general-purpose and segment registers, and an instruction set that is the ancestor of today's x86 processors.

Technical concepts: 8086, x86, ISA, segment:offset addressing, registers, the flags register (FLAGS).
BR

Bruce Ravenel

8086 architecture and implementation

Worked on the 8086 design team and in the early development of x86.

Technical significance

Processor architecture is teamwork that brings together instruction-set design, implementation constraints and system compatibility.

8086x86execution unit
Within the semiconductor constraints of the late 1970s, the 8086 team balanced transistor budget, performance, code density, bus design and compatibility goals.

Technical concepts: 8086, x86, execution unit (EU), bus interface unit (BIU), instruction prefetch, segmented memory.
BP

Bill Pohlman

Intel 8086 project management

Managed the engineering work of the Intel 8086 development program.

Technical significance

A successful CPU design requires coordination among architecture, logic design, verification, manufacturing and product constraints.

8086Intelengineering
The 8086 is the product of engineering carried out under tight constraints: a limited transistor count, 16-bit arithmetic, a 20-bit address bus and a design that eased the move from earlier processors.

Technical concepts: 8086, Intel, engineering, bus width, address space, system integration.
SW

Sophie Wilson

ARM instruction set · RISC

Designed the original ARM instruction set architecture.

Technical significance

ARM showed that a small, regular instruction set can deliver high performance with low hardware complexity and low power consumption.

ARMRISCISA
Wilson's work on the ARM architecture emphasized simple instruction decoding, a load/store design, conditional execution and efficient use of registers.

Technical concepts: ARM, RISC, ISA, load/store architecture, registers, conditional execution.
SF

Steve Furber

ARM processor implementation

Co-designed the implementation of the first ARM processor.

Technical significance

His work tied the ideas of RISC architecture to a practical, low-power processor design.

ARMRISCdatapath
Furber helped turn the ARM instruction set into working silicon and later made important contributions to processor and neuromorphic-system research.

Technical concepts: ARM, RISC, datapath, control logic, low-power design.
JH

John Hennessy

RISC architecture · MIPS

Led groundbreaking work on RISC processor architecture and the MIPS family.

Technical significance

RISC research reshaped the balance that processors strike between instruction complexity, pipelining, compiler support and implementation speed.

MIPSRISCpipelining
Hennessy's work helped load/store instruction sets, large register files and pipelined execution become established design principles.

Technical concepts: MIPS, RISC, pipelining, load/store, joint design of compiler and architecture.
DP

David Patterson

RISC · Berkeley RISC

Led the Berkeley RISC project and helped define today's idea of reduced instruction set computing.

Technical significance

RISC designs showed that simpler instructions allow faster pipelines and more efficient implementations.

RISCRISC-Vpipelining
Patterson also helped establish the quantitative approach to computer architecture and later took part in the development of RISC-V.

Technical concepts: RISC, RISC-V, pipelining, quantitative architecture, instruction set design.
MF

Michael J. Flynn

Flynn's taxonomy · 1966

In 1966 classified processors into four classes, SISD, SIMD, MISD and MIMD, by the number of instruction streams and data streams processed at the same time.

Technical significance

The difference between a scalar core, a vector or array processor, a GPU and a multicore processor can be described in one table with this taxonomy. The SSE and AVX instructions of x86 belong to the SIMD class.

SIMDMIMDparallel architecture
The taxonomy was published in the paper “Very High-Speed Computing Systems” (Proceedings of the IEEE, 1966). Flynn worked on computer architecture at Stanford University.

Technical concepts: SISD, SIMD, MISD, MIMD, instruction stream, data stream, vector processing.
JB

John Bardeen

Transistor · 1947 · Bell Laboratories

Together with Walter Brattain, built the first working transistor, the point-contact transistor, at Bell Laboratories in late 1947.

Technical significance

The transistor does the switching and amplifying that the vacuum tube did with a much smaller, low-power and rugged semiconductor device. Every logic gate in a microprocessor is built from transistors.

transistorsemiconductorBell Laboratories
Bardeen, Brattain and Shockley shared the 1956 Nobel Prize in Physics for their work on the transistor. In 1972 Bardeen received a second Nobel Prize in Physics for the theory of superconductivity (BCS theory); he is the only person to have won this prize twice.

Technical concepts: point-contact transistor, germanium, amplification, switching, solid-state electronics.
WB

Walter Brattain

Transistor · 1947 · experimental physics

At Bell Laboratories, built and operated the first point-contact transistor on a germanium crystal together with Bardeen (1947).

Technical significance

Turning the theoretical idea into a working device was possible through experimental work on semiconductor surfaces. The transistor was announced in 1948 and began the age of microelectronics.

transistorgermaniumsurface physics
Brattain was an experimental physicist who studied the electrical behavior of semiconductor surfaces. He shared the 1956 Nobel Prize in Physics with Bardeen and Shockley.

Technical concepts: point-contact transistor, semiconductor surface, germanium crystal, current gain.
WS

William Shockley

Junction transistor · the beginning of Silicon Valley

Led the transistor group at Bell Laboratories and developed the theory of the junction transistor; the device was announced in 1951.

Technical significance

The junction transistor was more robust than the point-contact transistor and better suited to mass production. The transistors in integrated circuits descend from this line.

junction transistorsemiconductor industryFairchild
In 1956 Shockley founded Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory in Mountain View, California. In 1957 eight researchers left it, among them Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore, and founded Fairchild Semiconductor. Intel was later born from this team. Shockley shared the 1956 Nobel Prize in Physics with Bardeen and Brattain.

Technical concepts: junction transistor, p-n junction, silicon, semiconductor manufacturing.
RN

Robert Noyce

Integrated circuits · one of the founders of Intel

One of the inventors of the integrated circuit; co-founded Intel.

Technical significance

Integrated circuits made it possible to manufacture dense, reliable processor chips economically.

integrated circuitIntelsemiconductor manufacturing
Noyce's work on planar integrated circuits and semiconductor manufacturing formed the technological base of commercial microprocessors.

Technical concepts: integrated circuit (IC), Intel, semiconductor manufacturing, planar process.
GM

Gordon Moore

Semiconductor scaling · Intel

One of the founders of Intel; he stated the scaling trend that was later named Moore's law.

Technical significance

For decades the rapid growth in transistor density determined processor performance, the level of integration, cache size and architectural complexity.

Moore's lawtransistor densityscaling
Moore's observations became a planning principle in the industry for semiconductor integration and product roadmaps.

Technical concepts: Moore's law, transistor density, scaling, integration, Intel.
JK

Jack Kilby

Integrated circuit

Built and operated one of the first integrated circuits.

Technical significance

Integration replaced hand-wired assemblies with small semiconductor circuits and opened the way to ever more complex digital systems.

integrated circuitsemiconductor integrationminiaturization
Kilby's work began the integrated-circuit era in which microprocessors were born.

Technical concepts: integrated circuit, semiconductor integration, miniaturization.
RD

Robert Dennard

DRAM · scaling

Invented the one-transistor DRAM cell and formulated Dennard scaling.

Technical significance

DRAM became the dominant main-memory technology, while the scaling rules influenced the course of processor frequency and power consumption.

DRAMmemoryDennard scaling
Dennard's contributions tie the development of processors directly to memory density and to the power characteristics of transistors.

Technical concepts: DRAM, memory, Dennard scaling, power density, semiconductor physics.
FW

Fred Weber

AMD K8 · the x86-64 era

Led the major processor architecture work at AMD during the K8 generation.

Technical significance

AMD64 extended x86 to 64-bit addressing while keeping compatibility with existing x86 software.

AMD64x86-64integrated memory controller
The K8 architecture introduced a 64-bit ISA extension and moved the memory controller onto the processor die.

Technical concepts: AMD64, x86-64, integrated memory controller, compatibility, 64-bit ISA.
DM

Dirk Meyer

AMD processors · x86 implementation

Held key management and engineering roles in the development of AMD processors.

Technical significance

Today's x86 processors have to turn a complex ISA into deeply pipelined, out-of-order internal execution.

x86AMDmicroarchitecture
AMD's designs show that architectural compatibility can coexist with far-reaching innovation in the microarchitecture.

Technical concepts: x86, AMD, microarchitecture, out-of-order execution, compatibility.
TG

Tom Gunter

Motorola 68000 family

Was the chief architect of the Motorola 68000 project; he formed the design team in 1977.

Technical significance

The 68000 family was influential in workstations, personal computers and embedded systems.

Motorola 68000ISA designregisters
With a relatively orthogonal instruction set and a large set of registers visible to the programmer, it offered a design approach different from that of the early x86 processors.

Technical concepts: Motorola 68000, ISA design, registers, embedded systems.
CP

Chuck Peddle

MOS 6502

Led the development of the MOS Technology 6502.

Technical significance

The inexpensive 6502 helped microprocessors find their way into the first personal computers and embedded products.

6502low-cost CPUpersonal computers
The 6502 was the processor of systems such as the Apple II, Commodore computers and many game consoles.

Technical concepts: 6502, low-cost CPU, personal computers, embedded systems.
BM

Bill Mensch

Motorola 6800 · 6502

Worked on the development of the Motorola 6800 and co-designed the 6502.

Technical significance

His work spans two influential 8-bit processor families of the early period.

680065028-bit processors
Mensch later founded Western Design Center, the company that continued the development of 6502-derived processors.

Technical concepts: 6800, 6502, 8-bit processors, CPU implementation.
IS

Ivan Sutherland

Computer systems · influence on VLSI education

Contributed through his research and teaching to the development of thinking in computer systems and VLSI.

Technical significance

VLSI design methods made it possible to handle increasingly complex processor chips systematically.

VLSIcomputer systemsintegrated design
His broader work influenced the culture of integrated digital-system design on which today's processor implementation rests.

Technical concepts: VLSI (very large-scale integration), computer systems, integrated design, chip design methods.
JB

John Backus

Fortran · 1957 · IBM

Led the team at IBM that developed Fortran, the first widely used high-level language, and its compiler (1957).

Technical significance

The Fortran compiler showed that programs written in the form of formulas could be turned into machine code nearly as efficient as hand-written code. The shift of programming from assembly to high-level languages began with this work.

Fortrancompilerhigh-level language
The Backus–Naur form (BNF), used to define the syntax of programming languages, also bears his name. He received the Turing Award in 1977.

Technical concepts: compiler, source code, machine code, syntax, BNF.
