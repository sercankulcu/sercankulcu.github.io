Two's Complement Lab

Goal: See how the same bit pattern reads differently as unsigned, signed (two's complement), hexadecimal and packed BCD, and what NEG, NOT, CBW and CWD do to the bits.

How to use it

  • Click bits to flip them, or type a value in the box and press Load: decimal (-5, 200), hexadecimal (0FBh) or binary (11111011b).
  • NOT shows the one's complement and NEG the two's complement (NOT and +1) step by step. ADD 1 and SUB 1 compute the carry (CF) and overflow (OF) flags.
  • At 8 bits CBW sign-extends AL into AX, at 16 bits CWD sign-extends AX into DX:AX: all the new upper bits are copies of the sign bit.
  • Encode BCD converts the unsigned value to packed BCD with one nibble per decimal digit (at most 99 at 8 bits, 9999 at 16 bits).
  • Try it yourself: questions on signed values, two's complement, sign extension and BCD; type the answer in decimal or hexadecimal (with a trailing h).

Reminder

  • In n-bit two's complement the weight of the most significant bit is −2n−1 (−128 at 8 bits, −32768 at 16 bits). Range: −128 … 127 at 8 bits, −32768 … 32767 at 16 bits; the unsigned range is 0 … 255 and 0 … 65535.
  • −x = NOT x + 1. The only exception is the most negative number: NEG turns 80h into 80h again and sets OF = 1. For NEG, CF = 1 whenever the operand is not zero.
  • CF shows unsigned overflow (carry/borrow), OF shows signed overflow: 7Fh + 1 = 80h reads as 127 + 1 = −128 when signed (OF = 1, CF = 0); FFh + 1 = 00h means 255 + 1 does not fit when unsigned (CF = 1, OF = 0).
  • In packed BCD every nibble is a decimal digit from 0 to 9; the values A–F are invalid. In unpacked BCD each byte carries a single digit.
