Stock Span Simulator

What is the Stock Span Problem?

For each day, the stock span is the number of consecutive days ending on that day for which the price was less than or equal to the current price. A monotonic decreasing stack computes all spans in linear time.

Monotonic-stack algorithm

for i from 0 to n - 1:
    while stack is not empty
          and price[stack.top] <= price[i]:
        stack.pop()

    if stack is empty:
        span[i] = i + 1
    else:
        span[i] = i - stack.top

    stack.push(i)

Stock span concepts

Why store indices?

The algorithm needs both the previous greater price and its distance from the current day. Storing indices provides both pieces of information.

Monotonic invariant

Prices at stack indices remain strictly decreasing from bottom to top. Any smaller or equal price is removed because it can no longer limit a future span.

Amortized O(n)

Although one day may pop several entries, each index is pushed once and popped at most once. Therefore, the complete algorithm performs O(n) stack operations.

Span interpretation

A span of 1 means the previous day had a greater price. A span of i + 1 means no earlier greater price exists.
