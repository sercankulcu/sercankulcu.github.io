Important People in Data Structures

Many structures and algorithms in this course still carry the names of the people who invented them: Dijkstra’s algorithm, AVL trees, Kruskal’s algorithm. These profiles follow the chapter order of the course and link each contribution to the chapter where it is taught.

From arrays and linked lists to graphs and sets The goal is not biography for its own sake, but to connect each contributor to a concrete structure, algorithm, invariant, or performance idea.

Important contributors

DK

Donald E. Knuth

Analysis of algorithms

Wrote The Art of Computer Programming and made the mathematical analysis of algorithms a standard practice, including the use of O, Ω and Θ notation.

Engineering significance

His systematic treatment of searching, sorting, trees and hashing turned implementation techniques into an engineering discipline; the expected probe counts for linear probing are his results.

In the course: Chapter 1 · Introduction, Chapter 9 · Maps & Hash Tables

Big OAnalysisHashing
NW

Niklaus Wirth

Programs as algorithms on data

Designed Pascal and wrote the book Algorithms + Data Structures = Programs (1976).

Engineering significance

The title of his book states the central idea of this course: a program is an algorithm working on a well-chosen data structure.

In the course: Chapter 1 · Introduction

PascalAlgorithmsData Structures
BL

Barbara Liskov

Abstract data types

Introduced abstract data types with Stephen Zilles (1974) and built them into the CLU language; received the Turing Award in 2008.

Engineering significance

An ADT separates what the operations do from how they are implemented, which is exactly how stacks, queues, lists, maps and sets are defined in the course.

ADTInterfacesEncapsulation
JN

John von Neumann

Sorting · Merge sort

Described merge sort in 1945, one of the first algorithms written for a stored-program computer.

Engineering significance

Merge sort is the standard example of divide and conquer and of a stable O(n log n) sorting algorithm.

In the course: Chapter 2 · Arrays

CH

C. A. R. Hoare

Sorting · Quicksort

Invented quicksort (1959–1961) and later Hoare logic for reasoning about program correctness.

Engineering significance

Quicksort demonstrates partitioning, recursion, in-place rearrangement and the effect of the pivot choice on average and worst-case cost.

In the course: Chapter 2 · Arrays

QuicksortPartitioningRecursion
NS

Allen Newell, Cliff Shaw & Herbert A. Simon

Linked lists

Introduced linked lists in their Information Processing Language (IPL) in 1955–1956.

Engineering significance

Nodes connected by references made structures that grow and shrink at run time possible; linked lists, linked stacks, queues and trees all build on this idea.

In the course: Chapter 3 · Linked Lists

Linked ListNodesReferences
RF

Robert W. Floyd

Cycle detection · Heap construction

The tortoise-and-hare cycle detection algorithm is attributed to him; his Treesort 3 (1964) builds a heap bottom-up.

Engineering significance

Two pointers moving at different speeds find a cycle in O(n) time and O(1) space; bottom-up heap construction takes O(n) instead of O(n log n).

In the course: Chapter 3 · Linked Lists, Chapter 8 · Priority Queues & Heaps

Cycle DetectionTwo PointersHeap Construction
BS

Friedrich L. Bauer & Klaus Samelson

Stacks

Formulated the stack principle (Kellerprinzip, 1955–1957) for translating and evaluating arithmetic formulas.

Engineering significance

The last-in, first-out stack became the standard tool for expression evaluation, compilers and method-call management.

In the course: Chapter 4 · Stacks

StackLIFOExpressions
JŁ

Jan Łukasiewicz

Prefix notation

Introduced Polish (prefix) notation in the 1920s; reverse Polish (postfix) notation is derived from it.

Engineering significance

Prefix and postfix expressions need no parentheses and can be evaluated with a single stack in one pass.

In the course: Chapter 4 · Stacks

PrefixPostfixNotation
ED

Edsger W. Dijkstra

Shunting-yard · Shortest paths

Developed the shunting-yard algorithm for converting infix to postfix (1961) and the shortest-path algorithm for weighted graphs (1959).

Engineering significance

Shunting-yard uses an operator stack and precedence rules; the shortest-path algorithm combines relaxation with a min-priority queue.

In the course: Chapter 4 · Stacks, Chapter 11 · Graphs

JB

Joshua Bloch

Java Collections Framework

Designed the Java Collections Framework introduced in Java 1.2 (1998).

Engineering significance

The List, Set, Map and Iterator interfaces and implementations such as ArrayList, LinkedList and HashMap separate the ADT from its data structure, as in the course.

In the course: Chapter 6 · Lists

ListIteratorJava Collections
BM

Rudolf Bayer & Edward M. McCreight

B-trees

Developed the B-tree at Boeing Scientific Research Labs (1970, published 1972).

Engineering significance

High fan-out keeps the tree shallow, so few disk blocks are read per search; B-trees and B+ trees are the basis of database and file-system indexes.

In the course: Chapter 7 · Trees

B-treeB+ treeIndexing
JW

J. W. J. Williams

Binary heap · Heapsort

Introduced the binary heap and heapsort in 1964.

Engineering significance

An array-based complete binary tree gives O(log n) insertion and removal of the minimum or maximum, and an in-place O(n log n) sort.

In the course: Chapter 8 · Priority Queues & Heaps

FT

Michael L. Fredman & Robert E. Tarjan

Fibonacci heaps

Introduced Fibonacci heaps (1984).

Engineering significance

Amortized O(1) decrease-key shows how a specialized heap can speed up graph algorithms such as shortest paths.

In the course: Chapter 8 · Priority Queues & Heaps

HL

Hans Peter Luhn

Hashing

Proposed hashing with chaining in an internal IBM memorandum in 1953.

Engineering significance

Mapping a key directly to a bucket gives expected O(1) lookup; separate chaining remains a standard way to resolve collisions.

In the course: Chapter 9 · Maps & Hash Tables

AL

Georgy Adelson-Velsky & Evgenii Landis

AVL trees

Introduced the AVL tree in 1962, the first self-balancing binary search tree.

Engineering significance

Balance factors and single or double rotations keep the height O(log n), so search, insertion and deletion stay logarithmic.

In the course: Chapter 10 · Search Trees

AVLBalance FactorRotations
GS

Leonidas J. Guibas & Robert Sedgewick

Red-black trees

Introduced red-black trees in 1978, building on Rudolf Bayer’s symmetric binary B-trees (1972).

Engineering significance

Node colors and a few rotations keep the tree balanced; Java’s TreeMap and TreeSet are red-black trees.

In the course: Chapter 10 · Search Trees

Red-Black TreeBalancingTreeMap
ST

Daniel D. Sleator & Robert E. Tarjan

Splay trees

Introduced splay trees in 1985.

Engineering significance

Splay trees store no balance information; moving each accessed node to the root gives amortized O(log n) operations and favors recently used keys.

In the course: Chapter 10 · Search Trees

Splay TreeAmortized AnalysisLocality
EM

Edward F. Moore

Breadth-first search

Published breadth-first search in 1959 as a method for finding shortest paths through a maze.

Engineering significance

BFS visits vertices level by level using a queue and finds shortest paths in unweighted graphs in O(V + E) time.

In the course: Chapter 11 · Graphs

BFSQueueShortest Path
AK

Arthur B. Kahn

Topological sorting

Published the topological sorting algorithm based on in-degrees in 1962.

Engineering significance

Repeatedly removing vertices with in-degree zero orders the vertices of a directed acyclic graph and detects cycles.

In the course: Chapter 11 · Graphs

JK

Joseph B. Kruskal

Minimum spanning trees

Published his minimum spanning tree algorithm in 1956.

Engineering significance

Kruskal’s algorithm sorts the edges and uses a union-find structure to reject edges that would form a cycle.

Bernard A. Galler & Michael J. Fischer

Disjoint-set forests

Introduced the disjoint-set forest representation in 1964.

Engineering significance

Each set is a tree whose root is its representative, so union and find need only parent links.

In the course: Chapter 12 · Sets

RT

Robert E. Tarjan

Union-find analysis

Proved in 1975 that union by rank with path compression runs in O(m·α(n)) time.

Engineering significance

The inverse Ackermann function α(n) grows so slowly that each operation is effectively constant time in practice.
