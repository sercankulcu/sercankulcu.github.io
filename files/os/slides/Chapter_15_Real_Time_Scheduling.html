    Concept Guide

    Rate Monotonic (RM): shorter period means higher fixed priority. RM is the optimal fixed-priority policy when every deadline equals its period.

    Deadline Monotonic (DM): if deadlines may be shorter than periods (D < T), the optimal fixed-priority policy orders tasks by relative deadline D instead. This simulator switches the static policy to DM automatically when any task has D < T (with D = T for all tasks, DM and RM are identical).

    Earliest Deadline First (EDF): among ready jobs, the job with the nearest absolute deadline gets highest priority. For independent periodic tasks with implicit deadlines (D = T) on one preemptive CPU, EDF is schedulable if and only if ΣU ≤ 1, and EDF is optimal: if any algorithm can meet all deadlines, EDF can.

    Utilization: for periodic task i, utilization is Cᵢ/Tᵢ; ΣU > 1 means no scheduler can keep up.

    RM utilization bound (Liu & Layland): for n independent periodic tasks with D = T, ΣU ≤ n(21/n − 1) guarantees RM schedulability (0.828 for n = 2, about 0.693 for large n). The test is sufficient but not necessary: a task set above the bound may still be schedulable (e.g. harmonic periods), so it must be checked by response-time analysis or simulation. The bound does not apply when D < T.

    Course example: P1 (C = 25, T = 50) and P2 (C = 35, T = 80) give ΣU = 0.9375 > 0.828. Under RM, P2 has executed only 30 of its 35 units at t = 80 and misses its deadline; EDF meets every deadline over the hyperperiod of 400.

    Deadline miss: a job has remaining execution time when its absolute deadline arrives.

    Assumptions: one CPU, independent periodic tasks all released at t = 0, preemption at integer time units, no blocking or overheads, constrained deadlines (D ≤ T). A job that misses its deadline is aborted (dropped) at that instant. Ties are broken by the task's number (T2 before T10). One hyperperiod (the LCM of the periods) is enough to see the whole repeating schedule; lengths above 2000 are capped.
