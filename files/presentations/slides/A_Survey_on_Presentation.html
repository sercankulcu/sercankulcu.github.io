Summary

At a Glance

Social network analysis (SNA) needs two things at once: integrating heterogeneous social data into one consistent graph, and processing that graph at scale. The survey maps the technologies for both.

I · Introduction

Why Social Network Analysis Is Hard

  • A social network is a graph: nodes are real-world entities, edges their relations. SNA finds hidden connections and characterizes the structure, mostly with graph algorithms.
  • Typical questions: strategic positions, communities, influence, homophily and transitivity, longest distances.
  • Many graph mining problems, e.g. partitioning a graph into subgraphs, are NP-hard, so systems use heuristics and keep data distributed.
  • Social data grows fast in size, variety and complexity, and is mostly unstructured.
Challenge 1 · Scale

Process very large datasets in reasonable time → parallel frameworks and graph databases.

Challenge 2 · Integration

Merge distinct datasets into a larger, semantically consistent one → RDF and ontologies.

II · Semantic Web Technologies

The Semantic Web Toolkit

RDF

Graph data model. A statement is a triple <subject, predicate, object>: a labelled, directed edge.

RDF Schema

Vocabulary to describe classes and properties, i.e. relationships between classes.

OWL

Richer primitives than RDFS for machines to reason over classes and properties.

SPARQL

Pattern-matching query language for RDF graphs; all SNA algorithms can be written in it (Lee et al.).

Linked Data links distributed datasets into knowledge graphs: Cyc, Freebase, Wikidata, DBpedia, YAGO, NELL, Google's Knowledge Graph, Yahoo!'s Knowledge Graph, Microsoft's Satori and Facebook's Social Graph (based on RDFa).

Newer SPARQL features matter for SNA: property paths for path queries (Kostylev et al.) and a proposed recursion operator (Reutter et al.).

II.A · TripleStores

Triplestores: Databases for RDF

RDF is a graph data model, SPARQL a graph query language, and a triplestore a graph database that exposes a SPARQL endpoint. Performance matters for real-time use; FEASIBLE (Saleem et al.) benchmarks them.

Centralized (one machine)

Jena TDB, RDF4J (formerly Sesame), RDFSuite, AllegroGraph, RDF-3X, Hexastore, RDFox

Distributed (several machines, more scalable)

Virtuoso, EAGRE, TriAD, 4Store, TripleRush

Benchmark (Bizer et al.): of Jena TDB, Jena SDB, Sesame and Virtuoso, Virtuoso performs best on big datasets and Sesame on smaller ones.

II.B–C · RDF Vocabularies, Prototypes and Frameworks

Vocabularies and Prototype Systems

  • FOAF: user profiles and friendships; merges personal data from several sources.
  • SKOS: sharing and linking concepts and their relations.
  • SIOC: activities of online communities (blogs, forums, wikis); extensions SIOCT and ActOnto.
  • MOAT: the meaning of a tag, defined collaboratively.
  • SemSNI: visits and private messages between members.
  • Also SCOT, RELATIONSHIP, MUTO, AclOnto (access control), InterestOnto (tagging), OntoSNA.
  • Flink (Mika): extracts, aggregates and visualizes social networks with Semantic Web reasoning.
  • LinkProbe: predicts links between members.
  • RDF graph SNA (Opuszko and Ruhland): better link prediction with a semantic similarity measure.
  • SemTagP: detects communities in RDF graphs.
  • OBDA (Calvanese et al.): answers SPARQL over RDF graphs via ontologies.
  • Similar Twitter accounts with Virtuoso and FOAF (Razis et al.).

III.A · Parallel Processing Frameworks

From MapReduce to Spark

MapReduce and Hadoop

Google's data-parallel model; Hadoop is the open-source implementation with HDFS, replicated blocks and fault tolerance. Good for batch, weak for interactive or real-time queries. Twitter's "Who to Follow" runs on Hadoop and Pig.

Spark

Resilient Distributed Datasets keep data in memory and minimize disk access; iterative computations become easy. Spark processes distributed RDF queries more efficiently than Hadoop (HAQWA).

Scripting layers

Pig Latin (Yahoo), HiveQL (Facebook, Netflix) and LINQ for Dryad. SPARQL can be translated to MapReduce jobs; Presto scales better than Hive for RDF.

Spark vs Flink (Marcu et al.): Spark is better for large-scale graph processing, Flink for batch processing.

III.A · Graph Processing Systems · Interactive

Graph Processing Systems

Pegasus turns graph mining into MapReduce jobs; Pregel is vertex-centric and in-memory for iterative graph work, with Giraph as its open-source Hadoop-based version; GraphX extends Spark RDDs; GraphLab uses shared memory. Hover a bar.

  • GraphX is best for iterative operations such as connected components; Urika wins non-iterative ones such as degree distribution (Lim et al.).
  • Hadoop is the worst platform for multi-iteration algorithms (Guo et al.), who also warn that dataset, algorithm and platform diversity make such comparisons hard.

III.B · NoSQL Database Related Technologies

Tabular Stores and Graph Databases

Tabular

HBase (BigTable-like, real-time random access; used by eBay, Yahoo, Facebook), Cassandra (from Facebook; CumulusRDF builds an RDF store on it), Accumulo, MongoDB. For Giraph, HDFS and Hive storage beat HBase (Barnawi et al.).

Graph

Best for complex, densely connected data: nodes link directly, query time does not depend on graph size. RDF triplestores, Neo4j, DEX, InfiniteGraph, HyperGraphDB, FlockDB, OrientDB, Titan.

Which graph DB?

Of Neo4j, FlockDB, OrientDB, InfoGrid and AllegroGraph on SNA workloads, Neo4j performs best (Mathew and Kumar).

Graph query languages

Cypher, Gremlin, GraphQL, GraphLog, QGraph and SNQL: performance on SNA depends heavily on them.

IV · Future Research Directions · Interactive

Open Problems

  • Privacy and trust: social data is personal; preserving privacy is still open.
  • Partial graphs: services do not hand out the whole graph, so algorithms must work on limited data.
  • Profile matching of one user across several networks.
  • Scalable RDF processing: single machines are not enough, and Hadoop was not designed for RDF or iterative graph work, so systems are moving to Spark (Facebook ported from Hive to Spark).

V · Conclusion

Conclusion

  • Computing measures such as degree centrality or clustering coefficient on today's networks requires big data tools.
  • Big data is not only size but diversity: heterogeneous formats and sources, decentralized control.
  • The right platform depends on the task: Hadoop for batch jobs on very large data, Spark for interactive jobs and real-time queries.
  • Ontologies integrate schema-less, heterogeneous data; SPARQL queries must become parallel jobs, and decentralized triplestores are the key to large-scale RDF.

  4. When would you choose a graph database over a tabular NoSQL store?
  5. Name two open research problems in SNA from Section IV.
Show answers
  1. Processing very large data in reasonable time (Big Data) and integrating distinct datasets consistently (Semantic Web).
  2. <:sercan, :knows, :omer>: subject, predicate (the labelled edge) and object.
  3. Each iteration reads its input from HDFS and writes its result back; many map and reduce jobs are needed.
