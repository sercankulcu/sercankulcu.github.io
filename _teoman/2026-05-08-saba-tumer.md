## Saba Tümer ile Teoman

**CNBC-e — 8 May 2026**
**Interview by Saba Tümer**

This interview captures Teoman at a particularly clear late-career turning point.

For years, he had spoken about reducing production.

About no longer feeling the same need to write songs.

About becoming more interested in books, performances and reinterpretations of his existing catalogue.

In May 2026, he states the idea much more directly:

**he does not want to keep writing songs.**

But this is not another dramatic retirement announcement.

Teoman is no longer trying to disappear.

Instead, he is redefining what remaining active can mean.

### No More Need to Write Songs

The conversation reaches the subject almost immediately.

Teoman says the desire to write new songs has largely disappeared.

This is very different from writer's block.

He is not saying:

**I want to write but cannot.**

He is saying:

**I no longer particularly want to.**

That distinction has been developing for years.

In 2012, creative exhaustion frightened him.

In 2018, he was already more interested in reorganizing older songs.

By 2026, the absence of songwriting ambition has become something he can simply accept.

### The Final Album

Yet Teoman has not completely abandoned the idea of one last large musical statement.

He describes a plan for an enormous final album containing around **87 songs**.

The number itself is extraordinary.

This would not function like an ordinary new album.

It would be closer to an archive.

A final organization of the musical Teoman.

### Why 87 Songs?

The idea makes sense when placed beside his later career.

Teoman has spent years:

re-recording,

rearranging,

curating,

releasing anthologies,

allowing other performers to reinterpret songs,

and thinking about which parts of his catalogue should survive.

An 87-song final project would take this tendency to its logical extreme.

Instead of another chapter, it could become a musical collected works.

### The Album as Archive

This changes the definition of an album.

The young Teoman used an album to say:

**This is what I am making now.**

The older Teoman increasingly uses albums to ask:

**What from all of this should remain?**

The direction of attention has reversed.

Creation becomes curation.

### Becoming an “Entertainer”

Around the fifth minute of the conversation, Teoman describes himself increasingly as an **entertainer presenting his past**.

The phrase initially sounds dismissive.

But it contains an important recognition.

He has already created the songs.

Audiences still want to hear them.

Performing those songs can remain meaningful even if he no longer feels compelled to continually add new ones.

### Artist Versus Entertainer

Young musicians often define authenticity through continuous creation.

A real artist must always produce something new.

Teoman increasingly rejects this assumption.

There is another legitimate role:

performing an existing body of work well.

This distinction helps explain why he can imagine ending songwriting without ending concerts.

### The Catalogue Has Become the Show

The songs now possess enough accumulated cultural memory to sustain live performances independently of new releases.

*Paramparça.*

*İstanbul'da Sonbahar.*

*Rüzgar Gülü.*

*Gönülçelen.*

*Aşk Kırıntıları.*

The concert no longer requires a new album as justification.

The catalogue itself is the event.

### A Very Long Career Changes the Problem

This would have been impossible in 1999.

At that point Teoman needed more songs because the catalogue was still being built.

By 2026, scarcity has become abundance.

The problem is no longer:

**Do I have enough material?**

It is:

**What do I do with all the material I already have?**

### Daily Life and Boredom

Saba Tümer asks about Teoman's ordinary life.

Once again, boredom appears almost immediately.

This may be the single most persistent non-musical theme in the entire archive.

Teoman repeatedly structures life to avoid psychological stagnation.

Projects help.

Exercise helps.

Reading helps.

Performances help.

But the underlying vulnerability remains.

### Monotony

The older Teoman does not necessarily crave dramatic chaos.

In fact, his life has become highly organized.

The problem is monotony.

Routine can be useful while simultaneously becoming emotionally flat.

The challenge is therefore to preserve structure without losing curiosity.

### Health

Teoman also talks about health and exercise.

This represents another striking change from the classical rock-star image.

The aging performer has to actively maintain the physical system that makes performance possible.

Exercise is no longer optional lifestyle decoration.

It becomes professional infrastructure.

### The Body as an Instrument

For a singer approaching sixty, the body is part of the production system.

Sleep.

Exercise.

Nutrition.

Stamina.

Recovery.

These concerns are almost the opposite of the youthful mythology built around neglecting the body.

The older rock star survives by becoming disciplined.

### The Lazy Persona Again

Teoman still jokes about laziness.

He even mentions Okan Bayülgen's impressions of him.

But by now the contradiction is well established.

The public Teoman looks lazy.

The actual Teoman maintains:

concert schedules,

exercise,

books,

stage projects,

recording projects,

and long-term creative planning.

The laziness is partly style.

### Generation Z and Alpha

One of the more surprising parts of the interview is Teoman's enthusiasm for younger generations.

He describes himself as a fan of Generation Z and Alpha.

This matters because older celebrities often construct their identity through complaints about younger people.

Teoman does something closer to the opposite.

### Curiosity Instead of Cultural Superiority

He does not insist that his generation represents a lost golden age.

Instead, he is interested in how younger people think differently.

This continues the sociological curiosity visible throughout his interviews.

Different generations are not simply better or worse.

They are products of different conditions.

### Becoming the Older Generation

There is also irony here.

In 2000, Teoman was used by journalists to explain young people.

Now he is observing generations born after his rise to fame.

The cultural position has completely reversed.

He is no longer the young man explaining the present.

He is the older man trying to understand the future.

### Varoluşçuluk 101

The conversation naturally returns to *Varoluşçuluk 101*.

The project combines autobiography, music, humor and existential reflection.

By 2026, it has become one of the clearest examples of Teoman's new professional identity.

It is neither simply concert nor theatre nor stand-up.

The inability to categorize it is part of its meaning.

### The Performer After the Songwriter

This may be the role that replaces new songwriting.

Teoman can continue creating experiences without creating conventional songs.

The creative object shifts.

Instead of composing three minutes of music and lyrics, he can construct an evening.

Stories.

Songs.

Jokes.

Memories.

Characters.

The stage itself becomes the composition.

### Education

Saba Tümer also asks Teoman about his university life.

His Boğaziçi University sociology background and later academic experiences return to the conversation.

These details have appeared throughout the archive, but their meaning changes over time.

Teoman never became the academic he once imagined.

Yet sociological thinking clearly remained part of how he interprets:

people,

generations,

politics,

technology,

and himself.

### Fatherhood

At around eighteen minutes, the discussion turns toward his daughter.

Fatherhood has now been part of Teoman's life long enough that it no longer appears as a surprising contrast with the rock-star persona.

It has become an ordinary part of his identity.

This itself is significant.

### From Hypothesis to Normal Life

In 1999, having a child was a philosophical idea.

Teoman imagined that loving someone moving upward through life while he moved downward might provide meaning.

By 2026, the hypothetical child has long become a real person with an independent relationship to him.

The philosophical experiment became ordinary family life.

### A Father Rather Than “Teoman”

Fatherhood also creates one of the few relationships in which celebrity is largely irrelevant.

To his daughter, he is not primarily the author of *Paramparça*.

He is her father.

That gives him an identity not produced by an audience.

For someone who spent decades struggling with the distinction between person and persona, this is particularly important.

### Cinema and Acting

Teoman's earlier involvement with cinema also returns.

Film was once one of the careers he imagined might replace music.

That never happened.

But cinema remains part of the wider map of his creative identity.

The interview reinforces a broader pattern:

Teoman repeatedly explores forms without needing to permanently become “the filmmaker,” “the actor” or “the writer.”

### The Novel Becomes a Series

A particularly interesting development concerns his novel.

Teoman says there is work toward adapting it as a television series.

This is significant for the evolution of Timur and the fictional universe around *Sayın Bay Rock Yıldızı*.

The character may now move:

from novel,

to digital persona,

to screen adaptation.

### The Fiction Escapes the Book

This was already beginning in 2025 when Timur was being developed as a digital character.

A television adaptation would extend the process.

Teoman's literary creation begins acquiring independent media life.

Once again, a work starts separating from its author.

Exactly what Teoman has always wanted his songs to do.

### Aman Songs

Teoman also mentions a cover project referred to as **Aman Songs**.

This is another example of the later Teoman working through interpretation rather than only new composition.

The musical future increasingly involves:

covers,

re-recordings,

collaborations,

tributes,

and archival reconstruction.

### Reinterpretation Becomes the Main Method

This has been building since *Söz Müzik Teoman* in 2008.

At first, Teoman allowed others to reinterpret him.

Later, he reinterpreted his own catalogue.

Now reinterpretation has become almost an entire career philosophy.

A song does not need to be new to become artistically new again.

### 87 Songs and the Grand Finale

The conversation's most historically important music statement appears around the twenty-eighth minute.

Teoman describes the ambition for a massive final album of approximately 87 songs.

The project has the scale of someone thinking about a closing document rather than a normal commercial release.

### The Last Album as Self-Editing

If completed, such a project would represent Teoman editing his musical biography.

What deserves inclusion?

Which version of each song?

What order?

What should the final image of the catalogue be?

These are archival questions.

Not ordinary album questions.

### Home

Teoman also describes a relatively quiet home life.

This continues the demythologization visible throughout the 2020s.

The rock-star story once depended on movement:

bars,

nightlife,

relationships,

hotels,

concerts.

The older Teoman increasingly describes domestic routine.

### Love

Love and relationships are discussed again, but without the emotional centrality they possessed in early interviews.

This is another measurable chronological change.

The subject still exists.

It simply occupies a smaller percentage of the psychological landscape.

### The Archive and Flattery

Around the final third of the conversation, Teoman discusses documentary material and his dislike of excessive praise.

This fits his broader late-career resistance to monumentalizing himself.

He wants to preserve an archive.

But he does not necessarily want the archive to become worship.

### Anti-Hagiography

This distinction matters.

An archive says:

**preserve the evidence.**

A hagiography says:

**prove the greatness.**

Teoman seems much more interested in the first.

Failures.

Contradictions.

Embarrassment.

Bad decisions.

Boredom.

These belong in the story too.

### Restlessness

The interview closes by returning to the oldest problem.

Restlessness.

Depression.

Melancholy.

After everything else has changed, these remain.

This produces a remarkable circular structure.

Almost thirty years of success have altered:

his profession,

wealth,

relationships,

family,

public status,

age,

and artistic methods.

Yet the underlying psychological tone remains recognizable.

### No Final Cure

By this stage, Teoman no longer seems to expect a cure.

That may be the most important maturity in his later interviews.

The task is not to eliminate melancholy.

It is to prevent melancholy from controlling the whole life.

Exercise.

Projects.

Humor.

Family.

Books.

Stage work.

All are forms of management.

### Why This Interview Matters

This is currently one of the most important 2026 documents because it gives an unusually clear map of Teoman's intended final artistic phase.

It establishes that:

* Teoman no longer feels a strong desire to write new songs,
* he distinguishes songwriting from continuing as a performer,
* he increasingly sees himself as an entertainer presenting an established catalogue,
* he is considering an enormous approximately 87-song final album,
* *Varoluşçuluk 101* represents a new performance model beyond ordinary concerts,
* fatherhood is now an integrated rather than exceptional part of his identity,
* he remains interested in younger generations rather than dismissing them,
* his literary work may expand into television,
* reinterpretation and archival work are replacing continuous new songwriting,
* and melancholy remains a permanent psychological background rather than a problem he expects to solve.

The interview therefore gives us one of the clearest possible definitions of late Teoman:

**the songwriter is approaching an ending, but the artist is not necessarily ending with him.**

The next Teoman may be:

performer,

writer,

curator,

storyteller,

archivist,

or simply the man presenting the songs that survived.
