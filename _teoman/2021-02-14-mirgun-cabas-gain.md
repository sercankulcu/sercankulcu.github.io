## Teoman & Mirgün Cabas Söyleşisi

**GAİN — 14 February 2021**  
**Interview by Mirgün Cabas**

This long-form conversation captures a Teoman who has moved well beyond the central ambitions of his youth.

He has already experienced fame at its highest level.

He has left music.

Returned.

Written about himself.

Rearranged his catalogue.

Become a father.

And gradually reduced the importance of the rock-star identity that once defined much of his public life.

The conversation therefore asks a different question from the early interviews.

Not:

**How do you become successful?**

But:

**What remains after success stops being enough?**

### Fame Did Not Solve the Problem

Teoman speaks openly about reaching a level of fame that he once could only imagine.

Success arrived.

Recognition arrived.

Money arrived.

The career worked.

But the emotional result was not what the younger Teoman had expected.

This is one of the strongest continuities in the entire archive.

In the 2000 Best FM interview, he had already realized that becoming the person his seventeen-year-old self wanted to become did not automatically make him happy.

Twenty years later, he is still examining that discovery.

### The Problem of Saturation

The issue is not failure.

It is saturation.

Once a person has experienced the thing he desired intensely, repetition changes it.

The first successful concert is extraordinary.

The hundredth successful concert cannot produce the same sensation.

The first major public recognition changes life.

Permanent recognition eventually becomes normal.

Success therefore contains a psychological trap.

What once represented fulfillment can become routine.

### Leaving Music

Teoman revisits the period when he withdrew from music.

He explains the decision as connected with this saturation.

The career had stopped giving him the emotional reward it once promised.

He had seen what was at the top.

Continuing upward no longer seemed meaningful because the view was already familiar.

This makes the 2011 departure easier to understand.

It was not simply rebellion against the music industry.

It was a crisis of meaning.

### Going Somewhere Else Does Not Solve It

At one stage, Teoman also spends time abroad.

Physical distance appears to offer the possibility of psychological distance.

But dissatisfaction travels with the person.

Changing the city does not automatically change the internal condition.

This is an old philosophical lesson appearing in a very Teoman-like form:

you can leave the environment without leaving yourself.

### The Self Comes Along

This realization is significant because Teoman had long associated places with emotional states.

Beyoğlu.

Istanbul.

Foreign cities.

Nightlife.

Home.

Yet the later Teoman increasingly recognizes that geography has limits.

A new location can provide novelty.

It cannot permanently repair an internal absence of meaning.

### Fatherhood

One of the most important developments in the conversation is fatherhood.

Teoman now has a daughter.

And unlike fame, albums or professional achievement, this relationship produces a form of meaning that does not seem to expire in the same way.

This is a major change in the chronology.

### The Child He Wanted in 1999

The connection with the 1999 *Aklım Kendimde* interview is extraordinary.

At thirty-two, Teoman had already said that he wanted a child.

His reasoning was highly specific.

Adults constantly invent goals for themselves.

A child, he imagined, would provide something different: a person moving upward in life to love while he himself was moving downward.

More than twenty years later, that hypothetical child exists.

The old idea has become lived experience.

### A Different Kind of Meaning

Fatherhood changes the structure of desire.

Career goals are self-centered even when they involve an audience.

Make an album.

Become successful.

Write something important.

Leave a legacy.

A child creates an external center.

The meaning does not depend entirely on whether Teoman himself is entertained, satisfied or professionally fulfilled.

Someone else matters independently.

### Meaning Beyond the Self

This is especially important for a person who has spent decades describing intense self-observation.

Teoman frequently worries about himself.

His happiness.

His boredom.

His work.

His identity.

Fatherhood creates an emotional structure in which another person's future can become more important than his own mood.

This may be one reason he describes it as more durable than professional satisfaction.

### The Rock Star Becomes a Father

Culturally, the transformation is striking.

The public Teoman of the late 1990s was surrounded by stories about nightlife, women and emotional instability.

The later Teoman discusses responsibility toward his daughter.

The change is not merely biographical.

It alters the meaning of the old persona.

The “rock star” becomes a historical version of himself rather than the only available identity.

### Aging

Teoman is visibly and verbally conscious of aging.

But again, aging is not reduced to the body.

He speaks about limits.

Energy.

What the body can still tolerate.

What kinds of nightlife or professional intensity are no longer sustainable.

The later Teoman has to negotiate with physical reality in ways the younger one did not.

### Accepting Limits

This produces a more pragmatic attitude.

Youth often imagines freedom as the absence of limits.

Age makes limits unavoidable.

The question becomes whether one can build a satisfying life inside them.

Teoman increasingly appears to be doing exactly that.

Less excess.

More selection.

Fewer projects.

More deliberate use of energy.

### Few Plans

One of the most revealing ideas in the conversation concerns plans.

Teoman says he does not have an endless supply of them.

And this worries him.

Plans provide structure.

A project gives the future shape.

When projects disappear, time becomes frighteningly open.

This connects directly with his long-term struggle with boredom.

### Fear of Running Out of Things to Do

The problem is not conventional career anxiety.

Teoman does not need another achievement to prove his professional value.

The fear is existential.

What if the remaining projects are completed?

What then?

This is the later version of the question he first raised after becoming successful:

**What do I want next?**

### Productivity as Survival Strategy

Projects therefore serve more than artistic ambition.

They help organize life.

A book.

An album.

A concert project.

A film.

Something to research.

Something to finish.

Work becomes a way of giving time a direction.

### Discipline

Behind the careless public image lies a surprisingly disciplined person.

Teoman repeatedly describes systems for controlling his day and getting things done.

This has appeared in several later interviews.

The contradiction is important.

The persona suggests disorder.

The functioning adult depends on structure.

### Rock-Star Carelessness as Performance

Some of Teoman's apparent carelessness may therefore be aesthetic.

The cigarette.

The bar.

The tired voice.

The refusal to appear overly enthusiastic.

These become recognizable components of the character.

But behind them is someone capable of managing a long career and completing highly structured projects.

The image and the working method are not the same thing.

### Loving Fate

The conversation also gives the impression of a Teoman who has become more accepting of the life he actually received.

Not completely satisfied.

Not suddenly optimistic.

But less engaged in constant resistance.

This is an important change.

The younger Teoman repeatedly asked why life was not different.

The later Teoman increasingly seems interested in how to use what remains.

### Acceptance Is Not Happiness

This should not be confused with uncomplicated happiness.

Teoman still talks about boredom.

Mortality.

Meaning.

Aging.

Dissatisfaction.

But the tone suggests less expectation that these problems can be permanently solved.

Acceptance means living with them more effectively.

### The Search for the Next High Has Ended

Young Teoman could imagine that another achievement might finally produce the desired state.

Another album.

Another success.

Another relationship.

Another city.

The later Teoman has enough evidence to know that this logic does not work indefinitely.

There is always adaptation.

The extraordinary becomes normal.

The search for a permanent emotional high therefore has to be abandoned.

### Ordinary Things Become More Important

Once grand success loses its magical power, ordinary structures become more meaningful.

A child.

Daily routine.

Work.

Books.

Friends.

Small projects.

This would have sounded almost absurd beside the mythology of the young rock star.

But it may represent one of the largest philosophical changes in Teoman's life.

### Success as Information

Teoman's career has effectively allowed him to test a hypothesis.

Hypothesis:

**If I become successful enough, I will become satisfied.**

Result:

Not permanently.

This is valuable information.

The later Teoman can stop wasting energy trying to prove the same hypothesis again.

### What Success Was Good For

That does not mean success was meaningless.

It gave him freedom.

Financial security.

Cultural influence.

The ability to choose projects.

The ability to appear less frequently in the media.

And eventually the ability to organize his life according to personal priorities.

Success did not provide permanent happiness.

But it provided autonomy.

### Autonomy May Be the Real Prize

This may be the most defensible version of Teoman's relationship with fame.

Fame itself becomes tiring.

Recognition becomes normal.

But the independence created by a successful catalogue remains useful.

The artist can now say no.

That is a form of success with lasting value.

### The Live Performance

The GAİN conversation also included a special live performance.

Teoman performed:

- *Onyedi*
- *Kişisel Bir Şey*
- *İnsanlar*
- *Rüzgar Gülü*
- *Hayal Meyal*

The selection is interesting.

It spans different periods rather than functioning as promotion for one current release.

Again, Teoman is increasingly operating from the perspective of a catalogue rather than a conventional album cycle.

### Why This Interview Matters

This is one of the most important late-period conversations because several themes that began decades earlier finally connect.

The 1999 Teoman wanted a child because he imagined that loving someone moving upward through life might provide a natural purpose.

The 2000 Teoman discovered that professional success did not automatically create happiness.

The 2011 Teoman left music after saturation and loss of meaning.

The 2018 Teoman began organizing his past and worrying about what would remain.

The 2021 Teoman can now evaluate all of those experiments.

Fame was real.

Success was real.

Withdrawal was real.

Going elsewhere was real.

And fatherhood is real.

Among them, fatherhood appears to offer something structurally different.

It does not require Teoman to invent another version of himself to chase.

The center is somewhere else.

This may be one of the deepest transformations in the archive.

The young Teoman spent decades constructing Teoman.

The older Teoman gradually discovers that meaning may depend on thinking less about Teoman.
