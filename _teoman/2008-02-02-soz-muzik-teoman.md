## Söz Müzik Teoman

**PowerTürk — 2 February 2008**

This interview documents an unusual moment in Teoman's career.

Instead of releasing another conventional Teoman album, he gives his songs to other musicians.

The result is *Söz Müzik Teoman*, a project in which artists from very different musical backgrounds reinterpret songs that had already become strongly associated with Teoman's own voice.

This creates an interesting test.

If the singer changes, the arrangement changes and even the genre changes, what remains of a Teoman song?

### The Project Was Originally a Duet Album

The project did not begin in its final form.

Teoman explains that his record company initially proposed a duet album.

He liked the idea.

The plan was to combine older songs with new material and perform them together with other artists.

But something changed during preparation.

As Teoman began assigning songs to different musicians, he noticed that some of the interpretations worked better without him.

The songs no longer needed Teoman's voice.

That realization changed the entire project.

### Removing Himself

This decision is especially interesting considering Teoman's earlier concern with artistic control.

In 1996, control meant remaining at the center of his own work.

By 2008, he is confident enough to disappear from much of it.

The songs can leave him.

Other singers can transform them.

They may even improve them in unexpected ways.

That requires a different kind of confidence.

The songwriter no longer has to protect the definitive version.

### Songs as Material

Teoman uses a revealing metaphor.

He describes the songs almost as pieces of fabric.

Each piece can be taken somewhere else.

A different performer can reshape it.

The original arrangement therefore does not define the limits of the song.

What matters is whether the underlying material survives transformation.

This is one of the clearest statements in the archive about Teoman's understanding of songwriting.

A song is larger than its first recording.

### Different Genres

The album deliberately includes artists from different musical worlds.

Teoman says he does not believe musical genres should be ranked against one another.

One genre is not inherently superior to another.

This continues a position that had gradually developed throughout his earlier interviews.

The young Teoman often defined himself partly in relation to rock.

The older Teoman increasingly distrusts genre hierarchies.

### Even Arabesque

Teoman gives an especially useful hypothetical example.

If an arabesque singer took one of his songs and transformed it completely, he says he would enjoy the result.

Why?

Because the song would be able to travel somewhere Teoman himself could not take it.

This is almost the opposite of artistic possessiveness.

The reinterpretation becomes valuable precisely because it escapes the original author's capabilities.

### The Song Has Its Own Life

This introduces an important new idea in the chronology.

A song can become independent of its creator.

The songwriter produces the initial object.

But once the song exists, another performer can discover possibilities that the writer did not see.

This resembles what Teoman had previously said about audiences giving songs new meanings.

Now the same principle is extended to musicians.

The work continues evolving after authorship.

### Choosing the Performers

Teoman emphasizes that the project was not random.

He selected artists according to his own musical taste.

The diversity of the record therefore does not mean abandoning personal judgment.

There is still a curator behind the project.

He decides which voices might reveal something different in particular songs.

Control has not disappeared.

It has changed form.

Instead of controlling every performance, Teoman controls the conditions under which reinterpretation occurs.

### A Wide Musical Spectrum

The resulting album contains an unusually broad group of performers.

Among them are:

- Sezen Aksu,
- Yalın,
- Candan Erçetin,
- Yaşar,
- Nil Karaibrahimgil,
- Emre Aydın,
- İzel,
- Mirkelam,
- Yavuz Bingöl,
- Harun Tekin,
- Rashit,
- and Hayko Cepkin.

The list itself demonstrates Teoman's point.

These musicians do not belong neatly to a single genre or generation.

The common element is not style.

It is the songs.

### Paramparça Without Teoman

Perhaps the strongest example is *Paramparça* performed by Sezen Aksu.

By 2008, the song was already inseparable from Teoman's public identity.

Giving such a defining song to another major singer therefore carries symbolic weight.

If *Paramparça* can survive without Teoman's voice, then the composition possesses an identity independent of its original recording.

This is exactly what a songwriter hoping for permanence should want.

### From Performer to Songwriter

The project also changes what the public is being asked to notice.

Ordinarily, Teoman is experienced as a complete package:

voice,

appearance,

persona,

performance,

lyrics,

melody.

*Söz Müzik Teoman* deliberately removes much of that package.

What remains is **söz ve müzik**.

Words and music.

The project therefore foregrounds Teoman the songwriter rather than Teoman the rock star.

### The Importance of Authorship

This connects strongly with the 2007 Ayşe Arman interview.

There, Teoman said he wanted his songs to become classics and survive beyond him.

*Söz Müzik Teoman* almost functions as an experiment in that possibility.

Can the songs exist without their author standing in front of them?

The answer is yes.

Different voices reveal different structures hidden inside them.

### A Song Is Not Its Arrangement

One reason this works is Teoman's distinction between composition and arrangement.

The arrangement can change radically.

Tempo can change.

Instrumentation can change.

Genre can change.

Even the emotional tone can change.

Yet something recognizable remains.

That remaining structure is the song.

This suggests that Teoman understands songwriting at a relatively abstract level.

The original recording is one realization of the composition, not the composition itself.

### No Genre Is Superior

The interview's rejection of musical hierarchy deserves emphasis.

Teoman says directly that he does not see one musical genre as inherently better than another.

This is a substantial evolution from cultural attitudes often associated with Turkish rock in the 1990s.

Rock could easily be presented as more serious, authentic or intellectually respectable than mainstream pop or arabesque.

Teoman increasingly refuses that hierarchy.

A genre is a language.

What matters is what someone manages to say with it.

### Identity Through Difference

Paradoxically, hearing other singers perform Teoman's songs may make Teoman's own identity clearer.

When his voice disappears, listeners can separate the composition from the performance.

They can hear which elements were always embedded in the writing and which belonged specifically to Teoman's interpretation.

Difference becomes a tool for understanding identity.

### The Second Volume

Teoman also explains that the project was planned in two parts.

A second *Söz Müzik Teoman* album was expected later in the year.

This reveals that he did not see the first release as a novelty experiment.

The project was conceived as something substantial enough to continue.

It represented a deliberate re-examination of his catalogue.

### Looking Back at the Catalogue

This is another sign of Teoman entering a different stage of his career.

A young artist usually looks forward.

The central questions concern the next song, the next album and the next opportunity.

By 2008, Teoman has accumulated enough material to begin looking backward.

The catalogue itself has become an object worth examining.

Other musicians can now enter it.

### Reinterpretation as Criticism

A cover version is also a form of interpretation.

When another musician changes the rhythm, arrangement or emotional tone of a Teoman song, they are effectively making an argument about what the song contains.

They highlight one possibility and suppress another.

In this sense, *Söz Müzik Teoman* is almost a collection of musical essays about Teoman's writing.

Each performer answers the question differently:

**What kind of song is this really?**

### Why This Interview Matters

This interview marks an important development in Teoman's understanding of authorship.

Earlier Teoman emphasized control.

The 2008 Teoman is interested in what happens when control is deliberately released.

Several ideas become clear:

- a song is larger than its original recording,
- another performer can discover possibilities the songwriter cannot,
- genre does not determine artistic value,
- reinterpretation can strengthen rather than threaten authorship,
- Teoman increasingly identifies himself as a songwriter,
- and his catalogue has become substantial enough to exist independently of his public persona.

The transition is striking.

In 1996, Teoman wanted to ensure that his songs remained his.

In 2008, he can give them away temporarily and watch what other people do with them.

That does not weaken his authorship.

In a strange way, it proves it.

A song that can survive radical transformation may have a stronger identity than one that depends entirely on its original performer.

*Söz Müzik Teoman* therefore asks a question that had already appeared implicitly in the 2007 interview:

**Can Teoman's songs live without Teoman?**

The project suggests that they can.
