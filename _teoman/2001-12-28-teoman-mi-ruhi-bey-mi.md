## Teoman mı? Ruhi Bey mi?

**Milliyet — 28 December 2001**  
**Interview by Mefaret Aktaş**

With *Gönülçelen*, Teoman's songwriting begins to look increasingly like fiction.

Earlier albums had already contained recurring images, private details and characters. But in this interview, Teoman explains something more systematic: several songs can be understood as episodes in the life of a single fictional figure.

His name is **Ruhi**.

Ruhi is Teoman's alter ego.

But he is also connected to Teoman's father.

This makes the character one of the most interesting inventions in Teoman's work.

### Who Is Ruhi?

Teoman explains that his father's real name was Hasan Basri, but in Giresun he was known by the nickname **Ruhi**.

Nicknames, he says, could become so dominant that people's actual names were almost forgotten.

His father was apparently considered an emotional person, and the name Ruhi became associated with him.

Teoman later takes that name and gives it to a fictional character.

This is already interesting.

The name comes from a father Teoman barely knew, but the character becomes an alternative version of Teoman himself.

### An Alter Ego

Teoman describes Ruhi directly:

> “Ruhi karakteri benim alter egom.”

Ruhi allows him to write autobiographically without being trapped by literal autobiography.

A feeling may belong to Teoman.

A memory may resemble Teoman's.

A fear may clearly come from Teoman.

But once those elements are given to Ruhi, they enter fiction.

This creates freedom.

Teoman can exaggerate, rearrange time, invent events and combine different experiences while preserving emotional truth.

### One Character Across Several Songs

Ruhi does not belong to only one song.

Teoman imagines several songs as different moments in the same person's life.

The child in *İstasyon İnsanları* can be understood as a young Ruhi.

The womanizer who eventually dies in *Zamparanın Ölümü* can be an older version of the same person.

Different tracks therefore become episodes in a larger biography.

This extends Teoman's earlier idea that an album should resemble a novel.

By *Gönülçelen*, the principle has become more concrete.

The album does not merely possess a shared atmosphere.

It begins to contain something close to a recurring protagonist.

### A Child Who Is Already Nostalgic

*İstasyon İnsanları* contains a deliberate temporal joke.

Ruhi appears to be only eight or ten years old.

Yet he remembers an earlier Istanbul with nostalgia.

He remembers Cennet Plajı before it became a parking area.

Teoman recognizes the absurdity.

How much past can an eight-year-old possibly have?

That impossibility is part of the character.

Ruhi seems almost born nostalgic.

### Nostalgia as Personality

This detail tells us something larger about Teoman's writing.

Nostalgia does not always require old age.

It can be a way of seeing.

A person can experience the present as if it were already disappearing.

Ruhi looks backward even while still young.

That gives him a peculiar relationship with time.

He does not simply live.

He watches life becoming memory.

### İstanbul'da Sonbahar

Teoman describes *İstanbul'da Sonbahar* as the story of roughly ten minutes.

The narrator thinks about Istanbul.

The season triggers childhood memories.

Then another person enters his mind — perhaps a lover, perhaps a friend.

He wishes that person were there.

The structure is almost cinematic.

One thought produces another.

The city produces memory.

Memory produces absence.

Absence produces desire.

The song becomes less a conventional narrative than a reconstruction of how the mind moves.

### Istanbul as Emotional Geography

By this stage, Istanbul has become more than a setting in Teoman's work.

Places carry emotional memory.

Beyoğlu.

Cennet Plajı.

Stations.

Streets.

Autumn.

The city becomes a map through which private history is organized.

This continues a pattern already visible in the 1999 interviews, when Teoman described Beyoğlu as one of the places feeding his imagination.

### “Her Şey Ölümü Hatırlatır”

Death occupies a major place in the interview.

Teoman says:

> “Bana her şey ölümü hatırlatır.”

The statement appears while he explains *Doktor*.

The song includes the image of an internal emptiness that cannot be filled.

Teoman reveals that this idea came partly from something a doctor once told him.

The lyric therefore transforms a real encounter into fictional material.

Again, autobiography enters the song indirectly.

### Emptiness

The doctor's observation is especially important because it anticipates a theme that will remain central in later Teoman interviews.

Achievement does not necessarily remove emptiness.

Relationships do not necessarily remove it.

Popularity does not remove it.

New experiences may temporarily occupy it, but the underlying structure remains.

This connects directly with the Best FM interview from the previous year, where Teoman had already explained that achieving the dreams of his younger self did not automatically produce happiness.

### Death Is No Longer Abstract

Teoman's fear of death is not philosophical in an abstract sense.

By this point, several people around him have died.

He mentions friends lost through accidents involving alcohol.

He also talks about Yavuz Çetin.

These deaths make mortality immediate.

Death is not something belonging to old people at the distant end of life.

It is already occurring inside his social circle.

### Yavuz Çetin

Teoman speaks with striking bluntness about Yavuz Çetin's death.

Rather than presenting the expected public reaction, he says that he was not surprised because Çetin had been suffering intensely.

This is one of the passages that should be read with care.

It tells us more about Teoman's perception of his friend's suffering than it does about suicide in general.

What matters for the archive is how close death had become to Teoman's emotional world by 2001.

### Happy-Sad

When explaining *Gönülçelen*, Teoman uses a particularly useful expression:

**happy-sad**.

The rhythm can move quickly while the vocal line remains behind it.

The result is neither simply joyful nor simply melancholic.

It contains both.

This is closely connected with the irony Teoman had described in earlier interviews.

He does not want sadness to remain emotionally pure.

A small contradiction makes it more interesting.

### Irony Against Sentimentality

The same strategy appears in *Doktor*.

Teoman deliberately gives the arrangement an almost Eurovision-like quality so that the song will not become trapped in total depression.

The musical form introduces distance from the words.

This tells us something important about his aesthetic.

Teoman does not necessarily distrust sadness.

He distrusts **uninterrupted sadness**.

Irony protects melancholy from sentimentality.

### Gönülçelen

The album title comes from Teoman's favorite book: J. D. Salinger's *The Catcher in the Rye*, published in Turkish as *Gönülçelen*.

The choice is revealing.

Salinger's novel is deeply concerned with alienation, adolescence, authenticity and difficulty connecting with the adult world.

These themes fit naturally into Teoman's existing interests.

The reference also confirms how strongly literature had entered his understanding of songwriting.

### Learning to Write

Teoman says that he has been reading books about writing songs, screenplays and novels.

This is an important shift.

Earlier Teoman often described songwriting as observation combined with instinct.

By 2001, he is deliberately studying narrative technique.

He wants to become a better writer.

The result can be seen in the increasing use of characters, dialogue and connected stories.

### From Songwriter to Storyteller

Ruhi is therefore more than an interesting character.

He represents a change in Teoman's ambitions.

A songwriter can write an isolated emotional scene.

A storyteller needs characters who can exist beyond one scene.

Ruhi gives Teoman that possibility.

He even says that stories he had already written could now use Ruhi as their main character.

The fictional world can expand beyond music.

### Soluk Soluğa

The origin of *Soluk Soluğa* demonstrates Teoman's observational method.

He wakes after a night of drinking.

There is a faint smell of alcohol.

Clothes are scattered around the room.

Instead of simply cleaning the room and continuing with the day, he sees an image worth recording.

He takes out his notebook and writes.

This is exactly the process described in earlier interviews:

ordinary detail becomes artistic material.

### Zamparanın Ölümü

Teoman says the womanizer was always intended to die.

The later song completes a story begun earlier.

But he also uses the track to experiment formally.

There is relatively little conventional melody.

The words dominate.

This reveals another ambition:

Teoman wants listeners to accept increasingly text-heavy songs.

He wants language itself to carry musical attention.

### Admiration and Influence

The interview also reveals some of Teoman's musical references.

He mentions MFÖ, Fikret Kızılok and Bülent Ortaçgil among Turkish writers he respects.

He praises Göksel's writing particularly strongly.

He also refers to Serge Gainsbourg while discussing the kind of text-centered songwriting that interests him.

These references help place Teoman's own ambitions.

He wants popular songs, but he also wants words capable of sustaining close attention.

### Fiction and False Biography

One of the strangest parts of the interview concerns a résumé Teoman had written years earlier.

The document circulated online as if it were genuine biography.

Teoman explains that much of it had deliberately been constructed as a trap.

Facts were mixed with fictional references.

Readers who failed to recognize those references could mistake fiction for autobiography.

This is almost a miniature version of the problem surrounding Teoman's songs.

Which parts are real?

Which parts are borrowed?

Which parts are fictional?

The boundary is intentionally unstable.

### Gönülçelen and Betrayal

The title song also comes from Teoman's life, but he changes the point of view.

Instead of simply narrating his own experience, he tells the relationship from the other side.

This is another important development.

Autobiography becomes raw material rather than final form.

Even when an event belongs to his life, perspective can be rearranged.

### Why This Interview Matters

This is one of the most important early interviews in the archive because it reveals Teoman's movement from **songwriter toward storyteller**.

Several long-term themes come together:

- Ruhi as an alter ego,
- the memory of his father,
- recurring characters across songs,
- nostalgia,
- Istanbul as emotional geography,
- fear of death,
- internal emptiness,
- happy-sad emotional ambiguity,
- irony as protection from sentimentality,
- literature,
- deliberate study of writing,
- autobiographical material transformed through fiction,
- and increasing interest in text-heavy songs.

The earlier *Onyedi* interviews showed Teoman thinking of an album as a novel.

With *Gönülçelen*, that idea becomes more literal.

There is now a character.

There is a history.

There are different ages of the same person.

There are recurring emotional problems.

There may even be future adventures.

The important question in the title is therefore impossible to answer cleanly.

**Is Ruhi Teoman?**

Partly.

**Is Teoman Ruhi?**

Also partly.

That ambiguity is exactly what makes the character useful.

Ruhi allows Teoman to tell the truth without having to tell only facts.
