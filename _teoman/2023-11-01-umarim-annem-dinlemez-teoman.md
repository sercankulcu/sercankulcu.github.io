## Umarım Annem Dinlemez — Teoman

**Umarım Annem Dinlemez — November 2023**  
**Interview by Tuluğ Özlü**

This conversation shows a different side of Teoman.

There is less discussion of albums, artistic legacy or Turkish politics.

Instead, Tuluğ Özlü moves directly into subjects that had surrounded Teoman's public image for decades:

relationships,

sex,

fidelity,

dating,

nightlife,

and the mythology of the rock star.

The difference is that Teoman is now old enough to look back at that mythology almost from outside.

### The Rock Star as a Former Lifestyle

Teoman does not reject the phrase “rock star.”

In fact, he uses it himself.

When asked about memorable nights from the more intense periods of touring and nightlife, he responds with characteristic confidence:

> “Ben bir rock yıldızıyım.”

The line is humorous.

But it also acknowledges something real.

For years, Teoman actually lived within the social conditions associated with that identity:

concerts,

travel,

bars,

attention,

relationships,

and access to experiences unavailable to most people.

### No Apology for the Past

One striking aspect of the conversation is the absence of moral revisionism.

Teoman does not reinterpret his younger life as a mistake simply because he is older.

He does not say:

“I should never have lived that way.”

Nor does he try to present every experience as profound.

Some things were simply pleasurable.

Some nights were good.

Some relationships were temporary.

The past does not require either glorification or repentance.

### Sex Without Mythology

This is particularly visible when sex becomes the subject.

The public image around Teoman often treated sexuality as something glamorous, dangerous or rebellious.

The older Teoman speaks about it much more practically.

When asked where he prefers sex, he gives an almost comically ordinary answer:

> “Evde. Pratik ya.”

The response destroys much of the mythology surrounding the question.

There is no fantasy of hotel rooms, backstage areas or exotic locations.

Convenience wins.

### The Aging of Desire

This practical attitude is also a sign of age.

Teoman admits that he behaved differently when younger.

Experiences that once seemed exciting may now simply seem inconvenient.

The body changes.

Energy changes.

Risk tolerance changes.

The meaning of novelty changes.

Sex remains part of life, but it no longer has to function as evidence that someone is living intensely.

### From Excess to Control

Teoman says he now tries to behave more carefully.

That does not mean complete transformation.

He still identifies with the person who lived the earlier life.

But the relationship with excess has changed.

Young Teoman often seemed interested in testing limits.

Older Teoman increasingly seems interested in managing them.

This continues a pattern visible across his later interviews:

**less expansion, more selection.**

### Dating

One of the funniest parts of the conversation concerns modern dating.

Teoman says he finds conventional dates boring.

He has tried them only a few times and does not really know what to talk about.

This is revealing precisely because his public reputation would suggest the opposite.

The famous womanizer describes himself as inexperienced in one of the most ordinary structures of modern romantic life.

### Attraction Without Courtship

This suggests that Teoman's romantic history did not necessarily follow conventional courtship.

His relationships often emerged from environments in which he already knew people.

Music.

Nightlife.

Social circles.

Shared acquaintances.

The formal process of meeting a stranger for a scheduled date may therefore feel artificial to him.

Again, public mythology and ordinary social competence are not the same thing.

### Fidelity

The most provocative section concerns fidelity.

Teoman says he never naturally thought in terms of:

**“I have a girlfriend, therefore I must be faithful.”**

This statement attracted considerable media attention after the podcast was released.

But in the context of Teoman's earlier interviews, it is not completely surprising.

He has always been skeptical of conventional relationship structures.

### Relationships Without Conventional Morality

The important distinction is between emotional attachment and conventional rules.

Teoman does not necessarily deny love.

He has spoken many times about relationships that mattered deeply.

But he does not automatically treat exclusivity as the defining proof of that attachment.

This places him outside conventional romantic morality.

Whether one agrees with that position or not, it is consistent with the broader individualism visible throughout his archive.

### Freedom and Consequences

However, the later Teoman is also more conscious of consequences.

Freedom does not occur in a vacuum.

Other people can be hurt.

Jealousy exists.

Expectations exist.

Age has therefore not necessarily transformed his basic instinct toward freedom.

It has made him more conscious of what that freedom costs.

### The Old Self-Awareness Problem

This brings us back to one of the oldest Teoman themes.

He understands his own behavior remarkably well.

He can explain why relationships become difficult.

He can identify selfishness.

He can describe emotional distance.

But understanding does not automatically produce conventional behavior.

Again:

**self-awareness is not self-correction.**

This was already visible in 1999.

It remains visible more than twenty years later.

### No Great Romantic Conversion

The later Teoman has not transformed into a sentimental defender of monogamous romance.

That continuity matters.

Public figures often rewrite their younger selves when aging.

They present maturity as complete ideological conversion.

Teoman does not.

He allows contradiction to survive.

### Intimacy and Loneliness

Behind the humor, however, there is a deeper connection with loneliness.

Sex and relationships can temporarily reduce isolation.

Teoman had already described this very clearly in 2018.

Another person can function as temporary relief.

But intimacy does not automatically solve the underlying problem.

The body can be close while the person remains psychologically separate.

### Sex as Contact

This may be the most useful way to connect the conversation with earlier Teoman.

Sex is not merely pleasure.

It is also contact.

For someone whose biography repeatedly returns to loneliness, contact matters.

But temporary closeness and permanent belonging are not the same thing.

Teoman seems increasingly aware of that distinction.

### The Media Chooses Sex

The public reaction to the podcast is also revealing.

The conversation contained many statements.

Yet news coverage concentrated heavily on Teoman's sexual remarks.

Headlines emphasized:

where he prefers having sex,

his attitude toward fidelity,

his fantasies,

and his nightlife.

This demonstrates a phenomenon Teoman has complained about for decades.

A long conversation becomes reduced to the most commercially provocative sentence.

### The Public Teoman Returns

This is almost ironic.

Teoman spends years trying to widen the public understanding of who he is.

Writer.

Father.

Reader.

Songwriter.

Older man.

Social observer.

Then one podcast comment about sex immediately reconstructs the familiar character:

**Teoman the womanizer.**

The media persona is extremely durable.

### A Persona He Also Uses

But Teoman is not merely a victim of this process.

He also plays with the image.

Saying “I am a rock star” is a deliberate invocation of the mythology.

He understands that the character is useful.

It produces humor.

Recognition.

Narrative.

Teoman can both criticize the public persona and consciously perform it.

### Playing Teoman

This is one of the most interesting characteristics of the later period.

Young Teoman was trapped inside the character.

Older Teoman can sometimes take it out and use it.

The difference is control.

The persona has become a costume rather than a complete identity.

### No Shame, No Heroism

The tone of the conversation is therefore distinctive.

Teoman does not seem ashamed of the sexually adventurous life associated with his younger years.

But he also does not present it as heroic.

There is very little “sex, drugs and rock'n'roll” mythology in the grand sense.

Much of it is treated as ordinary biography.

Things happened.

They were enjoyable.

Then life changed.

### Aging Without Moral Superiority

This is another useful feature of Teoman's attitude toward aging.

He does not claim that the older person is morally superior to the younger one.

The younger Teoman wanted certain experiences.

The older Teoman wants different things.

Change does not necessarily mean that the earlier desires were invalid.

People move through different structures of desire.

### Practicality Wins

Perhaps nothing captures this better than the answer about preferring home.

The spectacular rock-star mythology ends in domestic practicality.

That is not necessarily decline.

It may simply be what happens when experience removes the need to prove anything.

The young person needs novelty partly because novelty confirms identity.

The older person already knows who he was.

### Why This Interview Matters

At first glance, this podcast may seem less significant than the long conversations about politics, art or mortality.

But it fills an important gap in the archive.

For decades, Teoman's public identity was strongly shaped by relationships, sexuality and nightlife.

Here, the older Teoman finally discusses that territory directly and without much defensive seriousness.

Several themes become clear:

- he does not regret the basic structure of his younger lifestyle,
- age has made him more practical and controlled,
- conventional dating does not suit him,
- exclusivity has never been an instinctive foundation of his relationships,
- sexuality and emotional attachment are not identical,
- self-awareness still does not necessarily produce conventional behavior,
- the media remains fascinated by the womanizer persona,
- and Teoman himself can now consciously play with that persona.

The most interesting transformation is therefore not moral.

It is **distance**.

The younger Teoman had to live inside the rock-star story.

The older Teoman can tell stories about the man who lived it.

And occasionally, with a smile, he can still say:

**“Ben bir rock yıldızıyım.”**
