## On Yedi Yaş Yaşamda Çok Önemli

**Cumhuriyet Gazetesi — 10 August 2000**  
**Interview by Barış Behramoğlu**

This interview is one of the clearest explanations Teoman gave during the *Onyedi* period about how he writes songs and how he constructs an album.

The conversation is relatively focused.

Rather than moving deeply into fame or private life, it stays close to the creative process: notes, words, melodies, album structure, adolescence and sound.

It therefore works particularly well as a companion to the Best FM interview from the same period.

### An Album Begins Before the Previous One Ends

Teoman explains that he does not think about albums as isolated projects.

While one record is ending, the next one is already beginning in his mind.

He had been thinking about *Onyedi* since 1998.

Not necessarily composing songs continuously, but collecting ideas.

For him, the end of one album leaves behind what he calls a kind of residue.

That residue affects the next work.

This suggests a continuous creative process rather than a sequence of completely separate projects.

### Writing Never Really Stops

Teoman makes an important distinction between music and words.

There may be periods when he does not compose.

But writing continues.

He records small observations, fragments of sentences, metaphors, references and combinations of only a few words.

These pieces do not necessarily become songs immediately.

They are stored.

Later they may enter a lyric when the right context appears.

This gives us a useful picture of his method:

**songs are partly assembled from a long-term archive of language.**

### Words Before Music

This statement also confirms something already visible in the 1996 interview.

Teoman's relationship with words is more continuous than his relationship with music.

Songwriting is therefore not simply a melody followed by lyrics.

Language itself is one of his primary materials.

He watches for phrases.

Images.

Small contradictions.

References.

Things worth remembering.

A song may begin years before it is completed because some of its language has already been collected.

### A Song and an Album Are Different Things

One of the strongest statements in the interview concerns the difference between writing a song and writing an album.

A good melody and a few strong lines may produce a song.

An album requires something larger.

Teoman compares it to a novel.

The question becomes:

**What should ten songs leave behind when they are heard together?**

That final emotional residue matters as much as the individual tracks.

This idea appears repeatedly in Teoman's 2000 interviews and seems central to how he understood *Onyedi*.

### The Album as a Novel

The novel comparison is especially useful.

Individual songs can be thought of as scenes or chapters.

They may contain different characters and situations.

But they must participate in a larger atmosphere.

The listener should leave the album with a feeling that cannot be reduced to any one track.

This explains why recurring themes, characters and images are so important in Teoman's records.

He is interested not only in memorable songs.

He is interested in creating a world around them.

### Growth After *O*

When asked how *Onyedi* differs from his previous work, Teoman gives a simple answer.

He has grown.

He considers himself a better songwriter and a more experienced musician than he was during *O*.

But he does not describe this as a complete transformation.

The underlying person remains the same.

The direction is continuous.

This is useful when considering Teoman's artistic development.

He does not imagine each album as a reinvention.

The change is evolutionary rather than revolutionary.

### Why Seventeen?

The title *Onyedi* comes from Teoman's interest in the age of seventeen.

He describes it as a particularly unstable moment.

Adolescence is nearly over.

Adulthood has not properly begun.

You may feel mature, but society does not yet fully recognize you as an adult.

You want independence, but you probably have little money.

You feel confidence and insecurity at the same time.

You believe a new life is beginning without knowing exactly what that life will be.

For Teoman, this contradiction makes seventeen psychologically important.

### Between Childhood and Adulthood

The age therefore represents a threshold.

A seventeen-year-old is not simply young.

He or she exists between categories.

This uncertainty is important because much of *Onyedi* is concerned with transitions:

youth and age,

hope and disappointment,

love and loneliness,

confidence and fear.

The title is not merely autobiographical nostalgia.

It represents a condition of being unfinished.

### Şebnem Ferah and the Duet

Teoman also discusses his duet with Şebnem Ferah.

The idea of recording a duet had existed since his first album.

Two singers allow something that a single narrator cannot easily create:

a relationship can be represented directly through two voices.

That makes the duet more than a commercial collaboration.

It becomes a storytelling device.

The interaction between voices can create a dramatic context inside the song.

### Reality in Lyrics

When asked about his lyric style, Teoman explains that he wants listeners to recognize something familiar.

He wants them to feel:

**this really happens.**

That desire for recognition is central.

He is not primarily interested in listeners admiring an original metaphor simply because it sounds clever.

The lyric should trigger emotion.

To strengthen the sense of reality, he adds small private details.

Some are autobiographical.

Others merely feel intimate enough to make the fictional world believable.

### Mystery Without Obscurity

At the same time, Teoman does not want everything to be completely transparent.

A small amount of mystery is useful.

Private references or unexplained details create depth.

The listener recognizes the emotional situation without necessarily understanding every autobiographical source behind it.

This balance helps explain an important quality of Teoman's songs:

they often feel personal without becoming straightforward diary entries.

### A Recognizable Voice

Teoman also wants authorship to be recognizable.

If two songs are placed next to one another, the listener should be able to identify which one he wrote.

This is a demanding artistic goal.

It means style should exist beyond voice or arrangement.

The writing itself should contain a recognizable fingerprint.

Small images, sentence structures, references and emotional perspectives should make a Teoman song identifiable even before the singer is considered.

### Imperfection as Authenticity

The discussion of the album's sound is particularly revealing.

Teoman wanted *Onyedi* to sound as if it had been recorded in an earlier period.

To achieve this, the musicians deliberately used old guitars and amplifiers.

Some of the equipment was technically inferior.

That was partly the point.

Modern perfection was not the desired aesthetic.

### Leaving the Mistakes

Teoman says that small mistakes were intentionally left in the recordings.

He did not want every imperfection cut away.

The goal was to preserve the spirit and atmosphere of the songs.

This reveals a broader principle:

**technical perfection can sometimes reduce emotional realism.**

A recording that is completely polished may sound less human.

The imperfections can become evidence of the performance itself.

### Authenticity Over Cleanliness

This preference connects naturally with his lyric writing.

In both cases, Teoman values the impression of reality.

In lyrics, small ordinary details make emotions believable.

In recording, small imperfections make performances believable.

The same aesthetic principle therefore operates in two different areas:

**leave enough irregularity for the human being to remain visible.**

### Why This Interview Matters

This interview is especially valuable because it provides a concise description of Teoman's creative method around *Onyedi*.

The most important ideas are:

- album ideas continue from one record into the next,
- writing is a continuous process of collecting fragments,
- words occupy a particularly important place in his creative life,
- an album must be designed as something larger than its individual songs,
- seventeen represents psychological uncertainty rather than simple nostalgia,
- lyrics should feel recognizable and real,
- private details create authenticity,
- an artist should develop a recognizable writing voice,
- and imperfections can improve rather than damage a recording.

The interview also strengthens a pattern already visible in the archive.

Teoman repeatedly resists perfection when perfection threatens personality.

He wants songs to sound authored.

He wants recordings to sound performed.

He wants albums to feel like coherent worlds.

By 2000, he is no longer merely explaining what he writes.

He is explaining **how he thinks about making art**.
