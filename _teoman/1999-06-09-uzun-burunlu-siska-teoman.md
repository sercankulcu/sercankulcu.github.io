## Uzun Burunlu Sıska Teoman

**Hürriyet Kelebek — 9 June 1999**

By the summer of 1999, Teoman was no longer the unknown musician introduced to newspaper readers only a few years earlier.

His concerts were crowded. His songs were being memorized. His clothes and manner were attracting attention. His relationships had begun to appear in the press.

The central question of the interview is therefore very different from the one asked in 1996.

The question is no longer whether Teoman will become famous.

It is how he understands the fact that he suddenly has.

### Sudden Fame

The article presents Teoman as one of the rapidly rising figures of Turkish popular music.

When asked about his sudden success, his answer is characteristically understated:

> “Belki yokluktan bu kadar meşhur oldum.”

Rather than presenting his rise as the result of a carefully designed career strategy, he describes it almost as an accident.

This attitude would appear repeatedly in later Teoman interviews. He often distances himself from the machinery of celebrity even while participating successfully in it.

The contradiction is already visible in 1999.

He wants recognition for his music, but he is suspicious of the identity that recognition creates around him.

### Beyoğlu

One of the most important geographical references in the interview is Beyoğlu.

Teoman describes it simply:

> “Beyoğlu'ndan besleniyorum, burası benim mekânım.”

This is more significant than it may initially seem.

Beyoğlu would become part of the cultural geography surrounding Teoman's music. Bars, streets, nightlife, loneliness, relationships and urban wandering all belong to the emotional landscape associated with his songs.

In this early interview, he openly identifies the neighborhood as a source of inspiration.

Teoman's Istanbul is therefore not an abstract city.

It is concentrated around particular streets and particular kinds of urban life.

### The Public Teoman and the Private Teoman

By 1999, Teoman's relationships with women had already become part of his media image.

He pushes back against this image.

He says that newspapers had invented relationships that never existed and points out that he had previously been in a four-year relationship that the media barely noticed.

More revealing is his desire to protect the image of the solitary singer.

He does not particularly like being publicly understood as one half of a couple.

This introduces a distinction that would become important throughout his public career:

**Teoman the individual** and **Teoman the public character** are not always the same person.

The public character is solitary, independent and difficult to attach to another person.

The private individual may live a much more ordinary emotional life.

Teoman already seems aware of this difference and, to some extent, actively protects it.

### Relationships and Distance

The interview's discussion of relationships also reveals an emotional distance that appears frequently in later Teoman conversations.

He resists conventional ideas of couples and public romantic identity.

This does not necessarily mean a rejection of intimacy. Rather, his language suggests discomfort with identities built around relationships.

The distinction is important.

He seems to prefer relationships that exist inside private life rather than relationships that become part of a public narrative.

That preference fits naturally with the solitary figure appearing in many of his songs.

### Becoming an Image

The 1996 Teoman interview showed a musician concerned with artistic control.

Three years later, that control has become more complicated.

Teoman can control songs.

He can control an album.

He can decide how he performs.

But once fame arrives, other people begin constructing a second Teoman through newspaper stories, photographs, rumors and expectations.

The 1999 interview captures him beginning to understand this problem.

His appearance becomes news.

His relationships become news.

The places he visits become part of his mythology.

Even his reluctance to become a celebrity becomes part of the celebrity image.

### The Beginning of the Teoman Persona

Looking back from later decades, this interview captures an important transition.

Several elements of what would become the recognizable Teoman persona are now present:

- the urban outsider,
- the Beyoğlu musician,
- the reluctant celebrity,
- the solitary man,
- the ironic observer,
- the musician suspicious of his own fame,
- and the man whose romantic life attracts more public attention than he wants.

Some of these characteristics certainly belong to Teoman himself.

Others were created or exaggerated by the media.

Most likely, the public Teoman emerged from the interaction between the two.

That makes this interview especially useful in a chronological archive.

The 1996 interview records **Teoman before fame**.

This 1999 interview records **Teoman discovering what fame does to identity**.
