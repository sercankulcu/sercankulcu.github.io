## Nasıl Olunur? #159 — Teoman

**Nasıl Olunur? — 24 June 2022**  
**Interview by Nilay Örnek**  
**Duration: 1 hour 32 minutes**

This is one of the most substantial long-form conversations with Teoman in the archive.

Unlike newspaper interviews built around a new album or a current controversy, Nilay Örnek gives Teoman enough time to move between different periods of his life.

Music.

Books.

Cinema.

Writing.

Fame.

Boredom.

Work.

Aging.

The conversation gradually produces something closer to a self-portrait than a promotional interview.

### A Person Who Works More Than His Image Suggests

Nilay Örnek introduces Teoman as someone whose public image can easily suggest spontaneity, nightlife and indifference.

But she immediately emphasizes something else:

Teoman has worked hard.

This distinction is useful.

The public character often appears lazy, detached or bored.

The actual career requires decades of writing, recording, rehearsing, performing and organizing projects.

The apparent lack of effort is partly style.

### Discipline Behind the Disorder

This contradiction has appeared before.

In the 2012 GQ interview, Teoman described surprisingly rigid personal routines.

Here again, the image of the permanently chaotic rock star becomes difficult to sustain.

Teoman may dislike conventional work structures.

That does not mean he avoids work.

Instead, he appears to prefer work whose logic he controls.

### Media Distance

By 2022, Teoman appears in the media less frequently than during the peak years of his fame.

This is deliberate.

He no longer needs constant visibility to maintain a career.

The reduced exposure also protects him from one of the problems he had identified much earlier:

the public figure eventually becomes trapped inside a repeated version of himself.

Less media means fewer occasions on which Teoman has to perform “Teoman.”

### Boredom

One of the central psychological themes is boredom.

Teoman is highly conscious of it.

He gets bored.

He tries to avoid getting bored.

He looks for projects that can occupy his attention.

This may sound trivial, but boredom has become one of the central organizing problems of his later life.

### After Ambition

The younger Teoman had obvious problems to solve.

Make an album.

Become successful.

Write better songs.

Reach an audience.

The older Teoman already possesses all of these things.

That creates a new problem:

**what should occupy someone after ambition has largely succeeded?**

Boredom is partly what remains when necessity disappears.

### Projects as Antidotes

Teoman's later career increasingly consists of specially designed projects rather than automatic album cycles.

He chooses things that interest him.

A book.

A reinterpretation.

A film-related idea.

An unusual musical project.

This is different from the industrial logic of releasing another record simply because enough time has passed.

The project must solve a psychological problem as much as a professional one.

It must make life interesting again.

### Reading

Reading remains one of the most stable elements in Teoman's biography.

It appeared in childhood stories.

It appeared in discussions of songwriting.

It appeared in references to Salinger, Aragon and other writers.

By 2022, reading still occupies a central place in how he thinks.

This continuity matters.

Many aspects of Teoman's identity changed with fame.

Reading predates fame and survives it.

### Reading as a Private Activity

Books also offer something celebrity cannot easily damage.

Reading requires no audience.

No stage.

No public image.

It is one of the few activities that allows Teoman to remain entirely private while still entering another person's world.

That may help explain why literature has remained important even when his relationship with music became unstable.

### Writing Beyond Songs

By this stage, Teoman is no longer simply a songwriter who once wrote an autobiography.

Writing has become a genuine second creative territory.

*Fasa Fiso* showed that prose could provide the novelty songwriting no longer always offered.

He is now working on further written projects.

This changes the balance of his artistic identity.

### A Beginner Again

Writing prose gives Teoman a valuable psychological position.

He can become inexperienced again.

A musician with decades of success has difficulty experiencing artistic innocence.

Every song is compared with earlier songs.

Every album enters an existing reputation.

Writing a different kind of book removes some of that burden.

The experienced artist can temporarily become a beginner.

### Cinema

Cinema remains another recurring interest.

Teoman had already acted, written and directed.

Film gives him access to narrative possibilities larger than songs.

This connects with the storytelling impulse visible since the Ruhi period.

Characters and scenes can exist longer.

A song can imply a world.

Cinema can physically construct it.

### The Problem with Repetition

Across music, media and ordinary life, Teoman seems especially sensitive to repetition.

Doing the same thing repeatedly reduces meaning.

A successful system can therefore become dangerous precisely because it works.

Once an album-tour-interview cycle becomes predictable, it stops producing curiosity.

This helps explain both his 2011 withdrawal and his later preference for unusual projects.

### Fame Is No Longer the Main Subject

One striking difference from earlier interviews is the reduced importance of fame itself.

In 1999 and 2000, fame was a new condition that required constant interpretation.

By 2022, it has become background.

Teoman has lived with public recognition for more than two decades.

The extraordinary has become ordinary.

### The Rock Star as Historical Character

The phrase “rock star” is still attached to Teoman.

But by this point, it almost functions as a historical description.

It refers to the figure constructed during his rise.

Teoman can now observe that figure from outside.

This distance allows more humor.

The younger man had to live inside the image.

The older man can analyze it.

### Aging

Aging remains a major subject, but the tone is less catastrophic than in some earlier interviews.

Teoman has been calling himself old for decades.

That history itself becomes almost comic.

At thirty-two, he already described a loss of passion.

At fifty, he described himself as psychologically old.

Now aging is not a sudden discovery.

It is a long-term condition he has learned to observe.

### Less Drama, More Management

The later Teoman often seems less interested in defeating aging than in managing it.

He knows some forms of excitement will not return.

The task is therefore to construct a life that still produces enough curiosity.

Reading.

Writing.

Projects.

Friends.

Music when it still feels meaningful.

The strategy is practical rather than heroic.

### Self-Knowledge

Teoman remains an intense observer of himself.

But the tone of self-analysis has changed.

The younger Teoman often spoke as if understanding a flaw might eventually solve it.

By the later period, he seems more accepting of personality's durability.

Some characteristics can be managed.

Not everything can be redesigned.

### Accepting the Fixed Parts

This is not necessarily resignation.

It may be a more realistic version of self-knowledge.

A person can spend decades attempting to become fundamentally different.

At some point, another possibility emerges:

identify the fixed parts and build around them.

Teoman's later routines and carefully selected projects seem consistent with this approach.

### Private Teoman and Public Teoman

The distinction between the person and the public character remains important.

But by 2022, Teoman has gained more control over the boundary.

He can choose fewer interviews.

He can choose projects.

He can reduce exposure.

The young musician needed the media.

The established musician can refuse it.

That changes the power relationship.

### Success Creates the Right to Disappear

This is one of the privileges of a long career.

Visibility is no longer essential.

The songs already circulate.

The audience already exists.

Teoman can disappear for periods without disappearing culturally.

This is very different from the fragile position of the artist in 1996.

### The Catalogue Has Become Infrastructure

By now, Teoman's older work supports his freedom.

The catalogue generates recognition independently of constant new production.

This means old songs are not only artistic legacy.

They are infrastructure.

They allow the artist to live outside the normal demands of continuous promotion.

### Conversation Rather Than Promotion

This is one reason the Nilay Örnek episode works so well.

The interview is not forced into a narrow promotional objective.

Teoman does not have to spend the entire conversation convincing the listener to buy a specific album.

Instead, the format allows digression.

And Teoman is often most interesting when allowed to digress.

### The Value of Long Form

Short newspaper interviews tend to produce declarative Teoman.

A question arrives.

A memorable sentence follows.

Long-form audio produces something different.

Ideas can develop gradually.

Contradictions remain visible.

Humor interrupts seriousness.

The listener can hear hesitation and changes of direction.

This creates a more complex record of personality.

### Teoman as Reader of Teoman

By 2022, Teoman has spent years looking back through his own archive.

Songs.

Interviews.

Books.

Memories.

He has effectively become an interpreter of his younger self.

The conversation often carries this historical awareness.

He no longer simply says what he believes.

He can also compare it with what he once believed.

### The Archive Becomes Part of the Person

This is especially relevant to a chronological interview archive.

By the later period, Teoman himself knows that earlier statements exist and can be compared.

The younger Teoman spoke into the present.

The older Teoman speaks inside a documented history.

That changes the meaning of every new interview.

### Why This Interview Matters

This episode is important less because of one dramatic statement than because of its scale.

Over ninety minutes, several versions of Teoman coexist:

- the hardworking musician behind the careless image,
- the reader,
- the songwriter,
- the filmmaker,
- the autobiographical writer,
- the public figure who has deliberately reduced his media presence,
- the person constantly trying to defeat boredom,
- and the aging artist designing projects to maintain curiosity.

It also shows how profoundly the central problem of Teoman's life has changed.

The young Teoman asked:

**How do I become what I want to become?**

The successful Teoman asked:

**Why didn't becoming it make me as happy as I expected?**

The middle-aged Teoman asked:

**Should I continue doing this at all?**

The later Teoman increasingly asks:

**How do I keep life interesting without repeating myself?**

That may be the central question of the 2022 Teoman.

Not fame.

Not success.

Not even legacy.

**Interest.**

Because once a person already has the career, the songs and the public recognition, the hardest thing to manufacture may simply be a reason to remain curious.
