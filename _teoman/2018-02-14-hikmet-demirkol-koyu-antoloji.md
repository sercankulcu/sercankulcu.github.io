## Teoman'dan Arşivlik Albüm: Koyu Antoloji

**Hürriyet — 14 February 2018**  
**Interview by Hikmet Demirkol**

This interview belongs to the same period as the Işıl Cinmen conversation, but its emphasis is different.

The Posta interview was highly personal.

Here the focus returns more directly to music.

Teoman explains why he returned to old songs, why he wanted to rearrange them, why writing new material has become difficult and how *Koyu Antoloji* functions as a retrospective project.

The result is one of the clearest documents of Teoman becoming conscious of his own catalogue as a historical object.

### Returning to Old Songs

*Koyu Antoloji* contains twenty-six songs selected from different periods of Teoman's career.

But Teoman does not want to release them simply as they originally appeared.

He revisits them.

Rearranges them.

Changes instrumentation.

Changes atmosphere.

The goal is not nostalgia in the simple sense.

He wants to hear what these songs can become now.

### The Older Teoman Rewrites the Younger Teoman

This creates an unusual collaboration.

The original songwriter is Teoman.

The later interpreter is also Teoman.

But they are separated by years of experience.

The person revisiting the songs has different tastes, different technical knowledge and a different emotional relationship with the material.

In this sense, *Koyu Antoloji* is almost a conversation between two versions of the same artist.

### Some Songs Aged Better Than Others

Looking backward also forces judgment.

Not every song appears equally strong after years have passed.

Some remain meaningful.

Some arrangements now sound dated.

Some songs reveal qualities Teoman did not fully appreciate when he first recorded them.

This is one of the benefits of distance.

The artist can finally hear his own work somewhat like a listener.

### The Problem with Hits

Commercial success can distort the memory of a catalogue.

A few songs become enormous.

They appear constantly in concerts.

Radio reinforces them.

Audiences request them.

Other tracks disappear.

Teoman does not necessarily believe the most famous songs are always the most representative.

*Koyu Antoloji* gives him the opportunity to restore some of the balance.

### What Should Survive?

This connects directly with Teoman's growing interest in legacy.

The project is partly a decision about preservation.

If future listeners encounter a limited selection of Teoman songs, which songs should those be?

What version should they hear?

Which tracks best represent his songwriting rather than simply his commercial success?

These are archival questions as much as musical ones.

### Why Dark?

The word *koyu* matters.

Teoman deliberately brings the songs into a darker common atmosphere.

Tracks originally recorded years apart now inhabit the same sonic world.

This gives the anthology coherence.

It is not merely a playlist of old songs.

It behaves more like a new album constructed from existing material.

### A New Album Made from the Past

That distinction is essential.

The raw material is old.

The artistic act is new.

Teoman treats the catalogue almost as another songwriter's work that he has been asked to interpret.

The past becomes material.

This makes *Koyu Antoloji* more interesting than a conventional greatest-hits release.

### Why Not Write New Songs?

The obvious question is why an artist would invest so much effort in rearranging old material rather than writing a completely new album.

Teoman's answer is connected with motivation.

Writing new songs no longer feels as natural or necessary as it once did.

The urge has weakened.

This does not mean he has lost all musical interest.

He remains interested in production, arrangement and interpretation.

What has diminished is the desire to continuously generate new compositions.

### Creative Exhaustion

This is different from simple writer's block.

Writer's block implies wanting to write and being unable to do it.

Teoman's problem is more fundamental.

He is not always sure he wants another song.

After decades of songwriting, the internal necessity has weakened.

That makes conventional advice about productivity almost irrelevant.

The problem is not technique.

It is desire.

### The Younger Artist Needed Songs

Young Teoman needed songs for practical reasons.

There was no catalogue.

Without new songs, there was no career.

Each album created the next stage.

The relationship between creativity and survival was direct.

The established Teoman lives in a different reality.

Hundreds of concerts can be performed using material that already exists.

A new song is no longer professionally necessary.

### Necessity and Creativity

This leads to an uncomfortable artistic question.

How much creativity depends on necessity?

When you desperately need songs, you produce them.

When you already possess a large successful catalogue, the pressure disappears.

Freedom should theoretically increase creativity.

But sometimes pressure was part of what created the work.

### The 2011 Withdrawal

The conversation also returns to Teoman's decision to leave music in 2011.

That decision now appears less like an isolated crisis and more like part of a long pattern.

Teoman became tired not only of individual songs or concerts but of the repetitive structure of the profession.

He had reached a point where music no longer felt like the open future it represented in his youth.

It had become something known.

### Coming Back Without Returning Completely

Teoman eventually returned to music.

But the return did not erase the reasons he had left.

This is important.

He can perform concerts again.

He can release albums again.

He can enjoy parts of the profession again.

Yet some of the old dissatisfaction survives.

The person who returned is not simply the same musician resetting the career to 2010.

### The End of Automatic Production

The traditional music industry assumes a cycle.

Album.

Tour.

Album.

Tour.

Repeat.

Teoman increasingly refuses that automatic rhythm.

A new record must have some reason to exist beyond the expectation that musicians periodically produce records.

This slows production.

But it also makes his later projects more deliberate.

### Self-Criticism

Revisiting older songs also reveals Teoman's willingness to criticize his own work.

He does not treat every previous recording as sacred.

Arrangements can be improved.

Vocals can be approached differently.

Songs can be stripped down.

Some choices can be reversed.

Artistic identity does not require defending every decision the younger artist made.

### Preservation Through Change

Paradoxically, Teoman preserves the songs by changing them.

A museum-like approach would leave them untouched.

Teoman instead believes some songs may survive better if they are reconstructed for the present.

Preservation therefore becomes active rather than passive.

### The Archive Is Alive

This gives the word “archive” an unusual meaning.

An archive normally stores fixed objects.

Teoman's musical archive remains editable.

He can reopen it.

Move things.

Reinterpret them.

Change emphasis.

The past is preserved, but it is not frozen.

### The Songwriter Versus the Performer

*Koyu Antoloji* also reinforces a distinction that became visible in *Söz Müzik Teoman*.

Teoman increasingly evaluates himself as a songwriter.

Performance can change.

Arrangement can change.

Singer can even change.

The deeper structure is the song.

This is why older compositions remain interesting to him even when their original recordings no longer satisfy him.

### Aging and Selection

There is also something distinctly middle-aged about the project.

Youth accumulates.

Age selects.

The younger Teoman created as much as possible.

The older Teoman begins sorting.

Which songs matter?

Which should be forgotten?

Which deserve another version?

Which belong to the image of Teoman and which belong to the actual songwriter?

Selection becomes part of authorship.

### Leaving a Curated Version of Himself

This connects directly to his earlier ambition that songs should survive after his death.

Survival alone is not enough.

Teoman increasingly wants some influence over **how** the work survives.

*Koyu Antoloji* is therefore a form of self-curation.

He is constructing a version of his own musical history for future listeners.

### Why This Interview Matters

This interview is particularly useful when read beside the Işıl Cinmen conversation from the same period.

Cinmen reveals the psychological context:

- aging,
- loneliness,
- pessimism,
- exhaustion,
- and looking backward.

Demirkol reveals the musical consequence:

- rearranging old work,
- selecting what matters,
- reconsidering hits,
- losing the automatic desire to write new songs,
- and transforming the catalogue into an archive.

Together, the two interviews explain *Koyu Antoloji*.

It is not simply an album of old songs.

It is part of a larger shift in Teoman's relationship with time.

Young Teoman wanted to create a future.

The fifty-year-old Teoman increasingly wants to organize the past.

And this organization is not passive nostalgia.

It is artistic editing.

He is asking:

**If I cannot control how long my work survives, can I at least help decide what survives with me?**

*Koyu Antoloji* is one answer.
