## Ne İşim Var Lan Benim Burada?

**Mirgün Cabas — 12 November 2025**  
**Duration: 28 minutes 44 seconds**

By late 2025, Teoman has found a new use for a problem that has followed him through almost his entire public life.

His dissatisfaction.

His boredom.

His anxiety.

His recurring question about what exactly he is doing here.

Instead of trying once again to eliminate these states, he has turned them into a stage performance.

The project is called *Varoluşçuluk 101*.

Teoman describes it neither as a normal concert nor as conventional stand-up.

It is something in between.

That ambiguity makes it an unusually appropriate Teoman project.

### Turning the Problem into the Work

For decades Teoman has spoken about dissatisfaction as something interfering with life.

Success did not remove it.

Relationships did not remove it.

Leaving music did not remove it.

New projects could distract from it, but not permanently.

By 2025, the strategy has changed.

The problem itself becomes material.

Rather than asking how to stop being Teoman, he puts the different Teomans on stage.

### Varoluşçuluk 101

The performance deals with subjects that have appeared throughout the interview archive:

the tragedy and comedy of life,

personal failure,

daily meaninglessness,

the search for meaning,

and Teoman's own history.

The official description calls it a **“musical persona stand-up.”**

That phrase is deliberately unstable.

It sounds partly serious and partly like a joke.

Teoman seems to like precisely that uncertainty.

### Not Quite Stand-Up

Teoman is aware that he is not a professional comedian.

He does not want to imitate conventional stand-up built around topical jokes or observations about public figures.

Humor has another role.

It allows reality to become visible from another angle.

This is entirely consistent with something Teoman has been saying since at least 2000:

irony makes difficult realities easier to look at.

### Irony Finally Becomes the Form

Earlier, irony existed inside the songs and interviews.

Now it structures an entire performance.

That is an important development.

Teoman once used humor to protect himself from melancholy.

In *Varoluşçuluk 101*, melancholy and humor are deliberately placed beside one another.

The defense mechanism becomes an artistic technique.

### “The Person Who Wrote Those Songs”

Teoman explains that one purpose of the show is to tell audiences more about **the person who wrote the songs**.

This is an important distinction.

The songs have survived.

The public character has survived.

But neither necessarily explains the human being who produced them.

The new show attempts to connect the three:

song,

persona,

person.

### Many Teomans

The promotional description of *Varoluşçuluk 101* makes this almost explicit.

There is:

Teoman himself,

the person Teoman thinks he is,

the person the audience thinks Teoman is,

the Teoman he likes people to imagine,

the Teoman he dislikes,

the Teoman he feels compassion for,

and the fictional Teoman-like characters he has created.

This is perhaps the most complete expression yet of a problem first visible in the archive in 1999.

There has never been only one Teoman.

### From Public Persona to Subject of Study

The young Teoman struggled with the fact that newspapers were constructing another version of him.

The middle-aged Teoman learned to joke about that version.

The later Teoman used the rock-star character as material for fiction.

By 2025, Teoman himself becomes almost the subject of a lecture-performance.

He is studying the character publicly.

### Ruhi, Timur and Teoman

This creates a fascinating line through the archive.

**Ruhi** allowed Teoman to fictionalize parts of himself in songs.

**Timur** allowed him to fictionalize the aging rock star in prose.

*Varoluşçuluk 101* brings those fictional and real selves back onto the same stage.

The distinction between autobiography and persona becomes the actual subject.

### Fasa Fiso Returns

At approximately 13:49 in the interview, Mirgün Cabas and Teoman return to *Fasa Fiso*.

This is appropriate because the autobiography already represented an attempt to organize the multiple versions of Teoman.

The book used old interviews, memories and stories to construct a written life.

The stage show now reuses some of that autobiographical material in another form.

### The Archive Keeps Producing New Work

This is one of the clearest patterns in Teoman's later career.

Archive does not mean dead material.

Old songs become *Koyu Antoloji*.

Old interviews become *Fasa Fiso*.

The rock-star persona becomes *Sayın Bay Rock Yıldızı*.

Autobiographical material becomes *Varoluşçuluk 101*.

The past repeatedly becomes raw material for the next project.

### Teoman as His Own Source Material

At some point, an established artist accumulates enough history that his own life becomes a substantial cultural archive.

Teoman has reached that point.

He no longer has to invent every subject from scratch.

There are decades of contradictions available:

young ambition,

success,

withdrawal,

relationships,

fatherhood,

politics,

aging,

failure,

and public mythology.

The later work increasingly interprets that archive.

### “Ne Yapıyorum Lan Ben?”

At 13:05, the official chapter title asks:

**“Ne yapıyorum lan ben, şovmen miyim?”**

The sentence is comic because it attacks the project while the project is still happening.

Teoman is suspicious even of his own new role.

Singer was understandable.

Songwriter was understandable.

Writer became understandable.

But performer of autobiographical existential stand-up?

The category feels ridiculous even to him.

### Refusing a New Grand Identity

That skepticism is healthy within the logic of Teoman's career.

Every fixed identity eventually becomes restrictive.

Rock star.

Writer.

Intellectual.

Now potentially showman.

By mocking the new label immediately, Teoman prevents it from becoming too serious.

### Artificial Intelligence

At 18:24, the conversation shifts toward artificial intelligence and the future of the music industry.

This had already become one of Teoman's major concerns in 2025.

He believes AI is not merely another technological tool comparable with previous industrial changes.

He expects it to cause major economic disruption, including in creative professions.

This is a strikingly different kind of future anxiety from his earlier concerns.

### From Piracy to AI

There is an interesting historical continuity.

In 2010, Teoman was discussing the collapse of physical album sales and the rise of MP3s.

Fifteen years later, the technological problem has changed completely.

The question is no longer merely:

**How will people distribute music?**

It is:

**Who or what will make the music?**

### The Artist May No Longer Be Necessary

Generative AI threatens something deeper than older forms of technological disruption.

Digital distribution changed the economics around the artist.

AI may challenge the economic necessity of the artist himself.

If unlimited functional music can be generated cheaply, many forms of professional music production may lose commercial value.

Teoman sees this as an economic problem, not simply an aesthetic curiosity.

### The Sociologist Returns

This is another example of Teoman using his sociology background.

He is less interested in asking whether AI music is philosophically “real art” than in asking what happens to labor, income and industries when automated production becomes abundant.

The question is structural.

Who survives?

Who gets paid?

What happens when scarcity disappears?

### Preparing for Change

Teoman also presents himself as someone who has been thinking about this change for years.

That fits his late-career emphasis on autonomy.

A large catalogue, established audience and diversified work give him more protection than a new artist entering a radically changing industry.

Once again, the songs created in the past function as infrastructure for the present.

### Tribute Album

At 22:37, Teoman discusses a tribute album project.

This is another sign that his relationship with the catalogue has changed.

A tribute album depends on the songs already having enough independent cultural life for other artists to reinterpret them.

This brings the archive back to *Söz Müzik Teoman*.

### The Circle Returns to 2008

In 2008, Teoman deliberately gave his songs to other performers to see what they could become.

By 2025, the principle is even more appropriate.

The songs have accumulated another seventeen years of cultural history.

Newer musicians can approach them from entirely different generations and genres.

The catalogue increasingly belongs to a shared musical culture rather than exclusively to Teoman.

### Legacy While Still Alive

Tribute projects usually carry an element of memorial culture.

But Teoman is present to watch the process.

That gives him an unusual privilege:

he can observe his own legacy forming while still participating in it.

The songs he once hoped would survive him are already becoming objects interpreted by later musicians.

### Turne

The final official chapter, beginning around 26:35, concerns touring.

This is important because despite all Teoman's discussions of retirement, writing and reduced interest in new songs, the stage remains part of his life.

He has not disappeared from music.

Instead, his relationship with it has become more selective.

### The End of Songwriting Is Not Necessarily the End of Music

Later in 2025, Teoman would state even more directly that he considered himself to be entering the final part of his creative career and that he did not intend to continue writing songs indefinitely.

But performing is different.

The existing catalogue can continue on stage.

This creates another separation:

**production may end before performance ends.**

### A Career with Different Endings

There may therefore be several endings rather than one.

The end of trying to become famous.

The end of automatic album production.

The 2011 retirement.

The end of writing new songs.

Eventually the end of touring.

And finally the biological ending Teoman has discussed since childhood.

The career does not stop in a single moment.

It gradually loses functions.

### Existentialism Without Grand Theory

Despite the title *Varoluşçuluk 101*, the project does not appear to be an academic introduction to Sartre or Camus.

The philosophical tradition provides a vocabulary.

The actual subject is Teoman's lived problem:

What makes a life meaningful?

What happens when goals are achieved?

Why does dissatisfaction return?

How seriously should a person take himself?

### Philosophy After Success

This makes Teoman's interest in existentialism particularly appropriate.

His existential problem is not produced by lack of opportunity.

It appears after many external goals have been fulfilled.

Career.

Money.

Recognition.

Creative freedom.

The question becomes harder because obvious explanations have disappeared.

### Bunalım as Biography

Mirgün Cabas's official description says Teoman has taken the depressions that dominated much of his life — and exhausted both himself and his audience — and turned them into the performance.

The wording is humorous.

But it accurately summarizes an extraordinary archival continuity.

From 1996 to 2025, dissatisfaction is perhaps the most stable psychological theme.

### From Suffering to Material

What changes is Teoman's position relative to it.

Young Teoman:

**Why am I like this?**

Successful Teoman:

**Why didn't success fix this?**

Retired Teoman:

**Maybe music is causing this.**

Older Teoman:

**Maybe this will never completely disappear.**

2025 Teoman:

**Fine. Put it on stage.**

That is a substantial philosophical evolution.

### Failure

The show also explicitly includes Teoman's failures.

This is important because celebrity storytelling naturally selects success.

Hit albums.

Large concerts.

Famous relationships.

Awards.

Teoman increasingly prefers another archive:

mistakes,

bad decisions,

foolishness,

depression,

and failed attempts to construct a different life.

### Failure Humanizes the Persona

This connects strongly with what Teoman said after performing *Varoluşçuluk 101*: the audience responds to the “human Teoman,” not star behavior.

Weakness creates access.

The rock star is distant.

The person who repeatedly makes mistakes is recognizable.

### The Final Teoman?

There is something almost retrospective about the project.

Teoman is still active.

But he is clearly thinking about the final section of his career.

The show does not simply advertise what he will make next.

It asks the audience to look back with him at what all the previous work meant.

That gives it the character of a late-career synthesis.

### Why This Interview Matters

This interview is an excellent endpoint for the current chronological archive because nearly every major Teoman theme returns in less than thirty minutes:

- dissatisfaction,
- boredom,
- existential anxiety,
- the difference between public Teoman and private Teoman,
- autobiography,
- irony,
- old songs,
- legacy,
- technological change,
- the future of the music industry,
- tribute projects,
- touring,
- and the question of how an artist should approach the final part of a career.

Most importantly, *Varoluşçuluk 101* suggests that Teoman has stopped demanding that his contradictions be resolved.

There can be many Teomans.

The depressed Teoman.

The successful Teoman.

The rock star.

The father.

The writer.

The fictional character.

The man who quit.

The man who returned.

The person the audience invented.

The person he invented for the audience.

Perhaps maturity does not require choosing the authentic one.

Perhaps all of them are parts of the same archive.

The title of the interview therefore works as a surprisingly good final question for the entire chronology:

**“Ne işim var lan benim burada?”**

After almost thirty years of interviews, Teoman still does not provide a final answer.

Instead, he has found something else to do with the question.

He turned it into the show.
