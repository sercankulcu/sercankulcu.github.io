## Orada Rahat mısın Teoman?

**31 May 2024**  
**Interview by Mirgün Cabas**

This conversation finds Teoman increasingly comfortable with a new identity:

not simply Teoman the songwriter,

but Teoman the writer looking back at the character once known as the rock star.

The immediate context is *Sayın Bay Rock Yıldızı*, a work of fiction centered on Timur.

But Timur is not entirely separate from Teoman.

Like Ruhi more than twenty years earlier, he gives Teoman another way to write about himself without being forced into literal autobiography.

### From Ruhi to Timur

Teoman has done this before.

In the *Gönülçelen* period, Ruhi functioned as an alter ego.

Personal material could be moved into a fictional life.

By 2024, Timur performs a related function.

He is not simply Teoman under another name.

But the distance between author and character allows Teoman to investigate experiences that would become too narrow if treated purely as memoir.

### Fiction Creates Freedom

Autobiography has a basic limitation:

the reader keeps asking whether something really happened.

Fiction changes the question.

Instead of asking:

**Did Teoman do this?**

the reader can ask:

**What does this reveal about this kind of person?**

That gives Teoman more freedom to exaggerate, combine experiences and invent outcomes.

The emotional truth can survive without factual loyalty.

### The Aging Rock Star

Timur also allows Teoman to examine a subject increasingly close to him:

the aging male celebrity.

What happens to the behaviors that once looked attractive?

The womanizing.

The nightlife.

The arrogance.

The endless attention.

The sense of being culturally central.

Do these qualities remain glamorous when the man grows older?

Or do they become ridiculous?

### The Caricature Problem Returns

This question connects directly with Teoman's 2007 observation that celebrities eventually risk becoming caricatures of themselves.

The young rock star can perform excess naturally.

The older rock star who repeats exactly the same gestures may look as though he is imitating his younger self.

Teoman has worried about this for years.

Timur gives him a fictional laboratory in which to examine it.

### Looking at the Rock Star from Outside

This is one of the biggest changes in Teoman's later career.

He can now observe “Teoman” almost as if the name belonged to another person.

The rock star becomes a character.

A historical construction.

Something created jointly by:

songs,

photographs,

media stories,

relationships,

concerts,

and Teoman himself.

Once sufficient distance exists, the persona becomes material for fiction.

### Self-Irony

Teoman does not treat this character with reverence.

Self-irony is central.

The older Teoman can laugh at the mythology of the younger one.

This does not mean the old image was completely false.

It means he no longer needs to protect it.

That is a form of freedom.

### The Writer Teoman

The conversation also demonstrates how seriously writing has entered Teoman's later life.

*Fasa Fiso* began as autobiographical archive work.

Later books expanded this interest.

With *Sayın Bay Rock Yıldızı*, fiction becomes increasingly important.

This is no longer simply a musician occasionally writing books.

Writing has become a second major artistic practice.

### Why Writing Became Attractive

There is a structural reason for this.

Teoman has decades of experience writing songs.

The challenge of songwriting is familiar.

Prose offers new problems.

Character.

Narrative.

Long-form structure.

Point of view.

Dialogue.

The musician who often complains about boredom receives something valuable from literature:

difficulty.

### Difficulty as Pleasure

This is an important later-life theme.

When success removes professional difficulty, the artist must create new difficulty deliberately.

Writing provides it.

Teoman does not have the same established mastery or public expectations in literature that he has in music.

That allows him to struggle again.

And struggle can produce curiosity.

### Money

Mirgün Cabas also asks Teoman about money.

The subject is particularly interesting because the young Teoman spent years building a career in which financial success was uncertain.

The older Teoman has achieved economic security.

Money therefore no longer means the same thing.

### Money as Freedom

Teoman does not present wealth primarily as luxury.

Its more important value is autonomy.

Money allows him to:

work less,

choose projects,

refuse opportunities,

avoid constant promotion,

and organize his time more freely.

This reinforces a theme visible in several late interviews:

the lasting benefit of success is not applause.

It is control over one's own time.

### Enough

The important question becomes:

how much is enough?

An artist can continue chasing larger numbers indefinitely.

More concerts.

More income.

More visibility.

But once financial security exists, accumulation may stop producing meaningful changes in life.

Teoman seems increasingly aware of this.

### Women

Women remain part of the discussion because they remain inseparable from the public Teoman mythology.

But Teoman now speaks about the subject with distance.

The older man can see how heavily his public image was constructed around relationships and sexual success.

That image was partly based on reality.

It was also amplified by media repetition.

### A Male Mythology

The rock-star womanizer is not simply an individual biography.

It is a cultural archetype.

Male celebrity is often measured through:

access,

sexual success,

freedom from conventional rules,

and apparent emotional independence.

Teoman lived inside that mythology.

Now he can examine its absurdities.

### What Looked Like Freedom

One of the interesting questions beneath the conversation is whether the old lifestyle actually represented freedom.

Multiple relationships can look like freedom.

Nightlife can look like freedom.

No schedule can look like freedom.

But the same patterns can become repetitive.

Once repetition appears, supposedly free behavior can become another kind of routine.

### Aging Changes the Meaning of Desire

The object of desire may remain similar.

But the meaning changes.

A younger man can interpret constant romantic pursuit as possibility.

An older man may interpret the same behavior as exhaustion.

Age therefore does not merely weaken desire.

It changes the story told about desire.

### Z Generation

The conversation also turns toward Generation Z.

Teoman is now in the unusual position of having listeners who were born long after his career began.

This forces him to consider how cultural transmission works across generations.

Young listeners encounter old Teoman songs without experiencing the context in which they first appeared.

### Songs Without Their Original Time

This is exactly the kind of survival Teoman wanted.

A song written around 2000 can reach someone born years later.

The original media environment disappears.

The song remains.

That is one practical definition of becoming a classic.

### Not Understanding the Young Completely

At the same time, Teoman does not pretend to completely understand younger people.

The generational distance is real.

Their social environment is different.

Technology is different.

Relationships are different.

Career expectations are different.

Rather than pretending continuity, he acknowledges distance.

### The Older Artist's Position

This creates a new cultural role.

Teoman is no longer the representative young man whose songs explain his generation.

He is now an older artist being encountered by another generation.

The perspective has reversed.

In 2000, Can Dündar could use Teoman to discuss young people.

In 2024, Teoman has to ask what young people see when they look at him.

### Writing for the Future

This generational awareness also strengthens the importance of writing.

Books may reach audiences differently from songs.

They create another archive.

Another Teoman can survive there:

not only the singer heard in recordings,

but the observer who wrote about his own era.

### The Public Character as Material

Perhaps the most important development in this interview is that Teoman no longer simply resists his public image.

He uses it.

The rock star becomes literary material.

The stories once written about him can be transformed into fiction.

The persona becomes raw material just like childhood, relationships or Istanbul.

### Owning the Myth by Rewriting It

This may be the most effective way to escape a public mythology.

Not denying it.

Not obeying it.

Rewriting it.

Once Teoman can turn the rock star into a fictional character, the power relationship changes.

He is again the author.

### Why This Interview Matters

This interview documents Teoman's transition from being primarily the subject of stories to increasingly becoming the person who writes those stories.

Several themes converge:

- fiction as a way to transform autobiography,
- Timur as a new alter-ego-like figure,
- the aging of the rock-star persona,
- self-irony,
- writing as a new source of creative difficulty,
- money as autonomy rather than status,
- women and sexuality as parts of a cultural mythology,
- generational distance,
- and the transformation of public identity into literary material.

There is a striking historical symmetry.

In 2001, Teoman created Ruhi in order to turn parts of himself into fiction.

In 2024, he is doing something similar again with Timur.

But the position of the author has changed.

Ruhi was created by a musician becoming increasingly ambitious as a storyteller.

Timur is created by an older man who has spent decades watching a real public character called Teoman accumulate around him.

The younger Teoman used fiction to expand himself.

The older Teoman uses fiction partly to escape himself.

Or perhaps more precisely:

**to become the author of Teoman again.**
