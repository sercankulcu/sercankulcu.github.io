## Kötü Komşu Ev Sahibi Yapar

**Antalya Altın Portakal Film Festivali — October 2024**

The *Evcilik* team talk at Antalya is important because it catches Nejat İşler's move toward producing before the later January 2025 interviews gave that move a clearer philosophy.

By early 2025, İşler would describe the producer as the person who builds the structure.

Here, the idea is still more practical.

A project exists.

It has waited for years.

The usual system has not made it happen.

So someone has to help make it possible.

That is where İşler enters not only as an actor, but as a producer.

### A Film Waiting for Years

Ümit Ünal explains that *Evcilik* had existed as a project for roughly a decade before finally reaching production.

This detail matters.

The film was not created quickly because an actor wanted another vehicle.

It was a story that had difficulty finding the conditions necessary to exist.

That immediately makes the production question central.

A script can be ready.

A director can believe in it.

Actors can want to participate.

None of that guarantees a film.

Cinema also needs structure.

### Nejat İşler as the Trigger

Ünal gives İşler a particularly important role in that process.

He says İşler had already been interested in producing independent work and became one of the people who pushed *Evcilik* toward actual production.

This is one of the clearest early records of the professional shift that becomes explicit in 2025.

The actor is no longer waiting only for the industry to bring him a project.

He is beginning to ask:

**What can we make happen ourselves?**

### From Complaint to Construction

This is a major development when placed against the older archive.

In 2010, İşler criticized the television system.

The complaint was clear:

the structure was consuming too much time and energy.

For years, his main solution was selective withdrawal.

Do fewer projects.

Prefer cinema.

Protect time.

By 2024, another possibility has emerged.

Instead of only rejecting bad structures, help construct another one.

### "Kötü Komşu Ev Sahibi Yapar"

The Turkish proverb in the title is unusually suitable:

**“Kötü komşu ev sahibi yapar.”**

A bad landlord or bad neighbor can push someone toward owning their own home.

The broader idea is:

bad conditions can force independence.

That is almost a summary of İşler's professional evolution.

Difficult production systems encouraged him first to leave and later to build.

### Producing as Self-Defense

Producing can therefore be understood partly as creative self-defense.

If the industry repeatedly offers:

safe stories,

repeated formulas,

unwanted conditions,

then one option is constant refusal.

Another option is to participate in creating alternatives.

İşler increasingly chooses the second.

### Tired of Safe Work

During the festival conversation, İşler also speaks about becoming bored with work that carries too little risk.

This does not mean risk for its own sake.

It means the difference between:

a predictable professional product

and

a project someone actually cares about.

An established actor can spend years choosing only safe roles.

The career survives.

The creative appetite may not.

### Sharing Financial Risk

The important difference with *Evcilik* is that İşler does not only say he likes the project.

He accepts part of the production risk.

That changes the meaning of commitment.

As an actor, saying yes risks:

time,

performance,

reputation.

As a producer, the risk becomes broader.

Money.

Organization.

The entire project's survival.

### The Actor Has Skin in the Game

This is one reason producing changes the relationship with a film.

An actor can complete the performance and leave.

A producer remains tied to:

whether the film is completed,

whether it finds an audience,

whether its financial structure works.

The commitment becomes institutional rather than only artistic.

### Independent Cinema and Scale

The festival setting is also important.

*Evcilik* belongs to a scale of filmmaking that fits İşler's later preferences.

Smaller teams.

Specific stories.

Direct relationships.

Less anonymous industrial machinery.

This resembles the values seen in *Tamirhane*.

The alternative to large-scale production is not necessarily amateur work.

It can be professionally serious cinema built at a more controllable scale.

### A Useful Parallel with Gümüşlükspor

Again, Gümüşlükspor provides an unexpected parallel.

İşler originally entered football from affection.

Then he became responsible for an organization.

In cinema:

he entered as an actor.

Then he became partly responsible for production.

Both experiences teach the same lesson:

liking something is different from running the structure that makes it possible.

### Responsibility Changes Perspective

Once a person becomes an organizer, many invisible problems become visible.

Budgets.

People.

Time.

Negotiation.

Unexpected failures.

Earlier, İşler could criticize production from the actor's side.

Producing forces him to understand the other side too.

That does not necessarily make the criticism disappear.

It makes it more informed.

### Ümit Ünal and Creative Trust

The collaboration with Ümit Ünal is important because this is not generic entrepreneurship.

İşler is not announcing a production company simply to expand his brand.

The motivation is attached to a filmmaker and a specific project.

That keeps the move close to story.

The production role grows from:

**I want this film to exist.**

That is very different from:

**I want to become a producer.**

### Production Follows Desire

This distinction helps explain why the move feels natural.

The title arrives after the need.

First there is a story.

Then the question:

how can we make it?

Producing becomes the answer.

### The Anti-Franchise Position

This also fits İşler's comments from the 2022 Oksijen interview.

There, he criticized the tendency to repeat successful projects simply because they had already worked.

Second films.

Second seasons.

More of the same.

Here, he supports almost the opposite model:

a project that has struggled to exist because it is not the obvious safe choice.

That consistency matters.

### Risk Is a Creative Filter

Risk can therefore function as a kind of filter.

If a project is difficult to make but still feels worth making, the commitment means something.

This does not automatically make risky cinema good.

But it forces everyone involved to answer a basic question:

**Why this film?**

### From Career Maintenance to Project Selection

This is another late-career shift.

Younger actors often need work.

Any work can be professionally valuable.

An established actor faces the opposite problem.

Too many possible projects.

The question becomes selection.

Which projects deserve:

time,

energy,

money?

Producing makes that selection even more serious.

### A Useful Comparison with 2017

The connection with “buying time” is direct.

2017 asks:

**How do I stop compulsory work from taking all of my life?**

2024 asks:

**What should I do with the work I still choose?**

The answer increasingly becomes:

use time on projects with enough meaning to justify the cost.

### A Useful Comparison with 2025 Habertürk

The January 2025 Habertürk interview gives the mature language.

Producer:

**“kuran adam.”**

The Antalya conversation shows the experience that likely helped produce that definition.

Here we see the practical problem.

Later we get the concept.

That makes the two files complementary rather than repetitive.

### First Build the Conditions

The chronology now becomes particularly clear:

**2010:** reject bad conditions.

**2015:** reduce dependence on them.

**2017:** protect personal time.

**2022:** work with trusted smaller teams.

**2024:** financially and organizationally help a chosen project exist.

**2025:** explicitly define producing as building the structure.

This is one of the strongest professional arcs in the entire archive.

### The Outsider Becomes an Institution Builder

There is also an irony here.

For years, İşler's mythology has presented him as anti-institutional.

But his later life repeatedly moves toward building structures.

Gümüşlükspor.

Film production.

Potential directing.

The real pattern may not be opposition to institutions.

It may be opposition to institutions he cannot believe in.

When he cares enough, he helps build one.

## Why This Interview Matters

The 2024 Antalya *Evcilik* conversation is an important bridge between Nejat İşler the selective actor and Nejat İşler the producer.

It shows that producing did not begin as an abstract ambition.

It emerged from a concrete problem:

a film worth making had spent years waiting for the conditions to be made.

İşler chose to help create those conditions and share the risk.

That makes this record particularly valuable when read before his January 2025 Habertürk interview.

Antalya shows the action.

Habertürk gives the philosophy.

Together they reveal one of the clearest transformations in his career:

**from escaping structures he disliked to helping build structures he wanted.**
