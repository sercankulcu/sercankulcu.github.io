## Diziler Bitti, Sinemaya Devam

**Anadolu Ajansı — 1 July 2015**

In July 2015, Nejat İşler made one of the clearest professional decisions of his career.

He was not retiring from acting.

He was not abandoning cinema.

He was not disappearing from public life.

He was drawing a line around television.

Speaking during the opening of Gümüşlükspor's summer football school, İşler stated:

**“Diziler bitti, sinemaya devam edeceğim.”**

Television series were finished.

Cinema would continue.

This distinction is central to understanding the next phase of his life.

### Leaving Television, Not Acting

İşler's statement is often easy to simplify into:

**Nejat İşler quit acting.**

That would be wrong.

In the same period, he was equally clear that he intended to remain an actor.

He described acting as something he would continue for the rest of his life.

So the actual decision was more precise:

**leave the industrial structure of television while preserving the profession itself.**

That difference had been developing for years.

### The 2010 Problem Becomes a 2015 Decision

Five years earlier, during the *Kapalıçarşı* period, İşler had already said that he wanted to leave the professional “market.”

He complained about:

- exhausting schedules,
- constant visibility,
- lack of privacy,
- the amount of time television consumed.

In 2010, this was still partly frustration.

By 2015, it had become a practical decision.

**2010:** I want to get out.

**2015:** I am no longer doing series.

The continuity is important.

His later lifestyle should therefore not be interpreted as a sudden post-illness personality change.

A withdrawal from television had been developing long before.

### Gümüşlük Becomes Home

The second major statement in the interview concerns geography.

İşler says that he will live in Gümüşlük and go to Istanbul only for work.

This reverses the normal relationship between actor and city.

For most Turkish actors, Istanbul is not simply where work happens.

It is the professional center.

The social center.

The media center.

The networking center.

The place where visibility is maintained.

İşler begins separating those functions.

Istanbul becomes:

**a place to work.**

Gümüşlük becomes:

**a place to live.**

That distinction will become even more philosophical in later interviews.

### Why Gümüşlük Matters

It would be easy to romanticize the move.

Sea.

Small town.

Quiet life.

Distance from celebrity.

But Gümüşlük represented more than scenery.

It gave İşler access to a different social scale.

People knew one another.

Daily life was less mediated by entertainment culture.

Football could become local rather than commercial.

Time could be organized differently.

The difference was not simply urban versus rural.

It was **industrial life versus local life**.

### Gümüşlükspor Changes the Meaning of Withdrawal

This is why the context of the interview matters.

İşler is not speaking from a private villa while disappearing from society.

He is at the opening of a football school connected to Gümüşlükspor.

His withdrawal from television is happening at the same time as deeper participation in a local institution.

This creates an important distinction:

**withdrawal from one system does not mean withdrawal from people.**

In fact, the opposite may be happening.

Less entertainment industry.

More local responsibility.

### The Actor Becomes an Organizer

By this period, Gümüşlükspor was becoming an important part of İşler's life.

This introduces a new Nejat into the archive.

Not:

the leading man.

Not:

the anti-hero.

Not:

the celebrity outsider.

But:

**the organizer.**

A football club requires:

- budgets,
- schedules,
- facilities,
- children,
- parents,
- coaches,
- administration,
- local relationships.

This is an interesting contrast with the public mythology of radical independence.

Running a local institution requires dependence on other people.

Cooperation.

Responsibility.

Patience.

### Cinema as a Different Kind of Work

Why cinema but not television?

Part of the answer is time.

A film has an end.

A television series may consume most of the year.

Film production is intense, but finite.

This difference fits İşler's developing philosophy.

He does not necessarily want to work less because work is meaningless.

He wants work to leave room for life.

Cinema allows a clearer boundary.

Television, at least in the production system he experienced, often did not.

### “Mesleğimi Ölene Kadar Bırakmam”

This is why another statement from the same period is so important.

İşler says, in effect, that he will not abandon his profession until death.

That sentence prevents a major misunderstanding.

The issue is not:

**acting versus not acting.**

It is:

**how much of life should acting be allowed to occupy?**

The answer is changing.

### Work Without Total Identity

Celebrity industries often encourage people to become identical with their professions.

Actor.

Musician.

Writer.

Director.

The job becomes the whole person.

İşler increasingly resists this.

He wants acting to remain important without becoming total.

That principle will later connect with:

- writing,
- football,
- Gümüşlük,
- friendship,
- production,
- time.

The self becomes larger than the career.

### The Health Crisis Is Still in the Background

The 2014 illness is obviously relevant to the timing of this decision.

But once again, the archive should avoid simplistic causality.

The desire for distance existed before the illness.

What the health crisis seems to change is not the existence of the desire but its urgency.

Before:

**I do not like working this way.**

After:

**Why should I spend limited time working this way?**

The problem becomes existential rather than merely professional.

### Istanbul as a Functional City

İşler's statement about Istanbul is especially revealing.

He no longer needs the city as a complete identity.

It becomes functional.

He goes there to work.

Then leaves.

This separation would have been difficult during the earlier stages of his career, when visibility and professional opportunity were tightly connected to being physically present in Istanbul.

By 2015, his established career gives him another option.

Fame, ironically, creates the possibility of escaping the geography that fame once required.

### Freedom Through Selectivity

The 2015 decision introduces a new definition of freedom.

Earlier public mythology often associated İşler's freedom with:

nightlife,

relationships,

rebellion,

not caring what others think.

The later version is less dramatic.

Freedom becomes:

**being able to say no to work.**

This is a much more material form of independence.

It requires enough professional security to reject offers.

It may require less financial appetite.

It requires accepting that more visibility is not always better.

### Less Career, More Life

The broader pattern is now becoming clear.

**2003:** the career is accelerating.

**2010:** the career system is becoming exhausting.

**2011–2012:** conventional ambition and success are increasingly questioned.

**2014:** mortality interrupts the trajectory.

**2015:** a different life structure becomes practical.

The question is no longer:

**How far can this career go?**

It becomes:

**How much career is enough?**

### A Useful Comparison with 2017

Two years later, İşler will explain the underlying philosophy much more elegantly.

His goal, he will say, is to **buy time**.

The 2015 interview shows that philosophy before it receives a name.

Living in Gümüşlük.

Going to Istanbul only for specific work.

Rejecting television series.

Continuing cinema.

These are already techniques for recovering time.

### A Useful Comparison with 2022

The Gümüşlük story will not remain permanent.

By 2022, İşler will explain that Gümüşlük itself has changed.

It has become more crowded.

Bodrum has expanded.

The quiet distance that once made the place valuable has weakened.

Eventually he closes his home there and spends more time in Istanbul again.

This matters because Gümüşlük should not be turned into a final destination myth.

It was a solution for a particular period.

### Place as a Strategy, Not Salvation

That later development helps interpret 2015 more accurately.

Gümüşlük is not:

**the place where Nejat İşler finally finds himself.**

It is:

**a practical attempt to organize life differently.**

When the conditions change, the strategy can change too.

This makes his relationship with place much less romantic and more consistent with his general philosophy.

He is not loyal to a lifestyle image.

He is loyal to control over his time.

### From Celebrity Individualism to Community

There is also an important moral transition.

The public Nejat İşler had often been imagined as a radical individualist.

The man who does what he wants.

The man who answers to nobody.

But Gümüşlükspor complicates that.

A football school is about other people's children.

A club is a collective institution.

Running it means accepting responsibility that does not exist merely for personal pleasure.

This anticipates what İşler will say explicitly in 2016:

after confronting mortality, he no longer wants to live only for himself.

The behavior begins appearing before the philosophy is fully articulated.

## Why This Interview Matters

The 1 July 2015 Anadolu Ajansı interview is one of the clearest transition documents in the Nejat İşler archive.

It establishes three things at once.

First:

**he is leaving television series, not acting.**

Second:

**Gümüşlük has become his primary home, while Istanbul becomes a place he visits for work.**

Third:

**withdrawal from the entertainment industry is accompanied by deeper involvement in a local community through Gümüşlükspor.**

This makes the interview an important bridge between the dissatisfaction expressed in 2010 and the more developed life philosophy expressed in 2017.

By 2015, Nejat İşler is no longer merely saying that he wants to escape the system.

He has begun building an alternative structure for his life.
