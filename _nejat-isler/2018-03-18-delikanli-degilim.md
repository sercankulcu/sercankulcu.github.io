## Kaybedenler Kulübü Yolda: Delikanlı Değilim

**Habertürk — 18 March 2018**  
**Interview by Mehmet Çalışkan**

Seven years after *Kaybedenler Kulübü*, Nejat İşler returned to Kaan Çaydamlı.

But neither the actor nor the character was the same.

The first film had helped create one of the strongest public myths around İşler.

Independent.

Rebellious.

Emotionally distant.

Successful with women.

Suspicious of conventional life.

A masculine outsider.

By the time *Kaybedenler Kulübü Yolda* arrived in 2018, that mythology had had years to harden.

The sequel therefore offered something unusual:

Nejat İşler was not only returning to a character.

He was returning to a public image partly built around that character.

### The Problem with "Delikanlı"

One of the most revealing moments in the interview comes when İşler addresses the masculine identity people attach to him.

He says, in effect, that people describe him as a “delikanlı,” but he does not think the label fits.

This is important because the word carries more than the literal meaning of “young man.”

In Turkish public culture, *delikanlı* can imply:

- toughness,
- courage,
- loyalty,
- masculine honor,
- directness,
- fearlessness,
- street credibility.

It is exactly the kind of identity celebrity culture had been attaching to İşler for years.

He resists it.

Again.

### Another External Identity

The pattern is now familiar.

**2003:** “leading man.”

**2011:** “loser / anti-hero.”

**2018:** “delikanlı.”

Each label contains something recognizable.

None is accepted as a complete definition.

İşler repeatedly finds himself in the same position:

other people build a simple character around him.

He complicates it.

### The Public Nejat Becomes More Stable Than the Private One

By 2018, the public mythology around İşler is stronger than ever.

The roles have accumulated.

*Barda.*

*Kaan.*

*Ercüment.*

Nightlife reports.

Media conflicts.

Gümüşlük.

Football.

Illness.

Each new layer reinforces an image of:

a hard,

independent,

unconventional man.

But the interviews repeatedly show a less coherent person.

He values home.

He values peace.

He dislikes excessive visibility.

He questions conventional success.

He wants companionship.

He worries about time.

That complexity does not fit neatly inside “delikanlı.”

### Returning to Kaan After Life Changed

The timing of the sequel is particularly important.

Between the first film in 2011 and the second in 2018, İşler had:

- criticized the television industry,
- survived a major health crisis,
- moved toward Gümüşlük,
- become involved with Gümüşlükspor,
- written a book,
- reconsidered money,
- reconsidered property,
- developed the idea of buying time.

So when he returns to Kaan, the actor has undergone major changes.

The same character is now being carried by a different biography.

### Aging the Anti-Hero

The first *Kaybedenler Kulübü* benefited from the seductive energy of its lifestyle.

Night.

Radio.

Women.

Drinking.

Conversation.

Friendship.

Refusal.

In youth, these can look like freedom.

In middle age, the same behaviors can acquire different meanings.

Repetition becomes visible.

Loneliness becomes more expensive.

Avoiding commitment can stop looking rebellious and start looking habitual.

The sequel therefore naturally raises a new question:

**What happens when the anti-hero grows older?**

### Freedom Has a Time Dimension

This question connects directly with İşler's own changing philosophy.

In 2011, low ambition and resistance to conventional expectations could still look like rebellion.

By 2017, freedom had become much more material:

control over time.

That difference matters.

Kaan's freedom is partly symbolic.

İşler's later freedom is logistical.

Where do I live?

How much do I work?

What do I own?

What can I refuse?

The actor has developed a more practical philosophy than the character.

### Women and the Persistence of the Persona

The interview again includes questions about women.

This is unsurprising.

Women had become one of the permanent subjects attached to Nejat İşler's public identity.

But the repetition itself is revealing.

The media keeps asking the same person to perform the same persona.

The audience expects continuity.

The celebrity is supposed to remain recognizable.

This is precisely the kind of repetition İşler had complained about in earlier interviews.

### Attraction Without Identity

İşler does not deny that female attention is pleasant.

But enjoying attention is different from building an identity around being desired.

That distinction is important.

The public mythology often reduces male celebrity into a form of conquest.

Women become proof of masculinity.

İşler's interviews are usually less triumphant than that framing.

This again complicates the “delikanlı” reading.

### Masculinity as Performance

There is a deeper archival theme here.

Nejat İşler repeatedly performs male characters who are:

- strong,
- damaged,
- violent,
- sexually confident,
- cynical,
- resistant to authority.

These performances create cultural models of masculinity.

But İşler himself repeatedly rejects being treated as the embodiment of those models.

This means that his career can be read as a tension between:

**performing masculinity**

and

**being assigned masculinity as identity.**

The two are not the same.

### The Actor Knows It Is a Role

This may sound obvious, but audience culture often forgets it.

The actor knows Kaan is constructed.

The audience can use Kaan to explain Nejat.

That asymmetry matters.

It is one reason why celebrity identity often becomes difficult to control.

The more convincing the performance, the stronger the temptation to treat it as biography.

### Acting Is Still Sacred

The interview also shows how seriously İşler continues to treat acting.

Despite years of criticizing the industry, distancing himself from television and reorganizing his life, the profession itself remains important.

This is consistent with his 2015 position:

**he can leave series without leaving acting.**

By 2018, the distinction has survived.

The industry is negotiable.

The profession is not.

### A Useful Comparison with 2012

The Marie Claire interview from 2012 becomes especially important here.

Then İşler said that the person he was at home did not resemble the person people imagined from outside.

Six years later, the same conflict appears in a different vocabulary.

2012:

**I am not really what I look like from outside.**

2018:

**People call me a delikanlı, but that is not really me.**

The continuity is striking.

### A Useful Comparison with 2003

The same theme can be traced all the way back.

2003:

the industry wants a leading man.

2018:

the public wants a masculine rebel.

The label changes as the career changes.

İşler's resistance remains.

This makes public image one of the strongest longitudinal themes in the archive.

### A Useful Comparison with 2022

By 2022, in the Oksijen interview, İşler will revisit another part of this mythology:

the audience's attraction to “bad” characters.

Again, he will separate performance from moral identity.

The long-term pattern becomes:

play the role fully,

but do not confuse the role with the person.

### Aging Without Reinventing the Brand

Another important aspect of the 2018 period is that İşler does not seem interested in reinventing himself through a polished “mature actor” image.

He is still sarcastic.

Still direct.

Still resistant to moral elevation.

This continuity matters.

Aging does not require turning the old persona into a respectable new brand.

Instead, he seems willing to let contradictions remain.

### The Cost of Becoming an Icon

*Kayıbedenler Kulübü* gave İşler enormous cultural recognition.

But recognition has a cost.

A successful role becomes a permanent reference point.

Years later, the actor may still be asked to explain:

Kaan,

women,

freedom,

losers,

masculinity.

The role that creates freedom professionally can create repetition publicly.

This is another paradox of celebrity.

### Returning Without Worshipping the Past

The sequel is therefore also a test of nostalgia.

It can celebrate the original mythology.

Or it can revisit it critically.

The aging of both actor and character creates space for the second possibility.

That makes the 2018 interview more interesting than simple promotion.

It becomes a conversation with an older version of the self.

### The Difference Between Image and Character

There is also an important distinction between:

**public image**

and

**fictional character.**

Kaan is constructed intentionally.

The public Nejat is constructed collectively.

By directors.

Journalists.

Photographers.

Audiences.

Gossip culture.

And İşler himself.

No single person controls the final result.

That may be why the public persona can become harder to escape than any single role.

### Why This Interview Matters

The March 2018 Habertürk interview is important because it captures Nejat İşler confronting one of the most durable identities attached to him:

the tough, rebellious, masculine “delikanlı.”

His rejection of the label continues a pattern visible since the beginning of the archive.

Other people repeatedly turn him into a type.

Leading man.

Loser.

Bad boy.

Delikanlı.

İşler repeatedly responds by making the picture less simple.

The return to Kaan Çaydamlı also gives the archive a rare longitudinal experiment.

The same actor plays the same culturally powerful character seven years later, after illness, Gümüşlük, writing, football and a major reorganization of life.

The result reveals a central tension:

**the public image wants continuity, while the person keeps changing.**
