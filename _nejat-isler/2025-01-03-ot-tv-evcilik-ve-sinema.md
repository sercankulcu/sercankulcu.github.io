## OT TV: Evcilik, Sinema ve Eski Nejat

**OT TV — 3 January 2025**

The January 2025 OT TV appearance is very different from Nejat İşler's more formal newspaper interviews.

The tone is loose.

Questions move quickly.

Serious cinema discussion can suddenly become a joke.

Personal memories sit beside provocative opinions.

That makes the interview less useful as a carefully structured statement of philosophy.

But it is valuable for another reason.

It shows Nejat İşler in a conversational mode much closer to the public personality people believe they know.

The difference between those two things is important.

### Evcilik Is the Starting Point

The first published section centers on *Evcilik*.

OT TV released the first substantial video on 3 January 2025 under the title:

**“Küçükken herkes ‘Evcilik’ oynadı.”**

The channel identifies İşler as its guest and frames the conversation around the new film.

The title is playful, but the idea behind the film is not.

*Evcilik* is built around people temporarily trying on another identity.

That makes the childhood game an effective metaphor.

Children play house by pretending to be adults.

The characters in the film also begin pretending.

The consequences are simply much more serious.

### Playing Another Person

This connects directly with acting.

An actor professionally pretends to be someone else.

But the actor knows it is a performance.

The danger in *Evcilik* begins when imitation enters ordinary relationships without the same distance.

People begin performing another couple's:

speech,

behavior,

desire,

social identity.

What begins as a game becomes humiliation.

### Identity Is Easier to Copy from Outside

The film again illustrates one of the strongest themes in İşler's archive.

People see surfaces.

They imitate surfaces.

But they do not necessarily understand the conditions underneath them.

This applies equally to:

class,

relationships,

celebrity,

masculinity.

It also applies to Nejat İşler himself.

### The OT TV Format Makes Persona Visible

This is why the interview format itself is interesting.

A formal newspaper interview usually creates a controlled Nejat İşler.

Long answers.

Clear themes.

Careful chronology.

OT TV produces something closer to:

banter,

provocation,

memory,

quick judgment,

irony.

This version is much closer to the public “Nejat” mythology.

But it would be a mistake to assume that informality means greater truth.

A relaxed interview is also a performance context.

### Every Interview Produces a Different Person

This archive increasingly demonstrates that there is no neutral interview.

Marie Claire produces one Nejat.

Socrates another.

Habertürk another.

Mirgün Cabas another.

OT TV another.

The interviewer, format and atmosphere change what becomes visible.

That is itself an important archival lesson.

### Family Memory

OT TV also published a second clip on 4 January under the title:

**“Herkese böyle bir anne tavsiye ederim.”**

This preserves a warmer family register that rarely dominates İşler's public image.

The contrast matters.

The actor who is culturally associated with:

darkness,

rebellion,

male independence

can shift almost immediately into affectionate family memory.

Again, the simplified public identity breaks apart.

### The Mother Behind the Rebel

Celebrity mythology often removes family.

The rebel appears to have created himself.

No parents.

No childhood dependence.

No domestic tenderness.

But İşler's interviews repeatedly return to:

his grandfather,

his father,

his mother,

his sister,

Eyüp.

The supposedly self-created outsider is deeply shaped by inherited relationships.

### Humor as Defense and Method

The OT TV clips also show how frequently İşler uses humor.

Sometimes to avoid seriousness.

Sometimes to make seriousness more tolerable.

Sometimes simply because the conversation is entertaining.

This is easy to underestimate in an archive dominated by themes such as:

mortality,

violence,

alienation,

work,

loss.

İşler is not consistently solemn.

Irony is one of his main ways of speaking.

### The Problem with Extracted Statements

The OT TV appearance later circulated heavily through short clips.

One of the most visible examples was his comparison of Nuri Bilge Ceylan and Zeki Demirkubuz.

OT TV itself later published a dedicated clip of that comparison.

Another channel circulated an edited version while explicitly identifying OT TV as the original interview source.

This is precisely where archival caution becomes necessary.

A long informal conversation can become publicly remembered through one provocative fragment.

### Clip Culture Creates Another Nejat

This process resembles celebrity mythology on a smaller scale.

A person speaks for a long period.

One sentence is extracted.

That sentence becomes:

headline,

short video,

social-media argument.

The fragment may be genuine.

But it becomes more important than the whole conversation.

The archive should therefore preserve context whenever possible.

### Nuri Bilge Ceylan and Zeki Demirkubuz

İşler's comparison between these two major directors became one of the most circulated parts of the interview.

The important archival point is not deciding whether the comparison was correct.

The important point is how easily a casual judgment becomes a definitive cultural position.

Later in 2025, İşler himself would revisit the matter in his Mirgün Cabas conversation with considerably more distance.

That later self-correction is more informative than treating the original remark as permanent doctrine.

### A Person Can Revise Himself

This is one of the advantages of a longitudinal archive.

A single interview freezes a person.

Twenty years of interviews show movement.

Someone can:

speak badly,

change position,

regret wording,

clarify,

laugh at an earlier self.

The archive should preserve the change rather than choose only the most dramatic quotation.

### Politics and the Risk of Casual Speech

The OT TV conversation also moved into politics.

Again, the informal atmosphere matters.

Political remarks made during conversational entertainment should not automatically be treated as carefully constructed political manifestos.

They are still part of the public record.

But their genre matters.

A casual opinion and a formal political statement are not equivalent.

### Why Verification Level Is Medium

### A Useful Comparison with Habertürk

This file should be read beside the 2 January Habertürk interview.

The dates are almost identical.

The versions of İşler are not.

**Habertürk:**

producer,

structure-builder,

professional thinker.

**OT TV:**

actor,

storyteller,

provocateur,

son,

public personality.

Neither invalidates the other.

Together they demonstrate the instability of public identity.

### A Useful Comparison with Mirgün Cabas

The later 2025 Mirgün Cabas interview is even more useful.

There, İşler looks back at some of the public controversies surrounding cinema with more reflection.

That creates a sequence:

**January:** spontaneous judgment.

**November:** retrospective evaluation.

A conventional celebrity profile might choose one.

An archive should preserve both.

### A Useful Comparison with Miras

There is also a subtler connection with *Miras*.

The OT TV conversation repeatedly allows fragments of older Nejats to appear.

Child.

Son.

Young cinema enthusiast.

Provocative actor.

Older professional.

This is similar to what *Miras* does more deliberately.

The present self contains previous selves.

They do not always agree.

### Why This Interview Matters

The January 2025 OT TV appearance is not one of the most philosophically systematic interviews in Nejat İşler's archive.

That is exactly why it deserves a carefully marked secondary place.

It reveals how the Nejat İşler public persona is produced in real time:

through jokes,

memories,

strong opinions,

informality,

and later extracted clips.

It also demonstrates why an archive must distinguish between:

**what was said**

and

**what later became famous.**

The most provocative fragment is not necessarily the most important part of a conversation.

And the person speaking in January does not have to remain identical to the person who looks back on those words in November.
