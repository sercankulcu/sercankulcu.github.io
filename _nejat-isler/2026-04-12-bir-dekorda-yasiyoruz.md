## Bir Dekorda Yaşıyoruz

**BirGün TV — 12 April 2026**  
**Cevap Hakkı #07 — Timur Soykan**

In April 2026, Nejat İşler returned to a question that had followed him for almost twenty years:

**How much does a person really need?**

The vocabulary has changed.

Earlier interviews spoke about:

money,

property,

time,

work,

Gümüşlük,

freedom.

In *Cevap Hakkı*, the same philosophy appears as a criticism of contemporary life itself.

People want more comfort.

More security.

More happiness.

More measurable success.

Yet İşler argues that this search may have made life less enjoyable rather than more.

His description is deliberately theatrical:

**“Bir dekorda yaşıyoruz.”**

We are living inside a set.

### Life Has Become Numbers

İşler describes modern life as increasingly reduced to people and numbers.

Income.

Prices.

Followers.

Targets.

Statistics.

Measurements.

Life begins to resemble administration rather than experience.

His complaint is not that numbers are useless.

It is that they can become the main language through which value is understood.

### From Living to Managing

There is an important difference between:

**living a life**

and

**managing a life.**

Management requires:

planning,

optimization,

risk reduction,

measurement.

All of these things are useful.

But when every part of life becomes a project to optimize, spontaneity begins to disappear.

The person becomes both manager and product.

### "Hayattan Zevk Alma Bitti"

İşler's most pessimistic claim is that enjoyment itself has weakened.

He says:

**“Hayattan zevk alma bitti.”**

He compares modern people to ants, moving constantly inside a system of tasks and calculations.

This is a late version of his much older criticism of overwork.

### The Ant Has Replaced the Rebel

The metaphor is interesting.

Earlier public Nejat İşler mythology was built around:

rebellion,

freedom,

refusal.

The 2026 problem is almost the opposite.

People no longer need obvious authorities forcing them to work continuously.

They organize themselves.

Schedules.

Goals.

Expenses.

Career plans.

Comfort requirements.

The system becomes internal.

### Comfort Can Become Another Obligation

İşler specifically criticizes the modern obsession with comfort.

Everyone wants a comfortable life.

That sounds reasonable.

But comfort has an economic cost.

The more comfort becomes necessary, the more income becomes necessary.

The more income is necessary, the harder it may become to refuse work.

Comfort can therefore reduce freedom.

### This Is an Old Nejat İşler Argument

The logic is almost identical to his 2007 and 2017 philosophy.

If lifestyle expands, compulsory work expands with it.

If life can remain smaller, a person may need less money.

If less money is required, more time can remain under personal control.

2026 gives the old equation another name:

**simplicity.**

### "Ne Yapayım Parayı?"

The official BirGün TV title foregrounds another striking statement:

**“Ne yapayım parayı?”**

What should I do with the money?

The title continues:

**“Sırt çantası kadar olmak güzel!”**

Being reduced to what fits into a backpack is beautiful.

This is perhaps the most compact late expression of his anti-accumulation philosophy.

### The Backpack as Philosophy

A backpack represents:

mobility,

limits,

selection.

You cannot carry everything.

So you have to decide what matters.

This is the opposite of property accumulation.

A house allows possessions to expand.

A backpack forces editing.

### Freedom Through Portability

The backpack also solves a problem that has appeared throughout İşler's life.

Property fixes the person to place.

Possessions require:

storage,

maintenance,

protection.

A smaller material life makes movement easier.

This connects directly with his long attraction to:

travel,

Gümüşlük,

temporary housing,

limited commitments.

### 2007 to 2026

The continuity is remarkable.

**2007:**  
money should buy time rather than a larger lifestyle.

**2017:**  
the goal is explicitly to buy time.

**2021:**  
he describes deliberately making life smaller.

**2025:**  
the best inheritance is memory, not property.

**2026:**  
a backpack can be enough.

These are not isolated provocations.

They form a philosophy lasting nearly two decades.

### More Comfort, Less Pleasure

The central paradox is this:

modern life has become more comfortable in many ways.

Yet İşler thinks people enjoy it less.

That is worth taking seriously even without accepting it completely.

Comfort removes difficulty.

But some experiences gain meaning partly because they require:

effort,

waiting,

uncertainty,

shared struggle.

If all discomfort becomes something to eliminate, life may also lose contrast.

### "Always Happy"

İşler criticizes another expectation:

people increasingly want to be continuously happy and never experience sadness.

This is perhaps an impossible psychological standard.

A life without sadness would also require a life without:

loss,

attachment,

risk,

love.

Happiness becomes artificial if every unpleasant emotion is treated as system failure.

### The Decor

This leads back to the interview's strongest metaphor.

A film set looks real.

A room.

A street.

A house.

But behind the visible wall may be nothing.

A set is designed to produce the appearance of reality.

When İşler says:

**“Bir dekorda yaşıyoruz,”**

the criticism is that contemporary life may increasingly prioritize appearance over lived substance.

### The Celebrity Knows Something About Sets

The metaphor is especially suitable coming from an actor.

İşler has spent decades working inside literal sets.

He knows professionally that something can:

look like a home

without being a home.

Look like a street

without being a street.

Look emotionally convincing

while being technically constructed.

So “decor” is not merely a random metaphor.

### Social Life as Production

The metaphor can be extended.

Modern identity increasingly requires production.

Photographs.

Profiles.

Homes.

Clothing.

Lifestyle.

Public opinions.

Success.

Everything can become part of a visible scene.

The person starts designing the life that others will see.

### Public Image Returns Again

This has an obvious connection with İşler's own celebrity experience.

For decades, he has argued that the public Nejat İşler is partly constructed.

Leading man.

Rebel.

Kaan.

Delikanlı.

Difficult man.

The image is a kind of decor.

The person lives behind it.

### The Difference Is That Everyone Now Has a Public Set

Celebrity culture once applied mainly to famous people.

Social media expanded some of its mechanisms to ordinary life.

People increasingly manage:

images,

audiences,

personal narratives.

In that sense, İşler's old problem with celebrity becomes a general social problem.

Everybody can now experience some pressure to perform an identity.

### Miras in the Background

BirGün TV introduces İşler in the context of his recent book *Miras*, emphasizing his writing, memory and the private fractures he puts into language.

That matters because the simplicity discussion is not separate from the late writing period.

*Miras* asks what remains.

The 2026 conversation asks what is actually necessary.

The questions are closely related.

### What Survives When the Decor Disappears?

Property can disappear.

Money can disappear.

Public images certainly disappear.

What remains longer?

Stories.

People's memories.

Shared experiences.

This was already the answer emerging from the 2025 MediaCat interview.

The 2026 philosophy reduces the external structure even further.

### The Late Nejat Is Not Asking for More

This is one of the clearest differences between conventional career narratives and İşler's late interviews.

A person with decades of success is normally asked:

What is next?

A bigger film?

Directing?

More books?

A larger company?

İşler repeatedly turns the logic downward.

Less.

Smaller.

Enough.

### Minimalism Without Branding

It would be misleading to turn this into fashionable lifestyle minimalism.

He is not presenting a clean design philosophy.

Nor a productivity system.

His version is more existential.

Possessions create needs.

Needs create obligations.

Obligations consume time.

Therefore reducing possessions may protect life.

### A Useful Comparison with 2007

The 2007 Ulusoy interview is now indispensable.

There İşler already said:

**“Amacım zamanı satın almak.”**

The 2026 backpack idea is almost the physical image of that philosophy.

If everything you need fits into a backpack, the amount of life that must be sold to maintain possessions becomes smaller.

### A Useful Comparison with 2021

In Milliyet Sanat, İşler called the strategy:

**making life smaller.**

2026 shows the concept reaching almost its logical extreme.

The smaller the material life,

the larger the possibility of movement.

### A Useful Comparison with 2025

MediaCat asked what kind of inheritance he wanted to leave.

His answer was essentially:

good memories.

2026 continues the same hierarchy.

Material accumulation moves downward.

Experience moves upward.

### A Useful Comparison with the Young Nejat

The young İşler sold books on the street.

He liked the ability to:

open when he wanted,

close when he wanted,

move.

The older İşler talks about a backpack.

The scale has changed.

The attraction to mobility has not.

### Is This Withdrawal?

As always, it would be easy to interpret the philosophy as withdrawal from society.

But the full archive argues against that reading.

İşler repeatedly participates in:

film,

writing,

football,

friendship,

public discussion.

He does not appear to want no life.

He wants less machinery around life.

### Less Structure, More Experience

That may be the best summary of the 2026 position.

Less:

property,

optimization,

compulsory comfort,

performance.

More:

time,

movement,

people,

experience.

### Why This Interview Matters

The April 2026 *Cevap Hakkı* interview is important because it shows that Nejat İşler's long-running philosophy of time and material simplicity remains intact in his mid-fifties.

In fact, it has become even more concentrated.

The central metaphor is no longer a house in Gümüşlük.

It is a backpack.

And the central criticism is no longer only television or celebrity.

It is modern life itself.

The late argument can be summarized like this:

**people have built increasingly comfortable lives, but maintaining those lives can make them less free.**

The alternative is not necessarily poverty or disappearance.

It is knowing what is enough.

Perhaps the entire twenty-year archive can be reduced to that word:

**enough.**
